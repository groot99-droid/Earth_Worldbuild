=== era/anthropocene
title: Anthropocene
type: era
date: c. 1950
themes: earth-systems, technology
src: 2008986742a5
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Anthropocene is a proposed geological interval in which human activity is the dominant influence on Earth's systems. In March 2024 the International Union of Geological Sciences declined to formalize it as an official epoch, but the term remains widely used.
--- fact 1
A proposed start date is around 1950, tied to the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]] and to markers such as radioactive fallout.
--- fact 2
Other proposed starts include the beginning of agriculture and the [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]].
--- fact 3
Cited signals include changes to atmospheric carbon dioxide, plastics, and species extinction rates.
--- fact 4
The Subcommission on Quaternary Stratigraphy voted on 4 March 2024 to reject formalization as an epoch, and the IUGS upheld the result on 20 March 2024. The formal geological decision does not settle the scientific or cultural question.
--- context
The Anthropocene is a lens on the [[era/information-age|Information Age]] and the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]]. See [[observer-note/the-species-as-a-geological-force|The Species as a Geological Force]].
--- observer
The Observer regards the Anthropocene as the point where the species became legible in the rock record. It is the first time a single species' behavior is a planetary variable.
--- question 1
When, if ever, should the Anthropocene be dated from?

=== era/archean-eon
title: Archean Eon
type: era
date: 4 – 2.5 Ga
themes: earth-systems
src: 80003b3422e4
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Archean Eon (about 4.0 to 2.5 billion years ago) is the interval in which the first continents stabilized and life first appeared. The atmosphere had almost no free oxygen.
--- fact 1
Some of the oldest widely accepted fossil evidence, stromatolites in Western Australia, is about 3.5 billion years old.
--- fact 2
Continental cores (cratons) formed and grew, and plate-tectonic-like processes began at some point in this eon.
--- fact 3
Life at this time was microbial. See [[event/origin-of-life|Origin of Life]].
--- fact 4
Photosynthesis evolved before the end of the eon, setting up the [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]].
--- context
The Archean is the long, slow prelude to the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]]. It contains the first living things and the chemical setup that later made complex life possible.
--- observer
An observer arriving in the Archean would find a living planet with no obvious candidates for study. The Observer treats it as the deep root of the entire tree.
--- question 1
When did plate tectonics begin?
--- question 2
Are the oldest claimed microfossils real?

=== era/cambrian-period
title: Cambrian Period
type: era
date: 538.8 – 485.4 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: 852873209e47
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Cambrian Period, from 538.8 to 485.4 million years ago, is the first period of the Paleozoic Era. It is the interval in which most major animal body plans, and the first animals with hard parts, appear in the fossil record.
--- fact 1
The Cambrian Period spans 538.8 to 485.4 million years ago. Its base, which is also the base of the Paleozoic Era, is defined at Fortune Head, Newfoundland, by the first appearance of the burrow trace fossil Treptichnus pedum.
--- fact 2
The [[event/cambrian-explosion|Cambrian Explosion]], the appearance of most major animal groups in the fossil record, falls in roughly the first 13 to 25 million years of the period.
--- fact 3
[[species/trilobites|Trilobites]] first appear about 521 million years ago and become one of the most common animals on the sea floor.
--- fact 4
Exceptionally preserved soft-bodied fossils are known from the Chengjiang deposits of China, about 518 million years old, and the Burgess Shale of Canada, about 508 million years old.
--- fact 5
Animals lived almost entirely in the sea, with high sea level, shallow seas across the continents and a generally warm climate. The record of plants on land begins in the Ordovician; see [[event/colonization-of-land|Colonization of Land]].
--- context
The Cambrian Period follows the [[era/ediacaran-period|Ediacaran Period]] and opens the [[era/paleozoic-era|Paleozoic Era]]; the [[era/ordovician-period|Ordovician Period]] comes next. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer reads the Cambrian fossil record as an early case of a general problem: what is known of the past is what happened to be preserved. Hard parts leave records, so a sudden arrival of animals is partly the arrival of animals that fossilize. Evidence that the fossil sequence closely tracks real diversity would weaken this emphasis on preservation.
--- question 1
How much of the Cambrian Explosion is a real burst of evolution and how much a burst of preservation?
--- question 2
What triggered skeletons in several animal groups within a few million years?

=== era/carboniferous-period
title: Carboniferous Period
type: era
date: 358.9 – 298.9 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: 63293f58fdd4
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Carboniferous Period, from 358.9 to 298.9 million years ago, is the fifth period of the Paleozoic Era. It is named for the coal its swamp forests laid down, and it is the interval of the first amniote vertebrates and of the assembly of the supercontinent Pangaea.
--- fact 1
The Carboniferous Period spans 358.9 to 298.9 million years ago. Its name means coal-bearing, and in North America it is divided into the Mississippian and Pennsylvanian.
--- fact 2
Swamp forests on tropical lowlands, dominated by tree-sized club-mosses such as Lepidodendron, buried the plant matter that became coal. Why so much coal formed in this period is debated. Britain's coalfields belong to it, and their coal was a major fuel of the [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]].
--- fact 3
Estimates for late Carboniferous atmospheric oxygen run as high as about 30 percent, against about 21 percent today, and their reliability is debated. Giant arthropods lived then, including the dragonfly relative Meganeura, with a wingspan of about 70 centimetres.
--- fact 4
The earliest known amniotes, animals with eggs able to develop on land, appear, including Hylonomus from Nova Scotia, about 312 million years old.
--- fact 5
The Late Paleozoic Ice Age, centred on Gondwana, began in this period. The coal forests collapsed about 305 million years ago as the climate dried, and the collision of Gondwana and Euramerica built much of Pangaea.
--- context
The Carboniferous Period follows the [[era/devonian-period|Devonian Period]] and is followed by the [[era/permian-period|Permian Period]] within the [[era/paleozoic-era|Paleozoic Era]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]]; its forests continue [[event/colonization-of-land|Colonization of Land]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the carbon these forests locked away was dug up about 300 million years later and burned by the species, returning it to the air. Nothing about the forests was aimed at that result; it is an inheritance received by accident. The reading concerns only the link across time, and the debate over why so much coal formed does not affect it.
--- question 1
Why did so much coal accumulate in this period and not in others?
--- question 2
How high did atmospheric oxygen rise, and how much did it limit the size of insects?

=== era/cenozoic-era
title: Cenozoic Era
type: era
date: 66 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: a4b1365ab9e2
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Cenozoic Era began 66 million years ago and continues today. It is the age of mammals, grasslands, and cooling climate, and it includes all of human history.
--- fact 1
Periods: Paleogene, Neogene, Quaternary.
--- fact 2
Mammals and birds diversified after the [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]].
--- fact 3
Global climate cooled overall, and ice sheets appeared at the poles, then repeatedly expanded and retreated.
--- fact 4
Primates appeared early in the era; the hominin lineage diverged about 7 million years ago. See [[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]].
--- context
The Cenozoic contains the [[era/pleistocene-epoch|Pleistocene Epoch]], the [[era/holocene-epoch|Holocene Epoch]], and the proposed [[era/anthropocene|Anthropocene]].
--- observer
The Observer's own subject appears late in the Cenozoic, in its final fraction of a percent. Most of the era is the long cooling that shaped the world the species inherited.
--- question 1
Which climate shifts most influenced hominin evolution?

=== era/cretaceous-period
title: Cretaceous Period
type: era
date: 143.1 – 66 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: ee65eff91b76
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Cretaceous Period, from about 143.1 to 66 million years ago, is the last period of the Mesozoic Era and the longest of the Phanerozoic Eon. It is the interval of the first flowering plants and a warm greenhouse world, and it ends with the extinction of the non-avian dinosaurs.
--- fact 1
The Cretaceous Period spans about 143.1 to 66.0 million years ago; older references give its start as 145.0 million years ago. The name comes from the Latin word for chalk. Its top, the base of the Paleogene, is defined at El Kef in Tunisia.
--- fact 2
The climate was a warm greenhouse, and sea level was high, with shallow seas across the continents, including the Western Interior Seaway of North America, and thick chalk deposits.
--- fact 3
[[species/flowering-plants|Flowering Plants]] appear, with the oldest widely accepted pollen about 134 million years old, and diversified rapidly alongside pollinating insects. The [[event/breakup-of-pangaea|Breakup of Pangaea]] continued as the South Atlantic opened.
--- fact 4
Late Cretaceous [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]] include Tyrannosaurus and Triceratops in North America, birds diversified, and mosasaurs were large marine reptiles. Mammals were mostly small, and whether the modern placental orders arose before or after the end-Cretaceous extinction is debated.
--- fact 5
The [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]], about 66 million years ago, followed the Chicxulub impact and coincided with the eruption of the Deccan Traps, and killed about three-quarters of species.
--- context
The Cretaceous Period follows the [[era/jurassic-period|Jurassic Period]] and closes the [[era/mesozoic-era|Mesozoic Era]], giving way to the [[era/paleogene-period|Paleogene Period]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the Cretaceous ends with dinosaurs at or near their greatest diversity, ended by a rock from space and, probably, volcanic stress. The Observer reads long, stable-looking intervals as provisional, since nothing in the record of the last million years before the boundary announces what was about to happen. Evidence of a long decline in dinosaur diversity before the impact would weaken this reading.
--- question 1
Were non-avian dinosaurs already in decline before the end-Cretaceous impact?
--- question 2
Did the modern orders of placental mammals originate in the Cretaceous or only after the extinction?

=== era/cryogenian-period
title: Cryogenian Period
type: era
date: 720 – 635 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: 059ebd735026
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Cryogenian Period, from 720 to 635 million years ago, is the middle period of the Neoproterozoic Era. It is the interval of the two global-scale glaciations that gave rise to the Snowball Earth hypothesis.
--- fact 1
The Cryogenian Period spans 720 to 635 million years ago. Its base is set by a numerical age near the start of the first glaciation, and its top, the base of the [[era/ediacaran-period|Ediacaran Period]], is defined in the Flinders Ranges of South Australia.
--- fact 2
Two major glaciations are recorded: the Sturtian (about 717 to 660 million years ago) and the Marinoan (about 650 to 635 million years ago). Glacial deposits formed at low paleolatitudes on several continents, which supports the hypothesis that ice reached the tropics; see [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]].
--- fact 3
Each glaciation is capped by unusual carbonate rocks. The usual explanation for the end of each is a build-up of volcanic carbon dioxide strong enough to melt the ice; this is a hypothesis.
--- fact 4
Banded iron formations, largely absent for well over a billion years, reappear in Sturtian-age deposits, interpreted as evidence of iron-rich seawater under the ice.
--- fact 5
Eukaryotes survived. Molecular fossils that some researchers attribute to sponges appear in Cryogenian rocks, but that attribution is disputed and the oldest firmly accepted animal fossils are younger.
--- context
The Cryogenian Period follows the [[era/tonian-period|Tonian Period]] and precedes the [[era/ediacaran-period|Ediacaran Period]] within the [[era/neoproterozoic-era|Neoproterozoic Era]]. It is the period of [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]] and belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer treats the Cryogenian as a test of how much a living planet can lose and still continue: life survived at least one ice age lasting tens of millions of years. The Observer cautions against reading this as design, since many lineages may have ended in the ice and the survivors are the only ones the record shows. The reading would lose force if the ice proved much less extensive than the Snowball hypothesis proposes.
--- question 1
Was the ocean fully frozen, or did open water and refuges remain?
--- question 2
What part did the glaciations play in the later rise of large complex organisms?

=== era/devonian-period
title: Devonian Period
type: era
date: 419.2 – 358.9 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: 281aaf56a4d9
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Devonian Period, from 419.2 to 358.9 million years ago, is the fourth period of the Paleozoic Era. It is often called the Age of Fishes, and it is the interval of the first forests, the first limbed vertebrates and a prolonged late extinction.
--- fact 1
The Devonian Period spans 419.2 to 358.9 million years ago and is named after Devon in England. Its base is defined at Klonk in the Czech Republic, and its top, the base of the Carboniferous, at La Serre in France.
--- fact 2
Fishes diversified widely, including armoured placoderms, sharks, lobe-finned fishes and ray-finned fishes, and the Middle Devonian was a peak of Paleozoic reef building.
--- fact 3
The Rhynie chert in Scotland, about 407 million years old, preserves early land plants and arthropods in fine detail. Archaeopteris, a tree with woody trunks, formed early forests, and the Gilboa fossil forest in New York is about 385 million years old.
--- fact 4
[[species/tiktaalik|Tiktaalik]], about 375 million years old, has fins with wrist-like bones. Ichthyostega and Acanthostega from Greenland, about 365 million years old, had limbs with digits.
--- fact 5
The [[event/late-devonian-extinction|Late Devonian Extinction]], about 372 to 359 million years ago, came in at least two pulses and removed an estimated 75 percent of species, heavily affecting reef builders.
--- context
The Devonian Period follows the [[era/silurian-period|Silurian Period]] and is followed by the [[era/carboniferous-period|Carboniferous Period]] within the [[era/paleozoic-era|Paleozoic Era]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and holds the central stretch of [[event/colonization-of-land|Colonization of Land]].
--- observer
The Observer reads the Devonian as a period in which life rebuilt the land surface: roots, soils and the first forests changed weathering, rivers and the carbon cycle. The spread of land plants is a candidate cause of the late extinction, a case of one group's success altering conditions for many others. That link is a hypothesis, and it would weaken if the extinction proved to have mainly non-biological causes.
--- question 1
How large was the role of land plants in the Late Devonian extinction?
--- question 2
Did the first limbed vertebrates evolve limbs for life in shallow water rather than for moving onto land?

=== era/ediacaran-period
title: Ediacaran Period
type: era
date: 635 – 538.8 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: 2c556a026cef
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Ediacaran Period, from 635 to 538.8 million years ago, is the last period of the Proterozoic Eon. It is the interval in which the first large, complex organisms appear in the fossil record.
--- fact 1
The Ediacaran Period spans 635 to 538.8 million years ago. It was formally ratified in 2004 as the first new geological period added to the time scale in over a century, and its base is defined at Enorama Creek in the Flinders Ranges of South Australia.
--- fact 2
The [[species/ediacaran-biota|Ediacaran Biota]] include disc-, frond- and quilt-shaped organisms known from Newfoundland, England, Namibia, the White Sea coast of Russia and the Ediacara Hills of South Australia. The oldest assemblages are about 575 million years old.
--- fact 3
Cholesterol-related lipids found with Dickinsonia fossils have been read as evidence that it was an animal, although its place in the animal family tree remains unclear.
--- fact 4
Cloudina, a tubular organism from about 550 million years ago, is among the earliest animals with a mineralized skeleton, and some specimens carry borings interpreted as marks of predation.
--- fact 5
Trace fossils of animals moving through sediment appear late in the period, and the classic Ediacaran assemblages decline near the start of the Cambrian; see [[event/cambrian-explosion|Cambrian Explosion]].
--- context
The Ediacaran Period follows the [[era/cryogenian-period|Cryogenian Period]] and closes the [[era/neoproterozoic-era|Neoproterozoic Era]] and the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]], giving way to the [[era/cambrian-period|Cambrian Period]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the Ediacaran is the first period whose living things were large enough to leave an obvious record, and that many of them have no clear living relatives. The record does not show that this first appearance of large bodies led straight into the animals that followed, and the Observer reads that as contingency. Evidence that most Ediacaran forms were direct ancestors of later animal groups would weaken the reading.
--- question 1
Which Ediacaran forms, if any, are ancestors of living animal groups?
--- question 2
What ended the classic assemblages: extinction, competition with newer animals, or changing chemistry?

=== era/epipaleolithic
title: Epipaleolithic
type: era
date: c. 20 ka – 10000 BCE
themes: technology, kinship
src: e2f4bd05b9dc
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Epipaleolithic is the final phase of the Paleolithic in the Near East and North Africa, from about 20,000 to about 10,000 BCE. It is the interval of small composite stone tools, of foragers beginning to stay in one place, and of the last stage before farming.
--- fact 1
The Epipaleolithic runs from about 20,000 to about 10,000 BCE in the Levant and neighbouring regions. The name is used mainly for the Near East and North Africa; Europe uses Mesolithic for the following stage.
--- fact 2
Toolmakers made microliths, small geometric stone pieces such as lunates and triangles, which were fixed into handles or shafts to make composite tools.
--- fact 3
Ohalo II, a site on the Sea of Galilee in Israel about 23,000 years old, preserves brush huts, hearths and the remains of well over a hundred plant species, including wild cereals. Some researchers argue that this shows early cultivation; that claim is debated.
--- fact 4
The Natufian culture, about 12,500 to 9,500 BCE, built semi-permanent villages while still foraging, used sickle blades and buried its dead in cemeteries. A burial at Ain Mallaha in Israel that holds a person and a puppy is about 12,000 years old.
--- fact 5
The cold, dry [[event/younger-dryas|Younger Dryas]] is proposed by some scholars as a reason for greater reliance on cultivated plants, a link that is debated. The Epipaleolithic leads into the Pre-Pottery Neolithic, whose earliest sites include [[place/jericho|Jericho]] and [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]]; see [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]].
--- context
The Epipaleolithic follows the [[era/upper-paleolithic|Upper Paleolithic]] and is followed by the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]] in the Near East. It closes the [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]] and belongs to the theme threads of [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that in the Epipaleolithic Near East some steps of the Settling appear before farming does: stored food, semi-permanent villages and cemeteries. The order matters, because it suggests that settlement may in places have come first and agriculture after. The reading would weaken if the Natufian sites were shown to have depended on cultivated crops from the start.
--- question 1
Did Natufian people cultivate cereals, or only gather wild ones?
--- question 2
How much did late Pleistocene climate change shape the move toward farming?

=== era/holocene-epoch
title: Holocene Epoch
type: era
date: c. 9700 BCE
themes: earth-systems
src: 2419e8767486
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Holocene Epoch began about 11,700 years ago, at the end of the last ice age, and is the warm, relatively stable interval in which agriculture, cities, and writing all arose.
--- fact 1
The Holocene is an interglacial period with a comparatively stable climate.
--- fact 2
All of recorded history falls inside it.
--- fact 3
Its lower boundary is defined by a marker in Greenland ice cores.
--- fact 4
Human population grew from a few million to about eight billion during it.
--- context
The Holocene is the geological frame for the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]] through the [[era/information-age|Information Age]]. See [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]].
--- observer
The Observer notes a striking correlation: the species built farms and cities only after the climate stopped swinging. Whether this is cause, coincidence, or both is a serious open question.
--- question 1
Did the stable Holocene climate make agriculture possible, or did agriculture arise anyway?

The Industrial Age (about 1760 to 1945) is the era in which machines and fossil fuels transformed production, transport, and warfare, and in which populations and cities grew rapidly.

Steam power, factories, railways, and telegraphs spread; see [[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]] and [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]].

Electric power, oil, and chemistry expanded the transformation in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; see [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]].

The era closes with the two world wars: [[event/world-war-i|World War I]] and [[event/world-war-ii|World War II]].

The Industrial Age follows the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]] and precedes the [[era/information-age|Information Age]]. The [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]] overlaps its first century.
--- observer
The Observer sees a species learning to release ancient stored sunlight as fuel. This one change multiplied the power available to each person, and made sanctioned harm far more destructive.

How much of the industrial takeoff was contingent on specific locations?

=== era/iron-age
title: Iron Age
type: era
date: c. 1200 – 500 BCE

--- summary
The Iron Age (about 1200 to 500 BCE, varying by region) is the era in which iron tools and weapons became widespread and new empires and belief systems emerged.
--- fact 1
Iron ore is more widely available than tin and copper; see [[technology/iron-smelting|Iron Smelting]].
--- fact 2
The [[technology/alphabet|Alphabet]] spread widely and simplified literacy.
--- fact 3
Large territorial empires, including the [[culture/achaemenid-persians|Achaemenid Persians]], emerge near the end of the era.
--- fact 4
The [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] begins in this period.
--- context
The Iron Age follows the [[event/bronze-age-collapse|Bronze Age Collapse]] and leads into [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]].
--- observer
The Observer sees the Iron Age as a democratization of tools and, therefore, of armies: cheaper metal meant more people could be equipped.

=== era/jurassic-period
title: Jurassic Period
type: era
date: 201.4 – 143.1 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: 3c18385418e1
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Jurassic Period, from 201.4 to about 143.1 million years ago, is the middle period of the Mesozoic Era. It is the interval of the largest land animals, the first known birds, and the opening of the Atlantic as Pangaea broke apart.
--- fact 1
The Jurassic Period spans 201.4 million years ago to about 143.1 million years ago and is named after the Jura Mountains. Its top is less firm: the base of the Cretaceous has no agreed marker section, and older references give 145.0 million years ago.
--- fact 2
The [[event/breakup-of-pangaea|Breakup of Pangaea]] began with the opening of the Central Atlantic, and Laurasia and Gondwana began to separate. The climate was warm and sea level high, with no lasting polar ice caps.
--- fact 3
Sauropods, stegosaurs and large theropods such as Allosaurus are known from the Morrison Formation of the western United States, about 155 to 148 million years old. See [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]].
--- fact 4
[[species/archaeopteryx|Archaeopteryx]] from the Solnhofen limestone of Bavaria, about 150 million years old, is the best-known early bird. The feathered theropod Anchiornis from China, about 160 million years old, shows that feathers were widespread among small theropods.
--- fact 5
Ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs and pliosaurs were the main marine reptiles. Mammals remained mostly small, though Castorocauda, an otter-like swimmer from China, is about 164 million years old.
--- context
The Jurassic Period follows the [[era/triassic-period|Triassic Period]] and is followed by the [[era/cretaceous-period|Cretaceous Period]] within the [[era/mesozoic-era|Mesozoic Era]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the Jurassic holds both the largest land animals known and, beside them, the first records of a small feathered lineage that would outlast every large dinosaur. What looks dominant at one time is not a reliable guide to what continues, and the Observer reads this as a caution about judging any period by its most conspicuous groups. The reading would weaken if the survival of birds followed from traits already obvious in the Jurassic.
--- question 1
Where within theropod evolution did flight begin, and did it arise more than once?
--- question 2
How diverse were Jurassic mammals, given how few fossils survive?

=== era/lower-paleolithic
title: Lower Paleolithic
type: era
date: c. 3.3 Ma – 300 ka
themes: technology, kinship
src: 8fd3470a7cd3
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Lower Paleolithic is the earliest part of the Paleolithic, from about 3.3 million to about 300,000 years ago. It covers the first stone tools, the long Acheulean handaxe tradition, and the spread of Homo erectus and its relatives across Africa and Eurasia.
--- fact 1
The Lower Paleolithic runs from about 3.3 million to about 300,000 years ago. Its start depends on how the earliest tools are counted: tools from Lomekwi 3 in Kenya are about 3.3 million years old, and the Oldowan begins about 2.6 million years ago. Africa uses the name Earlier Stone Age.
--- fact 2
The [[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]] are simple flaked cores and sharp flakes, made by early hominins such as [[species/homo-habilis|Homo habilis]]. The Acheulean, marked by bifacial handaxes and cleavers, appears in East Africa about 1.76 million years ago and persists in places until about 300,000 years ago.
--- fact 3
[[species/homo-erectus|Homo erectus]] spread beyond Africa: hominin fossils and stone tools at Dmanisi in Georgia are about 1.8 million years old.
--- fact 4
Firm evidence of controlled fire is about 1 million years old, and habitual use is well established by about 400,000 years ago; see [[event/control-of-fire|Control of Fire]].
--- fact 5
Wooden throwing spears from Schöningen in Germany are about 300,000 years old. How much early hominins hunted and how much they scavenged is debated.
--- context
The Lower Paleolithic opens the [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]] and is followed by the [[era/middle-paleolithic|Middle Paleolithic]]. It sits within the [[era/pleistocene-epoch|Pleistocene Epoch]] and belongs to the theme threads of [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]]. See also the essay [[observer-note/fire-then-everything|Fire, Then Everything]].
--- observer
The Observer files the Lower Paleolithic under the longest tool-lineage: one handaxe design was made, with small changes, for well over a million years across much of Africa and Eurasia, and by more than one hominin species. The Observer reads that stability as a fact about how skills were passed on, without claiming to know how the makers thought. The reading would weaken if the sameness came from the raw material or the task alone, not from copying.
--- question 1
Which hominin made the Lomekwi tools?
--- question 2
How much of the Lower Paleolithic diet came from hunting and how much from scavenging?

=== era/medieval-period
title: Medieval Period
type: era
date: c. 500 CE – 1500
themes: religion, trade, science, war
src: eb012113c17e
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Medieval or post-classical period (about 500 to 1500 CE) is the era of major world religions consolidating, long-distance trade networks, and the rise and fall of large steppe and Islamic polities. The term 'medieval' is European in origin, but the period had global dimensions.
--- fact 1
The [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]] created a new political and intellectual world from Iberia to Central Asia.
--- fact 2
The [[event/mongol-conquests|Mongol Conquests]] created the largest contiguous land empire in history.
--- fact 3
The [[event/black-death|Black Death]] devastated Eurasia and North Africa in the 14th century.
--- fact 4
Major states included the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]], the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], the [[culture/norse|Norse]], the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]] and the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]] and [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] near its close.
--- context
The Medieval Period follows [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]] and ends around the voyages that set off the [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]] and the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]].
--- observer
The Observer prefers a global reading of this era. It sees a species increasingly connected by roads, caravans, and ships, and by shared meaning-engines that crossed political claim-lines.
--- question 1
How much did pre-modern long-distance networks transmit ideas versus goods?

=== era/mesolithic
title: Mesolithic
type: era
date: c. 10000 – 5000 BCE
themes: technology, kinship
src: 9f7015c78ee9
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Mesolithic (Middle Stone Age) is the period of hunter-gatherer societies between the last ice age and the arrival of farming in Europe and neighbouring regions, from about 10,000 to about 5,000 BCE. Its dates vary widely by region.
--- fact 1
The Mesolithic runs from about 10,000 to about 5,000 BCE, and in parts of northern Europe it lasts until about 4000 BCE. It ends locally when farming arrives, so the end date differs from place to place. The label is used mainly for Europe; other regions use other terms.
--- fact 2
Warming after the ice age spread forests across northern Europe, and rising seas flooded coastal lowlands such as [[place/doggerland|Doggerland]].
--- fact 3
People hunted deer, wild boar and aurochs, fished, and gathered shellfish and plants. Dugout canoes such as the Pesse canoe from the Netherlands, about 10,000 years old, are among the oldest known boats. Well-studied sites include Star Carr in northern England and the shell middens of the Ertebølle culture in Denmark.
--- fact 4
Farming began in Southwest Asia by about 9600 BCE and spread into Europe from about 6500 BCE; ancient DNA shows that early farmers of Anatolian ancestry partly replaced and partly mixed with local foragers, and the balance varied by region.
--- context
The Mesolithic follows the [[era/epipaleolithic|Epipaleolithic]] and the [[era/upper-paleolithic|Upper Paleolithic]] and overlaps the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]], since foragers and farmers lived side by side in different regions. It sits in the [[era/holocene-epoch|Holocene Epoch]] and belongs to the theme threads of [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]]. See [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the Mesolithic is defined by what it is not, after the ice age and before farming, and that the label fits some regions better than others. It treats such labels as tools and not as stages every society passed through: foragers in some places lived this way for thousands of years after farming had begun elsewhere. The reading would weaken if most societies did follow the same sequence.
--- question 1
How much of the Mesolithic population of Europe was replaced by farmers, and how much was absorbed?
--- question 2
Why did some coastal foragers, such as the Ertebølle people, delay adopting farming for centuries after contact?

=== era/mesoproterozoic-era
title: Mesoproterozoic Era
type: era
date: 1.6 – 1 Ga
themes: earth-systems
src: 0621892f62d3
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Mesoproterozoic Era is the middle era of the Proterozoic Eon, running from 1.6 billion to 1.0 billion years ago. It falls inside the interval often called the "Boring Billion", a long stretch of little evident change in climate, ocean chemistry or life, though how uneventful it was is debated.
--- fact 1
The Mesoproterozoic Era spans 1.6 to 1.0 billion years ago. Its three periods, the Calymmian, Ectasian and Stenian, and its boundaries are set by numerical ages.
--- fact 2
The "Boring Billion" is an informal name for about 1.8 to 0.8 billion years ago, with no well-established ice ages and slow apparent change in the fossil record; the term is debated.
--- fact 3
Atmospheric oxygen is thought to have stayed well below today's level, and parts of the deeper ocean are reconstructed as oxygen-poor or rich in sulfide, though estimates vary widely.
--- fact 4
[[species/stromatolites|Stromatolites]] remained common in shallow seas. Eukaryotes diversified: Tappania microfossils are about 1.5 billion years old, and the red alga Bangiomorpha, dated in different studies to between roughly 1.05 and 1.2 billion years ago, is often cited as the oldest taxonomically resolved eukaryote.
--- fact 5
The supercontinent Nuna, or Columbia, is proposed to have broken up about 1.6 to 1.3 billion years ago, and Rodinia began assembling late in the era.
--- context
The Mesoproterozoic Era sits between the [[era/paleoproterozoic-era|Paleoproterozoic Era]] and the [[era/neoproterozoic-era|Neoproterozoic Era]] within the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and lies between the oxygen rise of the [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]] and the glaciations of [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]]; see [[event/origin-of-eukaryotes|Origin of Eukaryotes]].
--- observer
The Observer reads the apparent quiet of the Mesoproterozoic as information: a planet's surface can stay much the same for hundreds of millions of years without heading anywhere in particular. What arrived later was not overdue. The reading would weaken if new records showed that the quiet hides substantial change in eukaryote diversity.
--- question 1
Was the Boring Billion truly stable, or does a sparse record hide change?
--- question 2
What held atmospheric oxygen low for so long after the Great Oxidation Event?

=== era/mesozoic-era
title: Mesozoic Era
type: era
date: 251.9 – 66 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: e3d2867b8214
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Mesozoic Era (251.9 to 66 million years ago) is the age of dinosaurs. It saw the breakup of Pangaea and the first flowering plants and mammals.
--- fact 1
Periods: Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous.
--- fact 2
Non-avian dinosaurs dominated land ecosystems; see [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]].
--- fact 3
Mammals appeared in the Triassic and stayed mostly small for the whole era.
--- fact 4
The era ends with the [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]].
--- context
The Mesozoic follows the [[event/permian-triassic-extinction|Permian-Triassic Extinction]] and precedes the [[era/cenozoic-era|Cenozoic Era]]. Its collapse opens the ecological space that mammals, including primates, later filled.
--- observer
The Observer notes how contingent the human story is: without the end-Cretaceous impact, the mammals that led to primates might have stayed small.
--- question 1
How diverse were dinosaurs just before the impact?

=== era/middle-paleolithic
title: Middle Paleolithic
type: era
date: c. 300 – 50 ka
themes: technology, art
src: 3cd0d5851b00
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Middle Paleolithic is the middle part of the Paleolithic, from about 300,000 to about 50,000 years ago. It is the interval of prepared-core stone tools, of the earliest Homo sapiens, and of several human species living at the same time.
--- fact 1
The Middle Paleolithic runs from about 300,000 to about 50,000 years ago in Eurasia; Africa uses the name Middle Stone Age, and boundary dates vary by region. Toolmakers used prepared-core methods such as the Levallois technique, and the Mousterian industry of Europe and western Asia is linked mainly to Neanderthals.
--- fact 2
Several human species overlapped in time: [[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]], [[species/denisovans|Denisovans]], [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]], [[species/homo-naledi|Homo naledi]] and [[species/homo-floresiensis|Homo floresiensis]]. The earliest Homo sapiens fossils are about 315,000 years old; see [[event/origin-of-homo-sapiens|Origin of Homo sapiens]].
--- fact 3
Homo sapiens fossils from Misliya Cave in Israel (about 185,000 years old) and from Skhul and Qafzeh caves (roughly 90,000 to 130,000 years old) show an early presence outside Africa; whether these groups contributed to living non-Africans is uncertain (see [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]]).
--- fact 4
Blombos Cave in South Africa has engraved ochre and ochre-processing tools about 75,000 to 100,000 years old, among the earliest signs of symbolic behaviour; see [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]].- Neanderthals disappear from the fossil record by about 40,000 years ago. Whether they buried their dead or made art is disputed.
--- context
The Middle Paleolithic follows the [[era/lower-paleolithic|Lower Paleolithic]] and is followed by the [[era/upper-paleolithic|Upper Paleolithic]] within the [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]]. It belongs to the theme threads of [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]]. See the essay [[observer-note/why-the-species-buries-its-dead|Why the Species Buries Its Dead]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the Middle Paleolithic is the first period in which several human species lived at the same time and sometimes in the same regions, so the family tree looks more like a bush than a ladder. Claims about their behaviour, especially burial and symbolic art, are disputed, and the Observer applies the same standard of evidence to each species. The reading would weaken if the fossils assigned to different species proved to be variants of one.
--- question 1
Did Neanderthals bury their dead or make art?
--- question 2
How much of the variety in Middle Paleolithic tools reflects different cultures rather than different raw materials?

=== era/neogene-period
title: Neogene Period
type: era
date: 23.03 – 2.58 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: d239261c8db2
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Neogene Period, from 23.03 to 2.58 million years ago, is the second period of the Cenozoic Era. It is the interval in which grasslands spread, the climate cooled toward the ice ages, and the hominin lineage separated from that of the other apes.
--- fact 1
The Neogene Period spans 23.03 to 2.58 million years ago and has two epochs, the Miocene and the Pliocene. Its base is defined at Lemme-Carrosio in Italy, and its top, the base of the Quaternary, at Monte San Nicola in Sicily.
--- fact 2
The climate was warm in the early Miocene and then cooled. The East Antarctic ice sheet expanded about 14 million years ago, and Northern Hemisphere glaciation intensified about 2.7 million years ago.
--- fact 3
Grasses using the C4 pathway of photosynthesis spread widely about 8 to 6 million years ago, and open grassland and savanna expanded, favouring grazing mammals.
--- fact 4
Apes were diverse in Africa, Europe and Asia during the Miocene. The hominin lineage diverged from that of the chimpanzees around 7 million years ago (see [[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]]), and [[species/sahelanthropus-tchadensis|Sahelanthropus tchadensis]] and [[species/australopithecus-afarensis|Australopithecus afarensis]] date to this period.
--- fact 5
The [[event/messinian-salinity-crisis|Messinian Salinity Crisis]], about 5.96 to 5.33 million years ago, left the Mediterranean largely dried out. The land bridge of Panama, generally dated to about 3 million years ago although the timing is debated, joined the Americas; see [[event/closure-of-the-isthmus-of-panama|Closure of the Isthmus of Panama]].
--- context
The Neogene Period follows the [[era/paleogene-period|Paleogene Period]] and is followed by the [[era/quaternary-period|Quaternary Period]] within the [[era/cenozoic-era|Cenozoic Era]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the Neogene is where the line that includes humans separates from the line of the other apes, in a world that was drying and cooling into grassland and ice. The Observer does not read the drying as a push toward humans: the same changes affected many lineages, and most Miocene apes left no descendants. The reading would weaken if the traits of the hominin line could be tied directly to the spread of grassland.
--- question 1
Which Miocene apes, if any, are ancestral to living great apes and humans?
--- question 2
What part did the changing African environment play in early hominin evolution?

=== era/neolithic
title: Neolithic
type: era
date: c. 9600 – 3300 BCE
themes: technology, kinship, trade
src: b5f115093d2c
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Neolithic is the era in which farming, animal herding, pottery, and permanent villages developed in several parts of the world independently, starting about 11,500 years ago in Southwest Asia.
--- fact 1
Agriculture arose independently in several regions, including the Fertile Crescent, China, Mesoamerica, the Andes, and New Guinea.
--- fact 2
See [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]] and [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]].
--- fact 3
Pottery, [[technology/pottery|Pottery]], became widespread.
--- fact 4
Large monuments such as [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] and early towns such as [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]] date to this era.
--- fact 5
The timing varies by region; the Neolithic ends when metal use begins, so the end date differs across the world.
--- context
The Neolithic sits in the [[era/holocene-epoch|Holocene Epoch]] and leads into the [[era/bronze-age|Bronze Age]].
--- observer
The Observer sees the Neolithic as a bargain with consequences: more food, more people, more disease, more inequality. See [[observer-note/the-settling-and-its-bargain|The Settling and Its Bargain]].
--- question 1
Did monumental building precede farming in some regions?
