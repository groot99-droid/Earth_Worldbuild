=== renunciation-of-worldly-goods
title: St Francis Renounces His Worldly Goods
artist: Giotto di Bondone (c. 1267–1337)
year: c. 1297–1300
medium: Fresco
location: Upper Church, Basilica of San Francesco, Assisi
src: 70d063fdd606
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
One of 28 scenes from the Legend of St Francis in Assisi. The young Francis strips off his clothes and hands them back to his furious father, while the bishop covers him with his cloak. The painting splits into two crowds, family on one side and Church on the other, with an empty gap between them. Scholars still argue over how much of the cycle is Giotto's own work, but its solid figures and clear storytelling point toward the Scrovegni Chapel.

=== scrovegni-chapel
title: Scrovegni Chapel
artist: Giotto di Bondone (c. 1267–1337)
year: c. 1303–1305
medium: Fresco cycle
location: Scrovegni (Arena) Chapel, Padua
src: be3e1d2967de
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
The banker Enrico Scrovegni paid for this small chapel, and Giotto covered its whole interior with paint. Rows of scenes tell the lives of Joachim and Anna, the Virgin Mary and Christ, all under a deep blue vault scattered with gold stars. A Last Judgment fills the entrance wall. Below the stories, painted figures of the Virtues and Vices are done in grey to look like stone sculpture. It is the most complete surviving work by Giotto, and one of the great turning points in European art.

=== kiss-of-judas
title: The Kiss of Judas
artist: Giotto di Bondone (c. 1267–1337)
year: c. 1304–1306
medium: Fresco
location: Scrovegni Chapel, Padua
src: d9cead73938a
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
Judas wraps Christ in a sweeping yellow cloak as he leans in for the kiss that betrays him. The two faces meet in profile, calm against treacherous, while torches, clubs and lances crowd the sky above. At the left, Peter cuts off a servant's ear. Giotto puts the whole drama into one locked gaze, which makes this one of the most psychologically charged images of the Middle Ages.

=== lamentation

year: c. 1305
medium: Fresco
location: Scrovegni Chapel, Padua
src: 5f2b7cf88d78
mode: horror-prose
--- description
Mourners gather around the body of Christ. A bare, rocky ridge runs diagonally down the picture and leads the eye to his face, which Mary holds close to her own. === ognissanti-madonna
title: Ognissanti Madonna
artist: Giotto di Bondone (c. 1267–1337)
year: c. 1306–1310
medium: Tempera and gold on panel, 325 × 204 cm
location: Uffizi Gallery, Florence
src: 038370b6234d
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
Painted for the church of Ognissanti in Florence, this huge altarpiece shows the Virgin enthroned among saints and angels. Earlier Madonnas, such as Cimabue's, look flat and weightless. Giotto's Virgin has real bulk: her knees push forward under her robe, and her throne recedes like a piece of architecture. It hangs in the Uffizi beside the Madonnas of Cimabue and Duccio, so visitors can see the change for themselves.

=== campanile

year: begun 1334
medium: Marble-clad bell tower (architecture), 84.7 m tall
location: Piazza del Duomo, Florence
src: b74a8b474c8c
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
In 1334 Florence appointed Giotto master of works for its cathedral, and he designed this bell tower beside it. He lived to see only the lowest storey built. === ghent-altarpiece

year: completed 1432
medium: Oil on oak panels, 12 panels (open), 350 × 470 cm
location: St Bavo's Cathedral, Ghent
src: e61418d984ad
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A huge folding altarpiece. When open, it shows God enthroned between Mary and John the Baptist, Adam and Eve at the far edges, and below them crowds of saints, prophets and pilgrims streaming across a flowering meadow to worship the Lamb of God. === arnolfini-portrait
title: The Arnolfini Portrait
artist: Jan van Eyck (c. 1390–1441)
year: 1434
medium: Oil on oak panel, 82 × 60 cm
location: National Gallery, London
src: e05ada9e51ee
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
An Italian merchant, probably Giovanni di Nicolao Arnolfini, stands hand in hand with a woman in a green gown in a room in Bruges. On the back wall, a convex mirror reflects the couple from behind along with two more figures in the doorway. Above it, van Eyck signed in elegant script: "Jan van Eyck was here 1434". Every object is painted with microscopic care: the brass chandelier, the oranges on the windowsill, the little dog. The picture has invited endless theories about marriage, memory and symbolism.

=== haboku-landscape

year: 1495
medium: Hanging scroll, ink on paper, 148.6 × 32.7 cm (whole scroll)
location: Tokyo National Museum (National Treasure)
src: 6e533e54b191
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Painted for his disciple Josui Sōen as a certificate of his training, this landscape is made almost entirely of wet, splashed washes of ink. === lofty-mount-lu
title: Lofty Mount Lu
artist: Shen Zhou (1427–1509)
year: 1467
medium: Hanging scroll, ink and light colour on paper, 193.8 × 98.1 cm
location: National Palace Museum, Taipei
src: 998eef75d5bd
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A towering mountain of stacked peaks, waterfalls and pines, with a tiny scholar standing at its foot. Shen Zhou painted it as a 70th-birthday gift for his teacher Chen Kuan. The mountain's grandeur is a tribute to the old man's character. He had never visited Mount Lu. He painted it in the dense, textured manner of the Yuan master Wang Meng, and it is one of his largest and most ambitious works.

=== seven-deadly-sins

=== garden-of-earthly-delights
title: The Garden of Earthly Delights
artist: Hieronymus Bosch (c. 1450–1516)
year: c. 1490–1510
medium: Oil on oak panels, triptych, 205.5 × 384.9 cm (open)
location: Museo del Prado, Madrid
src: c9f543353973
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Bosch's most famous and mysterious work. The left panel shows God presenting Eve to Adam in Paradise. The huge centre panel is filled with naked figures frolicking among giant birds, strawberries and crystal fountains. The right panel is a nightmarish, frozen Hell of musical torture instruments and the "Tree-Man". Whether it is a warning against sin or a vision of a lost innocence, scholars still disagree after five centuries.
