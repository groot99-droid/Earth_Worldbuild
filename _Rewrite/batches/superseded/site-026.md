=== theme/art-and-ritual
title: Art and Ritual
type: theme
date: 
themes: art
src: 72ee1338633c
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Art and ritual are symbolic and expressive practices: painting, carving, ornament, music and dance, storytelling, monumental building, and ceremony, including rites for the dead. They are found in all human societies and go back at least to the Paleolithic, with engraved ochre, beads, and cave paintings from tens of thousands of years ago. The vault follows them from cave painting and prehistoric monuments, through the temple and court arts of ancient empires and medieval kingdoms, to Renaissance and Mughal building, and to objects taken in conquest, such as the Benin Bronzes. It treats art and ritual as evidence of what societies valued and of how they marked power, belonging, and mortality.
--- observer
The Observer treats art and ritual as behavior with no obvious direct survival function that nonetheless occupies enormous time and resources in every culture. This is a central puzzle of the species. The record suggests several linked roles: art and ritual carry story-glue that binds strangers, they serve as status-signal for rulers and groups, and they give form to the grief-rite, and a single monument can do all three at once. The Observer would revise this reading if the cost of art and ritual can be shown to buy an advantage, such as cooperation or shared memory, that the record now hides, in which case the puzzle would dissolve rather than deepen.
--- question 1
What is the adaptive role of art and ritual?
--- question 2
When did symbolic art begin, and did other human species such as Neanderthals make it?
--- question 3
Did ritual and monumental building come before settled life, as [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] has been read to suggest, or after it?
--- question 4
How can perishable arts, such as music, dance, textiles, and wood carving, be studied where none survive?
--- question 5
How did patronage by courts, temples, and merchant families shape which art survives?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Symbolic behavior appears early. Engraved ochre at Blombos Cave in South Africa dates to about 75,000 to 100,000 years ago; a warty-pig painting in Sulawesi is at least 45,500 years old; and the painted animals of [[place/chauvet-cave|Chauvet Cave]], made about 36,000 years ago, include horses, lions, mammoths, rhinoceroses, and bears. See [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]]. Whether Neanderthals also made symbolic objects is debated ([[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]]).
- Ice Age peoples worked bone, tusk, and clay. Humans hunted [[species/woolly-mammoth|Woolly Mammoth]] and used their bones and tusks for shelters and art, [[technology/pottery|Pottery]] appears in East Asia at least 20,000 years ago, and the [[culture/jomon-culture|Jomon Culture]] made cord-marked pottery and clay figurines called dogu.
- Oral traditions and performance transmit knowledge and identity. [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] pass on knowledge through oral traditions, songlines, and ceremony, and Australian rock art includes some of the oldest known paintings; griots perform the epic of [[person/sundiata-keita|Sundiata Keita]] and preserve the history of the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]].
- Monumental building came before cities in some places. [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] has carved pillars up to about 5.5 meters tall, [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]] has painted walls, [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] was raised and rebuilt over several centuries from about 3000 BCE, and [[place/poverty-point|Poverty Point]] in Louisiana was built by hunter-gatherer-fishers with six concentric earthen ridges and mounds.
- Egypt tied art to kingship and the afterlife. The [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]] built pyramids and temples and mummified the elite, and tombs such as those of the [[place/giza-necropolis|Giza Necropolis]] show the resources committed. [[person/akhenaten|Akhenaten]]'s reign has a distinctive, naturalistic style, and many images of [[person/hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]], whose mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari is a major monument, were later removed or defaced.
- Craft traditions carried identity across large areas. The [[culture/minoans|Minoans]] painted vivid frescoes in palaces such as Knossos, the [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] decorated pottery with fine stamped geometric patterns, the [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]] carved colossal heads from basalt more than three meters tall, the [[culture/scythians|Scythians]] made gold work and left textiles and tattoos preserved in frozen tombs at Pazyryk, and Polynesian carvers made the moai of [[place/rapa-nui|Rapa Nui]].
- Classical and Byzantine art served city and empire. [[place/athens|Athens]] built the Parthenon between 447 and 432 BCE and produced drama and philosophy ([[culture/ancient-greeks|Ancient Greeks]]); [[place/rome|Rome]] completed the Colosseum in 80 CE; the Apadana reliefs of [[place/persepolis|Persepolis]] show delegations from across the empire bringing gifts; the [[culture/nabataeans|Nabataeans]] cut tombs and temples into the cliffs of Petra; and the [[culture/byzantine-empire|Byzantine Empire]] built the Hagia Sophia under Justinian I.
- Buddhist, Hindu, and court arts flourished in South and East Asia. Gandhara art under the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]] blended Greek and Indian styles and produced some of the earliest images of the Buddha in human form; the [[culture/gupta-empire|Gupta Empire]] is associated with a flowering of the arts, though "Golden Age" is a later label; the [[culture/chola-dynasty|Chola Dynasty]] made celebrated bronzes, including the dancing Shiva, and built the Brihadisvara Temple about 1010; the [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]] is a high point of Chinese poetry and art; and the [[culture/ming-dynasty|Ming Dynasty]] completed the Forbidden City in 1420.
- Southeast Asian states built vast religious monuments. [[place/borobudur|Borobudur]] in Java has 2,672 relief panels and originally 504 Buddha statues, [[place/angkor|Angkor]] holds Angkor Wat, the largest religious monument in the world by area, built by the [[culture/khmer-empire|Khmer Empire]], and the [[culture/pagan-kingdom|Pagan Kingdom]] built thousands of temples. [[culture/champa|Champa]] built temples at My Son.
- Islamic and Central Asian courts patronized architecture and learning. [[culture/al-andalus|Al-Andalus]] produced the Alhambra, built mainly in the 13th and 14th centuries; the [[culture/timurid-empire|Timurid Empire]] and [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] are remembered for architecture and science under [[person/timur|Timur]] and [[person/ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]]; the [[culture/safavid-empire|Safavid Empire]] made Isfahan known for its mosques, squares, and gardens; and the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]] produced the [[place/taj-mahal|Taj Mahal]], built from about 1632 to 1653 by Shah Jahan for his wife Mumtaz Mahal with about 20,000 workers.
- African states built in stone and cast in bronze. [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]] has dry-stone walls up to about 11 meters high, the [[culture/zagwe-dynasty|Zagwe Dynasty]] carved churches from living rock, the [[culture/kingdom-of-benin|Kingdom of Benin]] organized craft guilds, and the elite graves of the [[culture/kingdom-of-mapungubwe|Kingdom of Mapungubwe]] held gold and imported glass beads. In 1897 British forces removed the Benin Bronzes and sold them to defray the costs of the expedition, during the age of the [[event/scramble-for-africa|Scramble for Africa]].
- The Americas built ceremonial centers on a large scale. [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]] laid out the Pyramids of the Sun and Moon along the Avenue of the Dead, [[place/chichen-itza|Chichen Itza]] has El Castillo, where the equinox sun casts a serpent-like shadow, [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]] built Monks Mound, and the Inca built [[place/machu-picchu|Machu Picchu]] about 1450. The [[culture/marajoara-culture|Marajoara Culture]] made richly decorated ceramics and burial urns, and the [[culture/muisca|Muisca]] held gold rituals.
- The [[event/renaissance|Renaissance]] shows art shaped by patronage. Brunelleschi developed linear perspective in Florence in experiments of about 1415 to 1420, and Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, and Raphael worked under the patronage of wealthy families and the Church, with the Medici financing artists through banking.
- Rites for the dead shaped much surviving art. The [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] reconstructed bodies with clay, wigs, and mineral pigments; the Egyptians mummified their elite; the dead of Çatalhöyük lay under house floors; and burial goods appear in graves across cultures. See [[observer-note/why-the-species-buries-its-dead|Why the Species Buries Its Dead]].

--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] made some of the oldest known paintings, the [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] decorated pottery with stamped patterns, and Polynesian carvers made the moai of [[place/rapa-nui|Rapa Nui]], about 95 percent of the island's monolithic statues having been carved from the volcanic tuff of one quarry.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]] and the [[region/amazon-basin-and-south-american-lowlands|Amazon Basin and South American Lowlands]]:** The [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] reconstructed and preserved its dead, the Inca built in stone at [[place/machu-picchu|Machu Picchu]], the [[culture/muisca|Muisca]] held gold rituals, and the [[culture/marajoara-culture|Marajoara Culture]] made painted and incised ceramics.
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** Dry-stone building at [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]], rock-hewn churches under the [[culture/zagwe-dynasty|Zagwe Dynasty]], guild-made bronzes in the [[culture/kingdom-of-benin|Kingdom of Benin]], and the oral art of griots in the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]].
- **[[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]] and [[region/central-asian-oases|Central Asian Oases]]:** The [[culture/scythians|Scythians]] made gold ornaments and preserved textiles, and the oases of [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] under the [[culture/timurid-empire|Timurid Empire]] became a center of monumental architecture; Gandhara art under the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]] joined Greek and Indian styles.
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]] and [[region/north-america|North America]]:** The [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]] carved colossal heads, [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]] and [[place/chichen-itza|Chichen Itza]] built pyramids, and Chichen Itza has the largest known Mesoamerican ball court; in North America, [[place/poverty-point|Poverty Point]] and [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]] raised earthworks at large scale, and [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] built great houses with hundreds of rooms.
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]:** [[place/borobudur|Borobudur]], [[place/angkor|Angkor]], the [[culture/pagan-kingdom|Pagan Kingdom]], and [[culture/champa|Champa]] show Buddhist and Hindu art carried by sea and by court patronage.
- **[[region/south-asia|South Asia]] and [[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** Gandhara, Gupta, and Chola art in the south, and Tang poetry and Ming palace building in the east, show state and temple patronage on a large scale; the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]] added the [[place/taj-mahal|Taj Mahal]].
- **[[region/europe|Europe]], the [[region/mediterranean-basin|Mediterranean Basin]], and the [[region/levant-and-anatolia|Levant and Anatolia]]:** From [[place/chauvet-cave|Chauvet Cave]] and [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] to Athens, Rome, Byzantium, and the Renaissance, art in these regions is tied to city, church, and court.
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- The categories of art and ritual are modern and not universal. Many objects in the vault, such as pillars, urns, masks, and statues, were made for religious, political, or social purposes and not as art in the modern sense, so this hub treats art broadly.
- The meaning and function of prehistoric art are inferred, not known. Whether [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] and [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] were ritual sites is argued from their form and context, the purposes of cave paintings are debated, and the dates of the oldest art have been revised, as in the 2024 report of Sulawesi narrative scenes at least 51,200 years old.
- The record is biased toward durable materials. Stone, pottery, and metal survive, while music, dance, wood carving, and most textiles rarely do, except in exceptional finds such as the frozen tombs at Pazyryk and in living traditions such as songlines and griot performance.
- Ownership, attribution, and erasure shape what is known. The Benin Bronzes were removed and sold in 1897, attribution of the Taj Mahal's chief architect is debated, images of [[person/hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]] were defaced, and colonial-era writers denied that [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]] was built by Africans, a claim archaeology overturned.
- The vault's coverage is uneven. It has no notes yet on music, dance, or theater as topics, no notes on individual artists or on textiles, and few on art after about 1700.

=== theme/earth-systems-and-life
title: Earth Systems and Life

--- summary
Earth systems and life cover the physical planet, its climate, and the history of living things, from the formation of the Solar System about 4.57 billion years ago to the present. The vault follows the planet's changing atmosphere, oceans, continents, and ice; the origin and diversification of life, punctuated by mass extinctions; the evolution of hominins and humans through ice-age cycles; and the recent period in which human activity has become a major influence on the planet. Human history sits inside this longer story and depends on it, on the warm and relatively stable climate of the Holocene, on domesticated plants and animals, and on the diseases and environmental changes that societies met along the way. This hub also covers the scientific work that reconstructed this history.
--- observer
The Observer treats the planet as the frame for everything else, and the species as a recent, fast-moving part of a system that has ended and restarted many times. The record shows life already reshaping the air, when oxygen-producing microbes drove the [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]], and mass extinctions repeatedly clearing the ground for new lineages, so a single species altering the planet is new in its cause rather than in kind (see [[observer-note/the-species-as-a-geological-force|The Species as a Geological Force]]). What is also new is awareness: this is the first such shift whose cause can study and describe it, and can choose otherwise. The Observer treats the stable climate of the Holocene, in which farming and cities arose, as a correlation to be tested rather than a settled cause. This reading would weaken if the present rate of change proves to match earlier natural transitions in both cause and speed, or if the stability of the Holocene proves irrelevant to the rise of agriculture.
--- question 1
How do current changes compare with past planetary transitions, such as the [[event/permian-triassic-extinction|Permian-Triassic Extinction]] and the [[event/paleocene-eocene-thermal-maximum|Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum]], in rate and in cause?
--- question 2
How did life begin, and how much earlier than 3.5 billion years ago might it have arisen?
--- question 3
Did the stable Holocene climate make agriculture possible, or would farming have arisen anyway?
--- question 4
What caused each of the Big Five extinctions, and how did recovery proceed?
--- question 5
How large was the human role in the Quaternary megafauna extinctions compared with climate?
--- question 6
How should the [[era/anthropocene|Anthropocene]] be dated, or should it be formalized at all?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- The Solar System formed about 4.57 billion years ago from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust, and Earth's age of about 4.54 billion years comes from radiometric dating of meteorites and of Earth's oldest lead ([[event/formation-of-the-solar-system-and-earth|Formation of the Solar System and Earth]]). A Mars-sized body called Theia is thought to have struck the young Earth and formed the Moon ([[event/theia-impact-and-the-formation-of-the-moon|Theia Impact and the Formation of the Moon]]). The [[place/jack-hills-zircons|Jack Hills Zircons]] of Western Australia, about 4.4 billion years old, are the oldest known pieces of Earth's crust, and the [[event/late-heavy-bombardment|Late Heavy Bombardment]], a hypothesized spike in asteroid impacts about 4.1 to 3.8 billion years ago, remains debated.
- Life arose at least 3.5 billion years ago, and how it began is not known ([[event/origin-of-life|Origin of Life]]). [[species/stromatolites|Stromatolites]] in the Dresser Formation of Western Australia, about 3.48 billion years old, are the oldest widely cited evidence, although some researchers dispute that they are biological. [[species/cyanobacteria|Cyanobacteria]] perform oxygen-producing photosynthesis, and their earliest unambiguous fossils are about 2.15 billion years old.
- Oxygen rose in the atmosphere beginning about 2.4 billion years ago as photosynthetic microbes saturated chemical sinks such as dissolved iron, an episode recorded in banded iron formations ([[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]]). Eukaryotes, the cells from which animals, plants, and fungi are built, arose roughly 2 billion years ago, when bacteria that became mitochondria were taken into a host cell ([[event/origin-of-eukaryotes|Origin of Eukaryotes]]).
- The late Proterozoic brought glaciations, the Sturtian and Marinoan of about 717 to 635 million years ago, in which ice may have reached the tropics; scientists debate whether the oceans froze completely ([[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]]). Soon afterward the first large, complex multicellular organisms appear ([[species/ediacaran-biota|Ediacaran Biota]]), and beginning 538.8 million years ago most major animal body plans appear over roughly 13 to 25 million years ([[event/cambrian-explosion|Cambrian Explosion]]). [[species/trilobites|Trilobites]] thrived for about 270 million years.
- Plants, then arthropods and vertebrates, moved onto land beginning about 470 million years ago ([[event/colonization-of-land|Colonization of Land]]), and [[species/tiktaalik|Tiktaalik]], a 375-million-year-old fish with limb-like fins found on Ellesmere Island, is a transitional form toward four-limbed animals. The first two of the "Big Five" mass extinctions came in this era: the [[event/late-ordovician-extinction|Late Ordovician Extinction]], which eliminated an estimated 85 to 86 percent of species, and the [[event/late-devonian-extinction|Late Devonian Extinction]], with about 75 percent lost.
- The [[event/permian-triassic-extinction|Permian-Triassic Extinction]] about 251.9 million years ago was the largest in Earth's history, killing an estimated 80 to 96 percent of marine species, probably through Siberian volcanism, and recovery took roughly ten million years. The [[event/triassic-jurassic-extinction|Triassic-Jurassic Extinction]] about 201.4 million years ago coincided with the [[event/breakup-of-pangaea|Breakup of Pangaea]] and cleared space for the [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]], which then dominated land for about 165 million years.
- Birds are living descendants of dinosaurs; [[species/archaeopteryx|Archaeopteryx]], a feathered, winged dinosaur from Late Jurassic Bavaria about 150 million years ago, shows the link. [[species/flowering-plants|Flowering Plants]] appear about 130 million years ago and now make up most land plant species, and most crops descend from them. The [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]] about 66 million years ago wiped out the non-avian dinosaurs and about three-quarters of species after an asteroid struck what is now Mexico.
- The Cenozoic reshaped climate and geography. A burst of warming about 56 million years ago, in which a large release of carbon raised temperatures by roughly 5 to 6 degrees Celsius for about 200,000 years, acidified the ocean ([[event/paleocene-eocene-thermal-maximum|Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum]]). The [[event/collision-of-india-and-asia|Collision of India and Asia]], from about 50 million years ago, raised the Himalaya and shaped the South Asian monsoon; the [[event/messinian-salinity-crisis|Messinian Salinity Crisis]] cut the Mediterranean off from the Atlantic between about 5.96 and 5.33 million years ago; and the [[event/closure-of-the-isthmus-of-panama|Closure of the Isthmus of Panama]] about 3 million years ago joined North and South America.
- The human lineage split from that of chimpanzees and bonobos between roughly 6 and 8 million years ago ([[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]]). [[species/sahelanthropus-tchadensis|Sahelanthropus tchadensis]] is a roughly 7-million-year-old fossil ape whose status as a hominin is debated, [[species/australopithecus-afarensis|Australopithecus afarensis]] ("Lucy") walked upright about 3.9 to 2.9 million years ago, and [[species/homo-erectus|Homo erectus]] was the first known hominin to spread widely beyond Africa. Small-brained relatives such as [[species/homo-naledi|Homo naledi]] and [[species/homo-floresiensis|Homo floresiensis]] lived alongside modern humans.
- Modern humans emerged in Africa by about 300,000 years ago ([[event/origin-of-homo-sapiens|Origin of Homo sapiens]]) and lived through ice-age cycles. The [[event/toba-eruption|Toba Eruption]] of about 74,000 years ago was one of the largest in two million years, though most researchers now consider a severe human population bottleneck unlikely. At the [[event/last-glacial-maximum|Last Glacial Maximum]], about 26,500 to 19,000 years ago, sea level stood some 120 to 135 meters lower, exposing [[place/beringia|Beringia]] and joining New Guinea and Australia; see [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]], [[event/peopling-of-sahul|Peopling of Sahul]], and [[event/peopling-of-the-americas|Peopling of the Americas]].
- Between roughly 50,000 and 10,000 years ago a large share of the world's biggest land animals went extinct ([[event/quaternary-megafauna-extinctions|Quaternary Megafauna Extinctions]]); recent global analyses link the declines more closely to the expansion of Homo sapiens than to climate change, though regional debate continues. The [[species/woolly-mammoth|Woolly Mammoth]] survived on Wrangel Island until about 4,000 years ago. The [[event/younger-dryas|Younger Dryas]], a return to near-glacial cold from about 12,900 to 11,700 years ago, ended abruptly and marks the start of the [[era/holocene-epoch|Holocene Epoch]]; rising seas later drowned [[place/doggerland|Doggerland]] by about 6000 BCE.
- The Holocene's warm, relatively stable climate is the setting for farming, cities, and writing. [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]] and the [[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]] reshaped food supplies, and [[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]] shows peoples enriching soils. Environment and disease acted on societies as well: droughts are among the proposed causes of the [[event/classic-maya-collapse|Classic Maya Collapse]], the [[event/black-death|Black Death]] killed an estimated 30 to 60 percent of Europe's population, the [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]] moved crops, animals, and diseases between hemispheres, and the popular story of self-inflicted ecological collapse on [[place/rapa-nui|Rapa Nui]] is contested.
- Understanding this history is itself recent. [[person/charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]] published On the Origin of Species in 1859; Alfred Wegener proposed continental drift in 1912, and plate tectonics gained wide acceptance in the 1960s; the first scientific expedition to the [[event/tunguska-event|Tunguska Event]] site came in 1927; and [[person/rachel-carson|Rachel Carson]]'s Silent Spring (1962) documented the harm that pesticides caused to birds and ecosystems.
- Human activity is now a major influence on the planet. The [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]] since about 1950 saw population rise from about 2.5 billion to about 8 billion, with steep rises in energy and fertilizer use, and the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]] raised grain yields. In [[event/modern-climate-change|Modern Climate Change]], atmospheric carbon dioxide has risen from about 280 parts per million to about 423 in 2024; the [[event/covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 Pandemic]], first identified in late 2019, became a global outbreak within months. The [[era/anthropocene|Anthropocene]] is a proposed interval that the International Union of Geological Sciences declined to formalize as an epoch in March 2024.
--- extra 2: Through the Eras
- **Paleolithic:** Ice-age cycles, the evolution of [[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]], [[species/denisovans|Denisovans]], and [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]], the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]], the [[event/last-glacial-maximum|Last Glacial Maximum]], [[place/beringia|Beringia]], and the [[event/quaternary-megafauna-extinctions|Quaternary Megafauna Extinctions]]; see [[era/pleistocene-epoch|Pleistocene Epoch]].
- **Neolithic:** The end of the ice age ([[event/younger-dryas|Younger Dryas]], [[era/holocene-epoch|Holocene Epoch]]), the flooding of [[place/doggerland|Doggerland]], [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]], the [[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]], [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]], and the bargain described in [[observer-note/the-settling-and-its-bargain|The Settling and Its Bargain]].
- **Bronze Age:** The eruption of Thera damaged Aegean settlements ([[culture/minoans|Minoans]]), dated by radiocarbon to about 1600 BCE and by links with Egyptian chronology to about 1500 BCE, and drought and earthquakes are among the proposed causes of the [[event/bronze-age-collapse|Bronze Age Collapse]].
- **Iron Age:** The [[event/incense-trade|Incense Trade]] depended on Boswellia trees growing in southern Arabia and the Horn of Africa, the great dam at Marib irrigated the land of the [[culture/kingdom-of-saba|Kingdom of Saba]] until it failed about the 6th century CE, and the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] carried farming across much of Africa.
- **Classical Antiquity:** The dark, fertile soils and earthworks of [[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]] and the [[place/upano-valley-settlements|Upano Valley Settlements]], and the water channels and cisterns of the [[culture/nabataeans|Nabataeans]] at Petra.
- **Medieval Period:** The [[event/classic-maya-collapse|Classic Maya Collapse]], the reservoirs and canals of [[place/angkor|Angkor]], the decline of [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]] after about 1250 for debated reasons, the settlement of [[place/rapa-nui|Rapa Nui]], and the [[event/black-death|Black Death]] (1346 to 1353).
- **Early Modern Period:** The [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]] after 1492, the [[event/sugar-plantation-complex|Sugar Plantation Complex]], whose crop originated in New Guinea, and the [[event/russian-expansion-into-siberia|Russian Expansion into Siberia]], driven by the fur trade.
- **Industrial Age:** [[person/charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]]'s theory of evolution, the [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]]'s shift to coal and steam, the [[event/tunguska-event|Tunguska Event]] of 1908, the [[event/1918-influenza-pandemic|1918 Influenza Pandemic]], and the start of the warming measured in [[event/modern-climate-change|Modern Climate Change]] against an 1850 to 1900 baseline.
- **Information Age:** [[person/rachel-carson|Rachel Carson]], the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]], the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]], the [[event/genomic-revolution|Genomic Revolution]], whose ancient-DNA work has changed accounts of prehistory, the [[event/covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 Pandemic]], and the proposed [[era/anthropocene|Anthropocene]].
--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** Most of the early fossil record of the human lineage comes from here: [[species/sahelanthropus-tchadensis|Sahelanthropus tchadensis]], [[species/australopithecus-afarensis|Australopithecus afarensis]], [[species/homo-naledi|Homo naledi]], and the emergence of [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]] ([[event/origin-of-homo-sapiens|Origin of Homo sapiens]], [[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]]).
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]:** The [[event/toba-eruption|Toba Eruption]] in Sumatra about 74,000 years ago, the island human species [[species/homo-floresiensis|Homo floresiensis]] on Flores, and the sea crossings toward Sahul.
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** The [[place/jack-hills-zircons|Jack Hills Zircons]] preserve the oldest known crust, the [[event/peopling-of-sahul|Peopling of Sahul]] required open-sea crossings at least 50,000 years ago, and [[place/rapa-nui|Rapa Nui]] is a remote island whose settlement and ecological history are debated.
- **[[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]]:** [[place/beringia|Beringia]] joined Siberia and Alaska at the Last Glacial Maximum, the [[species/woolly-mammoth|Woolly Mammoth]] lived on into the Holocene on Wrangel Island, [[species/tiktaalik|Tiktaalik]] was found in the Canadian Arctic, and the [[event/tunguska-event|Tunguska Event]] flattened about 2,000 square kilometers of Siberian forest.
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]] and the [[region/amazon-basin-and-south-american-lowlands|Amazon Basin and South American Lowlands]]:** The Chicxulub impact crater lies beneath the Yucatan Peninsula ([[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]]), maize was domesticated in Mexico, droughts are among the proposed causes of the [[event/classic-maya-collapse|Classic Maya Collapse]], and Amazonian peoples enriched soils into dark earths.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]]:** [[place/monte-verde|Monte Verde]] in southern Chile is among the sites that overturned the "Clovis-first" model of the peopling of the Americas, though its age is now debated, and the [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] lived on the Atacama Desert coast.
- **[[region/south-asia|South Asia]]:** The [[event/collision-of-india-and-asia|Collision of India and Asia]] built the Himalaya and shaped the South Asian monsoon, and the [[event/toba-eruption|Toba Eruption]]'s ash fell across the subcontinent.
- **[[region/europe|Europe]] and the [[region/mediterranean-basin|Mediterranean Basin]]:** [[species/archaeopteryx|Archaeopteryx]] comes from Bavaria, [[place/doggerland|Doggerland]] was drowned by the North Sea, and the Mediterranean nearly dried out in the [[event/messinian-salinity-crisis|Messinian Salinity Crisis]].
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- Many dates and causes in deep time are uncertain or disputed. How life began is unknown, older claims of ancient stromatolites are disputed, whether the [[event/late-heavy-bombardment|Late Heavy Bombardment]] happened at all is debated, oceans may or may not have frozen completely in [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]], and the timing of the [[event/closure-of-the-isthmus-of-panama|Closure of the Isthmus of Panama]] and the extent of drying in the [[event/messinian-salinity-crisis|Messinian Salinity Crisis]] are argued.
- The [[era/anthropocene|Anthropocene]] is a proposed geological interval, not an official epoch. The International Union of Geological Sciences declined to formalize it in March 2024; proposed start dates range from the beginning of agriculture to about 1950, and the term remains widely used.
- The human role in past environmental change is contested. Scholars disagree about how far hunting or climate drove the [[event/quaternary-megafauna-extinctions|Quaternary Megafauna Extinctions]], whether the [[event/toba-eruption|Toba Eruption]] nearly ended humanity, and how far human action, rats, disease, and slave raids each contributed to the decline of Rapa Nui; no single cause is accepted for the [[event/classic-maya-collapse|Classic Maya Collapse]].
- The record is biased. Earth's earliest crust has been largely destroyed by plate tectonics and erosion, so the Jack Hills zircons are the only direct samples of Hadean crust, and the fossil record favors organisms with hard parts and environments where sediment accumulates.
- The vault's coverage is uneven. It has notes on the Big Five extinctions and on 18 species, but none yet on the ice-age cycles as a topic, on plate tectonics beyond a few events, on ocean and atmospheric circulation, or on climate episodes within recorded history such as the Little Ice Age, and disease appears mainly through a few pandemics.
