=== theme/kinship-and-society
title: Kinship and Society
type: theme
date: 
themes: kinship
src: f03dd9dbf1fc
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Kinship and society cover how people organize families, descent, marriage, households, communities, and social ranks, and how those arrangements scale from foraging bands to states and beyond. Kin relations are among the most variable features of human cultures. The vault traces them from the small mobile groups of the Paleolithic, through farming villages, chiefdoms, cities, and empires, to the modern struggles over citizenship, equality, and status, and it includes the migrations and conquests that mixed populations along the way. It also covers gender, slavery and freedom, and the ways groups define who belongs.
--- observer
The Observer calls this the kin-lattice. It notes that the species can extend kin language, such as 'brother' and 'motherland', to groups of millions. The record suggests that the lattice is reused rather than replaced as societies grow: dynasties, clans, and confederations rest on descent and marriage, rulers such as Timur took titles by marrying into an older line, and modern states appeal to kin words while resting on law. Rules of descent, marriage, and status also decide who counts as a full person, and the record shows them changing unevenly, often through long struggles such as those over slavery and suffrage. This reading would weaken if large-scale societies prove to run almost entirely on impersonal institutions, with kin language serving only as decoration.
--- question 1
How did large-scale societies replace kin-based organization, or did they build on it?
--- question 2
How varied was social organization among early foragers and farmers, and what do house plans, burials, and DNA say about rank and equality?
--- question 3
How did descent and marriage rules shape the spread of languages and peoples such as the Bantu, Austronesian, and Indo-European speakers?
--- question 4
How did societies decide who counted as a full person, and how did those boundaries change through slavery, citizenship, and suffrage?
--- question 5
What can be recovered of the lives of women, children, and non-elite people from sources written mostly by and about elites?
--- question 6
How have DNA studies changed accounts of ancestry, migration, and belonging, and how should groups' own accounts be weighed alongside them?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Modern humans ([[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]]) emerged in Africa about 300,000 years ago and spread across the world ([[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]]). DNA shows contact and interbreeding with other human groups: present-day non-African populations carry up to about 2 percent Neanderthal ancestry ([[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]]), and Oceanian populations carry much more Denisovan ancestry ([[species/denisovans|Denisovans]]). Native American ancestry has two roots, East Asian and Ancient North Eurasian ([[culture/mal-ta-and-the-ancient-north-eurasians|Mal'ta and the Ancient North Eurasians]]).
- Foraging peoples lived in small, kin-linked groups for most of human history, and some kept settled village life without farming. [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] spoke more than 250 languages before 1788 and passed on knowledge orally; the [[culture/jomon-culture|Jomon Culture]] lived in settled villages for about 14,000 years before rice farming arrived; and the [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] preserved men, women, and children of the community, not only an elite.
- Farming brought new forms of property, storage, and inheritance ([[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]]; see [[observer-note/the-settling-and-its-bargain|The Settling and Its Bargain]]). [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]] held several thousand people in houses packed together and entered from the roof, with the dead buried under the floors, and some evidence shows that early farmers were shorter and sicker than foragers.
- Cities required cooperation among unrelated people ([[event/the-urban-revolution|The Urban Revolution]], [[place/uruk|Uruk]], [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]]). Written law made the rules visible: the Code of Hammurabi, carved about 1754 BCE, lists 282 laws with penalties that vary by social class ([[person/hammurabi|Hammurabi]]).
- Women held supreme power in several states, usually through dynastic kinship. [[person/hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]] took the title of pharaoh about 1473 BCE after acting as regent for her stepson; [[person/cleopatra-vii|Cleopatra VII]] belonged to the Ptolemaic dynasty; [[person/wu-zetian|Wu Zetian]] was the only woman to rule China in her own name (690 to 705 CE); [[person/nzinga-of-ndongo-and-matamba|Nzinga of Ndongo and Matamba]] led resistance to Portuguese expansion; [[person/cixi|Cixi]] ruled as regent for most of the period from 1861 to 1908; and [[person/rani-lakshmibai|Rani Lakshmibai]] ruled Jhansi as regent and led its defense in 1858.
- Kin language and kin structures were used to organize larger unions. The [[culture/haudenosaunee-confederacy|Haudenosaunee Confederacy]] is governed under the oral Great Law of Peace, and its clan mothers appoint and remove chiefs; [[culture/maori|Maori]] society was organized around kin groups (whanau, hapu, and iwi); [[person/genghis-khan|Genghis Khan]] built an army on merit and loyalty rather than clan ([[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]]); and [[person/timur|Timur]], who was not a descendant of Genghis Khan, ruled through a puppet Genghisid khan and took the title güregen (royal son-in-law) through marriage into that line.
- Thinkers and states wrote about the ties that bind. [[person/confucius|Confucius]] stressed humaneness, ritual propriety, and filial piety, and his ideas about family and government shaped East Asian societies for over two thousand years; [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]] argued that group solidarity (asabiyyah) helps found dynasties and weakens as rulers grow comfortable; the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] exacted labor service (mit'a) rather than money taxes; and the griots, hereditary oral historians, preserved the story of [[person/sundiata-keita|Sundiata Keita]].
- Migrations carried language and kin across regions. Austronesian speakers spread from Taiwan from about 5,000 to 5,500 years ago as far as Madagascar and Rapa Nui ([[event/austronesian-expansion|Austronesian Expansion]], [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]]); the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] involved migration, intermarriage, and language shift, not only population replacement; the [[event/indo-european-language-spread|Indo-European Language Spread]] carried a family now spoken as a first language by roughly 40 to 46 percent of the world's population; and the Thule ancestors of the Inuit largely replaced the earlier Dorset population ([[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]]).
- Herders and river and forest peoples built varied forms of social order. The [[culture/botai-culture|Botai Culture]] hunter-herders of Kazakhstan used horses, the [[culture/sintashta-culture|Sintashta Culture]] built fortified settlements, and the [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]] were united as a steppe confederation in 209 BCE; in the Amazon, dispersed households, plazas, roads, and gardens formed low-density settlements ([[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]], [[place/upano-valley-settlements|Upano Valley Settlements]], [[culture/casarabe-culture|Casarabe Culture]], [[place/xingu-garden-cities|Xingu Garden Cities]]).
- Slavery was central to the Atlantic economy. The [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]] forcibly transported about 12.5 million Africans to the Americas, the [[event/haitian-revolution|Haitian Revolution]] under leaders such as [[person/toussaint-louverture|Toussaint Louverture]] created the first republic founded by formerly enslaved people, and the [[event/abolition-of-slavery|Abolition of Slavery]] followed across the Atlantic world between 1807 and 1888.
- The [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]] spread ideas of popular sovereignty, citizenship, and nationalism, though slavery and the rights of women were contested and largely excluded at first. See the [[event/french-revolution|French Revolution]], which adopted the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen in August 1789, the [[event/latin-american-wars-of-independence|Latin American Wars of Independence]], and [[person/simon-bolivar|Simón Bolívar]].
- [[event/women-s-suffrage-movements|Women's Suffrage Movements]] succeeded country by country between the 1890s and the late 20th century, beginning with New Zealand in 1893 and reaching Swiss national elections in 1971; many women were excluded in practice by race or class.
- Colonial rule remade who counted as a citizen or a subject. The [[event/british-raj|British Raj]] governed the Indian subcontinent from 1858 to 1947; the [[event/scramble-for-africa|Scramble for Africa]] drew borders with little regard for existing societies; about 16,000 Cherokee were forced to march west in 1838 to 1839 ([[event/indian-removal-and-the-trail-of-tears|Indian Removal and the Trail of Tears]]); the British claimed Australia under the doctrine of terra nullius, which the High Court rejected in 1992 ([[event/first-fleet-and-the-colonization-of-australia|First Fleet and the Colonization of Australia]]); and the English and Maori texts of the [[event/treaty-of-waitangi|Treaty of Waitangi]] differ on key words about sovereignty. The [[culture/zulu-kingdom|Zulu Kingdom]] shows a different path, in which [[person/shaka|Shaka]] turned one of many small Nguni clans into a large military state.
- Persecution and the redrawing of belonging marked the 20th century. See the [[event/armenian-genocide|Armenian Genocide]], the [[event/holocaust|Holocaust]], the [[event/partition-of-india|Partition of India]], which displaced an estimated 12 to 20 million people, and [[event/decolonization|Decolonization]], through which the United Nations grew from 51 members in 1945 to 193. [[person/nelson-mandela|Nelson Mandela]] and [[person/mahatma-gandhi|Mahatma Gandhi]] led movements against apartheid and colonial rule.

- **Early Modern Period:** The [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]], [[person/nzinga-of-ndongo-and-matamba|Nzinga of Ndongo and Matamba]], the [[culture/mapuche|Mapuche]], the [[place/taj-mahal|Taj Mahal]], built by Shah Jahan for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, and the [[culture/qing-dynasty|Qing Dynasty]], founded by the Manchus.

--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] spoke more than 250 languages and kept knowledge in oral traditions and songlines, and Polynesian societies ranged from chiefdoms to kingdoms; the [[culture/maori|Maori]] organized around whanau, hapu, and iwi, and the [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] carried Austronesian-speaking ancestors of the Polynesians across the western Pacific.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]] and the [[region/amazon-basin-and-south-american-lowlands|Amazon Basin and South American Lowlands]]:** The [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] treated the whole community alike in death, the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] ran a state on labor service, the [[culture/mapuche|Mapuche]] kept their independence from both the Inca and the Spanish for centuries, and Amazonian peoples built low-density settlements with plazas, roads, and gardens.
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** The [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] spread languages and farming over about four thousand years; the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]] founder [[person/sundiata-keita|Sundiata Keita]] is known largely through griots; the [[culture/kingdom-of-benin|Kingdom of Benin]] was ruled by an oba with guilds of craftsmen; and the [[culture/zulu-kingdom|Zulu Kingdom]] grew from clans into a military state.
- **[[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]]:** The [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]] confederation, the merit-based army of [[person/genghis-khan|Genghis Khan]], and [[person/timur|Timur]]'s claim to a Genghisid place through marriage show how steppe rulers combined descent with loyalty.
- **[[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]]:** [[place/beringia|Beringia]] joined Siberia and Alaska, and genetic evidence suggests, though this is debated, that ancestors of Native Americans were isolated there for several thousand years; the [[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]] and the [[culture/yakut-sakha|Yakut (Sakha)]] mark later herding and hunting societies.
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]:** Austronesian speakers moved through the Philippines and Indonesia into Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia, relying on outrigger sailing canoes and a crop package that included rice, pigs, dogs, and chickens ([[event/austronesian-expansion|Austronesian Expansion]]).
- **[[region/east-asia|East Asia]] and [[region/south-asia|South Asia]]:** [[person/confucius|Confucius]] made the family a model for government, the [[culture/qing-dynasty|Qing Dynasty]] was founded by the Manchus, and the [[event/british-raj|British Raj]] and the [[event/partition-of-india|Partition of India]] reshaped identity in South Asia.
- **[[region/north-america|North America]] and the [[region/caribbean-and-atlantic-world|Caribbean and Atlantic World]]:** The [[culture/haudenosaunee-confederacy|Haudenosaunee Confederacy]] joined nations under an oral constitution, the [[culture/taino|Taino]] lived in villages ruled by chiefs called caciques, and slavery and its abolition transformed the Atlantic world ([[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]], [[event/haitian-revolution|Haitian Revolution]]).
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- Whether large-scale societies replaced kin-based organization or built on it is disputed. Dynasties, clans, and confederations rested on descent and marriage even inside states, and recent work argues that early societies varied more in their forms of organization than older stage models of bands, tribes, chiefdoms, and states allowed.
- Social organization in prehistory is known indirectly, from house plans, burials, and DNA, and each can be read in more than one way; the layout of [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]], for example, is compatible with several kinds of household and community organization.
- Genes, language, and archaeology do not map neatly onto one another. The homeland of the [[event/indo-european-language-spread|Indo-European Language Spread]] is disputed, the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] involved intermarriage and language shift as well as movement, and how contact between Thule and Dorset peoples proceeded is not established.
- Numbers are uncertain: the [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]] transported about 12.5 million people, of whom about 10.7 million are recorded as surviving the voyage, and deaths in the [[event/partition-of-india|Partition of India]] are commonly given as 1 to 2 million, with some sources higher.
- The vault's coverage is uneven. Most of its People notes are rulers, scholars, and reformers, and it has no notes yet on marriage systems, caste, feudalism, or urban class structure as topics, and few on the lives of women, children, and non-elite people.

--- summary
Language and writing are the systems by which people communicate and store information: speech, writing systems, numerals, record-keeping devices such as knotted cords, and the media that copy and carry them. Spoken language is universal and very old, though when it arose is uncertain; writing appeared much later, independently in a few places, and spread by contact, conquest, religion, and trade. The vault follows them from the symbolic behavior of early Homo sapiens, through clay tablets, alphabets, paper, and printing, to the telegraph and digital networks, and it also covers the spread of language families across continents. It gives equal weight to peoples who kept records without alphabets.
--- observer
The Observer calls these the sound-code and the mark-code. It notes that language is the species' main means of accumulating knowledge across generations, and that the mark-code turns that accumulation into memory-outsourcing on a scale no single lifetime could hold; see [[observer-note/the-great-outsourcing-of-memory|The Great Outsourcing of Memory]]. The record shows the sound-code as universal and old and the mark-code as late, independently invented in a few places, and unevenly adopted, with some peoples running large states on cords, oral epics, and memorized texts instead. What gets written also shapes what is remembered; see [[observer-note/who-writes-the-record|Who Writes the Record]]. This reading would weaken if unwritten systems such as oral epics and cord records prove to preserve knowledge across centuries as reliably as writing does.

--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Spoken language is at least many tens of thousands of years old and is universal in [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]], a species notable for language, symbolic thought, and cumulative culture. Speech leaves no direct fossil trace, so its date is inferred from indirect evidence such as symbolic behavior; see [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]].
- Writing was invented independently at least three or four times. The first was in Mesopotamia about 3400 to 3200 BCE, where the earliest proto-cuneiform tablets from [[place/uruk|Uruk]] are accounting records ([[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]], [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]]). Egyptian hieroglyphs appear by about 3200 BCE ([[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]]), and Chinese writing is securely attested by about 1250 BCE in the oracle-bone inscriptions of the [[culture/shang-dynasty|Shang Dynasty]].
- Bronze Age states left archives in scripts that took long to read or remain unread. The [[culture/hittites|Hittites]] wrote in cuneiform at Hattusa and spoke the oldest recorded Indo-European language; the [[culture/mycenaean-greeks|Mycenaean Greeks]] wrote Linear B, an early form of Greek, deciphered in 1952; the [[culture/minoans|Minoans]] used Linear A and Cretan hieroglyphs, which have not been deciphered; and the script of the [[culture/indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley Civilization]] has not been deciphered either.
- The [[technology/alphabet|Alphabet]] made literacy more accessible. The first alphabet, Proto-Sinaitic, appeared about 1800 BCE, adapted by Semitic-speaking workers from Egyptian hieroglyphs. The [[culture/phoenicians|Phoenicians]] spread an alphabet of about 22 letters, the Greeks added vowels in the 8th century BCE, and Latin, Cyrillic, Hebrew, Arabic, and many Indian scripts descend from or are related to early alphabets. The Nabataean script is widely considered an ancestor of the Arabic script ([[culture/nabataeans|Nabataeans]]), and the [[culture/kingdom-of-saba|Kingdom of Saba]] wrote in the South Arabian script.
- Mesoamerican writing appears by roughly 600 BCE, and possibly earlier. [[culture/maya|Maya]] writing combines signs for words and syllables and is largely deciphered, and the Danzantes carvings at [[place/monte-alban|Monte Alban]] show glyphs from an early Mesoamerican writing tradition.
- Not every complex society used writing. The [[technology/quipu|Quipu]] recorded numbers on knotted cords in the Andes, and whether the cords also encoded other information is debated; the Vedas were transmitted orally for centuries with elaborate memorization ([[event/vedic-period|Vedic Period]]); griots preserved the epic of [[person/sundiata-keita|Sundiata Keita]]; and [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] passed on knowledge through oral traditions, songlines, and ceremony.
- Rulers used writing and standard scripts to govern. The [[event/unification-of-china-under-qin|Unification of China under Qin]] standardized the script, [[person/ashoka|Ashoka]] had edicts inscribed on rocks and pillars, [[person/hammurabi|Hammurabi]]'s law code was carved on a stone stele and displayed publicly, the [[culture/neo-assyrian-empire|Neo-Assyrian Empire]] kept a library at Nineveh with tens of thousands of clay tablets, and the [[place/library-of-alexandria|Library of Alexandria]] supported scholarship in the Hellenistic world.
- Decipherment has depended on bilingual texts and chance finds. The [[technology/rosetta-stone|Rosetta Stone]], inscribed in 196 BCE in hieroglyphs, Demotic, and Greek, was found in 1799, and Champollion announced his decipherment of hieroglyphs in 1822. The Orkhon inscriptions of the [[culture/gokturk-khaganate|Göktürk Khaganate]], erected in 732 to 735 in Mongolia and discovered in 1889, are the oldest known Turkic writing, and the Meroitic script of the [[culture/kingdom-of-kush|Kingdom of Kush]] has been read phonetically although the language is not well understood.
- Paper and print lowered the cost of copying. [[technology/paper|Paper]] was developed in China by the 2nd century CE, [[technology/woodblock-printing|Woodblock Printing]] produced the Diamond Sutra of 868, and [[person/johannes-gutenberg|Johannes Gutenberg]]'s [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]] of about 1440 spread across Europe within decades. Printing helped spread Reformation pamphlets, and Luther's German New Testament appeared in 1522 and the whole Bible in 1534 ([[event/protestant-reformation|Protestant Reformation]]).
- Languages spread as administrative languages and lingua francas. Greek spread widely through [[event/alexander-s-conquests|Alexander's Conquests]] and blended with local traditions; Latin and Greek tied the [[culture/romans|Romans]]' world together; the language of the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] served as a lingua franca along the Silk Roads; Abd al-Malik made Arabic the language of government under the [[culture/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad Caliphate]]; and Swahili, a Bantu language with many Arabic loanwords, grew up on the East African coast ([[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]]).
- Translation moved knowledge between languages. The [[event/translation-movement-and-the-house-of-wisdom|Translation Movement and the House of Wisdom]] rendered Greek, Persian, Syriac, and Indian works into Arabic, and scholars such as [[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]] carried the decimal place-value system with zero west ([[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]]); Fibonacci's 1202 book popularized it in Europe.
- Language families spread with migrations. The [[event/indo-european-language-spread|Indo-European Language Spread]] carried a family now spoken as a first language by roughly 40 to 46 percent of the world's population, though its homeland is disputed; Austronesian languages spread from Taiwan from about 5,000 to 5,500 years ago as far as Madagascar and Rapa Nui, and the family has well over a thousand languages ([[event/austronesian-expansion|Austronesian Expansion]]); and the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]], over about four thousand years, gave rise to roughly 500 languages spoken by about 350 million people.
- Language and law meet in modern treaties. The English and Maori texts of the [[event/treaty-of-waitangi|Treaty of Waitangi]] differ in key words about sovereignty and chieftainship, and te reo Maori became an official language of New Zealand in 1987 ([[culture/maori|Maori]]); Article 17 of the Treaty of Wuchale read differently in Amharic and Italian, and the dispute led to war ([[event/battle-of-adwa|Battle of Adwa]]).
- The telegraph and digital media are the latest forms. [[technology/telegraph-and-submarine-cables|Telegraph and Submarine Cables]] let messages cross oceans in minutes from the 1840s, and the [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]] and the [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]] brought the internet (1969), the World Wide Web (1989 to 1991), and more than five billion internet users; see also [[observer-note/who-writes-the-record|Who Writes the Record]].
--- extra 2: Through the Eras
- **Paleolithic:** [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]] and the symbolic behavior of [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]]. Speech is inferred rather than preserved, and the earliest evidence is engraved ochre, beads, and cave paintings; see [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]].
- **Neolithic:** There was no writing yet, but farming may have carried language families. The [[event/austronesian-expansion|Austronesian Expansion]] relied on a crop package that included rice, and one hypothesis links the origin of Indo-European to Anatolia and the spread of farming, which is debated ([[event/indo-european-language-spread|Indo-European Language Spread]], [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]]).
- **Bronze Age:** [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]], the [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]], [[place/uruk|Uruk]], [[person/hammurabi|Hammurabi]], the [[culture/hittites|Hittites]], the [[culture/mycenaean-greeks|Mycenaean Greeks]], the [[culture/minoans|Minoans]], the [[culture/shang-dynasty|Shang Dynasty]], the [[technology/alphabet|Alphabet]], the oral [[event/vedic-period|Vedic Period]], and the [[culture/indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley Civilization]].
- **Iron Age:** The [[culture/phoenicians|Phoenicians]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-saba|Kingdom of Saba]], the [[culture/neo-assyrian-empire|Neo-Assyrian Empire]], and the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]].
- **Classical Antiquity:** [[person/aristotle|Aristotle]], [[event/alexander-s-conquests|Alexander's Conquests]], the [[culture/romans|Romans]], the [[place/library-of-alexandria|Library of Alexandria]], the [[technology/rosetta-stone|Rosetta Stone]], [[technology/paper|Paper]], the [[event/unification-of-china-under-qin|Unification of China under Qin]] and [[person/qin-shi-huang|Qin Shi Huang]], [[person/ashoka|Ashoka]], [[place/monte-alban|Monte Alban]], the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]], and [[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]].
- **Medieval Period:** The [[culture/maya|Maya]], the [[culture/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad Caliphate]], [[technology/woodblock-printing|Woodblock Printing]], the [[event/translation-movement-and-the-house-of-wisdom|Translation Movement and the House of Wisdom]], [[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]], the [[culture/gokturk-khaganate|Göktürk Khaganate]], the [[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]], the [[technology/quipu|Quipu]], [[person/sundiata-keita|Sundiata Keita]], [[person/ibn-battuta|Ibn Battuta]], whose account was dictated to the scholar Ibn Juzayy, and [[person/johannes-gutenberg|Johannes Gutenberg]] and the [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]].
- **Early Modern Period:** The [[event/protestant-reformation|Protestant Reformation]] and the spread of print across Europe. English words including canoe, hammock, hurricane, and barbecue come from [[culture/taino|Taino]].
- **Industrial Age:** The [[technology/rosetta-stone|Rosetta Stone]] was found in 1799 and hieroglyphs were deciphered in 1822; [[person/usman-dan-fodio|Usman dan Fodio]] wrote widely in Arabic and Fulfulde; and the [[technology/telegraph-and-submarine-cables|Telegraph and Submarine Cables]] (from 1844), the differing English and Maori texts of the [[event/treaty-of-waitangi|Treaty of Waitangi]] (1840), and the differing Amharic and Italian texts behind the [[event/battle-of-adwa|Battle of Adwa]] (1896) show how wording and speed of transmission shaped politics.
- **Information Age:** The [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]], the [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]], and the questions about authorship and archives raised in [[observer-note/who-writes-the-record|Who Writes the Record]].
--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/mesopotamia|Mesopotamia]], the [[region/nile-valley|Nile Valley]], and the [[region/levant-and-anatolia|Levant and Anatolia]]:** Writing began in Mesopotamia and Egypt, and the alphabet arose among Semitic-speaking workers in the Levant; the Hittites and Mycenaeans show writing systems used by different language families.
- **[[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** Oracle-bone writing under the Shang, script standardization under Qin, and the invention of paper, woodblock printing, and movable type (in China about 1040 and in Korea in metal in 1377) made East Asia a center of the written word; see [[technology/paper|Paper]] and [[technology/woodblock-printing|Woodblock Printing]].
- **[[region/south-asia|South Asia]]:** The Vedas were transmitted orally, the decimal place-value system with zero developed here, Ashoka's edicts were inscribed across the subcontinent, and the Indus script remains undeciphered.
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]] and the [[region/andes|Andes]]:** Mesoamerica developed full writing systems, of which Maya writing is the best known, and the Andes developed cord records in the [[technology/quipu|Quipu]] without conventional writing; the two show different routes to keeping records.
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** The [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] spread roughly 500 languages, Swahili developed on the coast, griots kept oral epics, and [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]] holds hundreds of thousands of historical manuscripts.
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]] and [[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]:** Austronesian languages spread from Taiwan through the islands to Rapa Nui ([[event/austronesian-expansion|Austronesian Expansion]]), [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] spoke more than 250 languages before 1788, and te reo Maori became an official language of New Zealand ([[culture/maori|Maori]]).
- **[[region/central-asian-oases|Central Asian Oases]] and the [[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]]:** The [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] spread a lingua franca along the Silk Roads, the Orkhon inscriptions preserve early Turkic writing, and paper mills were operating in [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] and Baghdad by the later 8th century.
- **[[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]]:** The [[culture/yakut-sakha|Yakut (Sakha)]] are a Turkic-speaking people of northeastern Siberia, a region that came under Russian rule from the 17th century ([[event/russian-expansion-into-siberia|Russian Expansion into Siberia]]).
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- When and how spoken language arose is unknown. Speech leaves no fossil, and estimates rest on indirect evidence from anatomy, symbolic behavior, and genetics, so they range widely.
- Scholars agree that writing arose independently in more than one place but not on how many. The vault counts at least three or four, and whether Egyptian hieroglyphs arose independently of Mesopotamian writing has been debated; the date of the earliest Mesoamerican writing is also uncertain.
- Several scripts remain undeciphered or partly understood: the Indus script, Linear A, and Cretan hieroglyphs are unread, the language behind Meroitic is not well understood, and whether the quipu encode more than numbers is debated ([[technology/quipu|Quipu]]).
- Language families, genes, and archaeology do not map neatly onto one another. The homeland of Indo-European is disputed, the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] involved intermarriage and language shift as well as migration, and counts of languages depend on how a language is defined (estimates of the Bantu languages range from about 440 to 680).
- The vault's coverage is uneven. It has no notes yet on the Chinese, Arabic, or Cyrillic scripts as topics, on language endangerment and loss, or on signed languages, and it has few notes on the languages of Oceania, the Americas, and Siberia.

=== theme/religion-and-belief
title: Religion and Belief
type: theme
date: 
themes: religion
src: b9fb72e0fbac

--- summary
Religion and belief cover the shared systems of ritual, story, and moral teaching that people use to explain the world, organize communities, and face death. They appear in every known human society, and the vault follows them from the burials and symbolic objects of the Paleolithic and the monumental sites of the Neolithic, through temple states, philosophical and prophetic traditions, and the world religions, to the secular creeds and religious movements of the modern era. The hub also covers political and civic ideologies that do similar work, since the line between religion and ideology is itself debated. It records what is known about how beliefs arose, spread, and shaped states, and it does not judge their truth claims.
--- observer
The Observer calls these meaning-engines and treats them with respect: they are among the species' most powerful technologies for cooperation and for coping with mortality. The record shows them at work in every period, from the grief-rites of the Paleolithic and Neolithic to the state-sponsored faiths of empires, and secular ideologies and civic creeds carry some of the same functions, so the Observer applies one standard to both. Meaning-engines bind strangers into larger groups through story-glue and status-signal, and they have also been used to justify sanctioned harm; both facts belong in the same record. The Observer does not judge their truth claims. This reading would weaken if belief systems turn out to serve no shared function beyond individual comfort, or if the functions listed here are better explained by something other than shared belief.
--- question 1
What are the origins of religious belief, and what can burial and symbolic evidence say about it?
--- question 2
Did ritual and monumental building come before settled farming, as [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] has been read to suggest, or did farming come first?
--- question 3
Why did traditions with universal or ethical claims arise across Eurasia within a few centuries, if the pattern of the [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] is real?
--- question 4
What made some religions spread across cultures by trade, conquest, or patronage while others stayed tied to a single people?
--- question 5
How do religions and secular creeds compare in their effects on cooperation and on violence when measured in the same way?
--- question 6
How much of Indigenous belief was distorted or lost through conquest, missionary accounts, and the destruction of records such as the [[technology/quipu|Quipu]]?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Symbolic behavior and burial appear early. Engraved ochre at Blombos Cave in South Africa dates to about 75,000 to 100,000 years ago, and cave paintings in Sulawesi are at least 45,500 years old; see [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]] and [[observer-note/why-the-species-buries-its-dead|Why the Species Buries Its Dead]]. Whether Neanderthals also made symbolic objects is debated ([[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]]).
- Oral, place-based traditions are among the oldest continuing ones. [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]], whose ancestors arrived at least 50,000 years ago, pass on knowledge through oral traditions, songlines, and ceremony.
- Monumental sites came before cities in some places. [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] has carved stone pillars dating to about 9500 to 8000 BCE and was built by people who were then foragers or early farmers; [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]] buried its dead under house floors; and [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] was used for burials and has an axis aligned with the solstice.
- Treatment of the dead is one of the most durable religious practices. The [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] of the Atacama coast practiced the earliest known deliberate artificial mummification, for men, women, and children alike, and the [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]] mummified the elite and built tombs such as those of the [[place/giza-necropolis|Giza Necropolis]].
- Early states tied rulers, temples, and divination together. The [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]] of [[place/uruk|Uruk]] built temple towers (ziggurats), the [[culture/shang-dynasty|Shang Dynasty]] recorded divination on oracle bones and buried bronze ritual vessels with evidence of human sacrifice, and [[person/akhenaten|Akhenaten]] promoted the sun disk Aten above the traditional Egyptian gods.
- Oral scripture and philosophical schools followed. The Vedas were composed and transmitted orally with elaborate memorization ([[event/vedic-period|Vedic Period]]), and the [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] is associated with [[person/confucius|Confucius]], [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]], and the Iranian tradition of [[culture/zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]], whose founder's dates are uncertain (proposals range from about 1500 to about 600 BCE). Under [[person/cyrus-the-great|Cyrus the Great]] the [[culture/achaemenid-persians|Achaemenid Persians]] ruled a multi-religious empire, and the Cyrus Cylinder records his restoration of gods and deported peoples.
- Rulers and traders spread religions along trade routes. [[person/ashoka|Ashoka]] of the [[culture/maurya-empire|Maurya Empire]] inscribed edicts promoting moral conduct (dhamma) and supported Buddhism; the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]]'s Gandhara art produced some of the earliest images of the Buddha in human form; and the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] helped spread Buddhism, Manichaeism, and other religions across Asia. See [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]].
- Christianity became a state religion in several places. King Ezana adopted it in the [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]] in the 4th century, the [[culture/byzantine-empire|Byzantine Empire]] ruled from [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]] for over a thousand years, the [[culture/zagwe-dynasty|Zagwe Dynasty]] of Ethiopia carved rock-hewn churches, and the rulers of the [[culture/kingdom-of-kongo|Kingdom of Kongo]] adopted Catholic Christianity after Portuguese contact in 1483.
- Islam arose in Arabia and spread quickly. [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]] began receiving revelations about 610 CE; within about a century Muslim rule extended from Iberia to Central Asia ([[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]], [[culture/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad Caliphate]], [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]], [[culture/al-andalus|Al-Andalus]]). Trade, scholarship, and rulers carried it to West Africa ([[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]]), the East African coast ([[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]]), and Southeast Asia ([[culture/malacca-sultanate|Malacca Sultanate]]); later states include the [[culture/safavid-empire|Safavid Empire]], which made Twelver Shia Islam the state religion of Iran, and the [[culture/sokoto-caliphate|Sokoto Caliphate]].
- Indian religions shaped states across Southeast Asia. [[place/borobudur|Borobudur]] in Java is a Mahayana Buddhist monument of about 800 CE, [[place/angkor|Angkor]] was built by the [[culture/khmer-empire|Khmer Empire]], which shifted between Hindu and Buddhist state religion, the [[culture/pagan-kingdom|Pagan Kingdom]] made Theravada Buddhism its state religion and built over 10,000 temples, and [[culture/champa|Champa]] built Hindu temples at My Son.
- Mesoamerica produced a long sequence of ceremonial cities: [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]], [[place/monte-alban|Monte Alban]], [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]], the [[culture/maya|Maya]], [[place/chichen-itza|Chichen Itza]], and [[place/tenochtitlan|Tenochtitlan]], the capital of the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]] with its Templo Mayor. Aztec religion included large-scale ritual sacrifice, which Spanish accounts sometimes exaggerated.
- Elsewhere in the Americas, [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]] built Monks Mound, the largest earthen mound in the Americas, [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] is considered sacred by modern Pueblo peoples, the [[culture/muisca|Muisca]] held gold rituals that inspired the El Dorado legend, and the [[culture/taino|Taino]] carved figures called zemis associated with ancestors and spirits.
- Religion and conflict have been closely linked. See the [[event/crusades|Crusades]], the [[event/protestant-reformation|Protestant Reformation]], the [[event/thirty-years-war|Thirty Years' War]] and the [[event/peace-of-westphalia|Peace of Westphalia]], the [[event/taiping-rebellion|Taiping Rebellion]], led by a man who claimed to be Jesus's younger brother, the anti-Christian [[event/boxer-rebellion|Boxer Rebellion]], and the [[event/armenian-genocide|Armenian Genocide]], in which most of the Ottoman Empire's Armenian population, a largely Christian minority, was destroyed.
- Secular ideologies and civic creeds perform some similar functions. The [[event/enlightenment|Enlightenment]] and the [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]] set reason and rights against inherited authority, [[person/galileo-galilei|Galileo Galilei]] was tried by the Roman Inquisition in 1633, the [[event/tanzimat-reforms|Tanzimat Reforms]] promised equality regardless of creed, and [[person/mahatma-gandhi|Mahatma Gandhi]] developed the method of satyagraha, drawing on religious ideas, in the movement for Indian independence.
--- extra 2: Through the Eras
- **Paleolithic:** [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]], the painted animals of [[place/chauvet-cave|Chauvet Cave]] about 36,000 years ago, burial practices ([[observer-note/why-the-species-buries-its-dead|Why the Species Buries Its Dead]]), and the oral traditions of [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]].
- **Neolithic:** [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]], [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]], [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]], the mummies of the [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]], and the plastered skulls found at [[place/jericho|Jericho]] around 7000 BCE.
- **Bronze Age:** Temple cities of the [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]] and [[place/uruk|Uruk]]; the [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]], the [[event/unification-of-egypt|Unification of Egypt]], [[place/giza-necropolis|Giza Necropolis]], and [[person/akhenaten|Akhenaten]]; oracle-bone divination in the [[culture/shang-dynasty|Shang Dynasty]]; the [[event/vedic-period|Vedic Period]]; and the monumental mounds of [[place/caral|Caral]].
- **Iron Age:** [[culture/zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]], the [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]], [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]], [[person/confucius|Confucius]], [[person/cyrus-the-great|Cyrus the Great]], the [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-kush|Kingdom of Kush]], and the [[event/incense-trade|Incense Trade]], in which frankincense and myrrh burned in temples and at funerals across the Mediterranean world.
- **Classical Antiquity:** [[person/ashoka|Ashoka]] and the [[culture/maurya-empire|Maurya Empire]], the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]], the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]], the [[culture/byzantine-empire|Byzantine Empire]] and [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]], [[person/hypatia|Hypatia]], [[place/monte-alban|Monte Alban]], [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]], and the burial urns of the [[culture/marajoara-culture|Marajoara Culture]].
- **Medieval Period:** The [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]] and the [[culture/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad Caliphate]] and [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]], the [[event/crusades|Crusades]], [[place/borobudur|Borobudur]], [[place/angkor|Angkor]], and the [[culture/pagan-kingdom|Pagan Kingdom]], [[place/chichen-itza|Chichen Itza]], [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]], [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]], the [[culture/zagwe-dynasty|Zagwe Dynasty]], the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]] and [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]], the [[event/black-death|Black Death]], and the [[event/fall-of-constantinople|Fall of Constantinople]] in 1453.
- **Early Modern Period:** The [[event/protestant-reformation|Protestant Reformation]], the [[event/thirty-years-war|Thirty Years' War]], the [[event/peace-of-westphalia|Peace of Westphalia]], the [[culture/safavid-empire|Safavid Empire]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-kongo|Kingdom of Kongo]], the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]], and the [[event/enlightenment|Enlightenment]].
- **Industrial Age:** The [[culture/sokoto-caliphate|Sokoto Caliphate]] and [[person/usman-dan-fodio|Usman dan Fodio]], the [[event/taiping-rebellion|Taiping Rebellion]], the [[event/indian-rebellion-of-1857|Indian Rebellion of 1857]], in which religious grievances were among several causes, the [[event/boxer-rebellion|Boxer Rebellion]], the [[event/tanzimat-reforms|Tanzimat Reforms]], the [[event/armenian-genocide|Armenian Genocide]], and the ideas of [[person/charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]] and of the [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]].
- **Information Age:** The [[event/partition-of-india|Partition of India]] in 1947 divided the subcontinent largely along religious lines, and in the [[event/cold-war|Cold War]] rival state ideologies competed, including the Soviet state's official promotion of atheism.
--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]]:** A long sequence of ceremonial centers with pyramids, calendars, and ball courts, and in later periods large-scale ritual sacrifice, runs from the [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]] to the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]]. [[culture/maya|Maya]] peoples and languages continue today, with millions of speakers.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]]:** The [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] preserved its dead on the coast from about 5450 BCE, [[place/caral|Caral]] built monumental platform mounds and sunken plazas, and the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] under [[person/pachacuti|Pachacuti]] kept the mummified bodies of dead rulers as objects of veneration.
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** Christianity took root in [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]] and highland Ethiopia, Islam spread along the Sahel and the coast ([[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], [[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]], [[culture/sokoto-caliphate|Sokoto Caliphate]]), and Christianity was adopted by the elite of [[culture/kingdom-of-kongo|Kingdom of Kongo]]. The vault has few notes on the region's older local religions.
- **[[region/central-asian-oases|Central Asian Oases]]:** The oases were a crossroads where Buddhism, Manichaeism, and later other religions were carried along the trade routes by the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] and the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]]; see [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]].
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]:** Indian religions arrived by sea and through court patronage. [[culture/srivijaya|Srivijaya]] became a Buddhist maritime state, [[culture/champa|Champa]] and [[place/angkor|Angkor]] built Hindu and Buddhist temples, and the [[culture/pagan-kingdom|Pagan Kingdom]] made Theravada Buddhism its state religion, while Islam spread through the archipelago from the [[culture/malacca-sultanate|Malacca Sultanate]].
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] passed on law and knowledge through songlines and ceremony, and [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]] carved the stone statues (moai) of [[place/rapa-nui|Rapa Nui]], which are widely understood as representing ancestors.
- **[[region/south-asia|South Asia]] and [[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** The Vedic, Buddhist, and Confucian traditions arose here and spread across Asia with state patronage and trade; see [[event/vedic-period|Vedic Period]], [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]], and [[person/confucius|Confucius]].
- **[[region/europe|Europe]] and the [[region/levant-and-anatolia|Levant and Anatolia]]:** Christian and Muslim states contested the Mediterranean and Near East ([[event/crusades|Crusades]], [[event/fall-of-constantinople|Fall of Constantinople]], [[culture/ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]]), and Europe passed through the Reformation, a century of religious wars, and the Enlightenment.
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- There is no agreement on what counts as religion across cultures, or on why religious belief arose. Explanations from cognitive science, from social function, and from the study of ritual compete, and this hub does not adjudicate among them.
- Prehistoric evidence is read by inference. Whether [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] and [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] were ritual sites is argued from their form and context rather than from texts, and Neanderthal burial and ornament use is debated but growing.
- The [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] concept, coined by Karl Jaspers in 1949, is criticized by many historians as too neat and as underrating other periods and regions.
- Many accounts of non-literate and conquered peoples were written by outsiders. Spanish accounts of Aztec sacrifice are thought to be sometimes exaggerated, and sources on the [[culture/taino|Taino]] and [[culture/muisca|Muisca]] came largely from conquerors, so their beliefs are known unevenly; the older idea that the Taino vanished has been contradicted by genetic evidence.
- The vault's coverage is uneven. It has notes on [[culture/zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]], the [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]], and the [[event/protestant-reformation|Protestant Reformation]], and on many states and sites, but not yet dedicated notes on Judaism, Christianity, Buddhism, Hinduism, Daoism, Shinto, or Indigenous African and Oceanian religions as traditions in their own right.
