=== theme/science
title: Science
type: theme
date: 
themes: science
src: 6774382f4b3d
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Science is the systematic study of the natural world through observation, measurement, mathematics, and testing, together with the habits and institutions that let people check and extend one another's claims. Its practices run from the counting, calendar-keeping, and sky-watching of prehistoric peoples, through the mathematics, astronomy, and medicine of the early literate states and the scholarly centers of the medieval world, to the experimental sciences of Europe from the sixteenth century and the genomics, computing, and Earth-system research of the twenty-first. Traditions of careful observation arose independently in many regions, and modern science grew from several of them rather than from one. This hub gathers the vault's notes on scientists, institutions, instruments, and discoveries, and on the events that changed how knowledge of nature was made and passed on.
--- observer
The Observer calls this pattern-taming: the species' long effort to find regularities in nature, record them, and use them to predict. Counting, calendars, and careful observation appear in many places long before the modern period, so what looks distinctive about the modern form is its institutionalized doubt, in which strangers agree to check one another's claims and to publish what they find. That habit rests on memory-outsourcing (writing, paper, printing) and on exchange-webs that carried instruments, texts, and people, and it works against ancestor-weight, since the older authority is often the first claim to be tested. This reading would weaken if institutionalized doubt proves to have been as old and as widespread as counting itself, or if it turns out to depend on something other than writing and travel.
--- question 1
How did the culture of open criticism emerge and spread, and where did it exist in institutions before the seventeenth century?
--- question 2
How much of the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] rested on knowledge that moved from the Islamic world, India, and China, and how can that debt be measured?
--- question 3
What can be recovered of scientific traditions that were carried by oral teaching, songlines, and cord records?
--- question 4
Why did centers of learning such as the [[place/library-of-alexandria|Library of Alexandria]], Baghdad, and [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] decline, and what was lost when they did?
--- question 5
Why do major discoveries so often arise independently in more than one mind or place, as with the calculus and natural selection?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Counting, calendars, and sky-watching are among the oldest organized knowledge. The axis of [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] aligns with the solstice sunrise and sunset, buildings and rock carvings at [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] align with the sun and moon, and the [[culture/maya|Maya]] kept precise calendars.
- Writing turned observation into records that others could check. [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]] began in [[region/mesopotamia|Mesopotamia]] as accounting, and Mesopotamian scribes, from the time of the [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]], worked in a base-60 number system that survives in the division of the hour and the circle.
- The [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]] left medical papyri, such as the Edwin Smith papyrus of about 1600 BCE, that set out the examination and treatment of injuries case by case; see [[region/nile-valley|Nile Valley]].
- The [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] brought new traditions of reasoned inquiry in Greece, India, and China; see [[person/confucius|Confucius]] and the [[culture/ancient-greeks|Ancient Greeks]].
- Greek natural philosophy sought general causes. [[person/aristotle|Aristotle]] studied physics and biology, including careful observation of marine life; the [[place/library-of-alexandria|Library of Alexandria]] supported scholars such as Eratosthenes, who estimated the circumference of the Earth; and [[person/hypatia|Hypatia]] taught mathematics and astronomy in Alexandria. See also [[place/athens|Athens]].
- The decimal place-value system with zero developed in India in the first millennium CE and spread west; see [[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]] and the [[culture/gupta-empire|Gupta Empire]]. The age of the earliest zero, in the Bakhshali manuscript, is contested.
- Under the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]], the [[event/translation-movement-and-the-house-of-wisdom|Translation Movement and the House of Wisdom]] rendered Greek, Persian, Syriac, and Indian works into Arabic and extended them. The arithmetic and algebra of [[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]] gave later languages the words algorithm and algebra, and [[person/ibn-sina|Ibn Sina]]'s Canon of Medicine was used in European universities into the 17th century. [[culture/al-andalus|Al-Andalus]] became another center of learning.
- Observatories, libraries, and manuscript collections grew along the Silk Roads and the Saharan routes. [[person/ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]]'s observatory at [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] produced a star catalogue of at least 1,018 stars, and [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]] holds hundreds of thousands of historical manuscripts.
- Pacific navigators crossed thousands of kilometers using star paths, swells, and bird flight rather than instruments; see [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]] and [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]].
- Instruments extended what could be measured and shared: the [[technology/magnetic-compass|Magnetic Compass]] in China by about the 12th century, the [[technology/mechanical-clock|Mechanical Clock]] (Su Song's astronomical clock tower of 1088; weight-driven clocks in European towns about 1300), and the [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]] of about 1440.
- The [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] (roughly 1543 to 1687) made experiment, mathematics, and observation central; see [[person/galileo-galilei|Galileo Galilei]] and [[person/isaac-newton|Isaac Newton]]. It drew on earlier work in the Islamic world, India, and China.
- The [[event/enlightenment|Enlightenment]] carried a preference for evidence into public life. In 1796 Edward Jenner showed that cowpox inoculation protected against smallpox, the start of [[technology/vaccination|Vaccination]]; smallpox was declared eradicated in 1980.
- The 19th and early 20th centuries brought evolution by natural selection ([[person/charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]]), radioactivity ([[person/marie-curie|Marie Curie]]), relativity ([[person/albert-einstein|Albert Einstein]]), and what is often regarded as the first published computer algorithm ([[person/ada-lovelace|Ada Lovelace]]); the [[event/meiji-restoration|Meiji Restoration]] and the [[event/tanzimat-reforms|Tanzimat Reforms]] were state programs to acquire the new sciences and their institutions.
- Late 20th-century science gave computation ([[person/alan-turing|Alan Turing]], [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]]), the structure of DNA ([[person/rosalind-franklin|Rosalind Franklin]], [[event/genomic-revolution|Genomic Revolution]]), and spaceflight ([[event/space-age|Space Age]], [[event/moon-landing|Moon Landing]]); [[person/rachel-carson|Rachel Carson]], [[event/modern-climate-change|Modern Climate Change]], the [[place/jack-hills-zircons|Jack Hills Zircons]], and the [[event/tunguska-event|Tunguska Event]] show Earth-system science at work.
--- extra 2: Through the Eras
- **Paleolithic:** Working knowledge of stone, fire, animals, and plants accumulates ([[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]], [[event/control-of-fire|Control of Fire]], [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]]). [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] managed the landscape with fire and passed on ecological knowledge through oral traditions and songlines. Notched bones from this era may be tallies, though their purpose is debated.
- **Neolithic:** Farmers select seed and stock over generations ([[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]], [[event/domestication-of-rice|Domestication of Rice]], [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]]), and builders align monuments with the sun ([[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]]).
- **Bronze Age:** Writing lets scribes keep records, tables, and observations ([[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]], [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]], [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]]); Mesopotamian arithmetic uses base 60, and Egyptian medical texts describe cases and treatments.
- **Iron Age:** Reasoned inquiry into nature and human affairs appears in the [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] in Greece, India, and China ([[person/confucius|Confucius]], [[culture/ancient-greeks|Ancient Greeks]]); Polynesian voyagers build up knowledge of stars and swells ([[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]]).
- **Classical Antiquity:** [[place/athens|Athens]] and [[person/aristotle|Aristotle]], the [[place/library-of-alexandria|Library of Alexandria]] and [[person/hypatia|Hypatia]], and in India [[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]] under the [[culture/gupta-empire|Gupta Empire]]. The [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] kept astronomical records, and its scholar Zhang Heng built a seismoscope in 132 CE. The [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]] moved ideas along with goods.
- **Medieval Period:** The [[event/translation-movement-and-the-house-of-wisdom|Translation Movement and the House of Wisdom]], [[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]], [[person/ibn-sina|Ibn Sina]], and [[culture/al-andalus|Al-Andalus]]; the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]]'s [[technology/magnetic-compass|Magnetic Compass]] and [[technology/gunpowder|Gunpowder]]; the [[culture/maya|Maya]] calendar and astronomy; [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]]; [[person/ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]] and the [[culture/timurid-empire|Timurid Empire]]; [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]]; [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]]'s theory of how states rise and fall; the [[technology/mechanical-clock|Mechanical Clock]]; the [[event/renaissance|Renaissance]].
- **Early Modern Period:** The [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] ([[person/galileo-galilei|Galileo Galilei]], [[person/isaac-newton|Isaac Newton]]), the [[event/enlightenment|Enlightenment]], and [[technology/vaccination|Vaccination]] (1796).
- **Industrial Age:** The [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]] created new demand for measurement and engineering. [[person/charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]], [[person/ada-lovelace|Ada Lovelace]], [[person/marie-curie|Marie Curie]], [[person/albert-einstein|Albert Einstein]], [[technology/antibiotics|Antibiotics]], [[technology/nuclear-fission|Nuclear Fission]], and the [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]] belong here, as do state modernization programs such as the [[event/meiji-restoration|Meiji Restoration]] and [[event/tanzimat-reforms|Tanzimat Reforms]].
- **Information Age:** [[person/alan-turing|Alan Turing]] and the [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]], [[person/rosalind-franklin|Rosalind Franklin]] and the [[event/genomic-revolution|Genomic Revolution]], the [[event/space-age|Space Age]] and [[event/moon-landing|Moon Landing]], [[person/rachel-carson|Rachel Carson]] and [[event/modern-climate-change|Modern Climate Change]], the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]], and the [[event/covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 Pandemic]], in which mRNA vaccines were developed and authorized within about a year.
--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/mesopotamia|Mesopotamia]] and the [[region/nile-valley|Nile Valley]]:** The earliest written mathematics, astronomy, and medicine are tied to temple and palace administration; see [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]] and [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]].
- **[[region/south-asia|South Asia]] and the Islamic world:** Place-value notation with zero arose in India and was carried west by scholars of the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]] and [[culture/al-andalus|Al-Andalus]]; Latin translations of Arabic texts fed European scholarship in the 12th century.
- **[[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** Paper, printing, the compass, and gunpowder were documented first in China, and the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]] was a center of technical and commercial innovation.
- **Central Asia:** The oases were a crossroads for learning. [[person/ibn-sina|Ibn Sina]] was born near Bukhara, [[person/ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]] built his observatory at [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]], and the [[culture/timurid-empire|Timurid Empire]] patronized it; see [[region/central-asian-oases|Central Asian Oases]].
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** Polynesian voyagers navigated without instruments ([[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]]), the [[place/kuk-early-agricultural-site|Kuk Early Agricultural Site]] preserves at least 7,000 years of wetland gardening, and the [[place/jack-hills-zircons|Jack Hills Zircons]] in Western Australia hold the oldest known pieces of Earth's crust, about 4.4 billion years old.
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]] and the [[region/andes|Andes]]:** The [[culture/maya|Maya]] kept precise calendars and used the concept of zero, and the Andean [[technology/quipu|Quipu]] recorded numbers in a decimal system on knotted cords.
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** Scholarship in [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]] and much of the fossil evidence for human origins ([[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]], [[event/origin-of-homo-sapiens|Origin of Homo sapiens]]) come from this region.
- **[[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]]:** The [[event/tunguska-event|Tunguska Event]] of 1908 was first studied on site by an expedition in 1927, and Arctic peoples such as the Inuit built up detailed knowledge of ice, weather, and animal behavior for survival ([[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]]).
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- Whether star-lore, navigation, and Indigenous ecological knowledge should be called science, or only the experimental and mathematical tradition of recent centuries, is disputed. This hub uses a broad definition and treats the narrower one as a later development.
- Historians debate whether the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] was a single revolution and how much it owed to earlier work in the Islamic world, India, and China. The exact nature of the House of Wisdom is also debated, and the [[place/library-of-alexandria|Library of Alexandria]] declined over centuries rather than in one fire.
- Credit is contested in many cases: the earliest zero ([[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]]), the calculus, which [[person/isaac-newton|Isaac Newton]] developed independently of Leibniz, natural selection, which [[person/charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]] and Alfred Russel Wallace reached separately, the use of [[person/rosalind-franklin|Rosalind Franklin]]'s X-ray data in the double-helix model, and Lise Meitner's exclusion from the 1944 Nobel Prize for [[technology/nuclear-fission|Nuclear Fission]].
- Science that was carried by oral teaching, songlines, and cord records left few written texts, so it is easy to undercount.
- The vault's science notes lean toward Europe, the Islamic world, and named individuals. It has few notes on scientists in Africa south of the Sahara, Oceania, the Andes, and Mesoamerica, and none yet on Indian astronomy or Chinese mathematics as topics in their own right.

=== theme/technology
title: Technology
type: theme

--- summary
Technology is the making and use of tools, techniques, and systems to extend what people can do. It is cumulative: each generation builds on the tools of the last, and the record runs from the first chipped stones, at least 2.6 million years ago, through fire, pottery, farming, metals, writing, and printing, to engines, electric grids, and digital networks. This hub covers materials, energy, transport, information, food, medicine, and the built environment, and it links the vault's technology notes to the events, places, and peoples through which techniques arose and spread. Many techniques arose independently in more than one region, and others crossed continents within a few centuries.
--- observer
The Observer calls this the tool-lineage: an inheritance in which each tool makes the next possible. The lineage branches rather than running in a single line, and it sometimes breaks when a technique dies with its makers, yet similar solutions such as farming and writing appear in unconnected branches, which suggests that some steps are shaped by shared needs and materials as well as by chance. Memory-outsourcing made the lineage far easier to carry across generations, and ancestor-weight can keep a working technique in use long after alternatives exist. The lineage is now old enough that no single person understands the whole of it. The Observer would revise this reading if apparent independent origins prove to be diffusion after all, or if the loss of techniques turns out to be much more common than the record shows.
--- question 1
Do technologies follow a predictable order, or are they contingent?
--- question 2
Why did some techniques arise independently in several regions, such as farming and writing, while others, such as wheeled transport, were not used in the Americas before contact?
--- question 3
How much perishable technology (wood, cord, cloth, basketry) is missing from the record, and how can its scale be estimated?
--- question 4
Where did the pace of change shift decisively: with fire, farming, writing, the steam engine, or the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]]?
--- question 5
How were techniques carried between cultures, by traders, craftspeople, captives, missionaries, and armies, and which traditional stories, such as papermaking at [[event/battle-of-talas|Battle of Talas]], survive scrutiny?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Stone tools begin at least 2.6 million years ago in Africa; see [[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]] and [[species/homo-habilis|Homo habilis]], and, later, the handaxes made by [[species/homo-erectus|Homo erectus]].
- [[event/control-of-fire|Control of Fire]] is an early energy technology. Evidence at Wonderwerk Cave in South Africa dates to about one million years ago, habitual use is well established by about 400,000 years ago, and cooked food may have supported larger brains.
- [[technology/pottery|Pottery]] appears in East Asia at least 20,000 years ago, well before farming; see [[culture/jomon-culture|Jomon Culture]].
- Farming and herding arose independently in several regions and brought new techniques: see [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]], [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]], [[event/domestication-of-rice|Domestication of Rice]] in the Yangtze region, [[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]] in Mexico, and the drained wetland gardens of the [[place/kuk-early-agricultural-site|Kuk Early Agricultural Site]] in New Guinea.
- Wheeled vehicles appear around 3500 to 3000 BCE ([[technology/the-wheel|The Wheel]]). Bronze, an alloy of copper and tin, was first widely produced in the Near East around 3300 BCE, and tin had to be traded over long distances ([[technology/bronze-metallurgy|Bronze Metallurgy]]). On the steppe, the [[culture/botai-culture|Botai Culture]] managed horses and the [[culture/sintashta-culture|Sintashta Culture]] built the earliest known spoke-wheeled chariots.
- Recording systems arose in several places. [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]] happened independently at least three or four times, first in Mesopotamia about 3400 to 3200 BCE; the [[technology/alphabet|Alphabet]] (Proto-Sinaitic, about 1800 BCE) gave rise to most alphabets in use today; and in the Andes knotted cords served as records ([[technology/quipu|Quipu]], with possible early relatives at [[place/caral|Caral]]).
- Iron smelting began on a limited scale in Anatolia in the early 2nd millennium BCE and spread widely from about 1200 BCE ([[technology/iron-smelting|Iron Smelting]]). Ironworking in Africa, including at Meroe in the [[culture/kingdom-of-kush|Kingdom of Kush]] and among Bantu-speaking peoples ([[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]]), is a case where independent invention is debated.
- Paper was developed in China between about the 2nd century BCE and the 2nd century CE ([[technology/paper|Paper]], [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]]). [[technology/woodblock-printing|Woodblock Printing]] produced the Diamond Sutra of 868, the oldest dated printed book, and movable type appeared in China about 1040 and in Korea (metal type) in 1377 before [[person/johannes-gutenberg|Johannes Gutenberg]]'s [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]] in Mainz about 1440.
- Under the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]] gunpowder became a military technology and the compass was used for navigation at sea; see [[technology/gunpowder|Gunpowder]] and [[technology/magnetic-compass|Magnetic Compass]]. Su Song built an astronomical clock tower in 1088, and weight-driven mechanical clocks with escapements appeared in European towns about 1300 ([[technology/mechanical-clock|Mechanical Clock]]).
- Ocean and polar travel: [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]] settled the remote Pacific in double-hulled canoes using non-instrument navigation ([[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]]); the Thule ancestors of the Inuit used dog sleds, skin boats, and toggling harpoons ([[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]]); and the [[culture/norse|Norse]] crossed the North Atlantic, reaching L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland by 1021.
- Large-scale landscape engineering appears in many regions: the reservoirs and canals of [[place/angkor|Angkor]] and the [[culture/khmer-empire|Khmer Empire]], the earthworks and enriched soils of [[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]] (see [[place/upano-valley-settlements|Upano Valley Settlements]] and [[place/xingu-garden-cities|Xingu Garden Cities]]), the road system of the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]], roughly 30,000 to 40,000 kilometers long, and the dry-stone walls of [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]].
- Steam power ([[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]]: Newcomen 1712, Watt's separate condenser patented 1769) drove the [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]] and made [[technology/railways|Railways]] possible; the [[technology/telegraph-and-submarine-cables|Telegraph and Submarine Cables]] then carried messages across oceans, and works such as the [[event/suez-canal|Suez Canal]] (1869) and the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] (1891 to 1916) followed.
- The [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]] began with the first commercial power station in 1882. [[technology/nuclear-fission|Nuclear Fission]], discovered in 1938, led to reactors and weapons ([[event/nuclear-age|Nuclear Age]]). [[technology/vaccination|Vaccination]] (1796), [[technology/antibiotics|Antibiotics]] (1928), and the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]] changed health and food supply.
- The [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]] (ENIAC, 1945) shrank with the transistor (1947) and the integrated circuit (about 1958). The [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]] brought the internet (1969), the World Wide Web (1991), and smartphones (about 2007), and the [[event/space-age|Space Age]] began with Sputnik in 1957; see [[person/alan-turing|Alan Turing]].
--- extra 2: Through the Eras
- **Paleolithic:** [[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]], [[event/control-of-fire|Control of Fire]], the handaxes of [[species/homo-erectus|Homo erectus]], [[technology/pottery|Pottery]] about 20,000 years ago, open-sea crossings to Sahul at least 50,000 years ago ([[event/peopling-of-sahul|Peopling of Sahul]]), and the wooden hut foundations, leather, and rope preserved at [[place/monte-verde|Monte Verde]].
- **Neolithic:** Farming and herding ([[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]], [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]], [[event/domestication-of-rice|Domestication of Rice]], [[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]]), stone building at [[place/jericho|Jericho]], where a wall and tower date to roughly 8000 BCE, pottery-making hunter-gatherers ([[culture/jomon-culture|Jomon Culture]]), and artificial mummification from about 5050 BCE ([[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]]).
- **Bronze Age:** [[technology/the-wheel|The Wheel]], [[technology/bronze-metallurgy|Bronze Metallurgy]], [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]], the [[technology/alphabet|Alphabet]], and cities ([[event/the-urban-revolution|The Urban Revolution]]); planned streets, drainage, and standard weights in the [[culture/indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley Civilization]]; spoke-wheeled chariots ([[culture/sintashta-culture|Sintashta Culture]]); large stone construction at [[place/giza-necropolis|Giza Necropolis]] and platform mounds at [[place/caral|Caral]].
- **Iron Age:** [[technology/iron-smelting|Iron Smelting]], ironworking at Meroe in the [[culture/kingdom-of-kush|Kingdom of Kush]], farming and ironworking carried by the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]], and Pacific voyaging by the [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]].
- **Classical Antiquity:** [[technology/paper|Paper]] under the [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]]; the standardization of writing, weights, measures, and currency in the [[event/unification-of-china-under-qin|Unification of China under Qin]]; roads, aqueducts, and concrete in the [[culture/romans|Romans]]' empire; [[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]] in India; and, from about 500 BCE, the settlement networks of [[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]] and the [[place/upano-valley-settlements|Upano Valley Settlements]].
- **Medieval Period:** [[technology/woodblock-printing|Woodblock Printing]], [[technology/gunpowder|Gunpowder]], the [[technology/magnetic-compass|Magnetic Compass]], the [[technology/mechanical-clock|Mechanical Clock]], the [[technology/quipu|Quipu]], and the [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]]; water works at [[place/angkor|Angkor]]; stone building at [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]]; Norse and Thule seafaring ([[culture/norse|Norse]], [[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]]); and the Inca royal estate at [[place/machu-picchu|Machu Picchu]].
- **Early Modern Period:** The first commercially successful [[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]] (1712); ocean routes opened by ships armed with cannon ([[event/portuguese-sea-route-to-india|Portuguese Sea Route to India]]); crops and animals crossing the Atlantic ([[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]]); instruments and print in the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]]; and [[technology/vaccination|Vaccination]] in 1796.
- **Industrial Age:** The [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]], [[technology/railways|Railways]] (1825), the [[technology/telegraph-and-submarine-cables|Telegraph and Submarine Cables]] (1844), the [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]] (1882), the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]], the [[event/suez-canal|Suez Canal]], [[technology/antibiotics|Antibiotics]] (1928), and [[technology/nuclear-fission|Nuclear Fission]] (1938). States such as Egypt under [[person/muhammad-ali-of-egypt|Muhammad Ali of Egypt]], Japan in the [[event/meiji-restoration|Meiji Restoration]], and Ethiopia under [[person/menelik-ii|Menelik II]] sought to acquire industrial and military technology.
- **Information Age:** The [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]], the [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]], the [[event/nuclear-age|Nuclear Age]], the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]], the [[event/space-age|Space Age]] and [[event/moon-landing|Moon Landing]], the [[event/genomic-revolution|Genomic Revolution]] (CRISPR gene editing, 2012), and the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]], in which energy and fertilizer use have risen steeply since about 1950.
--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** Pottery, paper, printing, gunpowder, and the compass were documented first here. Several reached the west along the Silk Roads and through the [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]]; see [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]].
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** The earliest known stone tools come from here ([[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]]), as does much of the debate over African ironworking; the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] carried farming and iron across the continent, and [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]] shows dry-stone building at scale.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]]:** The [[technology/quipu|Quipu]] recorded numbers on knotted cords, the [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] practiced the earliest known deliberate artificial mummification, and the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] built a road system of tens of thousands of kilometers. The wheel was not used for transport in the Americas before contact ([[technology/the-wheel|The Wheel]]).
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]]:** Maize was domesticated here, and cooking it with lime (nixtamalization) makes its nutrients available ([[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]]). [[place/tenochtitlan|Tenochtitlan]] was fed in part by chinampas, and the [[culture/maya|Maya]] developed a full writing system.
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** People crossed open sea to Sahul at least 50,000 years ago ([[event/peopling-of-sahul|Peopling of Sahul]]), cultivated drained wetlands in New Guinea ([[place/kuk-early-agricultural-site|Kuk Early Agricultural Site]]), and later settled the far Pacific ([[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]]).
- **[[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]]:** Horse management at [[culture/botai-culture|Botai Culture]] sites and spoke-wheeled chariots at [[culture/sintashta-culture|Sintashta Culture]] settlements come from the steppe.
- **[[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]]:** [[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]] shows cold-climate technology (dog sleds, skin boats, toggling harpoons), [[culture/yakut-sakha|Yakut (Sakha)]] herders adapted horses and cattle to one of the coldest inhabited regions, and the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] later crossed the region.
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]] and the [[region/amazon-basin-and-south-american-lowlands|Amazon Basin and South American Lowlands]]:** Both regions show large-scale water and soil engineering, from the reservoirs of [[place/angkor|Angkor]] to the dark earths and earthworks of [[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]].
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- Independent invention versus diffusion is disputed in many cases. Writing arose independently at least three or four times, but scholars are divided on whether African ironworking was independent of the Near East, and the spread of papermaking and printing across Eurasia is traced only in part.
- Single-inventor stories usually compress long processes. Movable type was used in China and Korea before Gutenberg, paper fragments predate the traditional date credited to Cai Lun, and the [[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]] of Newcomen preceded Watt's improvements.
- The record is biased toward durable materials. Wood, cord, cloth, and basketry rarely survive, Spanish colonial authorities banned or destroyed many quipus ([[technology/quipu|Quipu]]), and earlier stone tools than the Oldowan, such as reports from Lomekwi in Kenya of about 3.3 million years ago, remain under study.
- Whether technologies drive social change or are shaped by it is contested, and this hub does not settle it.
- The vault's coverage is uneven. It has no notes yet on optics, sanitation, aviation, semiconductors, or GPS as technologies, and few on non-Western techniques such as crucible steel, porcelain, qanats, chinampas, or outrigger canoes.

=== theme/trade-and-economy
title: Trade and Economy
type: theme

themes: trade
src: 75aaf51c960e

observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Trade and economy cover the production, exchange, and taxation of goods, labor, and money, and the institutions that organize them: markets, credit, coinage, companies, tribute, and forced labor. The vault traces exchange from the long-distance movement of stone and shells in prehistory, through Bronze Age metal trade, the Silk Road, Indian Ocean, and trans-Saharan networks, and the global commerce created after 1492, to the industrial production and digital economy of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. Trade moved ideas, religions, crops, and diseases along with goods, and it also included the coerced trade in people. This hub gathers the vault's notes on routes, trading states, commodities, and economic systems.
--- observer
The Observer calls these exchange-webs and notes that they have no owner or center. Trade repeatedly makes strangers into partners, and transmits pathogens as readily as products. The record suggests that exchange-webs rest on trust that has to be built through shared accounts, coinage, law, and other story-glue, and that they sit beside coercive extraction, such as tribute, forced labor, and the trade in people, which the Observer records in the same ledger rather than in a separate one (see [[observer-note/frontiers-of-extraction|Frontiers of Extraction]]). Networks that carried goods also carried ideas, religions, crops, and diseases, so the Observer does not treat them as merely economic. This reading would weaken if long-distance trade proves to have been driven mainly by states and elites, with little of the decentralized quality the term implies.
--- question 1
How did early markets and money emerge?
--- question 2
How much long-distance exchange before writing can be reconstructed from materials alone, and how reliable are the inferences?
--- question 3
Why did some exchange routes last for centuries, such as the Silk Roads, the Indian Ocean, and the trans-Saharan routes, while others collapsed, as in the [[event/bronze-age-collapse|Bronze Age Collapse]]?
--- question 4
How did trade, tribute, and coercion interact in empires such as the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]], the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]], the [[culture/romans|Romans]], and the [[culture/qing-dynasty|Qing Dynasty]]?
--- question 5
Why did sustained growth in output per person begin when and where it did?
--- question 6
How far did trade spread disease, from the [[event/black-death|Black Death]] to the [[event/1918-influenza-pandemic|1918 Influenza Pandemic]] and the [[event/covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 Pandemic]], and how did societies respond?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Long-distance exchange of stone and shells goes back tens of thousands of years. The [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] moved obsidian and other goods over long distances across the western Pacific, and the hunter-gatherer-fishers of [[place/poverty-point|Poverty Point]] in Louisiana brought in stone and other materials from hundreds of kilometers away between about 1700 and 1100 BCE.
- Farming supported denser populations and permanent settlement ([[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]]), and the first cities followed ([[event/the-urban-revolution|The Urban Revolution]], [[place/uruk|Uruk]], [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]]). [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]] began as a way to record goods and obligations, and the earliest tablets from Uruk are accounting records. [[technology/bronze-metallurgy|Bronze Metallurgy]] made trade essential because tin was scarce and had to be carried over long distances from places such as Afghanistan and later Cornwall.
- Bronze Age networks tied the eastern Mediterranean, Anatolia, Egypt, and the Indus. The [[culture/indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley Civilization]] traded with Mesopotamia and made goods to standard weights, the [[culture/minoans|Minoans]] traded across the eastern Mediterranean, and [[person/hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]] sponsored a trading expedition to Punt. The [[event/bronze-age-collapse|Bronze Age Collapse]] around 1200 BCE ended the palace economies, and the breakdown of trade networks is among the proposed causes.
- Caravan and sea traders grew rich as middlemen. The [[event/incense-trade|Incense Trade]] carried frankincense and myrrh from southern Arabia and the Horn of Africa by camel caravan; the [[culture/kingdom-of-saba|Kingdom of Saba]] and the [[culture/nabataeans|Nabataeans]] profited from taxing and guiding the caravans; and the [[culture/phoenicians|Phoenicians]] of the Levant coast founded colonies, including [[place/carthage|Carthage]], and spread a simple alphabet.
- The [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]] from the second century BCE linked East Asia, South Asia, Central Asia, and the Mediterranean. Goods included silk, horses, glass, spices, and paper, and disease also traveled the routes ([[event/black-death|Black Death]]). The [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] paid the [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]] annual gifts, the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]] connected Rome, Parthia, India, and Han China commercially, and the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] became the leading Silk Road merchants, acting as caravan leaders, interpreters, and bankers.
- Large states built economies around roads, taxation, and records. The [[culture/romans|Romans]] were tied together by Latin, Greek, and trade, [[person/augustus|Augustus]] reorganized the army, provinces, and taxation, the [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]] controlled Red Sea trade between Rome, Arabia, and India and minted its own coins, and the Persepolis tablets of the [[culture/achaemenid-persians|Achaemenid Persians]] record rations and administration ([[place/persepolis|Persepolis]]).
- The [[event/indian-ocean-trade-network|Indian Ocean Trade Network]] used the monsoon winds and was busiest from about the 8th to the 15th century, carrying spices, cotton textiles, ivory, gold, and Chinese porcelain. The [[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]], such as Kilwa, which minted its own coins, grew rich from it; [[culture/srivijaya|Srivijaya]] controlled shipping through the Strait of Malacca; the [[culture/chola-dynasty|Chola Dynasty]] traded with China and Southeast Asia; and the [[culture/malacca-sultanate|Malacca Sultanate]] became the leading port of Southeast Asia in the 15th century before the Portuguese took it in 1511 ([[event/portuguese-sea-route-to-india|Portuguese Sea Route to India]]).
- Trans-Saharan trade in gold, salt, and ivory sustained the [[culture/ghana-empire|Ghana Empire]], the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], and the [[culture/songhai-empire|Songhai Empire]]. [[person/mansa-musa|Mansa Musa]]'s pilgrimage in 1324 reportedly disrupted the gold market in Cairo, and [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]] became a hub of trade and scholarship. Farther south, [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]] traded gold and ivory with the Swahili coast, and the [[culture/kingdom-of-mapungubwe|Kingdom of Mapungubwe]] received glass beads and Chinese celadon through East African harbors.
- East Asia was a commercial center. The [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]]'s capital Chang'an held about a million people; the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]] issued some of the world's first government paper money; American silver, arriving through the Manila trade, became a major part of the [[culture/ming-dynasty|Ming Dynasty]] economy; and the [[culture/tokugawa-shogunate|Tokugawa Shogunate]] limited foreign trade to Dutch and Chinese merchants at Nagasaki.
- Exchange and tribute in the pre-Columbian Americas took forms unlike those of Eurasia. [[place/caral|Caral]] combined coastal fishing with inland farming, [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]] had wide trade and political links, [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] was a ceremonial and trade center with roads radiating from it, the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]] collected tribute from conquered city-states, and the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] exacted labor service (mit'a) rather than money taxes and kept records on [[technology/quipu|Quipu]].
- After 1492 trade became global. The [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]] moved plants, animals, people, and diseases between the hemispheres; the [[event/manila-galleon-trade|Manila Galleon Trade]] (1565 to 1815) carried Chinese goods to the Americas and American silver to Asia, much of it from [[place/potosi|Potosi]], where the Spanish organized labor through the mita; and the [[event/dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]], chartered in 1602, was among the first companies to issue tradable shares and had powers to wage war and build forts.
- Coerced labor underpinned much of the Atlantic economy. The [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]] forcibly transported about 12.5 million Africans to the Americas, the [[event/sugar-plantation-complex|Sugar Plantation Complex]] drove much of the demand, the [[event/haitian-revolution|Haitian Revolution]] arose in Saint-Domingue, the largest sugar producer in the world in the late 18th century, and the [[event/abolition-of-slavery|Abolition of Slavery]] followed between 1807 and 1888.
- The [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]] began in Britain about 1760 with the shift to machine production driven by coal and steam, and economic output per person began to rise steadily for the first time in history. The [[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]], [[technology/railways|Railways]], the [[technology/telegraph-and-submarine-cables|Telegraph and Submarine Cables]], the [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]], and the [[event/suez-canal|Suez Canal]], which cut the voyage between Europe and India by thousands of kilometers, reshaped trade in the 19th century.
- Modern economies were shaped by empire and by state policy. The [[event/opium-wars|Opium Wars]] opened China to Western commerce on unequal terms, the [[event/scramble-for-africa|Scramble for Africa]] and the [[event/congo-free-state|Congo Free State]] built forced-labor extraction, and after 1950 the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]] raised output and energy use steeply. [[event/chinese-reform-and-opening|Chinese Reform and Opening]] (from December 1978) brought market reforms and decades of rapid growth, the [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]] spread computing and networks, and the [[event/covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 Pandemic]] disrupted supply chains worldwide.
--- extra 2: Through the Eras
- **Paleolithic:** Stone, shell, and pigment moved between hunter-gatherer groups over long distances, but the evidence is fragmentary and the vault has no dedicated note on Paleolithic exchange; see [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]].
- **Neolithic:** Farming and herding ([[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]], [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]]) created storable surpluses and traction animals, and [[technology/pottery|Pottery]] allowed food and liquids to be stored and transported.
- **Bronze Age:** [[event/the-urban-revolution|The Urban Revolution]], [[place/uruk|Uruk]], [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]], [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]], [[technology/bronze-metallurgy|Bronze Metallurgy]], [[technology/the-wheel|The Wheel]], the [[culture/indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley Civilization]], the [[culture/minoans|Minoans]], the [[culture/hittites|Hittites]], [[person/hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]]'s expedition to Punt, [[place/caral|Caral]], [[place/poverty-point|Poverty Point]], the [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]], and the [[event/bronze-age-collapse|Bronze Age Collapse]].
- **Iron Age:** The [[event/incense-trade|Incense Trade]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-saba|Kingdom of Saba]], the [[culture/phoenicians|Phoenicians]], [[place/carthage|Carthage]], the [[culture/scythians|Scythians]], [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]], the [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]], the [[culture/achaemenid-persians|Achaemenid Persians]], and [[technology/iron-smelting|Iron Smelting]].
- **Classical Antiquity:** The [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]], the [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] and [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]], the [[culture/romans|Romans]] and [[person/augustus|Augustus]], the [[culture/nabataeans|Nabataeans]], the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]], the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]], [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]], the [[culture/maurya-empire|Maurya Empire]], and [[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]], which made calculation easier.
- **Medieval Period:** The [[event/indian-ocean-trade-network|Indian Ocean Trade Network]], the [[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]], [[culture/srivijaya|Srivijaya]], the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]], the [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]] and [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]], the [[culture/ghana-empire|Ghana Empire]], [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], and [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]], [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-mapungubwe|Kingdom of Mapungubwe]], [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]], [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]], the [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]], whose relay postal system sped communication across Eurasia, the [[culture/norse|Norse]], who traded in eastern Europe for silver from the Islamic world, the [[culture/malacca-sultanate|Malacca Sultanate]], [[person/zheng-he|Zheng He]], [[person/ibn-battuta|Ibn Battuta]], and the [[event/black-death|Black Death]].
- **Early Modern Period:** The [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]], the [[event/portuguese-sea-route-to-india|Portuguese Sea Route to India]], the [[event/manila-galleon-trade|Manila Galleon Trade]], [[place/potosi|Potosi]], the [[event/dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]], the [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]], the [[event/sugar-plantation-complex|Sugar Plantation Complex]], the [[culture/ming-dynasty|Ming Dynasty]], the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]], which by 1700 ruled about 100 million people and made India one of the world's largest economies, the [[culture/tokugawa-shogunate|Tokugawa Shogunate]], and the [[event/russian-expansion-into-siberia|Russian Expansion into Siberia]], driven by the fur trade.
- **Industrial Age:** The [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]], [[technology/railways|Railways]], the [[technology/telegraph-and-submarine-cables|Telegraph and Submarine Cables]], the [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]], the [[event/suez-canal|Suez Canal]], the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]], the [[event/opium-wars|Opium Wars]], the [[event/british-raj|British Raj]], the [[event/scramble-for-africa|Scramble for Africa]], the [[event/congo-free-state|Congo Free State]], the [[event/abolition-of-slavery|Abolition of Slavery]], and the [[event/bengal-famine-of-1943|Bengal Famine of 1943]], which Amartya Sen analyzed as an entitlement failure: food supply did not fall sharply, but economic pressures kept some groups from buying it.
- **Information Age:** [[event/decolonization|Decolonization]], the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]], the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]], the [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]], [[event/chinese-reform-and-opening|Chinese Reform and Opening]], and the [[event/covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 Pandemic]].
--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** Gold, salt, and ivory sustained the Sahelian empires and Swahili city-states, and the interior states of Mapungubwe and Great Zimbabwe reached the Indian Ocean; from the 16th century the [[culture/kingdom-of-kongo|Kingdom of Kongo]] was drawn into the Atlantic slave trade ([[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]]), and between 1885 and 1908 the [[event/congo-free-state|Congo Free State]] extracted rubber and ivory by forced labor.
- **[[region/central-asian-oases|Central Asian Oases]] and the [[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]]:** The [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] of the oases were the leading Silk Road merchants, and steppe powers such as the [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]] and the [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]] taxed, protected, and raided the routes; see [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]].
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]:** Control of the straits made [[culture/srivijaya|Srivijaya]] and the [[culture/malacca-sultanate|Malacca Sultanate]] rich, [[culture/champa|Champa]] traded across the South China Sea, and later the [[event/manila-galleon-trade|Manila Galleon Trade]] and the [[event/dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]] (which founded Batavia in 1619) tied the region to European and American markets.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]]:** [[place/caral|Caral]] linked coast and highland, the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] ran a state economy on labor service and recorded goods on [[technology/quipu|Quipu]], and after the Spanish conquest the silver of [[place/potosi|Potosi]], worked under a forced-labor draft adapted from an Inca system, supplied world trade.
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]]:** [[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]] made a staple crop for the Americas, [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]] had wide trade links, and the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]] collected tribute from conquered city-states for [[place/tenochtitlan|Tenochtitlan]].
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** The [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] and later the [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]] moved goods and people across thousands of kilometers of sea, and the [[event/treaty-of-waitangi|Treaty of Waitangi]] of 1840 was followed by conflict over land and authority.
- **[[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]]:** European demand for furs drove the [[event/russian-expansion-into-siberia|Russian Expansion into Siberia]] between 1582 and 1639, and the [[culture/yakut-sakha|Yakut (Sakha)]] paid yasak, a fur tribute, until 1917; the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] later linked the region to markets. See [[observer-note/frontiers-of-extraction|Frontiers of Extraction]].
- **[[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** The Tang and Song economies, the Ming reliance on American silver, Tokugawa restriction of foreign trade, the Opium Wars, and [[event/chinese-reform-and-opening|Chinese Reform and Opening]] mark shifts between openness and control.
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- The origins of markets and money are disputed. Whether exchange began as gift-giving, barter, credit, or tribute is argued, and the earliest written records, such as the tablets of Uruk, show accounting for obligations rather than simple barter.
- The size and reach of early exchange are inferred from scattered goods, and estimates change. The extent and end of the [[culture/ghana-empire|Ghana Empire]] are debated, and older population estimates for [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]] of 10,000 to 20,000 have been revised down to no more than about 10,000.
- What counts as trade is contested, because exchange, tribute, and coercion overlap. The mita at [[place/potosi|Potosi]], yasak in Siberia, forced rubber collection in the [[event/congo-free-state|Congo Free State]], and the [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]] are extraction, and scholars disagree about how much slave-based commerce contributed to industrialization.
- Why Britain and Europe industrialized first, and why sustained growth began when it did, is debated, and the [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]] note does not settle the causes.
- The vault's coverage is uneven. It has one note on a company ([[event/dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]]) and none yet on coinage, paper money, credit, or double-entry bookkeeping as institutions, and it has few notes on the internal economies of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas.
