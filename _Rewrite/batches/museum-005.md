=== zodiac
title: Zodiac
artist: Alphonse Mucha (1860–1939)
year: 1896
medium: Colour lithograph
location: Paris, France
src: 6544c0e018fe
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Originally a calendar design for a printing house, later reissued on its own as a decorative panel, showing a woman in profile crowned by a jewelled halo bearing the twelve zodiac signs. Its ornamental frame, rather than the figure itself, became the design's most imitated element, reproduced on countless later calendars, biscuit tins and packaging.

=== the-seasons

year: 1896
medium: Colour lithograph, four-panel decorative series
location: Paris, France
src: 29a0aeef206b

A set of four decorative panels personifying Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter as women set against stylised landscapes, sold as affordable prints for middle-class homes rather than as advertising. Series like this made Mucha's decorative style available well beyond the theatre-poster market that had launched him.

=== vanishing-lady
title: The Vanishing Lady
artist: Georges Méliès (1861–1938)
year: 1896
medium: Silent film, black-and-white, 35mm, about 1 minute
location: Star Film Company, Montreuil, France
src: a2ae345ad739
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
One of Méliès's earliest trick films, made within months of discovering, by accident, that stopping and restarting his camera mid-shot could make an object or person vanish and reappear. Here a woman disappears from a chair, is replaced by a skeleton, and reappears, the basic "stop trick" that launched his career as cinema's first great illusionist.

=== a-trip-to-the-moon
title: A Trip to the Moon
artist: Georges Méliès (1861–1938)
year: 1902
medium: Silent film, black-and-white and hand-tinted colour, 35mm, about 13 minutes
location: Star Film Company, Montreuil, France
src: 5779d62793ec
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Cinema's first true science-fiction film, loosely adapted from Jules Verne and H. G. Wells, following astronomers who fire themselves to the Moon by cannon and battle its insect-like inhabitants, the Selenites. Its image of the capsule lodged in the Man in the Moon's eye is among the most reproduced in film history. Méliès hand-painted colour prints frame by frame in his own studio.

=== kingdom-of-the-fairies
title: The Kingdom of the Fairies
artist: Georges Méliès (1861–1938)
year: 1903
medium: Silent film, black-and-white, 35mm, about 17 minutes
location: Star Film Company, Montreuil, France
src: d46f5caac8b8
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
An elaborate fairy-tale spectacle, one of the longest and most expensively staged films of its day, moving a kidnapped princess through underwater grottoes, an ice palace and a wedding procession across more than twenty painted sets. It shows Méliès at the height of his output as, in effect, the theatre magician he had been before cinema, now with a camera as his stage.

=== impossible-voyage
title: The Impossible Voyage

year: 1904
medium: Silent film, black-and-white and hand-tinted colour, 35mm, about 24 minutes
location: Star Film Company, Montreuil, France
src: 33f1e94413c2

A follow-up to *A Trip to the Moon*, sending a party of scientists by train, car, submarine and balloon across the globe and into the sun itself, satirising Jules Verne's adventure novels and the era's faith in engineering. A single, unedited shot of employees streaming out of the Lumière family's own photographic plate factory at the end of the working day. === demolition-of-a-wall

artist: Auguste and Louis Lumière (1862–1954)
year: 1896
medium: Silent film, black-and-white, 35mm, under 1 minute
location: Lyon, France
src: 0235e775ec19
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Workers topple a brick wall with picks and a rope, a mundane demonstration of the new camera's ability to record real motion. The Lumières discovered that running the film backwards made the wall reassemble itself, one of the medium's first accidental special effects and an early hint of cinema's power to reverse and manipulate time.

=== beethoven-frieze
title: Beethoven Frieze (installation view)
artist: Gustav Klimt (1862–1918)
year: 1902
medium: Casein paint, gold, and semi-precious stones on plaster, 2.15 × 34.14 m (whole frieze)
location: Secession Building, Vienna
src: af0bfcb4e4fc
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Made for the 1902 Secession exhibition in honour of Beethoven, this frieze runs around three walls of a room high above the viewer. It interprets the Ninth Symphony as humanity's search for happiness, beginning with floating figures and a knight in golden armour, passing the "Hostile Forces" and ending in the "Kiss to the whole world" from Schiller's *Ode to Joy*. The end wall, seen here, shows the Hostile Forces: the ape-like giant Typhoeus surrounded by his daughters, the Gorgons, and the figures of Sickness, Madness and Death. Meant to be temporary, the frieze was saved and is now on permanent display.

One of ten huge paintings describing the stages of human life, from childhood to old age. Af Klint painted them in a few months, on paper laid on the floor. A great circle, ringed by a serpent, holds a pink, flower-like form divided into quarters and scattered with geometric signs: triangles, a crescent, black bars and coloured ovals. One of eight delicate works on paper showing the biblical tree of knowledge as a symmetrical, diagram-like form of branches, birds, circles and hearts.
