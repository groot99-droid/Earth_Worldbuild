=== species/australopithecus-afarensis
title: Australopithecus afarensis
type: species
date: c. 3.85 – 2.95 Ma
themes: earth-systems, science
src: b4972c28249c
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Australopithecus afarensis was an early hominin that lived in East Africa about 3.9 to 2.9 million years ago and walked upright, and is best known from the fossil 'Lucy'.
--- fact 1
'Lucy' was found in Hadar, Ethiopia, in 1974 and is about 3.2 million years old.

Footprints at Laetoli, Tanzania (about 3.66 million years old), record upright walking.
--- fact 3
It had a small brain (estimates range from about 380 to 550 cubic centimeters) and long arms adapted to climbing.
--- fact 4
It is often considered close to the ancestry of the genus Homo.
--- context
The species follows the [[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]] and precedes [[species/homo-habilis|Homo habilis]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that upright walking came long before big brains. The species' most distinctive trait, a large brain, was a late addition to a lineage that had been walking for millions of years.
--- question 1
Was A. afarensis a direct ancestor of Homo?

=== species/cyanobacteria
title: Cyanobacteria
type: species
date: 2.7 Ga
themes: earth-systems
src: 3a65180a9260
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Cyanobacteria are bacteria that perform oxygen-producing photosynthesis. Their activity led to the oxygenation of Earth's atmosphere.
--- fact 1
The earliest unambiguous cyanobacterial fossils are about 2.15 billion years old, and direct fossil evidence of photosynthetic membranes is about 1.75 billion years old. Biomarker evidence once cited for 2.7 billion years was later shown to be younger contamination, so the 2.7-billion-year start date used for this note reflects an older, disputed estimate and is approximate.
--- fact 2
Chloroplasts in plants and algae descend from cyanobacteria through endosymbiosis.
--- fact 3
They still live in oceans, lakes, and soils around the world.
--- fact 4
The [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]] followed their spread.
--- context
Cyanobacteria are the biological agents of the [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]], which reshaped the planet's chemistry in the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]].
--- observer
The Observer regards them as the earlier example of a species that changed the atmosphere. Their oxygen was a byproduct, not a goal.
--- question 1
When did oxygen-producing photosynthesis first evolve?

=== species/denisovans
title: Denisovans
type: species
date: c. 400 – 30 ka
themes: kinship, earth-systems
src: 85f884ca3c47
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Denisovans are an extinct human group known first from DNA in a finger bone found in Denisova Cave in Siberia in 2010.
--- fact 1
Very few fossils are known, including a jaw from Xiahe in Tibet.
--- fact 2
Some living populations, especially in Oceania and East Asia, carry Denisovan DNA.
--- fact 3
A gene variant helping Tibetans adapt to high altitude came from Denisovans.
--- fact 4
The Harbin cranium (at least 146,000 years old, once nicknamed 'Dragon Man' and named Homo longi) was linked to Denisovans in 2025, using mitochondrial DNA recovered from dental calculus and ancient protein evidence, in studies published in Cell and Science. It is the first Denisovan skull identified.
--- context
Denisovans are close relatives of [[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]] and interbred with [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]] during the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]].
--- observer
The Observer notes a species identified mostly from its DNA, before its appearance was known. The molecular record now reveals populations that fossils alone did not.
--- question 1
What did Denisovans look like, and how widely did they live?

=== species/ediacaran-biota
title: Ediacaran Biota
type: species
date: 574 – 538.8 Ma
themes: earth-systems
src: 0db939dca7e3
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Ediacaran biota are the first large, complex, multicellular organisms in the fossil record, living in the sea in the last stretch of the Proterozoic, before the Cambrian.
--- fact 1
The Ediacaran Period spans about 635 to 538.8 million years ago.
--- fact 2
The biota are named after the Ediacara Hills of South Australia and are known also from Newfoundland, Namibia, and the White Sea coast of Russia.
--- fact 3
Many forms were soft-bodied and frond-, disc-, or quilt-shaped.
--- fact 4
The relationship of many Ediacaran forms to living animal groups is unclear.
--- fact 5
The classic assemblages largely disappear near the start of the Cambrian.
--- context
The Ediacaran biota follow the glaciations of [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]] and precede the [[event/cambrian-explosion|Cambrian Explosion]]. They belong to the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]]. Ediacaran Biota sits in the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]] and the region [[region/planet-wide|Planet-wide]], and belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that Earth ran a large-bodied experiment in multicellular life that left no obvious descendants. Continuity is not guaranteed: some lineages simply end. This reading would be undermined if most Ediacaran forms were shown to be ancestral to living animals.
--- question 1
Which Ediacaran forms, if any, were true animals?
--- question 2
What ended the assemblage: extinction, competition, or changing chemistry?

--- summary
Flowering plants (angiosperms) appear in the fossil record in the Early Cretaceous, about 130 million years ago, and now make up most land plant species.
--- fact 1
The oldest widely accepted angiosperm pollen dates to the Hauterivian and Barremian ages of the Early Cretaceous, about 133 to 125 million years ago.

Flowering plants radiated rapidly in the Cretaceous alongside pollinating insects.
--- fact 5
Cereals, legumes, fruits, and most other crops descend from this group.
--- context
Flowering plants diversified in the age of the [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]] and survived the [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]]. --- observer
The Observer notes that the species' whole agricultural existence rests on one plant lineage that evolved to attract animals into carrying its pollen and seeds. Farming can be read as the species joining a partnership that plants had run with insects for more than a hundred million years.

=== species/stromatolites
title: Stromatolites
type: species
date: 3.48 Ga
themes: earth-systems
src: 39d75d10baab
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Stromatolites are layered rock structures built by communities of microbes trapping sediment and precipitating minerals; the oldest widely cited examples are about 3.48 billion years old.
--- fact 1
Stromatolites form when microbial mats, often including cyanobacteria, bind sediment into thin repeating layers.
--- fact 2
The oldest widely cited examples are in the Dresser Formation of the Pilbara region of Western Australia, dated to about 3.48 billion years; some researchers dispute that they are biological.
--- fact 3
Living stromatolites grow today in a few places, including Shark Bay in Western Australia.
--- fact 4
Stromatolites were abundant in shallow seas for much of the Precambrian.
--- fact 5
They declined later, a decline often attributed to grazing animals, though other causes have been proposed.
--- context
Stromatolites are the main fossil record of early microbial life and connect the [[event/origin-of-life|Origin of Life]] to the oxygen-producing [[species/cyanobacteria|Cyanobacteria]] that drove the [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]]. Stromatolites sits in the [[era/archean-eon|Archean Eon]] and the region [[region/planet-wide|Planet-wide]], and belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that for more than three billion years the only visible monuments on Earth were built by organisms without brains. Later monument-builders, the species itself among them, are a very recent development in that longer record.
--- question 1
Are the oldest structures truly biological?
--- question 2
When did stromatolite-building microbes first make oxygen?

=== place/aksum
title: Aksum
type: place
date: c. 50 CE
themes: trade, religion, art
src: ea8c5064f198
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Aksum is a town in the Tigray region of northern Ethiopia that was the capital of the Kingdom of Aksum, a trading state active from about the 1st century CE to the 7th century and later, known for its stone stelae, coinage and early adoption of Christianity.
--- fact 1
Aksum lies in the highlands of northern Ethiopia, and the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea, a Greek trade guide of about the 1st century CE, mentions Aksum and its port Adulis on the Red Sea.
--- fact 2
Aksumite rulers minted gold, silver and bronze coins from about the late 3rd century CE, and King Ezana adopted Christianity around 330 CE; coins struck afterward bear the cross.
--- fact 3
Ezana's inscriptions were written in Ge'ez, Sabaean and Greek.
--- fact 4
Aksum's monolithic stelae mark burial sites; the largest, about 33 meters high, fell and broke, and a 24-meter stele taken to Rome by Italian forces in 1937 was returned in 2005 and re-erected in 2008.
--- fact 5
Ethiopian Christian tradition holds that the Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion at Aksum houses the Ark of the Covenant; the claim cannot be examined.
--- fact 6
Aksum declined from the 7th century, and proposed causes include changes in Red Sea trade after the rise of Islam, environmental stress and internal conflict; the causes are debated.
--- fact 7
UNESCO inscribed Aksum in 1980.
--- context
Aksum was the capital of the [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]], which had contacts with [[culture/kingdom-of-saba|Kingdom of Saba]] across the Red Sea; its stele was taken during the [[event/second-italo-ethiopian-war|Second Italo-Ethiopian War]]'s Italian occupation. Later Ethiopian rulers built the [[place/rock-hewn-churches-of-lalibela|Rock-Hewn Churches of Lalibela]]. Aksum sits in [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]] and the region [[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]], and belongs to the theme threads of [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]], [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that Aksum shows a state announcing its meaning-engine through stone and metal: giant stelae over graves, and coins that carried a cross after Ezana's conversion, so that a portable object spread the ruler's story-glue across the trading network. --- summary
