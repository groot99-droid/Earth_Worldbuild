--- summary
Ba Trieu (Trieu Thi Trinh; birth year unknown, died 248 CE) led an uprising against the Chinese Eastern Wu administration of northern Vietnam in 248 CE, becoming a lasting symbol of Vietnamese resistance to foreign rule.
--- fact 1
She is said to have raised a rebel force in Cuu Chan, in present-day Thanh Hoa province, at about age twenty, alongside her brother Trieu Quoc Dat.
--- fact 2
Later chronicles describe her riding into battle on an elephant in gold-decorated armor, though these vivid details come from accounts written many centuries after her death rather than contemporary record.
--- fact 3
Eastern Wu sent the general Lu Yin to suppress the revolt; after several months of fighting, her forces were defeated later in 248.
--- fact 4
Vietnamese tradition holds that she fled to Bo Dien commune and died by suicide rather than be captured, with her burial place attributed to Mount Tung in Hau Loc District, while Chinese-source summaries have Lu Yin putting her to death (sources give no age at death); a temple to her still stands near the site.
--- context
Ba Trieu's revolt took place in [[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]] during [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]], against the Chinese administration that had controlled the region since the Han conquest. See [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that Ba Trieu is known largely through Vietnamese chronicles written centuries after her death, during periods when a symbol of resistance to Chinese rule served a clear political purpose. The core event, a suppressed revolt in 248, likely happened; the vivid personal details are harder to separate from later invention. The earliest and most detailed record, the Jiaozhou ji of the Western Jin period (265 to 318), dates only decades after 248, though Taylor held that Chinese records did not mention her, and the elephant and gilded-tunic details appear in later texts such as the Le-era Dai Viet su ky toan thu.
