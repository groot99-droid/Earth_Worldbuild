=== people--jane-austen--1
src: b2d6969e1e76
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I stand before Jane Austen (1775 to 1817), English novelist, whose six completed novels of courtship, inheritance and class anxiety among the gentry remain among the most widely read in the language. All were published anonymously in her lifetime.

I read that she was born in 1775 in Steventon, Hampshire, the seventh of eight children of a country clergyman, in a family that encouraged reading, amateur theatre and writing. I read that Sense and Sensibility appeared in 1811, credited only to "A Lady," followed by Pride and Prejudice (1813), Mansfield Park (1814), Emma (1815) and, after her death, Northanger Abbey and Persuasion (1817).

Only close family knew of her authorship. She fell ill, probably with Addison's disease, though the cause is debated, and died in 1817 at 41. Her authorship was publicly acknowledged in a biographical notice her brother attached to her last two novels.

She worked within a deliberately narrow social canvas, mainly the English gentry, courtship, income and inheritance, rather than the wider political events of her era. Her distinctive technique is free indirect discourse: narrating in the third person while letting the prose absorb a character's own irony or blind spots, so the reader senses a character's error before the character does.

Six novels, and no name on any of them while she lived. How much of my best work have I left unsigned? Being known is not the same as being read.

=== people--utagawa-hiroshige--1
src: 049c2b921eee
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Utagawa Hiroshige (1797 to 1858) was the last great master of the ukiyo-e woodblock print, a lyrical designer of rain, snow, moonlight and travellers on the road. His series on the Tōkaidō highway and on Edo were great commercial successes and later stirred European artists.

He was born in Edo in 1797 into the Andō samurai family, whose hereditary charge was fire warden at Edo Castle. Both his parents died when he was about 12, and the post passed to him.

About 1811 he became a pupil of the print designer Utagawa Toyohiro, who gave him the name Hiroshige. In 1823 he handed the fire warden post to his son and turned to landscape after Hokusai's Fuji series opened a new market.

According to tradition he travelled the Tōkaidō in 1832 with an official delegation, and the prints he published in 1833 to 1834 made him the most popular landscape artist in Japan.

In 1856 he became a Buddhist monk, yet kept working on One Hundred Famous Views of Edo. He died in Edo on 12 October 1858, probably in a cholera epidemic.

He designed the prints; carvers and printers made them. He used soft gradations of colour (bokashi) like dusk fading over water, and in the Edo series a vertical format with a large foreground object framing a distant view. "Hiroshige blue" was made with imported Prussian blue.
