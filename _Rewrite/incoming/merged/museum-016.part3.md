=== people--knud-rasmussen--1
src: fa4af7372add

--- description
Knud Rasmussen (1879 to 1933) was a Danish-Greenlandic explorer and ethnographer who gathered Inuit oral traditions and led the Fifth Thule Expedition (1921 to 1924), a dog-sled journey from Greenland across Arctic Canada to Alaska, over ice that runs to the edge of the world.

He was born on 7 June 1879 in Jakobshavn (Ilulissat), Greenland, to a Danish missionary father and a mother of part Greenlandic Inuit descent, and he grew up speaking Greenlandic.

In 1910 he and Peter Freuchen founded the Thule Trading Station at Cape York in northwest Greenland, and it helped pay for his expeditions.

On the Fifth Thule Expedition he travelled with Greenlandic Inuit companions across northern Canada to Alaska. The expedition's archaeologist, Therkel Mathiassen, dug sites that led to the identification of the Thule culture.

He gathered songs, stories and accounts of belief from the Iglulik and other Inuit groups, and wrote Across Arctic America (1927).

He fell ill on the Seventh Thule Expedition and died in Copenhagen on 21 December 1933.

John Curtin (1885 to 1945) was Prime Minister of Australia from 1941 to 1945, leading the country through the Pacific war against Japan during the Second World War.

A former journalist and trade unionist who had opposed conscription during the First World War, he became Labor Party leader in 1935 and prime minister in October 1941.

After Japan entered the war in December 1941 and bombed Darwin in February 1942, he redirected Australian forces from the European theatre and, over British objections, recalled Australian troops from the Middle East to defend against Japanese advance. He committed Australia more closely to the United States for wartime defense, stating in December 1941 that Australia looked to America "free of any pangs" about its traditional ties to Britain.
