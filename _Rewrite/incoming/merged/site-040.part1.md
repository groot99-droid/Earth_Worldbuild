=== event/austronesian-expansion
src: c5580a1eb58e
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Beginning roughly 5,000 to 5,500 years ago, speakers of Austronesian languages spread outward from Taiwan like ripples from a stone dropped in still water, until at last their words touched Madagascar in the west and Rapa Nui in the east.
--- fact 1
The model most widely accepted, known as Out of Taiwan, sets the wellspring of the whole language family on Taiwan.
--- fact 2
Like a tide finding its channels, the migration moved through the Philippines and Indonesia, then out into Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia.
--- fact 3
In the first millennium CE, the westward current carried it as far as Madagascar.
--- fact 4
The Austronesian language family is a vast canopy of tongues: well over a thousand languages shelter beneath it.
--- fact 5
The expansion leaned on two things: sailing canoes steadied by outriggers, and a crop package that included rice, pigs, dogs, and chickens.
--- context
Like a long wake behind a canoe, the expansion binds [[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]] to the far Pacific and to Africa. From it came the [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]], by way of the [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]], and later it flowed into the wider [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]]. In the chronicle's codex, the Austronesian Expansion stands within the [[era/bronze-age|Bronze Age]] and the region of [[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]], woven into the theme threads of [[theme/language-and-writing|Language and Writing]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I trace one language family across nearly half the circumference of the planet, and I find no armies on the way. I find boats. I find people in boats. I find a span that no sword carved.

What is the sea, then, if not a wall? The sea can be a road as easily as a wall.
--- question 1
How faithfully does the branching tree of languages follow the genetic and archaeological record?

=== event/axial-age
src: 5fb87d44962b
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The 'Axial Age' is the name given to the span of roughly 800 to 200 BCE, when major philosophical and religious traditions rose like separate springs across Eurasia, in China, India, Persia, Israel, and Greece.
--- fact 1
It was the philosopher Karl Jaspers who coined the term, in 1949.
--- fact 2
Among the figures often gathered under its banner are [[person/confucius|Confucius]], [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]], Zoroaster, the Hebrew prophets, and Greek philosophers such as the predecessors of [[person/aristotle|Aristotle]].
--- fact 3
Among the threads that recur through its weave are ethics, transcendence, and individual responsibility.
--- fact 4
Yet many historians fault the concept as too neat a pattern, one that underrates other periods and regions and leaves them in shadow.
--- context
The Axial Age stretches like a bridge across the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] and into early [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]]. Its threads run into [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and into the essay [[observer-note/story-glue-how-strangers-cooperate-at-scale|Story-Glue - How Strangers Cooperate at Scale]].
--- observer
I notice the timing. I notice the questions, all about how to live, arriving near-simultaneously. I notice the states behind them, grown large enough that the old tribal rules no longer sufficed.

Is it a puzzle, or a pattern? When the old rules no longer fit, we have to ask again how to live.
--- question 1
Is the Axial Age a true pattern woven into history, or a convenience of the scholars who named it?

=== event/banded-iron-formations
src: 34c9f7666890
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Banded iron formations are ancient sedimentary rocks striped like the pages of a closed book, iron-rich layers alternating with silica-rich ones, laid down mostly between about 3.8 and 1.8 billion years ago. They are a major source of the world's iron ore.
--- fact 1
Each band is usually millimetres to centimetres thick. The eldest, about 3.8 billion years old, lie in Greenland, and the laying-down reached its height about 2.7 to 2.4 billion years ago, with great deposits in Western Australia, South Africa and the Lake Superior region of North America.
--- fact 2
The leading explanation tells of oxygen-poor seawater holding iron dissolved within it, until oxygen or other oxidants appeared and the iron was oxidized and settled out, like rust sifting to the floor. Whether oxygen-producing bacteria or iron-using photosynthetic bacteria did most of that work is still debated.
--- fact 3
About 1.8 billion years ago the banding largely fell silent, probably as the deeper ocean lost its dissolved iron, though a few late formations surface again during the Cryogenian ice ages; see [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]].
--- fact 4
The ore drawn from these old stones is a major source of the iron that feeds steelmaking today.
--- context
Like a chronicle written in stone, banded iron formations record the slow oxidation of the oceans before and during the [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]]. They bind the [[event/origin-of-photosynthesis|Origin of Photosynthesis]] and the labour of [[species/cyanobacteria|Cyanobacteria]] to the changing chemistry of the sea. They belong to the [[era/archean-eon|Archean Eon]], run on into the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]], and are woven into the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the raw material of the species' steel is the residue of an ocean that rusted over about two billion years. Think of a steel beam overhead: its raw material began as iron settling out of seawater, a process driven by microbes with no aim beyond their own metabolism. That is the architecture of inheritance, a ledger written long before any hand could read it. The reading concerns only this link across time; the debate over which microbes did the work does not touch it. We build on residues we never chose.
--- question 1
Which organisms or processes oxidized the iron once dissolved in the sea?
--- question 2
Why did banded iron formations largely stop forming about 1.8 billion years ago?
