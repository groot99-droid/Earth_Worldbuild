=== people--jose-marti--1
src: c2aeffcb20e1
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
José Martí (1853 to 1895) was a Cuban poet, journalist and organizer who founded the Cuban Revolutionary Party in 1892 and helped kindle the 1895 war of independence against Spain. He was killed in one of its first battles.

He was born in Havana on 28 January 1853. Arrested at 16 over a letter criticizing a schoolmate who had joined the Spanish army, he was sentenced to six years' hard labor and deported to Spain in 1871. Afterward he lived in Mexico, Guatemala, Venezuela and, from 1880, mainly New York, a voice carried from shore to shore.

He published Ismaelillo (1882), Versos sencillos (1891) and the essay "Nuestra América" (1891), which argued that Latin American countries should govern from knowledge of their own peoples.

In 1892 he founded the Cuban Revolutionary Party and its newspaper Patria, arguing for a republic open to all races and against United States expansion into Cuba.

He landed in eastern Cuba on 11 April 1895 and was killed at Dos Ríos on 19 May in his first engagement. Cuba became formally independent in 1902, under limits set by the United States.

Both the Cuban government and Cuban exile groups have since claimed him as a symbol.

He became president in 1901 after the assassination of William McKinley, at 42 the youngest person to hold the office, and was elected in his own right in 1904.

Theodore "Teddy" Roosevelt (1858 to 1919) was the 26th President of the United States, serving from 1901 to 1909, remembered for trust-busting antitrust policy, a bold foreign policy, and a great widening of federal land conservation.

He became president in 1901 after the assassination of William McKinley, at 42 the youngest ever to hold the office, and he was elected in his own right in 1904.

He brought antitrust action against great corporate combinations, among them a successful suit against the Northern Securities Company railroad trust in 1904, and he mediated the 1902 coal strike partly by threatening to send federal troops to run the mines.

He established the United States Forest Service and by his executive authority created national forests, wildlife refuges and national monuments, sheltering roughly 230 million acres of land, forest and plain and mountain, in his presidency.

After leaving office he sought a return in the 1912 election on a progressive "Bull Moose" third-party ticket, splitting the Republican vote and helping to elect Woodrow Wilson instead.
