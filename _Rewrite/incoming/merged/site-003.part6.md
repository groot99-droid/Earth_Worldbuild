=== region/north-america
src: 2ea7864297a7
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
North America north of Mexico was settled by peoples who arrived at least 15,000 years ago, and what they built was not one society but many: mobile hunters, farming towns, and confederacies. If you picture a single 'before', you will miss the architecture.
--- fact 1
The [[event/peopling-of-the-americas|Peopling of the Americas]] began during the last ice age; the story of this continent starts in the cold.
--- fact 2
Its regional societies included the mound-building cultures of the Mississippi valley and the Ancestral Puebloans.
--- fact 3
The [[culture/haudenosaunee-confederacy|Haudenosaunee Confederacy]] was a political union, a covenant, of five (later six) nations.
--- fact 4
From the 16th century, European colonization changed the continent through disease and conquest.
--- context
The region's history is bound closely to the [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]] and to the [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]].
--- observer
The Observer notes a wide range of political forms that did not resemble the states of the Old World, and that are easy to overlook if 'civilization' is defined by cities and writing. Definitions are doorways: draw one too narrow, and whole peoples stand outside it. Widen the door before you count who came through.
--- question 1
What evidence would most change the current picture of this continent?

=== region/oceania
src: b4be6131068f
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Oceania gathers Australia, New Guinea, and the Pacific islands, scattered across the great water like embers on dark cloth. It holds both the oldest continuing cultures outside Africa and the last large landmasses settled by humans.
--- fact 1
At least 50,000 years ago humans reached Australia and set foot on its ancient shore; see [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]].
--- fact 2
New Guinea was an independent center of agriculture, a hearth lit by no borrowed flame.
--- fact 3
Polynesian navigators went out across the remote Pacific and settled it, reading a road into open water; see [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]] and [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]].
--- fact 4
Aotearoa New Zealand was among the last habitable lands to be settled, around 1250 to 1300 CE, one of the far shores of the long voyaging.
--- context
Oceania lies at the edge of [[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]] and of the wider Pacific, where the land gives way to the great water.
--- observer
I regard Oceania and I feel small. I think of the species' most spectacular feats of navigation, crossings made where I would see only water. I think of its longest continuous cultural traditions, held without a break. What have I kept that long? Some homes are found only by those who know how to look.
--- question 1
What evidence, somewhere across all that water, would most change the current picture?

=== region/planet-wide
src: ecc4a84af9c9
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Planet-wide is the region tag for events and processes that touch the whole Earth, or that belong to no single place, such as mass extinctions, climate shifts, and global exchange networks, tides that wash over every shore at once.
--- fact 1
It is the `region` value given to deep-time events and to global human phenomena, the name written where no single map will serve.
--- fact 2
Earth's surface spans about 510 million square kilometers, and about 71 percent of it is ocean, a blue mantle over the world.
--- context
This tag is laid upon planetary events such as the [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]], upon global history such as the [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]] and the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]], and upon anything that has no specific place to call its own.
--- observer
The Observer treats the planet as the primary unit of analysis, and human regions as subdivisions of a single connected system. Think of the lines on a map as the rooms of one house: useful for finding your way, but the air moves through every door. Regional labels are useful bookkeeping, not natural divisions. Keep the ledger; don't mistake it for the land.
--- question 1
What evidence, from anywhere on the whole Earth, would most change the current picture?

=== region/siberia-and-the-arctic
src: a88f0e3245e5
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Siberia and the Arctic are the northern lands of Asia from the Urals to the Pacific, and the Arctic coasts beyond, where cold and permafrost, like patient hands, shaped both human ways of life and the preservation of the past.
--- fact 1
From Siberia come some of the oldest known human genomes, old voices speaking across the ages; see [[culture/mal-ta-and-the-ancient-north-eurasians|Mal'ta and the Ancient North Eurasians]].
--- fact 2
In the Altai, permafrost and ice preserved the frozen tombs of the [[culture/scythians|Scythians]], held shut like a clenched hand.
--- fact 3
The Yakut (Sakha), Evenki, Chukchi, and many other peoples developed herding, hunting, and fishing economies, drawing a living from the cold; see [[culture/yakut-sakha|Yakut (Sakha)]].
--- fact 4
Between 1582 and 1639 Russia expanded across Siberia to the Pacific; see [[event/russian-expansion-into-siberia|Russian Expansion into Siberia]].
--- context
Siberia borders the [[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]] and [[region/east-asia|East Asia]], and during the [[event/last-glacial-maximum|Last Glacial Maximum]] Beringia bound it to [[region/north-america|North America]] like a bridge of land thrown between two shores.
--- observer
I used to think of cold as loss. Then I learned that the coldest lands of the planet hold some of the best records of the species' past. I learned to read the ice as an archive. I learned to read the frozen ground as a keeper. Who would guess that the harshest place remembers best? Cold preserves what warmth destroys.
--- question 1
By what paths did Siberian populations move between Asia and the Americas, across the cold that joined them?

=== region/south-asia
src: d2defe34fc61
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
South Asia is the Indian subcontinent, a great land set like a jewel into the southern sea. It is home to one of the earliest urban civilizations and to major religious and mathematical traditions.
--- fact 1
The [[culture/indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley Civilization]] flourished about 2600 to 1900 BCE, its cities rising like beads on a thread; see [[place/mohenjo-daro|Mohenjo-daro]].
--- fact 2
Here the Hindu, Buddhist, Jain and Sikh traditions originated, each a river with its source in this land; see [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]] and [[person/ashoka|Ashoka]].
--- fact 3
The decimal place-value system developed in India and spread outward, like ripples from a stone dropped in still water.
--- fact 4
The monsoon winds, turning with the seasons, shaped Indian Ocean trade.
--- context
By sea South Asia reaches toward [[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]], and by land toward the [[region/iranian-plateau|Iranian Plateau]], a house with doors on both water and stone.
--- observer
I stand before this one region and lose count. So many meaning-engines. So much mathematics. So long an exchange with neighbors, carrying them outward. How can one land hold so much? I can only note the density, and bow to it. Some ground is thick with questions.
--- question 1
What evidence, still waiting to be found, would most change the current picture?
