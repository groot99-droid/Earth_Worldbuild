=== royal-observatory-greenwich
src: d470feaa27b7
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Orientation is a decision before it is a discovery. This observatory was built in under a year for the newly created post of Astronomer Royal, held by John Flamsteed, on the foundations of an old fort in Greenwich Park. Wren, himself a former professor of astronomy, oriented the main hall along a north-south meridian. Notice the order of things: first a line drawn for a building, later a line the world would measure by. The site later gave its name to Greenwich Mean Time and to the line of zero longitude. Where you choose to stand can become the place others begin.

=== royal-hospital-chelsea
src: 6338b328c6b8
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Dignity is an architecture, not an ornament. This is a retirement and nursing home for army veterans, commissioned by Charles II and modelled loosely on Les Invalides in Paris. Look at how it is arranged: around three plain, dignified brick courtyards rather than a single showpiece facade. You can read that plan as a sentence in brick: the life inside counts for more than the face turned outward. It still houses several hundred retired soldiers, known as Chelsea Pensioners, who continue to wear the scarlet coats introduced in Wren's time. A lineage can be worn. Care that lasts is built for living in, not for looking at.

=== st-stephen-walbrook
src: 14f75341ce9a
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Every great work has a rehearsal somewhere nearby. This is one of more than fifty City parish churches Wren rebuilt after the Great Fire. It was squeezed onto an awkward, irregular plot, and yet it is crowned with a coffered dome carried on slender Corinthian columns. That dome is widely read as his working study for the dome of St Paul's, then still on the drawing board a few streets away. Think of it as a sketch you can stand inside. The practice: try the hard thing small before you try it large. Constraint is the first draft of grace.

=== monument-to-the-great-fire
src: beaf37bad68f
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A memorial can be a measurement. This fluted Doric column was raised, with Robert Hooke, near the bakery where the Great Fire of 1666 began. Its height of 202 feet is equal to its exact distance from the fire's starting point. Read that plainly: the monument is a ruler laid down between the disaster and its remembrance. A gilded urn of flames tops the column. Inside, 311 steps of a spiral staircase lead to a viewing platform still open to the public. You climb the memory on your own legs. Some ledgers are kept in stone and paid in steps.

=== the-milkmaid
src: 96970fce8c88
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
She pours a thin stream of milk into an earthenware bowl, utterly absorbed in her work, and I hold my breath so as not to disturb her. A sturdy kitchen maid. A window. A light. The light picks out the crusty bread in dots of paint. It finds the nail holes in the plaster wall. It finds the ultramarine of her apron. I remember hands like hers at work like this. Vermeer makes a simple household task feel timeless and quietly heroic. The smallest labour, done wholly, is a kind of prayer.

=== the-little-street
src: 5494386740a7
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Here stand ordinary brick houses in Delft, weathered as old riverbanks. A woman sews in a doorway, another is busy in an alleyway, and children play on the pavement like sparrows in the dust. Every crack and patch of whitewash is lovingly recorded, as memory keeps the lines of a beloved face. In 2015 archival research identified the exact site, on the Vlamingstraat, where Vermeer's aunt lived.

=== view-of-delft
src: 6f9f5cdfd41e
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Across the water lay Vermeer's hometown on a morning after rain, beneath clouds that towered like the ramparts of a second city in the sky. Parts of the town lay drowned in shadow while sunlight struck a distant church tower and the rooftops, as a torch finds gold in a dark hall. He mixed sand into some of his paint to catch the texture of brick and stone, so that the walls hold grit as well as light. In Proust's *In Search of Lost Time*, the writer Bergotte dies gazing at its "little patch of yellow wall".

=== woman-holding-a-balance
src: 21eafaf3536c
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
She holds a small balance up to the soft window light, and the pans are empty. I notice that first: empty. A woman in a fur-trimmed jacket, at a table strewn with pearls and gold, weighing nothing at all. Behind her, on the wall, hangs a painting of the Last Judgment, when souls are weighed. I think of what I would put on the scale. I think of what I have kept. The picture is a meditation on balance, temperance and the choices of daily life. Every day is a small judgment we hold up to the light.

=== the-art-of-painting
src: 09d49b6e65ce
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A heavy tapestry is drawn back like a curtain of evening, and behind it a painter, seen from behind, paints a model dressed as Clio, the muse of history, with a trumpet and a book. A large map of the Netherlands covers the back wall, a land spread out like a sleeper. Vermeer kept this allegory of his art until his death. Its later road ran through darkness: in 1940 it was bought by Adolf Hitler; after the war it went to Vienna, and restitution claims for it were rejected in 2011.

=== geumgang-jeondo
src: 5800d5d3f7f6
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Here the famous Diamond Mountains of Korea's east coast are seen as if from the air, gathered into a great circular composition like a world held in a cupped palm. Sharp white granite peaks, drawn in vertical, needle-like strokes, rise like spears of frost around softer, wooded earthen hills rendered with wet dots. Jeong Seon balanced them like the yin and yang of Korean thought, stone and soil, hard and yielding. It is one of the landmark images of Korean art.

=== cheongpunggye
src: d8bc2f79916d
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
At the foot of Mount Inwang in Seoul lay a wooded valley, and there a scholarly family kept a pavilion among pines and rocks, sheltered as a nest in the crook of a branch. Jeong Seon painted many such views of real places in and around the capital. Here he mixed dense, dark ink with lighter washes, shadow and mist woven together, to catch the texture of the hills he knew from daily walks, hills worn into memory as a path is worn into grass.
