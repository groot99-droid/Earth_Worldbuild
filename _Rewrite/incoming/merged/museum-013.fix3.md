Savitribai Phule (1831 to 1897) was an Indian educator, poet and social reformer in Maharashtra who, with her husband Jyotirao Phule, opened one of the first schools for girls in Pune in 1848. She died of plague in 1897 after nursing patients. Her life is a lesson in teaching as an act of courage.

She was born on 3 January 1831 in Naigaon, Maharashtra, and married Jyotirao Phule in 1840 as a child. He taught her to read, and she turned that gift outward.

In 1848 the couple opened a school for girls in Pune. She is often called India's first woman teacher; earlier girls' schools existed, so the "first" claim is contested, and historians also credit Fatima Sheikh as a co-worker. A movement is rarely the work of one name.

Accounts say she was harassed with stones and dung on her way to teach.

She and Jyotirao later opened further schools, including for children of lower castes, and in 1863 they founded a home for the prevention of infanticide.

She published a book of poetry, Kavya Phule, in 1854.
