=== people--peter-the-great--1
src: cf19653bb34b
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Peter I, called Peter the Great (1672 to 1725), ruled Russia from 1682, jointly at first and alone from 1696, until 1725. He drove a sweeping program of westernizing reform and turned Russia into a major European power, as a river is turned to a new course.

In 1697 to 1698 he travelled incognito through Western Europe on the "Grand Embassy," working for a time as a shipwright in the Netherlands to learn European shipbuilding and technology with his own hands.

He fought a long war against Sweden, the Great Northern War from 1700 to 1721, winning a decisive victory at the Battle of Poltava in 1709 and gaining lands on the Baltic Sea that made Russia a major naval power.

In 1703 he founded the city of St. Petersburg on newly won Baltic ground, building it as a planned European-style capital, and moved the seat of government there from Moscow.

He reshaped the Russian government, military and church administration along Western European lines, and he required Russian nobles to adopt Western dress; in a well-known instance he cut off boyars' traditional long beards with his own hand to mark the change.

=== people--jeong-seon--1
src: 7db84ee00931
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Jeong Seon (1676 to 1759), pen name Gyeomjae, was a Korean painter of the Joseon dynasty who remade landscape painting by showing the real scenery of Korea instead of imagined Chinese scenery. His "true-view landscape" (jingyeong sansuhwa) gave voice to a growing pride in Korean culture.

He was born in 1676 in Hanyang (modern Seoul), into a scholarly family of modest means. His father died when he was young. He trained in painting and in Neo-Confucian learning.

Through recommendations he held government posts, among them county magistrate at Hayang and Yangcheon, and in old age he rose to high official rank.

He lived near Mount Inwang in Seoul, and his friend the poet Yi Byeong-yeon traded poems and paintings with him for decades.

In 1711 and 1712 he journeyed to the Diamond Mountains (Geumgangsan), and the albums he made there made his name. He died in 1759 at 83.

For centuries Korean painters had followed Chinese models. He painted the true scenery of Korea and invented his own brushstrokes: sharp vertical strokes for granite peaks, like rain falling on stone, and soft wet dots for forested earth mountains.
