=== people--diego-velazquez--1
src: 806c0b197c8b
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Diego Velázquez (1599 to 1660) was the leading painter of Spain's Golden Age and court painter to King Philip IV for 37 years. His loose, luminous brushwork and unflattering portraits of kings, courtiers and jesters made him a "painter's painter." Notice that word, unflattering: at a court, honesty was his distinction.

He was baptised in Seville on 6 June 1599. At about 11 he entered the workshop of Francisco Pacheco, and in 1618 he married Pacheco's daughter Juana. His early Seville work shows the influence of Caravaggio.

In 1623 a portrait of the young Philip IV won him the post of court painter, and he moved to Madrid. He rose through court offices to become chamberlain of the palace.

Peter Paul Rubens, on a diplomatic mission to Madrid in 1628, encouraged him to visit Italy. His first Italian trip (1629 to 1631) transformed his colour and handling; a second (1649 to 1651) produced his portrait of Pope Innocent X. Sometimes the most useful advice is simply: go and look.

He painted Las Meninas in 1656 and in 1659 was admitted to the noble Order of Santiago, a rare honour for a painter. He died on 6 August 1660.

He painted alla prima, often without drawing, and altered compositions on the canvas; X-rays reveal many changes of mind. His palette was restrained, built on greys, blacks and earth tones. Changing your mind on the canvas is not failure; it is the work.

He kept working. I read those three words again. What else is there to do with grief but keep looking?
