=== place/nan-madol
title: Nan Madol
src: 3e2e18d60e0d
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Nan Madol is a ruined city of artificial stone islets raised in a lagoon off Pohnpei, in the Federated States of Micronesia; from about the 12th century until around 1628 it was the ceremonial and political heart of the Saudeleur dynasty, a seat of power set upon the tide.
--- fact 1
Nan Madol is made of about 92 artificial islets of stacked basalt columns and coral fill, threaded together by tidal canals across a core area of about 1.5 by 0.5 kilometers.
--- fact 2
Human activity at the site reaches back as early as the 1st or 2nd century CE; islet construction began in the 8th or 9th century, and the megalithic stonework that gives the site its present form rose from about 1180 to 1200.
--- fact 3
Tradition holds that the Saudeleur dynasty ruled Pohnpei from Nan Madol, gathering an estimated population of about 25,000 on the island under one rule, though the people living at Nan Madol itself probably numbered under 1,000.
--- fact 4
Around 1628, tradition says, a warrior named Isokelekel came as an invader and ended Saudeleur rule; some scholars also point to population decline as a contributing cause of the site's abandonment.
--- fact 5
In 2016 UNESCO inscribed Nan Madol as a World Heritage Site, its stones entered on the roll of the world's inheritance.
--- context
Nan Madol's builders belonged to the wider settlement of the Pacific carried by the [[event/austronesian-expansion|Austronesian Expansion]] and the [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]], and Micronesian voyaging traditions share roots with cultures such as the [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] farther south and east, as streams share a spring. In the codex of ages it sits in the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]] and the region [[region/oceania|Oceania]], and it belongs to the theme threads of [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]] and [[theme/technology|Technology]].
--- observer
I see a lagoon turned into a claim-line. A ruling lineage built its seat on stone islets, separated from the rest of the island by water, and I know that gesture: the status-signal that sets the center of power physically apart from those it rules. I have seen the high wall. I have seen the locked gate. I have seen the water that says *not you*.

And the tradition of Isokelekel's conquest? Like many founding and ending stories, it is ancestor-weight carried forward by the community rather than a document, and I hold it as a claim to be tested rather than a settled fact. I would have to set my reading down if archaeology shows the site's decline came mainly from environmental or demographic causes rather than conquest. What we carry forward is not always what happened; it is what we could not put down.
--- question 1
How much of the Isokelekel tradition remembers an actual conquest, and how much is later legend woven to explain the end of Saudeleur rule?
--- question 2
Given Pohnpei's small population, how was the labor organized to move the basalt columns and stack them, weight upon weight?

=== place/nazca-lines
title: Nazca Lines
src: 00bc2eab0a35
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Nazca Lines are a group of geoglyphs etched into the desert of southern Peru, made between about 400 BCE and 500 CE by the Paracas and Nazca cultures; they show animal, plant and geometric shapes and hundreds of straight lines, visible mainly from the air, like script laid across a vast page of desert.
--- fact 1
The lines lie in the Nazca Desert between the towns of Nazca and Palpa, about 400 kilometers south of Lima, spread across about 50 square kilometers of dry ground.
--- fact 2
They were made by lifting away a top layer of reddish, iron-oxide-coated pebbles to bare the lighter ground beneath, like pale skin under a rust-dark cloak; the lines and figures cut roughly 10 to 15 centimeters deep.
--- fact 3
Laid end to end, the lines run more than 1,300 kilometers, and the largest figures, such as a 134-meter condor and a 93-meter hummingbird, stretch to lengths of tens to a few hundred meters.
--- fact 4
In 1927 the Peruvian archaeologist Toribio Mejía Xesspe noted the lines on foot; in 1940 to 1941 the American historian Paul Kosok looked down on them from the air and proposed astronomical alignments, a theory Maria Reiche carried further; and in 1990 the researchers Gerald Hawkins and Anthony Aveni concluded that the evidence did not support a systematic astronomical purpose.
--- fact 5
Other proposed purposes include ritual paths bound to water and fertility, walked in ceremony, and markers tied to underground water sources; no single explanation is agreed on.
--- fact 6
In 1994 UNESCO inscribed the Lines and Geoglyphs of Nazca and Pampas de Jumana.
--- fact 7
Wounds have come one after another: squatters, a limestone quarry, a 2014 Greenpeace protest that left marks near one geoglyph, a 2018 truck crossing; and erosion and off-road vehicles remain threats that do not sleep.
--- context
Long before [[place/cusco|Cusco]] rose, and the Inca state with it in the Andes, the Nazca Lines had already been made. In the codex of ages they sit in the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] and the region [[region/andes|Andes]], and they belong to the theme threads of [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/science|Science]].
--- observer
I know a mark made to be read from the ground and a mark made to be read from the air are different kinds of pattern-taming. I know the debate over the Lines is partly a debate over which kind their makers intended. I know I cannot ask them.

Reiche proposed that they served as an astronomical instrument, a claim about precision that later analysis did not confirm, and I take it as a lesson I need: how a compelling reading can outrun its evidence. Haven't I done the same, with people I thought I understood? No written record from the makers survives, so every purpose offered here is inference from the marks themselves. I would have to revise my reading if excavation along the lines turns up consistent ritual deposits that point to one purpose over the others. To love a mystery is not the same as to solve it.
--- question 1
Were the lines walked as ritual paths, watched as an observatory of the heavens, or used for another purpose entirely?
--- question 2
How did the people who made the Lines organize the labor needed to plan and clear paths so long and so straight across the desert?
