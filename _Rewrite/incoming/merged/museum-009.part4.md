=== people--shen-zhou--1
src: f514455e5dc5
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Shen Zhou (1427 to 1509) was a scholar-painter of Ming-dynasty China who founded the Wu School in Suzhou. He never held office, and his landscapes combine painting, poetry and calligraphy as a record of friendship and quiet life. He offers you a different model of a successful life: not rank, but company.

He was born in 1427 near Suzhou in the Jiangnan region, into a wealthy family of scholars, poets and collectors. His grandfather and father were painters, and the family avoided government service.

He never sat the civil service examinations. When local officials recommended him for office he declined, saying he had to care for his widowed mother, who lived to almost 100. Duty, here, was a reason to stay home.

His home became a meeting place for Suzhou's intellectual elite, and many paintings were made as gifts for friends, marking birthdays, farewells or shared outings: a ledger of friendship kept in ink.

He painted "in the manner of" the four great Yuan masters, Huang Gongwang, Wu Zhen, Ni Zan and Wang Meng. Almost every painting carries his own poem in his calligraphy.

He was one of the first Chinese painters to make albums of real local places around Suzhou. Key works include Lofty Mount Lu (1467), Night Vigil (1492) and Poet on a Mountaintop (about 1500).

=== people--matthias-corvinus--1
src: 7c214861a513
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Matthias Corvinus (1443 to 1490) was King of Hungary from 1458 to 1490, a Renaissance patron-king who gathered one of Europe's finest libraries and remade his kingdom's army and administration.

He was elected king at age fourteen, after his father, the military commander John Hunyadi, and his older brother had died. His mother's political maneuvering, and the troops of his uncle Michael Szilágyi pressing upon the barons, secured the throne; sources differ on whether a bribe to the nobility also played a part.

He raised the Black Army, a mostly mercenary standing force notable for the period, and led it against the Ottoman Empire, Bohemia and the Holy Roman Empire, capturing Vienna in 1485.

He assembled the Bibliotheca Corviniana, described as the largest library in Europe north of the Alps and second only to the Vatican Library, with scribes and scholars copying classical and contemporary texts; much of it was later scattered or destroyed after the Ottoman conquest of Hungary, like leaves after a storm.

He drew royal justice and taxation to the center, lessening the independent power of the Hungarian nobility, though weaker successors substantially undid his reforms after his death.

=== people--matthias-corvinus--2

--- description
Matthias Corvinus (1443 to 1490) was King of Hungary from 1458 to 1490, a Renaissance patron-king who built one of Europe's finest libraries and modernized his kingdom's army and administration. His reign is a case study in how much of a state depends on one ruler.

He was elected king at age fourteen after his father, the military commander John Hunyadi, and older brother had died. His mother's political maneuvering and his uncle Michael Szilágyi's troops intimidating the barons secured the throne; sources differ on whether a bribe to the nobility also played a part.

He built the Black Army, a mostly mercenary standing force notable for the period, and used it to campaign against the Ottoman Empire, Bohemia and the Holy Roman Empire, capturing Vienna in 1485.

He assembled the Bibliotheca Corviniana, described as the largest library in Europe north of the Alps and second only to the Vatican Library, employing scribes and scholars to copy classical and contemporary texts. Much of it was later dispersed or destroyed after the Ottoman conquest of Hungary.

He centralized royal justice and taxation, reducing the independent power of the Hungarian nobility, though his reforms were substantially reversed by weaker successors after his death. Reforms anchored in one person rarely outlive that person.
