=== rain-steam-and-speed
src: ba609d2e45b8
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Out of a golden haze of rain and light, a steam train charges toward us across Maidenhead Railway Bridge over the Thames, its black funnel rising from the glow like a dark horn out of a sunlit storm. Ahead of it, small as a single heartbeat, a tiny hare races along the tracks. This is one of the first great paintings of modern technology, and one of Turner's most daring dissolutions of form into atmosphere, where rain, light and iron melt together as a river melts into mist.

=== sense-and-sensibility
src: ac145ca47bf9
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Austen's first published novel went out into the world anonymously, signed only "By a Lady," and it follows two sisters of contrasting temperament, as unlike as still water and a running stream: one guided by prudent restraint, the other by open emotion. Their father's death leaves them dependent on relatives, and the two are carried through courtship and financial precarity like leaves upon the same uncertain current.

=== pride-and-prejudice
src: 407ee3c4c13e
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Austen's best-known novel follows the sharp-witted Elizabeth Bennet and the proud, at first off-putting Mr Darcy, two tempers that meet like frost and flint. Misunderstanding and social pressure gather over five sisters' marriage prospects like weather over a valley, and slowly, as a thaw loosens a frozen river, they give way to mutual respect. It remains one of the most widely read novels in the English language.

=== mansfield-park
src: cedef5b6cb27
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Consider the architecture of a comfortable house, and then ask what holds it up. Austen's longest and most morally serious novel follows the quiet, principled Fanny Price, a poor relation raised among wealthy cousins, and it touches, more directly than her other novels, on the West Indian slave-plantation wealth underpinning the English gentry households she otherwise portrays with comic detachment. That is the lesson for us: every ledger of ease has a column we are not shown.

=== emma
src: 3fdc2bd15bde
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A comedy of misplaced matchmaking. The wealthy, well-meaning but overconfident Emma Woodhouse keeps trying to arrange other people's romances, and her attempts repeatedly misfire. The novel is widely admired for the tightly controlled irony of its narration, which lets readers see through Emma's misjudgements well before she does. Think of watching a friend walk confidently the wrong way: you see it, she does not, and that gap is the whole architecture of the comedy. Irony is humility, learned first at someone else's expense.

=== northanger-persuasion
src: 67d51a502f0c
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Two novels, bound together and published the year after Austen's death, carried a biographical notice by her brother that lifted the veil from her authorship publicly for the first time. Northanger Abbey gently satirises Gothic novel conventions, as a lantern laughs at shadows, while Persuasion, her last completed work, turns to a quieter, more autumnal story, like leaves settling on still water, of a woman reconsidering a broken engagement years later.

=== nihonbashi-morning
src: 6376fe7ef3c1
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Here, with its first print, begins the series that made Hiroshige famous. At dawn, as the light comes up pale, a feudal lord's procession marches over Edo's Nihonbashi bridge, the starting point of the Tōkaidō highway, and fish sellers hurry past beneath their loads. The great wooden gates swing open like a curtain drawn back, and the whole long journey waits beyond them.

=== kanbara-night-snow
src: 4e2bb8995eae
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Snow lies over a mountain village at night like a held breath, muffling every sound, and three figures trudge uphill beneath umbrellas and straw capes. Yet Kanbara, on the warm Pacific coast, rarely sees snow. Hiroshige invented this winter for its poetic mood, summoning it as a poet summons a season. The subtle grey gradations of the night sky, printed by wiping the block, sink from dark to pale like dusk settling into water, and show the refinement of Edo printmaking.

=== shono-driving-rain
src: 424f28de3e39
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A sudden downpour sweeps across a hillside like a host of silver spears. Palanquin bearers and travellers run for shelter, bent against the wind as reeds bend before a flood, while bamboo groves toss in silhouette, black against the grey. The rain is drawn as fine diagonal lines across the whole sheet, a loom of water strung from sky to earth. It stands among the most celebrated images of weather in Japanese art.

=== plum-park-kameido
src: f6aa91a0be69
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A gnarled plum branch, old as a dragon's claw and dotted with white blossoms, fills the foreground so closely that it seems to press against our very eyes. Beyond it, visitors admire the orchard beneath a sky that shades from red to white, the way dawn fades into snow. Vincent van Gogh copied this print in oil in 1887, and its dramatic cropping became a lesson for European modernists, carried west like a seed on the wind.

=== sudden-shower
src: 7e1893f4dc57
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A summer storm breaks over a wooden bridge in Edo, as if the sky had tipped itself out. People scurry across under hats and straw mats, and the far bank fades into grey, as though the river had begun to forget its shore. The rain is printed from two separate blocks, their lines crossing at different angles, to suggest a heavy downpour, the heavens woven twice over. Van Gogh painted his own version, *Bridge in the Rain*, in 1887.

=== fireworks-at-ryogoku
src: c59da00afcb6
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A single firework bursts over the Sumida River at night and falls in a shower of sparks, like a star that has spent itself in one bright breath. Below the Ryōgoku bridge, pleasure boats crowd the dark water. The deep, velvety blues and blacks, and the tiny points of light scattered across them like embers on a still pool, anticipate Whistler's *Nocturne in Black and Gold: The Falling Rocket* (1875).
