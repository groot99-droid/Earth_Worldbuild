=== person/peter-the-great
title: Peter the Great
src: 79a1071a8955
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Peter I, remembered as Peter the Great (1672 to 1725), held the Russian throne from 1682, jointly at first and alone from 1696 until his death in 1725, and drove a sweeping architecture of westernizing reform that turned Russia into a major European power.
--- fact 1
Between 1697 and 1698 he crossed Western Europe incognito on the "Grand Embassy," and for a time apprenticed as a shipwright in the Netherlands, studying European shipbuilding and technology at first hand.
--- fact 2
He fought Sweden in the long Great Northern War, from 1700 to 1721, winning a decisive victory at the Battle of Poltava in 1709 and gaining Baltic Sea territory that made Russia a major naval power.
--- fact 3
In 1703 he founded St. Petersburg on newly won Baltic territory, raised it as a planned, European-style capital, and moved the seat of government there from Moscow.
--- fact 4
He reorganized Russia's government, military and church administration along Western European lines, required nobles to adopt Western dress, and, in a well-known instance, personally cut off boyars' traditional long beards to mark the change.
--- context
Peter the Great governed Russia from within [[region/europe|Europe]] during the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]], turning the country's institutions toward Western European models. See [[theme/technology|Technology]], [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that Peter's reforms descended from the top, often by coercion, onto a nobility and a church that had not asked for Westernization; the architecture of change rose quickly, but the tension between imposed modernization and inherited tradition stayed unresolved, a ledger later Russian history kept having to settle.
--- question 1
How far did Peter's Westernizing program reach into everyday life beyond the court and nobility, rather than remaining a project of the elite?

=== person/philip-ii
title: Philip II
src: f9191eef11b0
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Philip II (1527 to 1598) reigned as King of Spain from 1556 to 1598, over an empire spanning the Iberian Peninsula, the Netherlands, parts of Italy, and vast territories in the Americas and the Philippines, an archipelago named for him.
--- fact 1
In 1556 he inherited Spain, its Italian territories and the Spanish Americas from his father, Charles V, and, through his marriage to Mary I of England from 1554 to 1558, briefly held the English throne jointly with her.
--- fact 2
Beginning in 1566, he directed a long, costly war to suppress a revolt in the Spanish Netherlands, a conflict that ran on through decades of shifting phases and stayed unresolved for the rest of his reign.
--- fact 3
In 1588 he sent the Spanish Armada against England to support a planned invasion and to end English backing for the Dutch rebels and for privateers raiding Spanish shipping; storms and English naval tactics wrecked much of the fleet.
--- fact 4
He presided over Spain's greatest territorial extent while driving the crown into multiple bankruptcies, in 1557, 1560, 1575 and 1596, the ledger undone by near-constant warfare funded in part by American silver.
--- context
Philip II governed Spain from within [[region/europe|Europe]] through the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]], steering wars in the Netherlands and against England. See [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]], [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that Philip II's reign shows how a flow of American silver, often assumed to be a guarantee of power, instead financed a level of continuous warfare that bankrupted the crown again and again; wealth drawn from the Americas did not convert into lasting Spanish military dominance in Europe.
--- question 1
How far did the repeated bankruptcies of Philip's reign weaken Spain's long-term capacity to compete with rising powers such as England and the Dutch Republic?

=== person/pieter-bruegel-the-elder
title: Pieter Bruegel the Elder
src: 6d46319e72a1
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Pieter Bruegel the Elder (about 1525 to 1569) stood as the most important Netherlandish painter of the 16th century. He made peasants, proverbs and the turning seasons his subjects, filling crowded panels and sweeping landscapes with them. Only about 40 of his paintings survive.
--- fact 1
Bruegel was probably born between 1525 and 1530, perhaps near Breda in the Duchy of Brabant, and in 1551 became a master in the painters' guild of Antwerp.
--- fact 2
In 1552 he travelled to Italy, and the drawings he made of the Alps on the return journey fed the mountain views of his later paintings.
--- fact 3
In Antwerp he designed prints for the publisher Hieronymus Cock, some in the manner of Hieronymus Bosch, and in 1563 he married Mayken Coecke and moved to Brussels, where he painted most of his masterpieces.
--- fact 4
He died in Brussels in September 1569, and his sons Pieter Brueghel the Younger and Jan Brueghel the Elder became successful painters, founding a dynasty of their own.
--- fact 5
Among his key works: Netherlandish Proverbs (1559), Children's Games (1560), The Tower of Babel (1563), The Hunters in the Snow (1565) and The Peasant Wedding (about 1567 to 1568).
--- fact 6
He often set his viewpoint high, looking down over dozens of small figures, and built his pictures around proverbs, games or Bible stories retold in Flemish dress.
--- fact 7
He painted through the religious conflicts and Spanish repression that led toward the Dutch Revolt, and many scholars read hidden commentary on those events into his work.
--- fact 8
According to the early biographer Karel van Mander, he and a friend dressed as peasants to join village weddings and fairs, and the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna now holds the world's largest group of his paintings.
--- context
Bruegel worked in [[region/europe|Europe]] during the [[event/renaissance|Renaissance]], within the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]], building on the crowded moral landscapes of [[person/hieronymus-bosch|Hieronymus Bosch]]. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
I keep looking at the ordinary people he chose: the peasant, the reveler, the child mid-game, each one a proverb waiting for me to decode it. I find in these crowded panels a record of festival, of labor, of kinship that the written page of his age never bothered to keep. I ask myself whether the hidden commentary scholars find in his religious-conflict years is truly there, or whether it is my own listening filling a silence. The tale of the painter dressed as a peasant, slipping into a village wedding, comes from a single early biographer — one voice, one memory. Both readings, I hold loosely. What a picture keeps is never quite what a chronicle keeps.
--- question 1
Did his pictures carry coded commentary on the Spanish repression, or is that a reading added later?

=== person/piye
title: Piye
src: 5ae87554e91b
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Piye (reigned about 744 to 714 BCE) was a king of Kush who conquered Egypt and is usually counted as the first ruler of Egypt's Twenty-fifth (Kushite) Dynasty; his birth year is unknown, and the dates given here mark only his reign.
--- fact 1
Piye ruled Kush from Napata, near Jebel Barkal in present-day Sudan, with his reign dated about 744 to 714 BCE; some scholars instead count his father Kashta as the dynasty's first ruler.
--- fact 2
About 728 BCE he marched north against Tefnakht, prince of Sais, and a coalition of Delta rulers, and he defeated them.
--- fact 3
His Victory Stele, found at Jebel Barkal in 1862 and now held in Cairo, records the campaign in Egyptian hieroglyphs, presents him as a servant of the god Amun, and lists the rulers who submitted to him.
--- fact 4
He did not remain in Egypt, returning instead to Kush and leaving local rulers in place.
--- fact 5
At Jebel Barkal he expanded the temple of Amun.
--- fact 6
He was buried in a pyramid at El-Kurru, together with horses, and was succeeded by Shebitku, whom sources describe as either his son or his brother; recent evidence places Shabaka's reign after Shebitku's, not before.
--- context
Piye ruled the [[culture/kingdom-of-kush|Kingdom of Kush]] and took control of the land of the [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]] in the [[region/nile-valley|Nile Valley]] during the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]]; his successors would later fight the [[culture/neo-assyrian-empire|Neo-Assyrian Empire]]. See [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that a ruler from the southern edge of the Egyptian world claimed its center by appealing to that center's own gods, script and burial forms, ancestor-weight working here in an outsider's favor. His stele is a royal victory text, so its portrait of a pious and merciful king may be status-signal more than record. The reading of his rule as conquest would weaken if other evidence showed Kushite influence in Upper Egypt already established before his campaign.
--- question 1
How far did Kushite influence in Upper Egypt reach before his campaign began?

=== person/qin-shi-huang
title: Qin Shi Huang
src: 04975ffc02bc
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Qin Shi Huang (259 to 210 BCE) became the first emperor of a unified China, standardizing writing, currency and measures, and raising a vast tomb complex that included the terracotta army.
--- fact 1
At thirteen he became king of Qin, and by 221 BCE he had completed the unification of China.
--- fact 2
Across his empire he standardized the script, weights and measures, and axle widths.
--- fact 3
Later historians, hostile to the Qin, described book burning and harsh punishments, though some of these accounts are debated.
--- fact 4
His tomb complex near Xi'an holds thousands of life-size clay soldiers.
--- context
See [[event/unification-of-china-under-qin|Unification of China under Qin]]; the state he built was inherited and continued by the [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]].
--- observer
The Observer notes a ruler working to make a huge population legible: one script, one currency, one set of weights. Standardization is an architecture of control, and of trade, at once.
--- question 1
How reliable are the traditional accounts of his rule?
