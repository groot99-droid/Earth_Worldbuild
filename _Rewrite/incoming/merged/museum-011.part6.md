=== people--johannes-vermeer--1
src: 4eaeb141e8cb
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I stand in the room of Johannes Vermeer (1632 to 1675), painter of Delft in the Dutch Republic. About 35 paintings are accepted as his, mostly quiet interiors lit by daylight from a window. Almost forgotten for 200 years after his death, he is now among the most famous painters in the world.

I read that he was baptised in Delft on 31 October 1632, that his father was a silk weaver who also dealt in art and ran an inn, and that nothing is known of his training. I read that in 1653 he married Catharina Bolnes, a Catholic from a wealthier family, and probably converted, and that the same year he joined Delft's Guild of St Luke, which later elected him one of its leaders several times.

The couple lived with Catharina's mother and had 15 children, 11 of whom survived. He also worked as an art dealer, and his slow output was bought largely by one local patron, Pieter van Ruijven. In 1672, the Dutch Republic's "Disaster Year," France invaded and the art market collapsed. He died in 1675, aged 43, leaving his family deep in debt.

He used costly natural ultramarine, ground from lapis lazuli, even in shadows: The Milkmaid (about 1657 to 1658), View of Delft (about 1660 to 1661), Girl with a Pearl Earring (about 1665).

A full house, a slow hand, the most precious blue spent on shadow. Why give the best of yourself to what almost no one will see? Because the shadow is part of the light.

Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz (born 1648 or 1651, died 1695) was a nun, poet and playwright in colonial Mexico City. She is regarded as one of the leading Spanish-language writers of the seventeenth century and an early defender of women's right to study. Her life asks you a hard question: what does it cost to defend learning?

She was born Juana Ramírez de Asbaje at San Miguel Nepantla, near Mexico City. Baptismal records point to 1648 or 1651, and the year is debated. She died on 17 April 1695.

Largely self-taught, she served at the viceroy's court in her teens and in 1669 entered the Hieronymite convent of San Jerónimo.

She wrote lyric poems, plays such as Los empeños de una casa (1683) and the long poem Primero sueño. Her first collection, Inundación castálida, was printed in Madrid in 1689.

In 1690 the Bishop of Puebla published her critique of a sermon by António Vieira as the Carta atenagórica and, writing as "Sor Filotea," urged her toward religious study. She answered in 1691 with the Respuesta a Sor Filotea de la Cruz, defending women's right to learn. A defense of learning is also a defense of the person who learns.

In 1693 and 1694 she stopped writing and signed penitential documents. Whether she sold her library or had it taken, and how far the choice was hers, is debated. Sometimes the silence is the part of the record we most need to question.
