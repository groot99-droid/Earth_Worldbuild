=== poet-on-a-mountaintop
title: Poet on a Mountaintop
src: 361b4e71912b
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A scholar stands alone on a clifftop, gazing across an empty valley veiled in mist, like one who has reached the rim of the world. Shen Zhou's poem, written into the sky itself, imagines the white clouds like a sash wound around the mountains. The vast empty space, the simple forms and the fusion of painting, poetry and calligraphy, three streams meeting in one still pool, make this small album leaf one of the most loved images in Chinese art.

=== crabs
title: Crabs
src: 94ca36ef5f50
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Greatness does not live only in the grand. Three crabs clamber among reeds at the water's edge, each drawn with a few wet, confident strokes of ink, and a short poem in Shen Zhou's hand is written above them. He enjoyed painting everyday subjects such as vegetables, fruit, birds, cats and crabs, which were a favourite autumn delicacy in Suzhou. These quick, humorous sketches show the lively side of an artist better known for grand landscapes. Think of it as the other half of the ledger: the mountain and the supper. A whole life keeps room for both.

=== seven-deadly-sins
title: The Seven Deadly Sins and the Four Last Things
src: 272bb9986b6f
mode: horror-prose
--- description
A painted tabletop. Arranged like a giant eye. At its centre, inside the "pupil", Christ rises from his tomb above the Latin warning *Beware, beware, the Lord sees*. Around him, seven scenes of everyday life act out the sins. Gluttony. Sloth. Lust. Pride. Anger. Envy. Avarice. Four roundels in the corners. Death. Judgment. Heaven. Hell. Philip II of Spain kept it in his bedroom at the Escorial. Some scholars now credit it to Bosch's workshop or a follower. Whoever's hand it was, the eye is open.

=== ship-of-fools
title: The Ship of Fools
src: 94626fd345cf
mode: horror-prose
--- description
A leaky boat. Drifting nowhere. Its crew: a monk, a nun, a crowd of drunken revellers. They sing. They gorge. They snap at a dangling cake. A tree serves as the mast. In its branches, a jester perches. It is a fragment of a larger triptych, and it mocks human folly and corrupt clergy with the sharp humour of its day. Still leaking. Still drifting. Still nowhere.

=== temptation-of-st-anthony
title: The Temptation of St Anthony
src: 8ae88e6e7fb4
mode: horror-prose
--- description
The desert hermit Anthony kneels in prayer. Around him, chaos swirls. Fish that fly like airships. Demons in armour. A black mass in a ruined tower. A city burning on the horizon. Bosch invented a whole world of hybrid monsters to show the saint's inner struggle against temptation. He kneels. It swirls. The painting inspired later Surrealists.

The monsters kept breeding.

=== garden-of-earthly-delights
title: The Garden of Earthly Delights
src: c9f543353973
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Bosch's most famous and mysterious work, a world told in three panels like a saga that runs from dawn to ruin. The left panel shows God presenting Eve to Adam in Paradise. The huge centre panel teems with naked figures frolicking among giant birds, strawberries and crystal fountains, a summer without a horizon. The right panel is a nightmarish, frozen Hell of musical torture instruments and the "Tree-Man". Whether it is a warning against sin or a vision of a lost innocence, scholars still disagree after five centuries, and the garden keeps its secret like a sealed spring.

=== haywain-triptych
title: The Haywain Triptych
src: c68c99272530
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Look at what people will fight for. A giant cart of hay, a Flemish symbol of worthless worldly goods, rolls toward Hell pulled by demons. People of every class, from pope and emperor down, grab at the hay or fight and kill each other to get it. On top, a pair of lovers ignore the angel praying beside them. The story reads left to right, from the Fall in Eden to damnation, a ledger kept in paint. Read it as a doctrine of appetite: the cart keeps rolling while you grab. What you clutch at is also what carries you.

=== christ-carrying-the-cross
title: Christ Carrying the Cross
src: b3de480d7536
mode: horror-prose
--- description
No landscape. No sky. Only faces. A tight crush of grotesque, jeering faces, pressed around the calm, closed-eyed Christ. Closer. Closer. Saint Veronica, holding the cloth with his image, turns away in the corner. The composition is claustrophobic and almost modern. Its attribution to Bosch himself is debated. The faces stay.

=== dancing-dervishes
title: Dancing Dervishes (attributed)
src: 4f8174237cf2
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I stand among the ones who look on. Sufi mystics whirl and leap in ecstatic dance in a flowering garden while others watch, some calm, some overcome with emotion. Which of them am I? Every figure has a distinct pose and expression, and I search the page for mine. The painting captures the *sama*, the Sufi ceremony of music and movement, with a liveliness that marks the new naturalism of the Herat school. I remember being moved and not knowing why. Some of us dance and some of us are undone, and both are a way of standing near the holy.

=== timur-granting-audience
title: Timur Granting an Audience at Balkh (attributed)
src: af7fa2752339
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
From an illustrated history of the conqueror Timur (Tamerlane), made for Sultan Husayn Bayqara. Timur sits enthroned beneath a canopy at his accession in 1370, like a sun at the centre of its turning court, while courtiers and guards fill a flowering garden in a crowded yet ordered composition. This is one half of a double-page scene attributed to the young Behzād. It shows the courtly world he would record throughout his career, the first page of a long chronicle of gardens and gold.

=== seduction-of-yusuf
title: The Seduction of Yusuf
src: efd4ac29f7ea
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Resistance has an architecture. In Behzād's most famous painting, the story of Yusuf (Joseph) and Zulaikha (Potiphar's wife) is set in a fantastic palace of staircases, doorways and tiled rooms. Zulaikha has led Yusuf through seven locked chambers, but as she reaches for him, the doors fly open and he escapes. See how the building does the teaching: the maze-like architecture becomes an image of the soul resisting desire. The signed work is one of the high points of Persian painting. Every locked door is also a threshold out.

=== construction-of-khawarnaq
title: The Construction of the Palace of Khawarnaq
src: 03810bc20781
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Instead of a royal scene, Behzād painted the hands that raise a palace: labourers carry bricks up ladders rung by rung, masons lay courses, a man mixes mortar, another hauls water. Each figure is individually observed, each bent to a different task, as every star keeps its own place in a constellation. It is one of the most celebrated images of labour in Islamic art, and it shows Behzād's interest in the everyday world, the ground on which every throne is built.
