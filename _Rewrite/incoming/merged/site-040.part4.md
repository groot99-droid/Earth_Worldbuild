=== event/covid-19-pandemic
src: d8aaa1ee9b38
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The COVID-19 pandemic was a global outbreak of the coronavirus SARS-CoV-2, first identified in late 2019.
--- fact 1
The World Health Organization declared a pandemic on 11 March 2020 and closed the global health emergency on 5 May 2023.
--- fact 2
More than 7 million deaths were officially reported; the WHO estimated about 14.9 million excess deaths across 2020 and 2021 — a range of 13.3 to 16.6 million — nearly three times the officially reported total for that period.
--- fact 3
Vaccines built on mRNA technology were developed and authorized within about a year.
--- fact 4
Lockdowns, remote work, and supply-chain disruption reached economies worldwide.
--- context
The pandemic followed the pattern of earlier disease spread along trade networks; compare the [[event/black-death|Black Death]]. It also depended on the [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]] and on modern [[technology/vaccination|Vaccination]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that a virus crossed the planet in weeks, carried along the same exchange-webs that carry goods. It notes, too, an unusual feature of this architecture: scientists around the world shared data in real time and produced effective vaccines in a fraction of the historical time it once took.
--- question 1
What were the origins of the virus, and how can future pandemics be prevented?

=== event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction
src: bb2828298a1f
mode: horror-prose
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
About 66 million years ago, a rock fell on what is now Mexico. The Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction followed. Every non-avian dinosaur, gone. About three-quarters of all species with them.
--- fact 1
The Chicxulub crater lies buried beneath the Yucatan Peninsula.
--- fact 2
A global layer rich in iridium — an element common in asteroids — reported by Luis and Walter Alvarez and colleagues in 1980.
--- fact 3
Volcanism in the Deccan Traps. Probably adding stress of its own.
--- fact 4
Birds survived. Descended from small feathered dinosaurs. See [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]].
--- context
The [[era/mesozoic-era|Mesozoic Era]] ends here. The [[era/cenozoic-era|Cenozoic Era]] begins. Mammals — the ancestors of primates among them — found new room to grow.
--- observer
The Observer regards this as the most consequential accident in the species' own story. In a real sense, human history begins with a rock falling on Yucatan.
--- question 1
How much did volcanism matter, compared with the impact?

=== event/crusades
src: 54e2160afa24
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Crusades were a series of religiously sanctioned military expeditions, launched by Western European Christians from 1095, largely to capture and hold Jerusalem and other holy sites in the Levant.
--- fact 1
Pope Urban II called for the First Crusade at the Council of Clermont in 1095.
--- fact 2
The First Crusade captured Jerusalem in 1099 and established Crusader states.
--- fact 3
Saladin retook Jerusalem in 1187.
--- fact 4
The Fourth Crusade sacked the Christian city of Constantinople in 1204.
--- fact 5
The last Crusader stronghold on the mainland, Acre, fell to the Mamluks on 28 May 1291.
--- context
The Crusades drew Western Europe into sustained contact with the Islamic world and the [[culture/byzantine-empire|Byzantine Empire]], and included the sack of [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]] in 1204. They followed the [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]] and the conquest of the Levant. Crusades sits in the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]] and the region [[region/levant-and-anatolia|Levant and Anatolia]], within the theme thread of [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that a claim on a strip of ground was fought over for two centuries because both sides held it sacred. The architecture of meaning can turn ground into something worth dying for in a way no economic calculation ever would.
--- question 1
How much did the Crusades change trade and the exchange of knowledge?

=== event/deccan-traps-eruptions
src: 8672a231f276
mode: horror-prose
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Lava poured across west-central India. Huge. The main phases of the Deccan Traps eruptions: about 66.3 to 65.5 million years ago. They overlap the end-Cretaceous extinction. What role they played in it is still debated.
--- fact 1
Among the largest volcanic provinces on Earth. Flows piled more than two kilometres thick in the Western Ghats. They once covered well over a million square kilometres.
--- fact 2
Radiometric dating places most of the volume within about a million years of the Cretaceous-Paleogene boundary — large pulses both before and after the Chicxulub impact. The order is debated.
--- fact 3
Carbon dioxide. Sulfur gases. Warming, cooling episodes, acid rain — all could have followed. Ecosystems stressed before the impact ever came.
--- fact 4
Some recent studies conclude the impact, not the volcanism, drove the extinction. The question stays open.
--- fact 5
The eruptions trace back to the Réunion hotspot, which India passed over as it drifted north.
--- context
The Deccan Traps Eruptions fall at the end of the [[era/cretaceous-period|Cretaceous Period]] and overlap the [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]]. They came before the [[event/collision-of-india-and-asia|Collision of India and Asia]]. The event belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the end-Cretaceous extinction had at least two candidate causes arriving at nearly the same time, and that telling them apart depends on dating precision of about a hundred thousand years out of sixty-six million. A single-cause story is easier to tell than to verify. This reading would weaken if dating showed the eruptions had ended before the impact.
--- question 1
How much did the eruptions contribute to the extinction, relative to the impact?
--- question 2
How did the timing of the biggest lava pulses compare with the impact?

=== event/decolonization
src: 72fb87013c88
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Decolonization was the process by which most colonies of European empires became independent states, largely between 1945 and 1975.
--- fact 1
The United Nations began with 51 members in 1945 and counts 193 today, most of that growth owed to decolonization.
--- fact 2
India and Pakistan became independent in 1947; Ghana, in 1957, was the first sub-Saharan African colony to gain independence; 17 African states became independent in 1960.
--- fact 3
Independence came through negotiation in some places and war in others, as in Algeria in 1962.
--- fact 4
The Portuguese colonial empire in Africa ended in 1974 to 1975, after the Carnation Revolution of April 1974 overthrew the regime in Lisbon.
--- context
Decolonization followed [[event/world-war-ii|World War II]] and reshaped [[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]], [[region/south-asia|South Asia]], and [[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]. See [[person/mahatma-gandhi|Mahatma Gandhi]] and [[person/nelson-mandela|Nelson Mandela]].
--- observer
The Observer notes the rapid rewriting of the world's claim-lines: within a single generation, most of the planet's land changed from subject to sovereign. Many of the borders drawn by former empires remain in place.
--- question 1
How did colonial borders shape later conflicts?
