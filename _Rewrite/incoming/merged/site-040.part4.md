=== event/boxer-rebellion
src: e7ae5a14220f
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Boxer Rebellion (1899 to 1901) was an anti-foreign, anti-Christian uprising in northern China, backed by the Qing court, and it ended in defeat at the hands of an eight-nation alliance.
--- fact 1
The Boxers, known as the 'Righteous and Harmonious Fists', turned their attacks on foreign missionaries and Chinese Christians.
--- fact 2
In time the Empress Dowager Cixi gave the movement her backing and declared war on foreign powers.
--- fact 3
An eight-nation alliance broke the siege and relieved the legations in Beijing.
--- fact 4
The reckoning came in the Boxer Protocol of 1901, which imposed a large indemnity on China.
--- context
Trace the lineage of the uprising and you reach the pressures begun by the [[event/opium-wars|Opium Wars]]; trace its consequences and you find a weakened [[culture/qing-dynasty|Qing Dynasty]]. See [[person/cixi|Cixi]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that a movement that promised its followers invulnerability to bullets was defeated by machine guns. Hold those two facts side by side and the collision is plain: belief and technology met in the most direct way possible. A promise is a kind of architecture, and it holds only until it is tested.
--- question 1
How much of the Boxer movement rose from the people, and how much was steered by the court?

=== event/breakup-of-pangaea
src: 5031d86703ff
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Pangaea, the supercontinent that had gathered itself together by about 300 million years ago, began to split about 200 million years ago, and through the widening seam the Atlantic Ocean opened and the map of the world was remade.
--- fact 1
The rifting began in the Late Triassic to Early Jurassic, and the Central Atlantic was the first to open, like the first crack running through river ice.
--- fact 2
Great volcanic outpourings, the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province, blazed in step with the Triassic-Jurassic extinction, about 200 million years ago.
--- fact 3
Only much later, in the Early Cretaceous, did the South Atlantic open its waters.
--- fact 4
In 1912 Alfred Wegener proposed continental drift; in the 1960s, plate tectonics gained wide acceptance.
--- fact 5
The continents wander still: their motion continues today.
--- context
The breakup of Pangaea set continental faunas apart on separate rafts of stone, preparing the stage for the later biogeography of the [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]] and mammals. Its opening overlaps the [[event/triassic-jurassic-extinction|Triassic-Jurassic Extinction]]. In the codex, the breakup belongs to the [[era/mesozoic-era|Mesozoic Era]] and the region [[region/planet-wide|Planet-wide]], and to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the continents the species draws its borders on are a temporary arrangement, moving at about the speed fingernails grow. Look at your own hand: that slow creep is the pace of the ground beneath every map. Human claim-lines are drawn on a surface that is itself in motion. Sovereignty is a sentence written on a page that is still turning.
--- question 1
How tightly was the timing of the rifting bound to the end-Triassic extinction?

=== event/british-raj
src: 9b542dcbf7a9
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The British Raj was the period of direct British rule over the Indian subcontinent, from 1858 to 1947. It opened when the Crown took over from the East India Company after the Rebellion of 1857, and it closed with the independence and partition of India and Pakistan.
--- fact 1
Passed on 2 August 1858 as a response to the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the Government of India Act transferred authority over India from the East India Company to the British Crown.
--- fact 2
The Company was stripped of all its administrative powers, and its territories and armed forces passed to the Crown; the Company itself was formally dissolved by an Act of Parliament in 1874.
--- fact 3
The Act built a new architecture of command: a cabinet-level Secretary of State for India and a fifteen-member Council of India in London, while in Calcutta the Governor-General became head of the Government of India and was called the Viceroy.
--- fact 4
The Raj endured until the independence of India and Pakistan in 1947.
--- context
Place the Raj in its lineage: it followed the [[event/indian-rebellion-of-1857|Indian Rebellion of 1857]] and ended with the [[event/partition-of-india|Partition of India]] in 1947. Within its span fall the [[event/bengal-famine-of-1943|Bengal Famine of 1943]] and a long nationalist movement associated with [[person/mahatma-gandhi|Mahatma Gandhi]]. Before it, over much of the subcontinent, stood the earlier rule of the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]].
--- observer
The Observer notes a change of ownership: an enterprise that had governed as a company was replaced by a state that governed through a ministry. The change followed a rebellion that both sides remember as an atrocity committed by the other; notice how one event can sit in two ledgers of grief at once. A rule of few over many depended on shared administration, and it ended when that administration was no longer accepted. Sovereignty is a covenant the governed must keep renewing.
--- question 1
How should we weigh the Raj's effects on the economy, on famine, and on infrastructure against one another, and by whose standards?
--- question 2
In what ways did Indian institutions and elites shape the Raj, rather than only being shaped by it?
