=== righteous-shall-receive
src: b949bd0e3c25
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Notice what is missing here: a single painted line. This memorial window is built almost entirely from richly textured "drapery glass," sheets of glass manipulated while molten to form folds resembling actual fabric, and with them Tiffany could suggest robes and landscape without a brush. The effect is unique to his studio's glass-making technique. Here the material does the drawing. When the grammar lives in the medium itself, you no longer need to write over it.

=== laurelton-hall
src: b5d69687c48d
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Imagine a house built as one complete sentence. Tiffany's own eighty-room estate on Long Island was designed as a total showcase of his work, from its stained-glass windows and mosaic-tiled courtyard fountain to its furniture and landscaping. It was largely abandoned after the Depression and destroyed by fire in 1957. What survives, its architectural fragments and windows, is now held by the Charles Hosmer Morse Museum of American Art. Even a total architecture comes down to fragments; what we keep is the part that can still carry light.

=== galaxy-of-musicians
src: 1cc20e2a37f0
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Eleven women gather like stars drawn into one constellation, each dressed in the clothing of a different region or community of India, and they play instruments including the veena, tanpura, sitar and tabla. The painting is a celebration of the subcontinent's diversity brought together in harmony, many rivers flowing into a single sea. It is sometimes read as an early image of an Indian nation.

=== there-comes-papa
src: 0ee0dd98161c
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I know this pointing. The artist's daughter Mahaprabha stands holding her infant son and points toward the unseen father arriving at the left, and a small dog looks the same way. I know the doorway everyone turns to. I know the waiting that is nearly joy. I know the arrival we never get to see.

Painted with European realism and set in an elegant Kerala interior, it was shown at the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago in 1893. Critics have linked its absent father to the matrilineal customs of Kerala's Nair community. Who is missing from the pictures of my own family? Sometimes the one we cannot see is the one the whole room turns toward.

=== jatayu-vadham
src: f7617a0a43f2
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A scene from the *Ramayana*, told in storm and feather: the demon king Ravana, carrying off Sita in his flying chariot, slashes the wing of the brave vulture-king Jatayu, who tried to rescue her. Sita recoils in horror. Ravi Varma staged the myth like a Western history painting, full of movement, theatrical gesture and dramatic sky, the clouds heaving like a sea about to break.

=== shakuntala
src: 5aa821ce69b9
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Shakuntala, heroine of Kalidasa's great Sanskrit play, pretends to pull a thorn from her foot so that she can look back for a last glimpse of King Dushyanta, as a flower turns after the departing sun, while her friends tease her. The twist of her body is a door left open, and it invites us into the story. Through prints, the image travelled like seed on the wind until it became one of the most familiar in India.

=== hamsa-damayanti
src: 783d2624c68a
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Princess Damayanti leans against a pillar by a palace pool, still as the water beside her, listening to a golden swan that tells her of the virtues of King Nala. The story comes from the *Mahabharata*. Her pensive, graceful pose and elegant sari made this one of Ravi Varma's most popular images of ideal Indian womanhood, reproduced endlessly in calendars and prints, multiplied like reflections on a rippling pool.

=== victory-of-meghanada
src: b800cbfecc15
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Another *Ramayana* scene. Ravana's son Meghanada (Indrajit), having defeated the god Indra, presents the spoils of heaven to his father's court, including captive celestial women, as if the sky itself had been emptied into a single hall. The large, crowded composition, painted in the artist's last years, shows his ambition to create an Indian equivalent of European grand history painting, a monsoon of figures gathered into one frame.

=== casa-vicens
src: ff10e8378e4a
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Every architect has a first declaration, and this was Gaudí's: his first major commission, built as a summer house for a tile manufacturer, and the building that announced his style. Look at the Mudéjar-inflected brickwork studded with green-and-white ceramic tiles shaped like marigolds, the flower that grew wild on the site. Iron palm-frond railings, cast from real leaves, guard the garden gate. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2005 and opened to the public as a museum in 2017. Begin with what already grows where you build, and the ground will lend you its grammar.

=== palau-guell
src: 23886509ca16
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A townhouse built just off Barcelona's Rambla for his lifelong patron, the industrialist Eusebi Güell. The problem was a dark, narrow site. Behind a plain stone facade, Gaudí stacked parabolic arches to bring light into it, and on the roof he set twenty tiled chimneys and ventilation cones, each a distinct sculptural experiment in trencadís, mosaic made of broken tile. It marks his shift from historical revival styles toward a structural language of his own. That is how an architecture becomes yours: not by borrowing a style, but by solving a problem until the solution speaks.

=== park-guell
src: 8fef451f9378
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Some failures become gifts. Eusebi Güell commissioned it as an English-style garden city of sixty houses, of which only two were ever built; the failed development became a public park instead. Its centrepiece is a Doric-columned market hall topped by a serpentine bench encrusted with shattered tile and glass, and on the entrance stairway sits a mosaic salamander that became an emblem of the city. Gaudí lived in one of its show houses for nearly twenty years. The plan that fails may still leave a threshold open to everyone.

=== casa-batllo
src: 33bed392ad22
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
This is a radical remodelling of an existing apartment block, and its facade is a rippling field of blue-green mosaic and bone-shaped balconies that earned it the nickname Casa dels Ossos, "House of Bones." The tiled, arched roofline is usually read as a dragon's back, tied to the legend of St George, patron saint of Catalonia. Step inside and almost no straight line survives; even the light-wells are tiled in shades of blue graded from dark at the top to pale at the bottom, to spread daylight evenly. Here ornament does a job: beauty and use share one architecture, and neither has to apologise to the other.
