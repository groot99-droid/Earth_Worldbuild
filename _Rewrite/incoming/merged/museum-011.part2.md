Michelangelo Merisi, called Caravaggio (1571 to 1610). An Italian painter. Around 1600 he changed European painting. Harsh, theatrical light. Street-level realism. His life was turbulent, and it ended at 38. Within a generation his influence had spread across Europe.

Born in Milan in September 1571. The name came from the nearby town his family came from. Trained in Milan with Simone Peterzano. In Rome about 1592.

Cardinal Francesco Maria del Monte took him into his household. His first public commission: two large paintings of St Matthew for the Contarelli Chapel in San Luigi dei Francesi (1599 to 1600). A sensation. Several later altarpieces were rejected as indecorous.

Police records. Brawls. Assaults. Insults. Then, in 1606, he killed a man, Ranuccio Tomassoni. He fled Rome under sentence of death. Naples. Malta. Sicily.

He died in July 1610 at Porto Ercole in Tuscany. On his way to Rome. Hoping for a papal pardon. It did not reach him in time.

He painted straight onto the canvas, apparently without preparatory drawings. Live models. One strong light. Behind them, dark and bare (tenebrism). The dark always waiting at the edge.

=== people--nur-jahan--1
src: be4731dea3ec
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Nur Jahan (1577 to 1645) was a Mughal empress, wife of the emperor Jahangir, who held great political power at court from about 1611 to 1627.

She was born Mehr-un-Nisa in 1577, daughter of Mirza Ghiyas Beg, a Persian in the service of the Mughal court.

Her first husband, Sher Afghan, died in 1607. In 1611 she married Jahangir and was given the title Nur Mahal, and in 1616 Nur Jahan, "Light of the World," a name like dawn over a city.

As Jahangir's health waned she took a growing share of government. Coins were struck in her name, and imperial orders bore her seal.

Her father became chief minister and her brother Asaf Khan a leading noble, so that her family held the key posts.

She commissioned her father's tomb at Agra (1622 to 1628), an early Mughal building faced in marble with inlaid stone.
