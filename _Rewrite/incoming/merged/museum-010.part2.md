Michelangelo Buonarroti (6 March 1475 to 18 February 1564) was an Italian sculptor, painter, architect and poet. His Pietà, David and Sistine Chapel paintings defined the High Renaissance, and he was the first Western artist to have a biography published in his lifetime.

He was born at Caprese in Tuscany and grew up in Florence. At 13 he was apprenticed to Domenico Ghirlandaio, then went to the sculpture garden of Lorenzo de' Medici, where he studied ancient statues, stone that remembered older hands.

The Pietà (1498 to 1499) made his name in Rome. In Florence he carved the David (1501 to 1504).

In 1505 Pope Julius II called him to Rome to design a colossal tomb, then turned him aside to paint the Sistine Chapel ceiling (1508 to 1512). The unfinished tomb haunted him for decades, a shadow that followed him from year to year.

In 1534 he settled for good in Rome and painted The Last Judgment (1536 to 1541). In 1546 he became chief architect of St Peter's Basilica, and his dome became a model for later domes.

He wrote hundreds of poems, many addressed to Tommaso de' Cavalieri and Vittoria Colonna. He died in Rome, and his body was secretly carried to Florence for burial in Santa Croce.

=== people--mirabai--1
src: cdf14650255d
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Mirabai (traditionally about 1498 to about 1546) was a Rajput princess of Rajasthan and one of the best-known poet-saints of the Bhakti movement, devoted to Krishna. Almost nothing of her life is documented, and the surviving poems were written down long after her time, like a song heard across a valley after the singer has gone.

By tradition she was born about 1498 at Kudki in Rajasthan, into the Rathore Rajput family of Merta, and died about 1546 or 1547. Both dates come from later tradition.

Tradition says she married Bhoj Raj, heir of Mewar, in 1516, and that he died about 1521 of battle wounds.

Her poems (padas) are in Rajasthani and related Hindi, and they speak to Krishna in the language of love and separation.

Tradition credits her with a very great number of poems. Scholars think only a few hundred may be authentic, and only two are known from manuscripts older than the early eighteenth century.

Stories that her in-laws tried to poison her, and that she vanished into an image of Krishna, are hagiographic legends. The earliest records, from the seventeenth century, do not mention her marriage or in-laws.
