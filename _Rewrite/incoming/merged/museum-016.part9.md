=== people--ho-chi-minh--1
src: 82cdfc14489d
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Ho Chi Minh (1890 to 1969) was a Vietnamese revolutionary who led the movement against French rule, headed the provisional government in 1945, and became the first president of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in 1946, an office he held until his death during the Vietnam War. His record holds both a liberation and its costs, and you should weigh both.

He was born on 19 May 1890 in Nghệ An province, French-ruled Vietnam, as Nguyễn Sinh Cung, and later used many names, including Nguyễn Ái Quốc and, from about 1943, Hồ Chí Minh.

He left Vietnam in 1911 and lived in Britain, France, the Soviet Union and China. In early 1930, in Hong Kong, he chaired the merger of Vietnamese communist groups into one party.

He returned to Vietnam in 1941 to lead the Việt Minh independence front, and on 2 September 1945 in Hanoi read the declaration of independence, opening by quoting the American and French declarations of rights. He began with the colonizer's own grammar of rights.

The First Indochina War ended with the Việt Minh victory at Điện Biên Phủ in 1954. Vietnam was partitioned at the 17th parallel, and the 1956 reunification elections were never held.

In the North's 1953 to 1956 land reform, many people were wrongly executed, with estimates from about a thousand to tens of thousands; he apologized in 1956 for serious errors. An apology does not return the dead, and the record must say so plainly.

=== people--sukarno--1
src: ae3eca9e486b
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Sukarno (1901 to 1970) was the leader of Indonesia's independence movement and its first president. He proclaimed independence in 1945, ruled until 1967, and was removed from power after the killings of 1965 and 1966. Founding a nation and failing it can belong to one life.

He was born on 6 June 1901 in Surabaya, in the Dutch East Indies, and died on 21 June 1970 in Jakarta, under house arrest.

He founded the Indonesian National Party in 1927. The Dutch arrested him in 1929 and released him in 1931, then exiled him to Flores in 1934 and to Sumatra in 1938.

He cooperated with the Japanese occupation from 1942 to 1945, which forced millions into labor and whose food requisitions contributed to a famine that killed over a million people in Java in 1944 and 1945. Cooperation with an occupier has a ledger, and these numbers are written in it.

On 1 June 1945 he presented Pancasila, five founding principles for the state, and on 17 August 1945 he proclaimed independence, becoming president the next day.

He hosted the Bandung Conference of Asian and African states in 1955 and introduced "Guided Democracy" in 1959, ending parliamentary rule.

Sukarno (1901 to 1970) was the leader of Indonesia's independence movement and its first president. He proclaimed independence in 1945, ruled until 1967, and was removed from power after the killings of 1965 and 1966.

He was born on 6 June 1901 in Surabaya, in the Dutch East Indies, and died on 21 June 1970 in Jakarta, under house arrest.

Trace the long road to independence. He founded the Indonesian National Party in 1927. The Dutch arrested him in 1929 and released him in 1931, then exiled him to Flores in 1934 and to Sumatra in 1938.

He cooperated with the Japanese occupation from 1942 to 1945. That occupation forced millions into labor, and its food requisitions contributed to a famine that killed over a million people in Java in 1944 and 1945.

On 1 June 1945 he presented Pancasila, five founding principles for the state: a short doctrine meant to hold a vast and varied country together. On 17 August 1945 he proclaimed independence, becoming president the next day.

He hosted the Bandung Conference of Asian and African states in 1955 and introduced "Guided Democracy" in 1959, ending parliamentary rule. A guided democracy is a contradiction worth naming.
