=== pungak-naesan-chongram
src: 123bf9dff3e9
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Here is another vision of the Diamond Mountains, this time in autumn, when they are called Pungak, "maple mountain", as though the season itself had renamed them. Temples and hermitages are tucked among the crowded peaks like pearls caught in folds of stone, and touches of red foliage glow among them like embers. Jeong Seon travelled to Mount Geumgang several times from 1711, and he painted it throughout his life, returning to it as a river returns to the sea.

=== bakyeon-falls
src: 6fd8c179d866
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Near Kaesong a waterfall, counted among the "three wonders" of that old capital, crashes down a sheer cliff into a deep pool like a white dragon diving for the dark. Far below, tiny visitors watch. Jeong Seon exaggerated the height of the falls and darkened the cliffs with heavy ink, so that the white water seems to roar out of the picture as thunder rolls out of a gorge.

=== inwang-jesaekdo
src: 4fad92749d28
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This is Jeong Seon's masterpiece, painted when he was 75. The granite peaks of Mount Inwang, behind the royal palace in Seoul, rise dark and wet after a summer rain, gleaming like the flanks of a great beast new from the river, while mist drifts through the pine woods below. The rock faces are built with broad, heavy sweeps of ink, a daring technique for its time. According to tradition, he painted it hoping for the recovery of his dying friend, the poet Yi Byeong-yeon, as if clearing skies could be wished upon a failing life. It entered the national collection in 2021 as part of the Lee Kun-hee donation.

=== cat-on-an-autumn-day
src: 9b06cae59bdc
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Not all of Jeong Seon's work is grand landscape; not every song he sang was of mountains. Here a cat sits among autumn flowers, alert and relaxed at once, like a calm pond that holds the whole sky. Small paintings of animals, insects and plants were popular with Korean collectors. This one shows his gentle observation of everyday nature, the eye that measured peaks now resting on a single cat.

=== nude-maja
src: 04d7c4582a43
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A young woman reclines on green cushions, arms behind her head, and looks straight at the viewer, needing neither shame nor a myth to hide behind, plain as daylight on water. It is one of the earliest Western paintings of a real nude woman who is not a goddess. It was probably made for the royal favourite Manuel Godoy, who also owned its clothed pendant, *La maja vestida*, the pair like day and its shadow. In 1815 the Inquisition summoned Goya to explain the "obscene" picture.

=== sleep-of-reason
src: ead9f5824e52
mode: horror-prose
--- description
A man at a desk. Asleep. Slumped. Perhaps Goya himself. Behind him the darkness moves. Owls. Bats. A lynx, staring. They swarm out of the dark and he does not wake. The title is inscribed on the desk. It comes from *Los Caprichos*, 80 satirical prints attacking superstition, ignorance and the abuses of Spanish society. The image has become an emblem of the Enlightenment. And of its fears.

The darkness behind him is still full.

=== family-of-charles-iv
src: 63b54998699d
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
They stand in a row in glittering silks and sashes, the Spanish royal family, and I look for the one who is not glittering. The queen, Maria Luisa, dominates the centre. The king looks vacant. The honesty of it has sometimes been read as satire, and I wonder when honesty began to look like mockery. Then I find him: in a nod to Velázquez's *Las Meninas*, Goya painted himself in the shadows at the left, working at a large canvas. I know that place. I have stood at the edge of rooms not quite mine. Sometimes the truest witness is the one in the dark.

=== second-of-may
src: 72417c81ff5f
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Some paintings refuse to give you a hero, and that refusal is the lesson. The people of Madrid rise up against Napoleon's troops, attacking the Egyptian Mameluke cavalry with knives in a tangle of horses, bodies and blood. It was painted after the French were expelled, yet it shows no heroes, only the chaos and fury of street fighting. Hold that plainly: war seen up close has no centre to stand in. Memory owes the dead the truth of the tangle.

=== hokusai-manga
src: 701639baeb9c
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A teacher's notebook can outgrow the classroom. Here are fifteen volumes of sketches, published from 1814 onwards, the last appearing long after Hokusai's death. They show thousands of subjects: people at work and play, animals, plants, gods, ghosts, landscapes and comic figures, all drawn with wit and economy. Think of it as a grammar of seeing, set down one line at a time. It was meant as a model book for students, and the *Manga* became a best-seller. It was also one of the first Japanese books to excite European artists in the 1850s–60s. Make a thing to teach, and it may teach far beyond your reach.

=== fine-wind-clear-morning
src: 8b6104dbaf40
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Mount Fuji rises alone, turned red by early morning light in late summer, like an ember set against a blue sky, and that sky is filled with rows of mackerel clouds, scaled like a great fish swimming overhead. Almost nothing else is shown: a few trees at its base, small as moss at a giant's heel. That utter simplicity makes it one of the boldest images of the series, and an icon of the sacred mountain.

=== thunderstorm-beneath-the-summit
src: be2d19140341
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This is the companion to *Red Fuji*. The mountain's peak rises serenely into clear sky, untroubled as a sleeping king, while far below, on its dark slopes, a zigzag of lightning flashes through the storm like a crack running through black glass. The contrast between the calm summit and the violent weather beneath suggests the mountain's permanence above the storms of the world: the thunder passes, the peak remains.
