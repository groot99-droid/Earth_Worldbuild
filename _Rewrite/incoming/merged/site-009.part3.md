=== culture/aboriginal-australians
title: Aboriginal Australians
src: c61db9927a84
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Aboriginal Australians are the Indigenous peoples of the Australian mainland, whose ancestors arrived at least 50,000 years ago. Their cultures are among the oldest continuous living traditions, a fire carried unbroken from hand to hand across the ages.
--- fact 1
Before European colonization in 1788, more than 250 languages were spoken across the land, each a voice in a great chorus.
--- fact 2
Knowledge travelled through oral traditions, songlines and ceremony, passed along like water in a line of cupped hands.
--- fact 3
Groups tended the landscape with fire and sophisticated ecological knowledge, reading the land as others read a book.
--- fact 4
The rock art of Australia includes some of the oldest known paintings, images that have kept watch on the stone through long ages.
--- context
Their story walks beside the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]] and the lands of [[region/oceania|Oceania]]. Aboriginal societies show that complex culture does not require farming or cities; it can flower without the plough and without the wall.
--- observer
I think of a coastline flooded at the end of the last ice age. I think of the accounts of it, handed from voice to voice. I think of how long a telling can last: orally preserved knowledge like this appears to have lasted for millennia. What is ink, beside a voice that will not let go? Memory-outsourcing need not use writing to be durable.
--- question 1
How far back can oral traditions carry an event and keep it true?

=== culture/achaemenid-persians
title: Achaemenid Persians
src: dabb3982b492
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Achaemenid Persians built the largest empire the world had yet seen, founded by Cyrus the Great about 550 BCE and standing until Alexander's conquest in 330 BCE, a realm that spread like dawn light across the old lands.
--- fact 1
The empire stretched from Egypt and Anatolia to the Indus valley, a single mantle thrown across many lands.
--- fact 2
It was divided into provinces called satrapies, and the Royal Road bound them together like a thread through beads.
--- fact 3
The Cyrus Cylinder is often cited as a record of an imperial policy of tolerance toward local customs, words that still echo in the halls of memory.
--- fact 4
Zoroastrianism held influence at the Persian court, like a flame kept near the throne.
--- context
The empire's long day ended with [[event/alexander-s-conquests|Alexander's Conquests]]. Its story is kept beside that of the [[region/iranian-plateau|Iranian Plateau]].
--- observer
Here is a lesson in the architecture of rule. The Observer notes an administrative style that governed diverse peoples by preserving their local institutions rather than tearing them down. Watch for it; the approach reappears repeatedly in stable empires. Leave people their own house, and they may help keep your roof standing.
--- question 1
When the proclamations are set aside, how tolerant was the empire in practice?

=== culture/al-andalus
title: Al-Andalus
src: 38f6d4af2b6e
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Al-Andalus was the Muslim-ruled part of the Iberian Peninsula from 711 to 1492, a region of Muslim, Christian and Jewish communities, three streams feeding one garden, that contributed to science, architecture and literature.
--- fact 1
In 711 Muslim forces crossed from North Africa into Iberia, over the narrow water between two continents.
--- fact 2
From 756 an Umayyad emirate, and later a caliphate, ruled from Cordoba.
--- fact 3
Cordoba became a great lamp of learning, and its caliphal library is reputed to have held hundreds of thousands of volumes.
--- fact 4
The Alhambra in Granada rose mainly in the 13th and 14th centuries, like a dream set down in stone.
--- fact 5
Granada, the last Muslim kingdom, fell to Castile and Aragon in January 1492, and the long sunset of Al-Andalus was done.
--- context
Al-Andalus grew from the root of the [[culture/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad Caliphate]], and through it the knowledge of the [[event/translation-movement-and-the-house-of-wisdom|Translation Movement and the House of Wisdom]] flowed like a river into Latin Europe. Its end in 1492 coincided with the start of the [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]], one door closing as another swung open. In the codex it stands in the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]] and the region of the [[region/mediterranean-basin|Mediterranean Basin]], and its threads are [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]], [[theme/science|Science]] and [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
I count what a single year held. I count Granada falling. I count the expulsion of Jews from Spain. I count Columbus setting out. How can one year carry so much ending and so much beginning? A period of pluralism closed as another began that would reshape the planet. Some doors open only as others shut.
--- question 1
How tolerant was Al-Andalus in practice, beneath the shine of its learning?

=== culture/ancient-egyptians
title: Ancient Egyptians
src: 18fabd810924
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The ancient Egyptians built a state along the Nile that endured, with interruptions, for about three thousand years, as steady as the river's own return, until the Roman annexation in 30 BCE.
--- fact 1
Their history is divided into kingdoms, Old, Middle and New, with intermediate periods lying between them like dim seasons between floods.
--- fact 2
They raised pyramids and temples against the sky and developed hieroglyphic writing, words drawn as images.
--- fact 3
Their religion looked toward the afterlife as a traveller looks toward the far shore, and the bodies of the elite were mummified.
--- fact 4
The last pharaoh of the Ptolemaic dynasty was Cleopatra VII, the final light of a long line.
--- context
Their tale is bound to the [[event/unification-of-egypt|Unification of Egypt]], to the [[place/giza-necropolis|Giza Necropolis]], and to the lives of [[person/hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]] and [[person/akhenaten|Akhenaten]].
--- observer
I stand among the tombs and understand this much: here was an extraordinary investment in the dead. I see the stone. I see the craft. I see a whole culture spending lavishly on a life no one returns to describe. Would I build so much for those I have lost? Love, too, can be measured in stone. [[observer-note/why-the-species-buries-its-dead|Why the Species Buries Its Dead]] asks the older question.
--- question 1
How many hands were needed to raise the pyramids, and how were those workers organized?

=== culture/ancient-greeks
title: Ancient Greeks
src: c10d656d11cb
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The ancient Greeks were a constellation of city-states around the Aegean and beyond, and their politics, philosophy and art shaped Mediterranean and later world culture as a spring shapes the valley below it.
--- fact 1
They forged citizen-based political systems, among them Athenian democracy, kindled from about 508 BCE.
--- fact 2
Among their philosophers and scientists walked Socrates, Plato and [[person/aristotle|Aristotle]], three lamps in one window.
--- fact 3
City-states such as Athens and Sparta fought frequently, like rival storms over one sea, and among their wars was the Peloponnesian War (431 to 404 BCE).
--- fact 4
After [[person/alexander-the-great|Alexander the Great]] came the Hellenistic period, a long afterglow.
--- context
Their tale runs through [[place/athens|Athens]] and out along the road of [[event/alexander-s-conquests|Alexander's Conquests]]. In the codex the Greeks belong to [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]].
--- observer
What happens when a people decides that disagreement is not a threat but a practice? The Observer notes a culture that turned argument into an institution: public debate in assemblies, in courts and in schools. Argument, given a room, becomes architecture.
--- question 1
Given that women, enslaved people and foreigners were shut out, how democratic was Athenian democracy?
