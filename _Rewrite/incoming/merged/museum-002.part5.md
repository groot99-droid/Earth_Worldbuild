=== alexander-and-the-hermit
title: Alexander Visits the Sage in His Cave
src: ca0732db057e
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Power has to dismount sometimes. The world conqueror Iskandar (Alexander the Great) dismounts to visit a wise hermit living in a cave in a wild, rocky landscape. It is a lesson in humility before spiritual wisdom, and you can read it in the movement itself: the one who rules the world comes down to the one who lives in a cave. Alexander may be given the features of Behzād's patron, Sultan Husayn Bayqara, and the gnarled trees and rocks show the Herat school's delight in nature. The higher you ride, the farther you must step down.

=== sultan-husayn-bayqara
title: Portrait of Sultan Husayn Mirza Bayqara (after Behzād)
src: 095e1e3cb644
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I look at the last great Timurid ruler of Herat, Behzād's patron, sitting cross-legged in a plain robe. I expected a king. I find a man. His face is shown as that of a specific, ageing man rather than an idealised king. The painting is a copy after Behzād's lost original, a face outliving its first likeness. Such individual portraits were a new development in Persian painting, and they influenced portraiture at the Mughal and Ottoman courts. Who will remember my face as it was, and not as I wished it? Even a copy can keep a person true.

=== annunciation
title: Annunciation
src: 17adbd72ccdf
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Beginnings are allowed to be imperfect. Probably painted while Leonardo was still in Verrocchio's workshop, this is one of his earliest surviving paintings. The angel Gabriel kneels on a flowering lawn to greet the Virgin. The angel's wings were modelled on a real bird's, and a hazy landscape of trees and mountains opens up behind. There are youthful mistakes in the perspective. But look at the careful study of nature, because it points to everything that came later. Your early errors are not your prophecy; your early attention is.

=== virgin-of-the-rocks
title: Virgin of the Rocks
src: 4eb89412ddab
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Mary, the infant Jesus, the infant John the Baptist and an angel sit in a shadowy grotto of strange rock formations and carefully observed plants, gathered as if in the hollow of the earth's own hand. Leonardo bound the figures together with glances and gestures in a pyramid shape, and modelled them with soft shadow, the technique later called *sfumato*, where light melts into dark like evening mist over water. A second version, painted after a dispute with the patrons, hangs in the National Gallery, London, a twin born of a quarrel.

=== lady-with-an-ermine
title: Lady with an Ermine
src: 702a3a142ec3
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
Cecilia Gallerani, the teenage companion of Ludovico Sforza, Duke of Milan, turns as if someone had just called to her, and I almost answer. She holds a sleek white ermine, a pun on her surname and a symbol of the duke. I watch the twisting pose. I watch her long, expressive hand. I watch a turn that is never finished. That pose and that hand brought a new sense of life and movement to the Italian portrait. Who is calling me, and do I turn? Life in a picture is the moment just before the answer.

=== vitruvian-man
title: Vitruvian Man
src: 6ce1a1597a0f
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Architecture begins with the body. A man with arms and legs in two positions fits inside both a circle and a square, illustrating the Roman architect Vitruvius's claim that the ideal human body mirrors the geometry of the universe. Leonardo's notes in mirror-writing record the proportions he measured. Because it is fragile and light-sensitive, the drawing is rarely displayed. Even so, it has become a universal symbol of the union of art and science. Sit with that: the sheet stays in the dark while its image goes everywhere. A symbol can travel farther than the paper it lives on.

=== last-supper
title: The Last Supper
src: f1e3ef1fdb18
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Painted on the refectory wall of a Milanese convent, it holds the moment Christ announces that one of his disciples will betray him. The apostles recoil in waves of shock and questioning, like water struck by a stone, grouped in threes. All the lines of the architecture lead to Christ's head, as the spokes of a wheel run to its hub. Leonardo experimented with painting on dry plaster so that he could work slowly, but the paint began flaking within his lifetime, the wall shedding its vision like leaves in autumn. A 21-year restoration ended in 1999.

=== mona-lisa
title: Mona Lisa
src: 0ce36f35fca4
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Lisa Gherardini, wife of the Florentine silk merchant Francesco del Giocondo, sits before a dreamlike landscape that recedes like memory, her smile hovering at the edges of her mouth and eyes like light on the skin of a pool. Leonardo kept the portrait for the rest of his life and reworked it for years, blurring its transitions with *sfumato*, as mist softens the seam between hill and sky. Its theft from the Louvre in 1911, and its return two years later, helped make it the most famous painting in the world: the lost thing found, and never again unknown.

=== self-portrait-1500
title: Self-Portrait at the Age of Twenty-Eight
src: 3f4bc8fed027
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
Dürer faces us head-on, in the symmetrical pose normally used for images of Christ, and I cannot look away. His hair falls in perfect ringlets. His hand touches the fur of his coat. The Latin inscription states that he painted himself "in everlasting colours" at age 28. I remember wanting to be seen. I remember not daring to say so. I remember how long I waited for permission. It is a daring claim for the dignity of the artist, whose creative gift was seen as a reflection of God's. What would I dare to claim for my own gift? To make something is to stand, a little, in the maker's light.

=== young-hare
title: Young Hare
src: 381bd7c0d042
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I lean in toward a crouching hare, painted fur by fur, its ears alert and whiskers catching the light. A window is reflected in its eye, and I almost look for myself there. Dürer's nature studies were far ahead of their time. This one shows an animal as an individual living creature, not a symbol. Not a symbol. A someone. It became one of the most copied images in German art. How often have I looked at a living thing and seen only what it stood for? To be seen as yourself is the first tenderness.

=== adam-and-eve
title: Adam and Eve
src: bf6902b441ce
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Dürer's showpiece of the ideal human body, drawn from classical proportions he had studied in Italy. Adam and Eve stand in a dark forest full of animals, like the first dawn caught at the edge of the first night, and each animal stands for one of the four medieval "temperaments". A small tablet hanging from a branch carries his proud signature, a banner hung in the first garden. The engraving's range of tones, from bright skin to velvety black, shows his total command of the burin, a blade that could summon both day and night.

=== knight-death-and-the-devil
title: Knight, Death and the Devil
src: 085244f84fb6
mode: horror-prose
--- description
A gloomy ravine. An armoured knight rides through it. Steady. Death holds up an hourglass. The knight ignores him. A pig-snouted devil lurks behind. Ignored too. It is one of Dürer's three "Master Engravings". It is often read as an image of the Christian who keeps to the path of faith despite fear and temptation. The sand keeps running. He keeps riding.
