=== the-sprinkler-sprinkled
title: L'Arroseur arrosé (The Sprinkler Sprinkled)
src: 55db6c0dc1f9
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A boy creeps up on a gardener watering a lawn, soft-footed as a fox at the edge of a field. He steps on the hose and the flow dies, like a stream stopped by a fallen stone; the gardener peers into the nozzle; the boy lifts his foot, and the water leaps out and soaks him. It was staged and acted rather than simply observed, a tale told rather than a moment caught, and it is generally regarded as cinema's first scripted comedy.

=== demolition-of-a-wall
title: Démolition d'un mur (Demolition of a Wall)
src: 0235e775ec19
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Workers topple a brick wall with picks and a rope, and it comes down as a cliff comes down into the sea: a mundane demonstration of the new camera's ability to record real motion. But the Lumières discovered that running the film backwards made the wall reassemble itself, rising out of its own rubble as a wave draws back up the sand. It was one of the medium's first accidental special effects, and an early hint of cinema's power to reverse and manipulate time, to make the river run uphill.

=== pallas-athene
title: Pallas Athene
src: 1ff011f1e975
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
The Greek goddess of wisdom stares out from a golden helmet and breastplate, her gaze level as a blade laid flat, and in her hand she holds a tiny figure of Truth, a nude woman with a mirror. It was painted in the year of the first Secession exhibition, and it announces, like a horn at a gate, Klimt's rebellion against academic art and the start of his use of gold, the first vein struck in the rock. Athena was the Secession's patron goddess.

=== judith-i
title: Judith I
src: de13eb234aee
mode: horror-prose
--- description
The biblical heroine who beheaded Holofernes. Here, a modern Viennese *femme fatale*. Eyes half closed. Lips parted. And at the edge of the frame, barely visible, the severed head. A heavy gold collar locks around her throat. A stylised golden background frames her face. Gold above. Gold behind. The head below. The painting shocked viewers with its fusion of violence and sensuality. Violence. Sensuality. One face.

=== beethoven-frieze
title: Beethoven Frieze (installation view)
src: af0bfcb4e4fc
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Made for the 1902 Secession exhibition in honour of Beethoven, this frieze runs around three walls of a room, high above the viewer like a band of cloud around a mountain. It interprets the Ninth Symphony as humanity's search for happiness, a pilgrimage told in paint: it begins with floating figures and a knight in golden armour, passes the "Hostile Forces" as a traveller passes a dark ravine, and ends in the "Kiss to the whole world" from Schiller's *Ode to Joy*. The end wall, seen here, holds the Hostile Forces: the ape-like giant Typhoeus, surrounded by his daughters, the Gorgons, and beside them the figures of Sickness, Madness and Death, gathered like crows on a ridge. Meant to be temporary, a thing of one season, the frieze was saved, and it is now on permanent display.

=== adele-bloch-bauer-i
title: Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I
src: 715bb6392777
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Think about what a title holds. The Viennese salon hostess Adele Bloch-Bauer, her face and hands painted realistically, seems almost dissolved in a shimmering field of gold ornament, eyes and spirals. Seized by the Nazis, the painting hung for decades in the Belvedere as "The Woman in Gold", her name set aside for a description. In 2006 her niece Maria Altmann won its return after a long legal battle, a story told in the 2015 film *Woman in Gold*. That is the ledger this painting keeps: a seizure, the long decades, a return. A portrait is also a record of who was owed.

=== the-kiss
title: The Kiss
src: f3d6128fed25
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I stand before a couple who embrace on the edge of a flowering meadow, wrapped together in a single golden cloak. I see his robe, patterned with black and white rectangles. I see hers, with colourful circles and flowers. I see how little of them is flesh: only their faces, their hands, her feet. How much of any embrace is the cloak we weave around it? It is the high point of Klimt's "golden period", and the Belvedere bought it even before it was finished. I understand that. Some loves are claimed before they are complete.

=== death-and-life
title: Death and Life
src: 78d6470e5448
mode: horror-prose
--- description
On the left, Death. A skeleton in a robe of crosses. Leering. On the right, a tangle of sleeping figures: babies, lovers, mothers, an old woman, woven into a colourful blanket of patterns. Close. Eyes shut. Breathing. Klimt reworked the painting after it won first prize in Rome in 1911. The gold background went. Blue-grey came in its place. The sleepers seem unaware of Death's presence. Look away. Look back. Still there.

=== primordial-chaos-16
title: Primordial Chaos, No. 16 (Group I, WU/Rose Series)
src: dae5b840a9ff
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This comes from the very first group of her *Paintings for the Temple*. Coiling spirals of green and yellow unwind across a deep blue ground, as young fern unfurls in a spring that has not yet learned its own name. They represent the origins of the world and the division of unity into opposites: male and female, spirit and matter, one light breaking into two. Painted in 1906, these small works are among the earliest abstract paintings in Western art, seeds from before the forest.

=== the-ten-largest-adulthood
title: The Ten Largest, No. 7, Adulthood (Group IV)
src: 4c0dd8e71883
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This is one of ten huge paintings describing the stages of human life, from childhood to old age, as a river is told from spring to delta. Af Klint painted them in a few months, on paper laid on the floor like fields laid open to the sky. Blossoming flower-like forms, spirals and Roman numerals drift over a lilac ground, petals on slow water. They seem to celebrate growth, love and maturity, the full noon of a life. The series was the centrepiece of the record-breaking Guggenheim exhibition of 2018–2019.

=== evolution-13
title: Evolution, No. 13 (Group VI)
src: 2dc531f1ce47
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A great circle, ringed by a serpent as a world is ringed by its sea, holds a pink, flower-like form divided into quarters and scattered with geometric signs: triangles, a crescent, black bars and coloured ovals, like marks cut on a shield. Reddish human figures twist toward it from either side, as plants lean toward the sun. The series traces humanity's evolution through the meeting of opposites, such as light and dark or male and female. Shortly afterwards af Klint stopped painting the temple works for four years, a long winter in the work, partly discouraged by Rudolf Steiner's lukewarm response.
