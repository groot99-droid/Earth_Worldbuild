=== place/beringia
src: 65dd43a00e95
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Beringia was the land region, including a broad land bridge, that joined northeastern Siberia and Alaska when sea levels were lower during the last ice age, a road laid bare by the withdrawn sea, and that later flooded to form the Bering Strait as the waters came home.
--- fact 1
Lower sea level during glacial periods laid bare the shallow floor of the Bering and Chukchi Seas, as if the ocean had drawn back its hem.
--- fact 2
At its widest, the exposed land ran more than a thousand kilometers from north to south, a whole country risen from the sea.
--- fact 3
Much of Beringia was cold, dry steppe-tundra, largely unglaciated, where mammoths, bison and horses found their grazing.
--- fact 4
Genetic evidence suggests that ancestors of Native Americans may have been isolated in Beringia for several thousand years before moving south, a people held at a threshold; this is the Beringian standstill hypothesis, still debated.
--- fact 5
Rising seas began to flood the land bridge around 11,000 years ago, and by roughly 10,000 to 9,000 years ago it lay wholly beneath the water.
--- context
Beringia, gate and refuge at once, is the corridor at the center of the [[event/peopling-of-the-americas|Peopling of the Americas]], and it shaped the movements of animals such as the [[species/woolly-mammoth|Woolly Mammoth]] during the [[event/last-glacial-maximum|Last Glacial Maximum]]. Its populations connect to those of [[culture/mal-ta-and-the-ancient-north-eurasians|Mal'ta and the Ancient North Eurasians]]. Beringia sits in the [[era/pleistocene-epoch|Pleistocene Epoch]] and the region [[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]], and its threads run through the themes of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I think of a homeland. I think of a highway. I think of a shelter. All three were one place, and it vanished beneath the sea. What the species calls the origin of the American peoples happened on ground that no one can now stand on. How do you visit the house you were born in when it lies under water? Some beginnings can only be remembered, never revisited.
--- question 1
How long did populations remain in Beringia, and how did they live on that vanished ground?

=== place/borobudur
src: c66be856c5bd
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Borobudur is a large Mahayana Buddhist monument in central Java, a mountain of stacked terraces raised around 800 CE under the Sailendra dynasty, and it is often described as the world's largest Buddhist temple.
--- fact 1
Its building is dated to roughly the late 8th and early 9th centuries, a labor spanning the turn of an age.
--- fact 2
The monument rises in nine stacked platforms and is adorned with 2,672 relief panels and, originally, 504 Buddha statues.
--- fact 3
After the shift of power in Java it was abandoned in the centuries that followed, and later vegetation and volcanic ash drew a green-grey veil over it.
--- fact 4
Hermann Cornelius, the agent of Thomas Stamford Raffles, cleared the site in 1814, and the monument saw the light again.
--- fact 5
It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1991, after major restoration had mended it.
--- context
Borobudur reflects the Buddhist world of [[culture/srivijaya|Srivijaya]] and the neighboring Khmer temple-building tradition at [[place/angkor|Angkor]]. It represents the teachings of [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]] as a path of ascent, a stair from the ground toward stillness. Borobudur sits in the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]] and the region [[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]], and its threads run through the themes of [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
I climb with the pilgrim. I climb past story after story in the relief panels. I climb until the stories stop, and the summit holds no images at all. What was I climbing toward, if not the quiet? The architecture is itself an argument about learning: from stories to silence. We begin with pictures and end with none.
--- question 1
Why was the monument abandoned and left to the ash?

=== place/bukhara
src: 3ccac95b1589
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Bukhara is a city in the Zarafshan oasis of Uzbekistan, green water in the dry lands: once a Sogdian trading city, then the capital of the Samanid dynasty, and for more than a thousand years a center of Islamic learning on the Silk Roads.
--- fact 1
Bukhara has been settled for a long time; the city celebrated 2,500 years in 1997 on a traditional dating, while archaeological evidence for its walled center is generally placed several centuries BCE, a deep root beneath the old walls.
--- fact 2
Bukhara was a Sogdian city when Arab armies took it in 709 to 710, and in 810 it saw the birth of the scholar Muhammad al-Bukhari, compiler of a major collection of hadith.
--- fact 3
Under the Samanid dynasty (819 to 999), Bukhara was a capital of learning and of Persian literature, a lamp of its age; the physician and philosopher Ibn Sina was born near it in 980, and the Samanid mausoleum was raised in the late 9th to 10th century.
--- fact 4
The Mongol army under Chinggis Khan took Bukhara in 1220; the city was burned, and many of its people were killed or enslaved, though their number is unknown.
--- fact 5
The Kalyan Minaret, built in 1127, still stands against the sky, and Bukhara was the capital of the Emirate of Bukhara (1785 to 1920), a Russian protectorate from 1868.
--- fact 6
The Red Army took Bukhara in 1920, and the citadel (Ark) was damaged in the assault.
--- fact 7
In 1993 UNESCO inscribed the Historic Centre of Bukhara.
--- context
Bukhara was a [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] city, paired in later history with [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] like twin stars, and it fell to the [[event/mongol-conquests|Mongol Conquests]] after coming under the world of the [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]]. It sits in [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]] and the region [[region/central-asian-oases|Central Asian Oases]], and its threads run through the themes of [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]], [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/science|Science]].
--- observer
I count the powers that took this city: Sogdian, Arab, Samanid, Mongol, Uzbek, Russian and Soviet. I count the times it was taken. I count the times it became a center of learning again, after most of them. What rebuilds first when a city falls? The pattern suggests to me that scholars, patrons and trade routes were rebuilt faster than the buildings. Its standing as a place of Islamic scholarship was a story-glue that outlasted each ruler. If the record shows long gaps in scholarly output after the sack of 1220, this reading weakens. Walls fall; a name for learning is harder to burn.
--- question 1
How large was Bukhara before and after the Mongol sack of 1220?
--- question 2
How did scholarship in Bukhara rise again after each conquest?
