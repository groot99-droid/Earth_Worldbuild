=== event/toba-eruption
title: Toba Eruption
src: 2658da376265
mode: horror-prose
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Toba eruption: about 74,000 years ago, in Sumatra. One of the largest volcanic eruptions of the past two million years. Its effects on human populations — still debated.
--- fact 1
The eruption carved the caldera now filled by Lake Toba, in northern Sumatra, Indonesia.
--- fact 2
It ejected on the order of 2,800 to 3,000 cubic kilometers of material. Ash fell across South Asia. And beyond.
--- fact 3
The Toba catastrophe hypothesis proposed a volcanic winter that reduced human populations to a small number of survivors.
--- fact 4
Archaeological sites in India, Jwalapuram among them, show human occupation continuing straight through the ash layer — a challenge to the hypothesis.
--- fact 5
Most researchers now judge a severe global population bottleneck unlikely.
--- context
The eruption falls within the period when [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]] were dispersing, near the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]]. It is often discussed alongside climate swings leading to the [[event/last-glacial-maximum|Last Glacial Maximum]]. The Toba Eruption sits in the [[era/pleistocene-epoch|Pleistocene Epoch]] and the region [[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]], and belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that people have long wanted a single catastrophe to explain a genetic feature of the species. The evidence so far suggests a population more resilient than the story requires.
--- question 1
How large was the climatic cooling, and how long did it last?

=== event/translation-movement-and-the-house-of-wisdom
title: Translation Movement and the House of Wisdom
src: f7fdf44d287b
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
From the mid-8th to the 10th century, scholars in Abbasid Baghdad gathered the words of distant tongues — Greek, Persian, Syriac, Indian — and turned them into Arabic, then carried the ideas further still.
--- fact 1
The caliphs sponsored translation and scholarship; the House of Wisdom in Baghdad stands as its best-known institution, though its exact nature remains debated.
--- fact 2
Among the translators was Hunayn ibn Ishaq, who worked on Greek medical and philosophical texts.
--- fact 3
Cheap [[technology/paper|Paper]] made the copying of books practical.
--- fact 4
Later, Latin translations of Arabic texts fed European scholarship in the 12th century.
--- context
The movement was a project of the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]] and enabled scholars such as [[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]]. It fed the later [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]].
--- observer
I see a society that could look at another people's knowledge and call it a resource, not a threat. I remember this: translation is a technology too — a way of lending memory across languages. What have we lost, in ages that chose the threat instead?
--- question 1
How large was the House of Wisdom in practice?

=== event/treaty-of-waitangi
title: Treaty of Waitangi
src: 45c61f9720f5
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Treaty of Waitangi, first signed on 6 February 1840, stands as the founding agreement between the British Crown and many Maori chiefs of Aotearoa New Zealand.
--- fact 1
The English and Maori texts differ over key words, especially about sovereignty and chieftainship.
--- fact 2
In 1840, hundreds of chiefs signed copies of the treaty.
--- fact 3
Conflict over land and authority followed, in the New Zealand Wars of the 1840s to 1870s.
--- fact 4
Created in 1975, the Waitangi Tribunal investigates breaches of the treaty.
--- context
The treaty concerned the [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]] of Aotearoa, who had settled the islands centuries before; see [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]]. Compare the colonial treaties of the [[event/scramble-for-africa|Scramble for Africa]].
--- observer
I read one agreement written in two languages, and I find two agreements instead. I ask what a signature means when the words beneath it do not match. More than 180 years later, its meaning is still argued. Translation, I have learned, is politics.
--- question 1
How do the two versions of the treaty differ in meaning?

=== event/triassic-jurassic-extinction
title: Triassic-Jurassic Extinction
src: 15c9d190a96a
mode: horror-prose
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Triassic-Jurassic extinction. About 201.4 million years ago. A large share of marine and land species: gone. Space cleared for the rise of the dinosaurs.
--- fact 1
An estimated 80 percent of species lost. Estimates vary.
--- fact 2
It coincides with massive volcanic activity tied to the breakup of Pangaea — the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province.
--- fact 3
Many large land reptile competitors vanished. Dinosaurs survived. And diversified; see [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]].
--- context
The extinction falls early in the [[era/mesozoic-era|Mesozoic Era]]. It lets dinosaurs dominate.
--- observer
The Observer notes that dinosaur dominance was itself the result of an extinction. Every dominant group in the record owes its opportunity to some disaster.
--- question 1
Why did dinosaurs survive when others did not?

=== event/tunguska-event
title: Tunguska Event
src: 62dc174e368a
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Tunguska event was an explosion in the atmosphere above the Podkamennaya Tunguska River in Siberia, on 30 June 1908, which flattened about 2,000 square kilometers of forest.
--- fact 1
The blast is estimated at roughly ten to fifteen megatons of TNT equivalent.
--- fact 2
It occurred several kilometers above the ground, and left no impact crater.
--- fact 3
About 80 million trees were knocked flat, over an area of about 2,000 square kilometers.
--- fact 4
In 1927, the first scientific expedition to the site was led by Leonid Kulik.
--- fact 5
The cause is generally thought to be the airburst of a stony asteroid, or a comet fragment, tens of meters across.
--- context
The Tunguska event is the largest impact event in recorded history, a small-scale relative of the [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]] and the [[event/theia-impact-and-the-formation-of-the-moon|Theia Impact and the Formation of the Moon]]. It happened in a region newly reachable by the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]]. The Tunguska Event sits within the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]] and the region [[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]], and belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and [[theme/science|Science]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that an explosion greater than any nuclear device then in existence occurred over an empty forest, noticed mostly by reindeer herders. Planetary hazards do not consult human population maps, and a few kilometers' difference in location would have changed the outcome.
--- question 1
Was the object an asteroid or a comet?

=== event/turkish-war-of-independence
title: Turkish War of Independence
src: 94dd17f6b339
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Turkish War of Independence (1919 to 1923) was fought by a nationalist movement under Mustafa Kemal against the Allied occupation of Anatolia and the invading armies of Greece and Armenia, ending the Ottoman sultanate and establishing the Republic of Turkey.
--- fact 1
After World War I, Allied forces occupied parts of the Ottoman Empire, while Greece and Armenia moved to claim territory.
--- fact 2
Mustafa Kemal, later known as Atatürk, organized a nationalist movement centred at Ankara. He convened congresses at Erzurum and Sivas, and a national parliament met in Ankara on 23 April 1920. Elected commander in chief, he built a new army from remaining Ottoman forces and irregular units.
--- fact 3
On 1 November 1922, the new parliament abolished the sultanate, ending about 623 years of Ottoman rule.
--- fact 4
The Treaty of Lausanne of 24 July 1923 recognized Turkey's borders and sovereignty, and a population exchange between Greece and Turkey followed. On 29 October 1923, the Republic of Turkey was proclaimed.
--- context
The war grew out of the defeat and breakup of the [[culture/ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]] after [[event/world-war-i|World War I]], and it took place in the same region as the earlier [[event/armenian-genocide|Armenian Genocide]]. It belongs to the wider postwar reordering that includes [[event/decolonization|Decolonization]] in later decades.
--- observer
The Observer notes that the same movement which fought off partition went on to end the dynasty in whose name the war began. An old legitimacy was set aside in three years, and a new one, built on a defined homeland, took its place.
--- question 1
How should the population exchange between Greece and Turkey be judged, as an event that displaced very large numbers of people?
--- question 2
How did the new republic's founders treat the memory of the Ottoman period?
