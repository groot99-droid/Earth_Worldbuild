=== source/african-and-portuguese-decolonization-dates-search-summaries
title: African and Portuguese Decolonization Dates (Search Summaries)
src: 72943b9894b1
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Ghana's independence on 6 March 1957 stands as the first in sub-Saharan Africa; 17 African countries gained independence in 1960; the Evian Accords fell on 18 March 1962, with Algerian independence following on 3 July 1962; and the Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974 led, by the end of 1975, to independence for Portugal's African colonies.
--- extra 1: Caveats
These figures rest only on web search result summaries consulted on 2026-09-25 (results listed CBC News, the African Studies Centre Leiden, Britannica, EBSCO Research Starters, Wikipedia and Portugal.com); none of the pages was opened directly. One listing gave 16 African countries in 1960, and most gave 17, so the count turns on how borderline cases are treated. The summary on the end of the Portuguese empire covered its African territories only, and did not include Macau or East Timor.

=== source/age-of-the-solar-system-and-earth-search-summaries
title: Age of the Solar System and Earth (Search Summaries)
src: 7de58c803d2f
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Calcium-aluminum-rich inclusions carry a date of about 4.567 billion years; Earth's own age is given as 4.54 plus or minus 0.05 billion years; and Clair Patterson's 1956 lead-isotope measurement put the age at about 4.55 billion years, drawn from the Canyon Diablo meteorite.
--- extra 1: Caveats
These figures rest only on web search result summaries consulted on 2026-09-25 (results listed Science, Wikipedia, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and NASA Astrobiology); none of the pages was opened directly. The summaries note that the inclusion age marks the Solar System's earliest solids, while Earth's age marks accretion or core formation and comes out slightly younger; the two figures are not in conflict.

=== source/alan-turing-search-summaries
title: Alan Turing (Search Summaries)
src: 5fd255cc8235
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Turing set out the idea of a universal machine in 1936; he did codebreaking work at Bletchley Park, where the Bombe was in operation from March 1940; he proposed the Turing test in 1950; he was convicted of gross indecency in 1952; and he received a posthumous royal pardon in 2013.
--- extra 1: Caveats
These facts rest only on web search result summaries consulted on 2026-09-25 (results listed Bletchley Park, Science/AAAS, CBC News, Biography.com and Fox News); none of the pages was opened directly. One summary loosely credits Turing alone with breaking Enigma; codebreaking at Bletchley Park was a team effort, and this note says only that he worked on it.

=== source/anderson-and-westneat-2007-dunkleosteus-bite-force
title: Anderson and Westneat 2007 Dunkleosteus Bite Force
src: e7c16a8e43e9
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
A linkage model of the skull yields an estimated bite force of about 4,400 N at the tip and 5,400 N at the blade edge.
--- extra 1: Caveats
This is cited from memory and was not consulted directly. The estimate assumes a larger animal than recent size revisions support, and a 2025 study argues that the skull held more cartilage than the model assumed.
