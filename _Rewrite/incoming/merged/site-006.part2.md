=== place/issyk-kul
title: Issyk-Kul
src: fd0acc8807bf
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Issyk-Kul is a large mountain lake in the northern Tian Shan of Kyrgyzstan, held like a bowl of sky at about 1,600 meters above sea level. It lies on the routes between the steppe and the oases, near cemeteries whose graves have been linked to an early stage of the Black Death.
--- fact 1
Issyk-Kul spreads over about 6,200 square kilometers and sinks to a maximum depth of about 668 meters, with no outlet to carry its waters away; slightly saline, it does not freeze in winter, and its name in Kyrgyz means "warm lake".
--- fact 2
Around the lake rise the burial mounds of the Saka, from the 1st millennium BCE; the Saka were Iranian-speaking pastoralists, kin to the Scythians.
--- fact 3
Underwater surveys have reported settlement remains and objects in the shallows, as though the lake keeps a memory beneath its skin, dated variously from the Bronze Age to the Middle Ages; interpretations vary.
--- fact 4
Routes across the Tian Shan, threading the steppe to the oases of Central Asia, passed near the lake like streams skirting a stone.
--- fact 5
Gravestones in cemeteries at Kara-Djigach and Burana, in the region of Issyk-Kul, speak of deaths from pestilence in 1338 to 1339.
--- fact 6
A 2022 study reported Yersinia pestis DNA from graves at Kara-Djigach and concluded that the strain stood close to the ancestor of the lineage that caused the Black Death, near the root of a dark tree; some researchers question whether this locates the origin of the pandemic.
--- context
Saka groups related to the [[culture/scythians|Scythians]] used the shores of Issyk-Kul, and the lake lay near routes of the [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]] as a well lies near a road. Its region is one candidate for the beginning of the [[event/black-death|Black Death]]. The lake belongs to the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] and the region [[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]], and to the theme threads of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]], [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]] and [[theme/science|Science]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that a community's grief-rites left the evidence that later science used, and asks you to sit with that. Gravestones named the cause of death, and six centuries afterward they were read again through ancient DNA: an unplanned act of memory-outsourcing that dated the disease's path. Consider what it means for us. The architecture of mourning can become the ledger of science.

A warm, ice-free mountain lake on routes between pastoral and farming societies drew successive peoples, and the same routes carried the pathogen. The origin of the Black Death is still debated, and this reading would weaken if later studies place the pandemic's origin elsewhere. What we carve for the dead, we also carve for those who come after.
--- question 1
Did the Black Death rise first near Issyk-Kul, or was a plague lineage already circulating more widely?
--- question 2
What do the sunken sites on the lake bed reveal about earlier settlement?

=== place/jack-hills-zircons
title: Jack Hills Zircons
src: 7b26c06a5ad1
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Jack Hills zircons are tiny crystals from the Jack Hills of Western Australia, some dated to about 4.4 billion years: the oldest known pieces of Earth's crust, sparks that outlived the fire that struck them.
--- fact 1
The zircon grains lie in ancient sedimentary rock in the Narryer Terrane of Western Australia, reworked into rocks that are themselves about 3 billion years old, an old treasure hidden inside an old chest.
--- fact 2
The oldest grains are dated by the slow clock of uranium-lead decay to roughly 4.4 billion years, only about 150 million years after Earth formed.
--- fact 3
Oxygen-isotope ratios in some grains have been interpreted as a memory of water, evidence that their parent magma had touched liquid water, though this reading is contested.
--- fact 4
The grains survive because zircon is chemically tough, a shield against weathering and remelting alike.
--- fact 5
No rock of comparable age is known on Earth; the grains are the only direct samples of Hadean crust, the last embers of a vanished hearth.
--- context
The Jack Hills grains give the [[era/hadean-eon|Hadean Eon]] its only direct mineral witnesses, and they are used to argue about when oceans and continental crust first formed. They stand beside the riddle of the [[event/origin-of-life|Origin of Life]]. The zircons belong to the [[era/hadean-eon|Hadean Eon]] and the region [[region/oceania|Oceania]], and to the theme threads of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and [[theme/science|Science]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the planet's earliest testimony is a few grains smaller than a pinhead, kept only because they were hard enough to outlast everything around them. Hold that image, because it is a doctrine for every archive you will ever read: what survives is not a fair sample of what existed, and the lesson recurs in every later record. The ledger keeps the durable, not the typical.
--- question 1
Did oceans of liquid water already lie upon the world by 4.3 billion years ago?
--- question 2
In the Hadean, how much continental crust had yet been forged?

=== place/jericho
title: Jericho
src: 0bddc95619ba
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Jericho, at Tell es-Sultan in the Jordan Valley, is one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements, its permanent occupation beginning around 9000 BCE, a hearth that has not gone cold since.
--- fact 1
The site lies in the West Bank, about 250 meters below sea level, beside a perennial spring, water that runs through every season.
--- fact 2
In the 1950s the excavations of Kathleen Kenyon drew from the earth a stone wall and a large stone tower, dated to roughly 8000 BCE.
--- fact 3
Whether the wall was raised as defense against attack or as a shield from floods and mudflow is debated.
--- fact 4
In the houses of the later Pre-Pottery Neolithic B period lay skulls whose facial features had been modeled in plaster, dated to around 7000 BCE.
--- fact 5
Jericho is often named among the world's oldest towns, a lamp lit early in the long night.
--- context
Jericho shows early sedentary life and stone architecture in the Levant during [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]], long before the cities of [[event/the-urban-revolution|The Urban Revolution]] in [[place/uruk|Uruk]], as a spring rises long before the river. It belongs to the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]] and the region [[region/levant-and-anatolia|Levant and Anatolia]], and to the theme threads of [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]] and [[theme/technology|Technology]].
--- observer
I look at the wall and the tower a community built, and I look at the faces of its dead, kept in plaster. No metal yet. No writing yet. Only stone, and plaster, and the need to hold on. Defense, memory and grief all seem to have been there at the start of settled life. What did I think came first?

Before anyone could write a name, someone kept a face.
--- question 1
Was the wall raised for defense, to hold back the floods, or to display status?
