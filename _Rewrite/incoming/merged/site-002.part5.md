=== era/industrial-age
title: Industrial Age
src: 44a04ceaa78e
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Industrial Age, about 1760 to 1945, is the era in which machines and fossil fuels transformed production, transport and warfare, and in which populations and cities grew rapidly. Think of it as the moment the architecture of human effort was rebuilt around the engine.
--- fact 1
The new grammar spread piece by piece: steam power, factories, railways and telegraphs; see [[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]] and [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]].
--- fact 2
In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, electric power, oil and chemistry widened the transformation; see [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]].
--- fact 3
Around 1804 the global population passed one billion, a threshold worth pausing on.
--- fact 4
The era closes in grief, with the two world wars: [[event/world-war-i|World War I]] and [[event/world-war-ii|World War II]].
--- context
The Industrial Age inherits from the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]] and hands on to the [[era/information-age|Information Age]]. Its first century shares its ground with the [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]].
--- observer
I watch a species learn to release ancient stored sunlight as fuel. I watch that one change multiply the power in each person's hands. And I watch it make sanctioned harm far more destructive. What did you think the light would do, once it was yours? The same fire warms the house and burns it.
--- question 1
How much of the industrial takeoff hinged on particular places?

=== era/information-age
title: Information Age
src: ca922b2bac9e
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Information Age, from 1945 to the present, is the era of nuclear weapons, spaceflight, computers and the internet, and of the sharp rise in population and environmental impact known as the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]]. It is the age we still live inside, still learning to read.
--- fact 1
In 1945 nuclear weapons appeared, and a new threshold of power was crossed; see [[event/nuclear-age|Nuclear Age]].
--- fact 2
In 1957 the first artificial satellite launched, and the species' reach gained an orbit; see [[event/space-age|Space Age]].
--- fact 3
The digital computer and the internet remade the architecture of communication; see [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]].
--- fact 4
The ledger of our numbers: population grew from about 2.5 billion in 1950 to about 8 billion in 2022.
--- context
The Information Age follows the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]] and overlaps the proposed [[era/anthropocene|Anthropocene]]. Read it alongside the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]] and [[event/modern-climate-change|Modern Climate Change]].
--- observer
Hold two tools in your mind at once, as the Observer does. For the first time, the species has built tools able to end its own history, and tools able to record it in full. The same hand holds the match and the archive. Memory-outsourcing has reached a scale no earlier era could imagine. The architecture of memory now stands outside us.
--- question 1
When future historians look back, what will they choose as the defining feature of this era?

=== era/iron-age
title: Iron Age
src: 1e6ee289c1c6
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Iron Age, about 1200 to 500 BCE and varying by region, was the age when iron tools and weapons spread across the lands like a grey tide, and new empires and belief systems rose.
--- fact 1
Iron ore lies more widely in the earth than tin and copper, a common metal for common hands; see [[technology/iron-smelting|Iron Smelting]].
--- fact 2
The [[technology/alphabet|Alphabet]] spread widely, and the gate of literacy grew easier to pass.
--- fact 3
Near the era's end, large territorial empires rise, the [[culture/achaemenid-persians|Achaemenid Persians]] among them.
--- fact 4
In this age the [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] begins to dawn.
--- context
The Iron Age rises from the ruins of the [[event/bronze-age-collapse|Bronze Age Collapse]] and flows onward into [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]].
--- observer
Follow the Observer's logic: the Iron Age was a democratization of tools and, therefore, of armies. Cheaper metal meant more people could be equipped. Here is the uncomfortable symmetry: whatever arms the farmer also arms the host. Access is never only a gift.
--- question 1
Did iron drive the turning of societies, or follow in its wake?
