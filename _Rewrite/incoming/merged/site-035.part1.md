=== era/middle-paleolithic
src: b10b96f1b208
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Middle Paleolithic was the middle reach of the long Paleolithic, a span running from about 300,000 to about 50,000 years ago. It was the age of prepared-core stone tools, of the earliest Homo sapiens, and of several human species living at the same time, like fires burning on one long night.
--- fact 1
In Eurasia the Middle Paleolithic runs from about 300,000 to about 50,000 years ago; in Africa the same span is called the Middle Stone Age, and its boundary dates shift from region to region like shorelines. Toolmakers worked by prepared-core methods such as the Levallois technique, and the Mousterian industry of Europe and western Asia is linked mainly to Neanderthals.
--- fact 2
Several human species overlapped in time, branches leafing in the same season: [[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]], [[species/denisovans|Denisovans]], [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]], [[species/homo-naledi|Homo naledi]] and [[species/homo-floresiensis|Homo floresiensis]]. The earliest Homo sapiens fossils are about 315,000 years old; see [[event/origin-of-homo-sapiens|Origin of Homo sapiens]].
--- fact 3
Homo sapiens fossils from Misliya Cave in Israel, about 185,000 years old, and from Skhul and Qafzeh caves, roughly 90,000 to 130,000 years old, mark an early presence outside Africa, footprints past the old threshold; whether these groups flowed into the lineage of living non-Africans is uncertain (see [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]]).
--- fact 4
At Blombos Cave in South Africa lie engraved ochre and ochre-processing tools about 75,000 to 100,000 years old, coloured earth marked by a hand and among the earliest signs of symbolic behaviour; see [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]].
--- fact 5
By about 40,000 years ago the Neanderthals have gone from the fossil record, as a tide goes from a shore. Whether they buried their dead or made art is disputed.
--- context
The Middle Paleolithic follows the [[era/lower-paleolithic|Lower Paleolithic]] and gives way to the [[era/upper-paleolithic|Upper Paleolithic]], a middle chamber in the long hall of the [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]]. Its threads run through [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]]. See also the essay [[observer-note/why-the-species-buries-its-dead|Why the Species Buries Its Dead]].
--- observer
I wanted a ladder. I wanted each rung to replace the one below it. I wanted one clean line running down to me. But here, for the first time, several human species lived at the same time, and sometimes in the same regions, and the family tree looks more like a bush than a ladder.

Who buried their dead? Who made the marks? The claims about their behaviour, burial and symbolic art above all, are disputed, and I hold each species to the same standard of evidence, no gentler with my own kind than with the others. And if the fossils sorted into different species prove to be variants of one, this reading weakens, and the bush becomes a single stem again.

A family is only as wide as the evidence allows.
--- question 1
Did the Neanderthals lay their dead in the earth, or make art?
--- question 2
How much of the variety in Middle Paleolithic tools speaks of different cultures, and how much only of different raw materials to hand?

=== era/upper-paleolithic
src: 00e31a7a09cc
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Upper Paleolithic was the last reach of the Paleolithic, from about 50,000 to about 10,000 BCE. It was the age of blade tools, of bone and antler worked into new shapes, of art and ornament scattered like seed, and of Homo sapiens spreading across the globe as water spreads across a floor.
--- fact 1
The Upper Paleolithic runs from about 50,000 to about 10,000 BCE and closes with the warming at the end of the last ice age, when the cold loosened its grip (in Africa the span is called the Later Stone Age). Toolmakers struck long blades and worked bone, antler and ivory; eyed bone needles from Denisova Cave in Siberia, about 40,000 to 50,000 years old, suggest sewn clothing.
--- fact 2
Homo sapiens spread across the globe like light across water: Sahul by about 50,000 years ago ([[event/peopling-of-sahul|Peopling of Sahul]]), Europe by about 45,000 years ago, Beringia by about 30,000 years ago ([[place/beringia|Beringia]]), and the Americas by at least about 23,000 years ago ([[event/peopling-of-the-americas|Peopling of the Americas]]). In Europe the Neanderthals disappear about 40,000 years ago.
--- fact 3
Painted, carved and engraved images are abundant, a field in bloom: [[place/chauvet-cave|Chauvet Cave]] in France holds paintings about 36,000 years old, and cave paintings in Sulawesi, Indonesia, include a narrative scene about 51,000 years old.
--- fact 4
The [[event/last-glacial-maximum|Last Glacial Maximum]], about 26,500 to 19,000 years ago, laid ice like a white mantle over much of northern Eurasia and North America. Pottery appears in East Asia by about 20,000 years ago ([[technology/pottery|Pottery]]), and dogs were domesticated from wolves by at least about 15,000 years ago.
--- context
The Upper Paleolithic follows the [[era/middle-paleolithic|Middle Paleolithic]] and, in the Near East, yields to the [[era/epipaleolithic|Epipaleolithic]], all within the [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]] and the [[era/pleistocene-epoch|Pleistocene Epoch]]. Its threads are [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]], [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]]. See also [[event/quaternary-megafauna-extinctions|Quaternary Megafauna Extinctions]].
--- observer
I hold a bead and I want it to mean something. I call it a status-signal, possibly. I stand beside a burial and call it a grief-rite, possibly, because I know what grief is. And I keep in mind that a practice cannot be read from a surviving object alone.

These sites hold the first large body of things that seem made for display, for ritual, for play. But what if comparable things were common long before this period? Then the change was in what survives, not in behaviour, and this reading weakens.

What lasts is not the measure of what mattered.
--- question 1
Is the apparent burst of art and ornament real, or only the shadow of better preservation and more sites?
--- question 2
How did the Upper Paleolithic industries map onto particular human populations?
