=== people--mimar-sinan--1
src: 0515880d6d56
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Mimar Sinan (about 1489 to 1588) was chief royal architect to three Ottoman sultans for nearly fifty years. He is credited with several hundred structures, and his Selimiye Mosque at Edirne is regarded as the peak of classical Ottoman architecture. His career is a lesson in how mastery is staged.

He was born about 1489 or 1490 in Ağırnas, near Kayseri in central Anatolia, into a Christian family; sources disagree on whether it was Greek or Armenian.

He was conscripted through the devşirme levy into the Ottoman elite corps, converted to Islam and trained as a Janissary. He rose through military engineering, building bridges and fortifications on campaign for Suleiman the Magnificent. The practical came first; the sublime grew out of it.

Chelebi Lütfi Pasha appointed him chief royal architect in 1539, when he became Grand Vizier, and Sinan held the post into his late nineties. He died in Istanbul in 1588.

His main buildings are the Şehzade Mosque (1543 to 1548), the Süleymaniye Mosque (1550 to 1557) and the Selimiye Mosque (1568 or 1569 to 1574 or 1575). He described them as his apprenticeship, journeyman work and masterpiece. Name your own stages honestly, and you can see how far you have come.

He worked to build domes wider and more daring than Hagia Sophia, a Byzantine cathedral converted to a mosque after 1453, using semi-domes, buttressing piers and pendentives. He also built aqueducts and bridges, some still in use.

=== people--suleiman-the-magnificent--1
src: 5e42bfbd174e
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Suleiman the Magnificent (1494 to 1566) was Ottoman sultan from 1520 to 1566, and under him the empire stood at its greatest territorial extent, its height of military power, and its summit of legal and cultural achievement.

His armies took Belgrade in 1521 and Rhodes in 1522, then pressed deep into Central Europe, defeating and killing the King of Hungary at the Battle of Mohacs in 1526, though a first siege of Vienna in 1529 failed to take the city.

He is known by the epithet Kanuni (the Lawgiver), a name absent from sixteenth and seventeenth-century Ottoman sources that may date only from the early 18th century. He issued a major legal code that brought Ottoman civil law into harmony with Islamic religious law across the empire's many lands.

His navies under admirals such as Barbarossa Hayreddin Pasha contested the Mediterranean with Spain and its allies, and Ottoman forces also pushed into Persia and Mesopotamia, taking Baghdad in 1534.

His reign saw Ottoman architecture flower under the chief architect Mimar Sinan, with court poetry, painting and decorative arts, all centered on Istanbul like light gathered in a lens.

=== people--suleiman-the-magnificent--2

--- description
Suleiman the Magnificent (1494 to 1566) was Ottoman sultan from 1520 to 1566, presiding over the empire's greatest territorial extent, military power, and legal and cultural achievement.

The military record first. His armies captured Belgrade in 1521 and Rhodes in 1522, then advanced deep into Central Europe, defeating and killing the King of Hungary at the Battle of Mohacs in 1526, though a first siege of Vienna in 1529 failed to take the city.

Then the law. He is known by the epithet Kanuni (the Lawgiver), though it is absent from sixteenth and seventeenth-century Ottoman sources and may date only from the early 18th century. He issued a major legal code harmonizing Ottoman civil law with Islamic religious law across the empire's diverse territories. A legal code is the architecture beneath an empire's daily life.

His navies under admirals such as Barbarossa Hayreddin Pasha contested Mediterranean control with Spain and its allies, and Ottoman forces also pushed into Persia and Mesopotamia, capturing Baghdad in 1534.

His reign saw a major flourishing of Ottoman architecture under the chief architect Mimar Sinan, and of court poetry, painting and decorative arts, centered on Istanbul.
