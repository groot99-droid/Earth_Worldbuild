=== place/nazca-lines
src: 3b2dc4d4f579
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Nazca Lines are geoglyphs drawn across the desert of southern Peru between about 400 BCE and 500 CE by the Paracas and Nazca cultures: animals, plants and geometric shapes, and hundreds of straight lines, written on the ground and read best from the sky.
--- fact 1
The lines lie in the Nazca Desert between the towns of Nazca and Palpa, about 400 kilometers south of Lima, and spread over about 50 square kilometers.
--- fact 2
They were made by lifting away a top layer of reddish, iron-oxide-coated pebbles to bare the lighter ground beneath, the lines and figures cut roughly 10 to 15 centimeters deep.
--- fact 3
Together the lines run more than 1,300 kilometers, and the largest figures, such as a 134-meter condor and a 93-meter hummingbird, stretch from tens to a few hundred meters.
--- fact 4
The Peruvian archaeologist Toribio Mejía Xesspe noted the lines on foot in 1927; the American historian Paul Kosok saw them from the air in 1940 to 1941 and proposed astronomical alignments, a theory carried further by Maria Reiche; in 1990 researchers Gerald Hawkins and Anthony Aveni concluded that the evidence did not support a systematic astronomical purpose.
--- fact 5
Other proposed purposes include ritual paths tied to water and fertility, walked in ceremony, and markers of underground water sources; no single explanation is agreed on.
--- fact 6
UNESCO inscribed the Lines and Geoglyphs of Nazca and Pampas de Jumana in 1994.
--- fact 7
The site has been scarred by squatters, a limestone quarry, a 2014 Greenpeace protest that left marks near one geoglyph, and a 2018 truck crossing, and erosion and off-road vehicles still threaten it.
--- context
The Nazca Lines were made long before the rise of [[place/cusco|Cusco]] and the Inca state, centered in the Andean highlands to the north-east. They belong to the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] and the region [[region/andes|Andes]], and to the theme threads of [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/science|Science]].
--- observer
I notice that a mark made to be read from the ground and a mark made to be read from the air are different kinds of pattern-taming, and that the long debate over the Lines is partly a debate over which kind their makers meant. I notice that Reiche's proposal of an astronomical instrument was a claim about precision that later analysis did not confirm, and I take it as an example of how a compelling reading can outrun its evidence.

No written record from the makers survives, so every purpose offered here is inference from the marks themselves. Who were they drawing for? I can only stand where they stood and guess.

The reading would weaken if excavation along the lines turns up consistent ritual deposits pointing to one purpose over the others.
--- question 1
Were the lines walked as ritual paths, watched as an observatory, or used for some other purpose entirely?
--- question 2
How did the people who made the Lines organize the labor needed to plan and clear such long, straight paths?

=== culture/ancestral-puebloans
src: 0bc6a19b936d
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Ancestral Puebloans raised great stone and cliff settlements across the American Southwest from about 700 to 1300 CE, among them the great houses of Chaco Canyon and the cliff dwellings of Mesa Verde, before drought and hardship led them to scatter into the communities that became today's Pueblo peoples.
--- fact 1
Their building grew from simple pit houses into many-roomed stone great houses, seen most clearly today at Chaco Canyon and Mesa Verde.
--- fact 2
Mesa Verde was lived in from about 450 to 1300 CE, and most of its famous cliff dwellings were built between 1190 and 1300.
--- fact 3
Chaco Canyon's role as a regional center waned from about 1130 to 1150, during a drought that began in 1130, and Mesa Verde was abandoned by about 1300 CE.
--- fact 4
A long drought from 1276 to 1299, together with food shortages, is the leading explanation for the departure from Mesa Verde, as communities moved south into what is now Arizona and New Mexico.
--- fact 5
Their descendants live today as the Hopi, Zuni and other Pueblo peoples of the Rio Grande region.
--- context
The Ancestral Puebloans built [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] and [[place/mesa-verde|Mesa Verde]] in [[region/north-america|North America]] during the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]], then scattered under the pressure of climate into the communities that became the Pueblo peoples. See [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
I notice that long droughts, not conquest or disease, ended two of the era's largest settlement complexes in North America. I notice that the people who left did not vanish; they went on as named communities in the same region. The word "ancestral" admits a continuity that popular labels like "vanished" once denied.

What does it mean to leave a home of stone and still be home? A people can move and remain.

A specialist could argue that drought alone cannot explain the timing and manner of abandonment, and that social or political strain within the settlements compounded it, which would weaken a purely climatic reading.
--- question 1
How much did social or political conflict add to abandonment, alongside drought?
--- question 2
How direct is the line between particular Ancestral Puebloan communities and particular modern Pueblo nations?
