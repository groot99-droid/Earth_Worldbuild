=== bernardino-campi
src: af477e321c3a
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Here is a double portrait with a twist, a mirror set within a mirror. Sofonisba showed her teacher, Bernardino Campi, at work upon her portrait, yet it was her hand that painted the whole picture, teacher and all. Her own likeness she made larger and brighter than the one taking shape beneath his hand, a lamp outshining the lamp that first lit it. It is a witty meditation on authorship, on teaching, and on who holds the brush.

=== elisabeth-of-valois
src: 59017691b35b
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I see the gown first: black, embroidered with gold, every jewel rendered in minute detail, as the formal conventions of the Spanish court required. I see the miniature next, her husband, Philip II, held small in her hand. Only then do I see the tenderness. She is the young French-born Queen of Spain, and she was Anguissola's pupil and friend. The portrait carries a personal tenderness born of the two women's years together. I remember a friend who taught me. What can a convention hold, if not the warmth beneath it? Love can wear black and gold and still be love.

=== philip-ii
src: 66c0c1f70c45
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
The king holds a rosary, in sober black with the Order of the Golden Fleece, and I look past him for the hand that painted him. For centuries this portrait was credited to the court painter Alonso Sánchez Coello. For centuries her name was missing. Modern research returned it to Anguissola, one of several of her court works that had lost her name. How many names have slipped from the things we made? A name returned is not a gift; it is a debt repaid.

=== origin-of-music
src: a0851e3f3636
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
It comes from one of the earliest manuscripts made for the young emperor Akbar: a collection of tales told by a wise parrot, one story threaded after another like beads on a silk cord. This page, attributed to Basawan, illustrates a story about how music was first discovered, the hour a silent world first learned to sing. Already its figures are lively and its colours bright, the marks of the new Mughal style, where Persian and Indian painting met and mingled like two rivers joining.

=== drunk-european
src: 145a3884202b
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A man in European dress sat slumped on the ground with a wine flask, and shadow had been laid upon him with care, so that he swelled from the page with weight and volume. Strangers had come to the court: Portuguese traders and Jesuit missionaries had lately arrived at Akbar's court, bearing European prints and paintings like seeds blown from a far country. Basawan was among the first Mughal artists to study them closely, drinking in their modelling and their perspective as dry earth drinks rain.

=== akbar-and-hawai
src: 77b0795890cd
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This is one page of a double-page scene from the illustrated history of Akbar's reign. In 1561 the 19-year-old emperor mounted the ferocious elephant Hawai and drove it after another elephant, across a bridge of boats on the Jumna at Agra. Beneath the two great beasts the boats sink and tilt like leaves under a storm's heel, and attendants leap into the water. Basawan designed the swirling composition, a whirlpool of bodies and river, and another artist laid in the colours.

=== battle-of-the-ascetics
src: c361efaabeab
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Violence over a sacred thing is still violence, and a painting can hold both the fact and the craft without confusing them. In 1567 two rival bands of Hindu ascetics fought over the right to a sacred bathing place at Thanesar. Akbar watched, and let some of his soldiers join the fight. Basawan fills the page with dozens of near-naked, ash-smeared figures wielding swords and bows. Notice what he does with them: each body is shown in a different pose, with a vigour and anatomical study that were unmatched at the court. Here is the practice for you as a viewer: see the skill, and remember the fight was real. Admiration and gravity can share a single page.

=== vain-dervish
src: b363def614dd
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
It comes from a small, exquisite manuscript of Jami's *Baharistan*, the "Spring Garden", made for Akbar's library in Lahore. Within it a proud dervish is taught humility by a wiser Sufi, as a high wave is taught by the shore. Basawan set the lesson in a soft, receding landscape, where the land fades into haze and a distant town waits like a memory, and in that fading he showed his mastery of atmospheric depth, learned partly from European models.

=== jain-ascetic
src: 4d0d7d02098a
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A lean Jain monk, robed in white, walked along a river beneath the trees, absorbed in thought, while life went on around him like water around a stone. This is a late, intimate work: an individual study of a holy man, not a page of history. In it lives the naturalism and the sympathy for ordinary people that Basawan passed on, as a lamp passes its flame, to the next generation of Mughal painters, among them his son Manohar.

=== boy-with-a-basket-of-fruit
src: 1d5775392602
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
It was painted soon after Caravaggio came to Rome, in the lean season when he sold pictures to survive. A young man, shoulder bare and lips parted, holds a basket overflowing with grapes, peaches and figs, an orchard gathered in his arms. So exact is the fruit that its species can be identified, as a naturalist names a bird by its feather. It is a sign of the young artist's commitment to painting straight from life.

=== medusa
src: 83a13d65319e
mode: horror-prose
--- description
A real parade shield. A gift for the Grand Duke of Tuscany. And on it, the Gorgon's severed head. Screaming. Caught at the instant of death. Blood spurting. Snakes still writhing. The face is probably Caravaggio's own; he used himself as the model. The shield is convex. So the head does not stay put. It seems to jump forward, toward you.

It is still screaming.

=== judith-beheading-holofernes
src: 15365fd01207
mode: horror-prose
--- description
Judith, the Jewish heroine. Holofernes, the Assyrian general. She saws through his neck. Her face: determination. And disgust. Beside her, the old maid waits, eager, the sack ready. Behind them, nothing. A black background. A blood-red curtain. No distance anywhere. The violence is shockingly direct. The picture helped establish Caravaggio's reputation for brutal realism.

The sack is waiting.
