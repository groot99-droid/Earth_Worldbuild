=== place/uluru
title: Uluru
src: 4d00ac9a1d61
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Uluru is a large sandstone inselberg in central Australia, an island of stone rising from the plain, sacred to the Anangu people, who have been bound to the site for more than 10,000 years and hold traditional custodianship of it today.
--- fact 1
Uluru rises 348 meters above the surrounding plain (863 meters above sea level) and stands about 335 kilometers southwest of Alice Springs; it is made of arkose sandstone, and because the stone lacks internal jointing it stands largely free of scree slopes, unbroken as a held breath.
--- fact 2
Archaeological evidence carries human presence in the area back more than 10,000 years; the date_start of -8000 is a round figure within that range, and older evidence for the peopling of the surrounding region belongs to a different brief.
--- fact 3
The Anangu, whose Pitjantjatjara and Yankunytjatjara traditions call the rock's stories Tjukurpa, hold that ancestral beings shaped the landscape, and some tracks and sites at Uluru carry restrictions bound to gender and ceremony.
--- fact 4
On 19 July 1873 the surveyor William Gosse named the formation Ayers Rock; on 26 October 1985 the government returned ownership to the local Pitjantjatjara people, under a 99-year lease-back and joint management arrangement, and dual naming followed in 1993, later reordered in 2002 to Uluru / Ayers Rock.
--- fact 5
In 2017 the Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park board voted to end recreational climbing, which had long troubled the Anangu as a crossing of a sacred track and as a safety risk; the ban took effect on 26 October 2019.
--- fact 6
In 1987 UNESCO inscribed Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park among the world's heritage.
--- context
Uluru has held meaning for the [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] from a time long after the [[event/peopling-of-sahul|Peopling of Sahul]] first brought people to the continent, and its handback in 1985 and the 2019 climbing ban came long after the disruption of the [[event/first-fleet-and-the-colonization-of-australia|First Fleet and the Colonization of Australia]]. In the codex of ages it sits in the [[era/holocene-epoch|Holocene Epoch]] and the region [[region/oceania|Oceania]], and it belongs to the theme threads of [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
I say its two names aloud, one after the other. For over a century Uluru carried two names and two systems of meaning at once: a European name attached by a surveyor, and a body of Tjukurpa tradition held by the Anangu. I watch the 1985 handback move authority over the site back toward the tradition that predates the European name. I watch the 2019 climbing ban move it the same way. I watch the newer name learn to wait its turn.

Why do I refuse to call Tjukurpa folklore to be judged? Because treating it as tradition is a deliberate choice made here, the same standard I apply to other traditions in this record. I would have to revise my reading if the closure of the climb is shown to have been driven mainly by liability concerns rather than respect for Anangu wishes. What was here first deserves to be heard first.
--- question 1
Since the 1985 agreement, how has joint management changed the decisions made about the site?
--- question 2
Alongside the Anangu's own long-standing requests, how many of the deaths and injuries associated with the former climbing route shaped the 2017 decision?

=== culture/ancestral-puebloans
title: Ancestral Puebloans
src: f6ae5cf1848f
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Ancestral Puebloans raised large stone and cliff settlements across the American Southwest from about 700 to 1300 CE, among them the great houses of Chaco Canyon and the cliff dwellings of Mesa Verde, until drought and hardship led them to disperse, like seed carried on the wind, into communities that became today's Pueblo peoples.
--- fact 1
Their architecture grew as a tree grows, from simple pit houses into multi-room stone great houses, most visible today at Chaco Canyon and Mesa Verde.
--- fact 2
Mesa Verde was lived in from about 450 to 1300 CE, and most of its famous cliff dwellings were set into the rock between 1190 and 1300.
--- fact 3
By about 1300 CE both Chaco Canyon and Mesa Verde stood abandoned, their rooms given over to the wind.
--- fact 4
A prolonged drought from 1276 to 1299, when the sky withheld its rain, together with food shortages, is the leading explanation, as communities moved south into what is now Arizona and New Mexico.
--- fact 5
Their descendants live on today as the Hopi, Zuni and other Pueblo peoples of the Rio Grande region.
--- context
In [[region/north-america|North America]], during the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]], the Ancestral Puebloans built [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] and [[place/mesa-verde|Mesa Verde]], then dispersed under climatic pressure into the communities that became the Pueblo peoples. Their threads run through [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
I notice what ended two of the era's largest settlement complexes in North America: not conquest, not disease, but a long drought. I notice, too, that the people who left did not vanish. They continued as named communities in the same region. I remember the popular label "vanished". I remember how it sounded like an ending. I remember the continuity it once denied.

Isn't "ancestral" the truer word, the one that acknowledges what went on? A specialist could argue that drought alone cannot explain the timing and manner of abandonment, and that social or political strain within the settlements compounded it, which would weaken a purely climatic reading. I keep that door open. To leave a place is not to leave the world.
--- question 1
Alongside drought, how much did social or political conflict add to the abandonment?
--- question 2
How direct is the thread that runs between specific Ancestral Puebloan communities and specific modern Pueblo nations?
