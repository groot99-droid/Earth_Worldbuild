=== event/xinhai-revolution
title: Xinhai Revolution
src: b20801278787
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Xinhai Revolution of 1911 to 1912 opened with an army mutiny at Wuchang and closed the Qing dynasty, which had ruled China since 1644, replacing imperial rule with the Republic of China.
--- fact 1
On 10 October 1911, members of the New Army mutinied in Wuchang, in Hubei province. The rebellion spread to surrounding regions, and by the end of 1911 revolutionaries were already forming a new government.
--- fact 2
Sun Yat-sen led the republican movement, though he lacked the means to overthrow the Qing alone. He reached a compromise with the powerful military leader Yuan Shikai, whose intervention led to the abdication of the child emperor Puyi on 12 February 1912.
--- fact 3
Sun Yat-sen resigned the presidency to unify the country, and Yuan Shikai was chosen as his successor. By early 1912, the Qing dynasty was gone, and the Republic of China had begun.
--- context
The revolution ended the [[culture/qing-dynasty|Qing Dynasty]] after decades of internal rebellion and foreign pressure, including the [[event/taiping-rebellion|Taiping Rebellion]] and the [[event/opium-wars|Opium Wars]]. The republic that followed proved unstable, with power passing quickly into the hands of military leaders.
--- observer
The Observer notes a transfer of authority arranged by compromise between a movement and an army, not by the movement alone. An architecture of legitimacy as old as the imperial title dissolved in a few months, but what replaced it had to be built while the guns were still in the hands of others.
--- question 1
How much did the Wuchang mutiny depend on planning, and how much on chance?
--- question 2
Why did the republic that replaced the Qing fail to hold power together in the years after 1912?

=== event/younger-dryas
title: Younger Dryas
src: 8e2c481c66ed
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Younger Dryas was a sudden return of near-glacial cold across the Northern Hemisphere, lasting from about 12,900 to 11,700 years ago, on the very threshold of the Holocene.
--- fact 1
It broke the warming that had followed the last glacial period, and it ended just as abruptly — several degrees of warming within mere decades.
--- fact 2
A leading explanation points to a disruption of Atlantic Ocean circulation by a great pulse of glacial meltwater.
--- fact 3
A proposed extraterrestrial impact trigger, the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis, remains disputed and is not widely accepted.
--- fact 4
Its end marks the start of the Holocene, dated to about 11,700 years before 2000 CE.
--- fact 5
It overlaps the early stages of plant management in Southwest Asia, though the causal link between the two is debated.
--- context
The Younger Dryas is the last major cold snap before the stable climate of the [[era/holocene-epoch|Holocene Epoch]], within which [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]] took place. It follows the [[event/last-glacial-maximum|Last Glacial Maximum]]. The Younger Dryas sits within the [[era/pleistocene-epoch|Pleistocene Epoch]] and the region [[region/planet-wide|Planet-wide]], and belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the climate stability in which farming and cities arose was not inevitable. It was preceded by a violent flicker between cold and warm that lasted about a thousand years, and the Observer wonders how populations managed to adapt to such swings within a few generations.
--- question 1
Did the cold snap influence the first cultivation of crops?

=== event/zanclean-flood
title: Zanclean Flood
src: fa300039c4d1
mode: horror-prose
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Zanclean flood: the refilling of the Mediterranean Sea by Atlantic water, through the Gibraltar region, about 5.33 million years ago. It ended the Messinian salinity crisis. It coincides with the start of the Pliocene.
--- fact 1
The Mediterranean, cut off from the Atlantic, had largely evaporated. Estimates of how far the sea level fell range from hundreds of metres to more than a kilometre.
--- fact 2
Seismic images show a buried erosional channel, running from the Gulf of Cádiz through the Strait of Gibraltar into the Alboran Sea — read as the path the flood carved.
--- fact 3
A 2009 study modelled a catastrophic flood, its peak discharge on the order of a hundred million cubic metres per second, refilling the basin within months to a couple of years.
--- fact 4
Other studies propose a slower or two-stage refilling. The speed of the flood: still debated.
--- fact 5
The return of the Atlantic ended the basin's isolation and restored marine life within it.
--- context
The Zanclean Flood ends the [[event/messinian-salinity-crisis|Messinian Salinity Crisis]] within the [[era/neogene-period|Neogene Period]] and falls at the start of the Pliocene. It belongs to the [[era/cenozoic-era|Cenozoic Era]] and to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that one of the largest floods in Earth's history left no human witness, and survives only as a buried channel and seismic images. The largest events in the record can be the ones least available to any memory. This reading would weaken if the refilling proved to have been slow and gradual.
--- question 1
Was the refilling a single catastrophic flood or a slower, staged process?
--- question 2
How dry did the Mediterranean become before the flood?
