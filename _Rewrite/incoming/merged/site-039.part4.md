=== culture/sogdians
src: 25f374260466
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Sogdians were an Iranian-speaking people of the Central Asian oases (Samarkand, Bukhara, Panjikent) who, from about the 4th century CE, became the leading merchants of the Silk Roads, their caravans threaded across the continent like beads on a cord.
--- fact 1
They built trading colonies from Central Asia to China, a chain of lamps along the road, and served as caravan leaders, interpreters and bankers.
--- fact 2
Their language served as a lingua franca along the Silk Roads and as a court language of the First Turkic Khaganate.
--- fact 3
The 'Ancient Letters' of about 313 CE, found in a Great Wall tower, show Sogdian colonies writing to the home base in Samarkand, voices carried back across the dust.
--- fact 4
Sogdians helped carry Buddhism, Manichaeism and, later, other religions across Asia, as rivers carry seed.
--- context
Through [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] the Sogdians carried the trade of the [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]], and they served [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]] China and the Turkic khaganates.
--- observer
The Observer notes that a people who never built a large empire shaped the economy of Eurasia. I have admired thrones. I have admired walls. I have seldom admired the one who translates at the gate. Merchants who speak many languages can be more influential than rulers who speak one.
--- question 1
How did Sogdian merchant communities arrange their credit, and how did they keep trust?

=== culture/sokoto-caliphate
src: 9594aebc0363
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Sokoto Caliphate was an Islamic state in what is now northern Nigeria, founded through the jihad of Usman dan Fodio from 1804, and in the 19th century it stood as the largest state in West Africa, spread like a great tree over the plain.
--- fact 1
The jihad began in February 1804 and ran until about 1808; by 1815 the caliphate had drawn in most of northern Nigeria and parts of Cameroon and Niger.
--- fact 2
Some estimates give the caliphate 10 to 20 million people by the 1830s, a very populous realm.
--- fact 3
It was a center of scholarship and of trade across the Sahel, a crossroads of ink and caravan.
--- fact 4
British forces conquered it in 1903.
--- context
The state inherited the lands of the [[culture/songhai-empire|Songhai Empire]] and fell during the [[event/scramble-for-africa|Scramble for Africa]]. See [[person/usman-dan-fodio|Usman dan Fodio]].
--- observer
The Observer notes a state founded by a scholar and a reformer, who wrote extensively as well as fought. The caliphate produced a large body of writing in Arabic, Hausa and Fulfulde. Notice the architecture here: the pen was built into the foundations alongside the sword. A state's library can outlast its borders.
--- question 1
How centralized was the rule of the caliphate?

=== culture/song-dynasty
src: 2bb813a843b3
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Song dynasty ruled China from 960 to 1279 CE and presided over great advances in commerce, technology and urban life, an age when its cities burned bright as lanterns.
--- fact 1
It issued some of the world's first government paper money, wealth made light as a leaf.
--- fact 2
Cities such as Kaifeng and Hangzhou were among the largest in the world, swollen like rivers in spring.
--- fact 3
Its advances included [[technology/gunpowder|Gunpowder]] weapons, the [[technology/magnetic-compass|Magnetic Compass]] for navigation, and movable-type printing.
--- fact 4
The examination system and Neo-Confucian thought shaped elite culture as a riverbed shapes water.
--- context
The Song built on the tradition of the [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] and was later conquered by the [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that a highly commercial, urbanized society experienced many innovations that later appeared elsewhere. Consider what that does to the ledger of history: the center of technological activity was not always where later histories place it. Read any map of invention with that in mind. The workshop moves; the story can stay behind.
--- question 1
Did the Song stand close to the threshold of industrialization?

=== culture/songhai-empire
src: 3084f73f8db2
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Songhai Empire was a West African state that followed Mali as the dominant power on the Niger River, from about 1464 until a Moroccan army defeated it in 1591, a reign that rose and fell like the river's flood.
--- fact 1
Sunni Ali widened the state and took Timbuktu in 1468.
--- fact 2
Askia Muhammad built a bureaucracy and fostered scholarship.
--- fact 3
Trans-Saharan trade in gold and salt sustained it, carried across the sand like blood through a body.
--- fact 4
At Tondibi in 1591, Moroccan forces with gunpowder firearms defeated it.
--- context
Songhai inherited the lands of the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]] and the scholarly city of [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]]. Its defeat shows what [[technology/gunpowder|Gunpowder]] weapons could do.
--- observer
The Observer notes that a large state was defeated by a smaller army carrying a newer weapon. Here is the fragile architecture of power: size is a wall, and a new tool is a crack in it. Technology can overturn a balance of power quickly.
--- question 1
How large was Songhai's army set against the Moroccan force?
