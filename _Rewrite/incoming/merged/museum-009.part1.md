=== people--yongle-emperor--1
src: 623cb017779b
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
The Yongle Emperor (Zhu Di; 1360 to 1424) was the third emperor of China's Ming dynasty. In his reign the capital moved to Beijing, and the ocean voyages of Zheng He set out under his banner.

He took the throne from his nephew, the Jianwen Emperor, in a civil war from 1399 to 1402, after his father, the dynasty's founder, had passed him over in the succession.

He carried the imperial capital from Nanjing to Beijing and began the Forbidden City in 1405, with most palaces finished by the end of 1417; in 1420 Beijing was officially declared the principal capital.

He commissioned Zheng He's treasure-fleet voyages, six of the seven falling in his reign (the seventh came under the Xuande Emperor). The first departed in 1405, and the fleets reached as far as the coasts of East Africa and Arabia, carrying Ming naval power across the Indian Ocean like a tide of sails.

He sponsored the Yongle Encyclopedia, an enormous gathering of Chinese knowledge finished in 1408 that ran to over 11,000 volumes, most of which was later lost, a library scattered like leaves.

=== people--zheng-he--1
src: 6ab34428dd9f
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Zheng He (1371 to 1433) was a Chinese admiral who led seven great naval expeditions between 1405 and 1433, reaching Southeast Asia, India, Arabia and East Africa.

He served the Ming emperor Yongle, and he was a Muslim of Yunnan origin.

His fleets carried very large ships and thousands of crew, a city afloat on the sea.

The voyages displayed Ming power and gathered tribute; then they were stopped.

They came decades before European ocean voyages of the same kind.

=== people--jadwiga--1
src: b602644b4568
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Jadwiga (about 1373 to 1399) was crowned "King" of Poland in 1384 at about age ten, often described as the first woman to rule Poland in her own right, and her marriage to the Grand Duke of Lithuania began a dynastic union between the two states.

She was crowned with the male title "rex" (king) rather than "regina" (queen), to declare that she held full sovereign authority in her own right, not merely as a royal consort.

In 1386 she married Jogaila, the pagan Grand Duke of Lithuania, who converted to Christianity and was crowned co-ruler as Wladyslaw II Jagiello, founding the Jagiellonian dynasty and beginning the Christianization of Lithuania.

She helped fund the restoration of the Krakow Academy, later the Jagiellonian University, through jewellery donated and, under her will, sold to help finance it, so that an institution revived and became a major center of learning in Central Europe, a lamp relit.

She died in 1399, about three and a half weeks after giving birth to a daughter who also died in infancy, four days before her, and her direct line ended; the Catholic Church canonized her in 1997.

=== people--ulugh-beg--1
src: 8c959f82206d
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Ulugh Beg (1394 to 1449) was a Timurid ruler and astronomer who raised an observatory at Samarkand and made one of the most accurate star catalogues before the telescope.

He became governor of Samarkand in 1409, at about sixteen.

He built a madrasa (1417 to 1420) and, in 1420, an observatory, a house for watching the stars.

His star catalogue, completed by about 1437, lists at least 1,018 stars.

He died in 1449 and was buried in the Gur-e-Amir mausoleum in Samarkand, beside his grandfather Timur.

=== people--johannes-gutenberg--1
src: 6617bc7cd580
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Johannes Gutenberg (about 1400 to 1468) was a German metalworker who developed a movable-type printing press in Mainz around 1440 and printed the Gutenberg Bible about 1455.

His invention was a combination: metal type, oil-based ink, and a press adapted from wine-making. Much of what we call invention is the right arrangement of things that already exist.

The 42-line Bible is the earliest major book printed from movable type in Europe.

He faced financial difficulties and lost control of his workshop to a creditor.

Printing spread rapidly across Europe within decades. An idea can outrun the person who made it possible.
