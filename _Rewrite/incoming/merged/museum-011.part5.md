=== people--kristina-of-sweden--1
src: 1941655b4274
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Kristina (1626 to 1689) was Queen of Sweden from 1632 to 1654, remembered for her patronage of scholars and the arts, for her unlooked-for abdication, and for her secret conversion to Catholicism in a firmly Protestant kingdom.

She became queen at age five, when her father Gustavus Adolphus fell in battle, and ruled under a regency until she took full power in 1644.

She summoned scholars, among them Rene Descartes, to her court in Stockholm. Descartes died there in 1650, reportedly worn down by the early-morning hours she kept for their lessons, lamps lit before a northern dawn.

In 1654 she gave up the throne in favor of her cousin, and after leaving Sweden she converted to Catholicism in secret in Brussels in December 1654, a choice scandalous in Lutheran Sweden, which she declared publicly in 1655.

She spent most of her later life in Rome under the pope's protection, a great patron of music and art, and she is one of very few women buried in St. Peter's Basilica.

=== people--christopher-wren--1
src: 8f75f9f58682
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Christopher Wren (1632 to 1723) was an English mathematician and astronomer, a founder of the Royal Society, who turned architect after the Great Fire of London in 1666. He designed more than fifty City churches and a new St Paul's Cathedral, topped out in 1708 and declared complete by Parliament in 1711, rising from ash like a white mountain.

He was born in East Knoyle, Wiltshire, son of a clergyman. At 25 he was appointed professor of astronomy at Gresham College, then Savilian Professor of Astronomy at Oxford, and in 1662 he was a founding member of the Royal Society.

His work as an architect began with the Sheldonian Theatre in Oxford. The Great Fire of September 1666 destroyed old St Paul's and most of the City.

In 1669 he was appointed Surveyor of the King's Works, and he gave the rest of his working life to the City's parish churches and to St Paul's. He died in 1723, aged 90, and lies in the cathedral crypt.

He joined the classical vocabulary of Inigo Jones and Palladio with French and Italian Baroque elements he studied on a research trip to Paris, the only time he travelled outside England.

The dome of St Paul's is three independent shells nested one inside another, so that the outer profile, the inner ceiling and the structural core could each be shaped for its own purpose.

=== people--isaac-newton--1
src: b332bd41c415
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Isaac Newton (1642 to 1727) was an English mathematician and physicist whose laws of motion and universal gravitation became the bedrock of classical mechanics.

His 'Principia' (1687) sets out the laws of motion and the law of gravitation, the same pull for a falling stone and a wheeling moon.

He developed calculus independently of Leibniz, and a quarrel over priority followed.

He studied optics, showing that white light is woven from colors.

He also gave much time to alchemy and theology.
