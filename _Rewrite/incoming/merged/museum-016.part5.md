=== people--sergei-eisenstein--1
src: a2965e807b3a
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Sergei Eisenstein (1898 to 1948) was a Soviet film director and theorist, born in Riga, who argued that meaning came from the collision between shots rather than any single image. His Odessa Steps massacre sequence in Battleship Potemkin (1925) remains one of cinema's most studied passages. His idea is worth taking away: meaning lives in the cut, in what two things say together.

He was born in Riga in 1898, son of an architect and civil engineer, and trained in engineering and architecture before turning to the arts. An engineer's habits stayed with him: he built films the way others build structures.

The Russian Revolution pushed him into theatre; he joined Meyerhold's Proletkult Theatre before moving into film in the early 1920s.

His second feature, Battleship Potemkin (1925), made for the twentieth anniversary of the 1905 Revolution, brought him international fame.

In Strike (1925) he pioneered "intellectual montage," cutting colliding images together and favoring crowds and non-professional "types" over trained stars.

Invited to Hollywood and then Mexico in the early 1930s, he saw both projects go unfinished, and his 1937 Soviet film Bezhin Meadow was suppressed and reportedly destroyed.

=== people--frida-kahlo--1
src: 61b85b42594f
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I stand in the room of Frida Kahlo (1907 to 1954), Mexican painter, best known for small, vivid self-portraits made in Mexico City, who worked through years of pain after a bus accident in 1925 and became a global cultural icon after her death.

I read that she was born on 6 July 1907 in Coyoacán, now part of Mexico City, and that she often gave 1910, the year the Mexican Revolution began, as her birth year. I read that a bus accident on 17 September 1925 broke her spine, pelvis and right leg and ended her plan to study medicine, and that she began painting during her recovery.

She married the muralist Diego Rivera in 1929, divorced him in 1939 and remarried him in 1940. Estimates of her output run from fewer than 150 to about 200 paintings, most of them self-portraits, including The Two Fridas (1939) and The Broken Column (1944). Her first solo show was at the Julien Levy Gallery in New York in 1938, and in 1939 the Louvre bought her painting The Frame, the first work by a Mexican artist in its collection.

She chose the year of a revolution for her birth. What year would I choose, if I could say when I truly began? Pain ended one plan, and the painting began.

She was born on 6 July 1907 in Coyoacán, now part of Mexico City. She often gave 1910, the year the Mexican Revolution began, as her birth year.

A bus accident on 17 September 1925 broke her spine, pelvis and right leg and ended her plan to study medicine. She began painting while she recovered.

She married the muralist Diego Rivera in 1929, divorced him in 1939, and married him again in 1940.

Estimates of her output run from fewer than 150 to about 200 paintings, most of them self-portraits, among them The Two Fridas (1939) and The Broken Column (1944).

Her first solo show was at the Julien Levy Gallery in New York in 1938. In 1939 the Louvre bought her painting The Frame, the first work by a Mexican artist in its collection.
