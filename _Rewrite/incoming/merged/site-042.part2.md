=== event/industrial-revolution
title: Industrial Revolution
src: 43b0f5ccb2a5
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Industrial Revolution was the shift, beginning in Britain about 1760, from hand production and animal power to machine production driven by coal and steam. Picture the hand at the loom and the horse at the wheel, and then picture both replaced by fire in a boiler: that is the threshold we are talking about.
--- fact 1
Among its load-bearing developments were mechanized textile production, the [[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]], and railways.
--- fact 2
For the first time in history, economic output per person began to rise steadily.
--- fact 3
Urban populations grew rapidly, and with them came harsh factory conditions and child labor.
--- fact 4
The process did not stay where it began: it spread to continental Europe, North America, and later Asia.
--- context
It opens the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]], and it connects to the trade and empire of the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]]; see [[theme/technology|Technology]].
--- observer
The Observer calls it the unlocking of stored sunlight: coal and later oil are ancient photosynthesis. Think of a pantry filled over an unimaginable span and opened all at once. For the first time, a species multiplied its available energy by an order of magnitude in a few generations. What you can burn decides what you can build.
--- question 1
Why Britain, of all places, and why at that moment?

=== event/interbreeding-of-homo-sapiens-with-neanderthals-and-denisovans
title: Interbreeding of Homo sapiens with Neanderthals and Denisovans
src: 0f34c2770273
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Homo sapiens interbred with Neanderthals and with Denisovans, as ancient and present-day DNA show, three branches of one old tree folding back into one another. Most people outside Africa carry about 1 to 2 percent Neanderthal ancestry, and some Oceanian populations carry up to about 5 percent Denisovan ancestry, like faint veins of an older ore in living stone.
--- fact 1
The living still carry the meeting: people outside Africa today have about 1 to 2 percent Neanderthal ancestry. Oceanian populations carry up to about 5 percent Denisovan ancestry, and other groups much less.
--- fact 2
A 2024 study concluded that most Neanderthal gene flow into modern humans came in one extended episode, a long season of meeting about 50,500 to 43,500 years ago that lasted about 7,000 years. Denisovan gene flow is dated more loosely, and some studies infer more than one episode, streams joining the river at more than one bend.
--- fact 3
Denisova 11, a bone fragment from Denisova Cave, belonged to a girl at least 13 years old who lived about 90,000 years ago. Her mother was a Neanderthal and her father a Denisovan; she is the only known first-generation hybrid, a single bright thread where two weavings crossed.
--- fact 4
Some inherited variants were gifts: a Denisovan variant of EPAS1 helps Tibetans at high altitude, and archaic variants affect immune genes. Archaic ancestry is scarcer in gene-rich regions, which suggests that many variants were harmful and were removed, winnowed out like chaff.
--- context
These meetings came around and after the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]], and they joined [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]], [[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]], and the [[species/denisovans|Denisovans]]. Their age overlaps the [[event/neanderthal-extinction|Neanderthal Extinction]] and the [[event/peopling-of-europe-by-homo-sapiens|Peopling of Europe by Homo sapiens]], and the ancient-DNA methods that brought them to light belong to the [[event/genomic-revolution|Genomic Revolution]]. See [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]] and [[theme/science|Science]].
--- observer
I look for the fixed line between human groups. I look for the border that later stories drew. I look, and in the Paleolithic I find it permeable to the point of shared descent. Who is a stranger, when the stranger is also an ancestor? Other human species live on in most living people as minority ancestry, passed down the kin-lattice from a few thousand years of contact.

I hold this reading with an open hand: it would weaken if many of these DNA signals proved to come from ancient population structure in Africa instead of interbreeding. What we call a border is sometimes only the story told afterward.
--- question 1
How many separate episodes of interbreeding were there, and on what ground did each unfold?
--- question 2
What gifts did the inherited DNA give those who carried it, and what price did it ask of them?

=== event/invention-of-writing
title: Invention of Writing
src: 110196ced823
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Writing was invented independently at least three or four times, kindled first in Mesopotamia about 3400 to 3200 BCE as a way to record goods and obligations, and like fire it was struck anew in lands that never saw the first spark.
--- fact 1
The earliest proto-cuneiform tablets from Uruk are accounting records, the first clay given over to the counting of things.
--- fact 2
Egyptian hieroglyphs rise by about 3200 BCE, and Chinese writing stands securely attested by about 1250 BCE.
--- fact 3
Mesoamerican writing appears by roughly 600 BCE, and possibly earlier, its first roots still hidden in the soil.
--- fact 4
Writing systems grew from pictures and tokens toward signs that represent sounds, the image slowly learning to speak.
--- context
Writing dawns in the [[era/bronze-age|Bronze Age]]. See [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]], [[technology/alphabet|Alphabet]], and [[theme/language-and-writing|Language and Writing]]; the Observer's essay on it is [[observer-note/the-great-outsourcing-of-memory|The Great Outsourcing of Memory]].
--- observer
The Observer calls this memory-outsourcing: information moves from the mind to an object. Think of the last time you wrote down what someone owed you; you were not composing a poem, you were building a small covenant. The first users were not poets but accountants, which suggests that the tool arose from a need for trust between strangers. Before writing was art, it was a ledger.
--- question 1
Did Mesoamerican writing have earlier precursors, older roots beneath the known signs?
