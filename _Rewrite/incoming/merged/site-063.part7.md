=== source/partition-of-india-search-summaries

--- summary
British India was divided into India and Pakistan in 1947, along a line that Cyril Radcliffe's Boundary Commission drew in a matter of weeks using out-of-date maps and census data; death estimates run from 1 to 2 million, up to 3 million in some sources, with about 15 million displaced, and Gandhi was assassinated on 30 January 1948.
--- extra 1: Caveats
This note stands only on web search result summaries from 2026-09-25 — EBSCO Research Starters, The Conversation, The Quint, Wikipedia and Facts and Details among the results — and none of the pages was opened. Death estimates remain contested, and no reliable count exists; the vault's earlier lower bound of about 200,000 was not supported by these summaries and has been removed.

=== source/pigati-et-al-2023-white-sands-footprints
src: 38cac7cf1b02
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The age of the White Sands footprints — reportedly about 21,000 to 23,000 years ago, from earlier radiocarbon dating of Ruppia seeds — rests here on two new checks named in the abstract: calibrated radiocarbon ages of terrestrial pollen from the same stratigraphic horizons, and optically stimulated luminescence ages of sediments from the footprint-bearing sequence. The authors conclude that the original chronology is robust, and that humans were present in North America during the Last Glacial Maximum.
--- extra 1: Caveats
Only the abstract was read here — the PubMed record, fetched on 2026-09-25 through NCBI E-utilities — since science.org returns HTTP 403 and the PubMed web page shows a cookie wall, neither of which was bypassed, and no open-access copy of the full paper turned up. So this source confirms only what the abstract itself says: it calls the dated material 'terrestrial pollen' and the luminescence dating 'of sediments', which means the wording 'conifer pollen', 'quartz' and the year of the first seed dates, 2021, that the Peopling of the Americas note used to carry are not confirmed here and were removed from that note until the full paper can be read; sample counts, error ranges and methods remain unread as well. The abstract says the earlier seed ages were controversial because of possible old-carbon reservoir effects. Whether the age is universally accepted is not something the abstract can show, so the vault treats it as well supported but contested, following Wikipedia on the Settlement of the Americas.
