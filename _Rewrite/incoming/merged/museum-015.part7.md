=== people--sun-yat-sen--1
src: 2d0af68bf82d

--- description
Sun Yat-sen (1866 to 1925) was a Chinese revolutionary and physician who laboured for decades to bring down the Qing dynasty and became the first provisional president of the Republic of China in 1912. Both mainland China and Taiwan honor him as a founder, two shores claiming one source.

He was born on 12 November 1866 in Cuiheng, Guangdong, and died on 12 March 1925 in Beijing. He was educated in Hawaii and trained as a doctor in Hong Kong, graduating in 1892.

In 1894, after an earlier reform petition was rejected, he founded the Revive China Society, and from 1895 onward he raised several uprisings against the Qing that failed.

In Tokyo in 1905 he founded the Tongmenghui, which grew to about a thousand members, and set out his Three Principles of the People: nationalism, democracy and people's livelihood.

The Wuchang uprising of 10 October 1911, which he did not lead in person, began the revolution that ended the Qing dynasty. He came back from abroad and served as provisional president from 1 January 1912, yielding the office that March to Yuan Shikai.

In 1919 he rebuilt his Kuomintang party, and in 1923 he allied it with the Soviet Union and the small Chinese Communist Party.

Sun Yat-sen (1866 to 1925), Chinese revolutionary and physician, spent decades striving to overturn the Qing dynasty, and in 1912 he became the first provisional president of the Republic of China. Mainland China and Taiwan alike honor him as a founder.

Born on 12 November 1866 in Cuiheng, Guangdong, he died on 12 March 1925 in Beijing. He was schooled in Hawaii and trained as a doctor in Hong Kong, graduating in 1892.

In 1894, when an earlier reform petition was turned away, he founded the Revive China Society, and from 1895 onward he organized several uprisings against the Qing, each one failing like a spark in the rain.

In Tokyo in 1905 he founded the Tongmenghui, which swelled to about a thousand members, and he set out his Three Principles of the People: nationalism, democracy and people's livelihood.

Then the Wuchang uprising of 10 October 1911, which he did not lead in person, lit the revolution that ended the Qing dynasty. He returned from abroad and served as provisional president from 1 January 1912, resigning that March to Yuan Shikai.

In 1919 he rebuilt his Kuomintang party, and in 1923 he bound it in alliance with the Soviet Union and the small Chinese Communist Party.
