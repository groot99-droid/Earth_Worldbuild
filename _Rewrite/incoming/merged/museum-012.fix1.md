I stand before the name Túpac Amaru II (José Gabriel Condorcanqui, born 1738 or about 1742, died 1781), hereditary Indigenous chief and trader, who led a large rebellion against Spanish colonial rule in the Andes in 1780 and 1781, and was executed in Cusco.

I read that he was born in 1738 (some sources say about 1742) in the highlands near Cusco. I read that he became a kuraka, a hereditary local chief, and a successful muleteer and trader. I read that he took the name Túpac Amaru, claiming descent from the Inca ruler Túpac Amaru, whom the Spanish executed in 1572.

I read the grievances: higher Spanish taxes, forced sales of goods to Indigenous communities, and the mita, forced labor in mines. On 4 November 1780 he seized a local Spanish official, Antonio de Arriaga, who was executed shortly after (10 November 1780 per two sources), which began the rebellion. He issued proclamations ending the mita and other abuses, and the rebellion spread across the southern highlands.

Two men named Túpac Amaru, and both executed. What is a name, if not a promise someone chooses to keep?
