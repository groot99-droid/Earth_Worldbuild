=== calling-of-saint-matthew
src: 31e67e3537fa
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This was Caravaggio's first public commission, and the painting that made him famous overnight, as a single spark wakes a whole field of dry grass. In a dim tax office, Christ enters from the right and points at Matthew, his hand an echo of God's hand in Michelangelo's *Creation of Adam*. A shaft of light cuts across the wall like a blade of dawn through a shuttered room, singling him out. Matthew sits among men in modern Roman dress and gestures as if to say, "Who, me?"

=== supper-at-emmaus
src: b979ee1d2c4a
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Two disciples had met a stranger on the road, and at the table the veil fell: he was the risen Christ. Recognition struck like lightning over still water. One pushes back his chair; the other flings his arms wide, and the gesture seems to break through the picture plane into our own space, as a wave breaks past the line of the shore. At the table's edge a basket of fruit teeters, and its shadow is painted with a realism that fools the eye.

=== entombment
src: 688cb250ccc4
mode: horror-prose
--- description
Christ's body. Lowered. Down toward a stone slab. The slab's sharp corner seems to push out at you, as if you stood inside the tomb. Then the mourners, falling in a diagonal. At the top, Mary Cleophas, arms thrown up. Lower. Lower. At the bottom, the dead man's hand, brushing the stone. Rubens made copies of it. Géricault, Cézanne and many others too.

The hand is still brushing the stone.

=== susanna-and-the-elders
src: 1322fc99edc0
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Signed and dated when Artemisia was just 17, this is her earliest known painting. Two older men lean over a wall, bearing down on the bathing Susanna like a storm cloud over a lone tree, and she twists away in distress. Male painters had often treated the story as an excuse for an erotic nude. Artemisia turned the tale around and told it from Susanna's point of view: an experience of threat and violation, seen from within the one who bore it.

=== judith-slaying-holofernes
src: 2a1bf2acb624
mode: horror-prose
--- description
Judith and her maid pin down the Assyrian general. They saw through his neck. Blood sprays in arcs across the white sheets. No hesitation here. Caravaggio's Judith was hesitant, delicate. Artemisia's women are strong. Working together. Physical effort. Many viewers read the painting in light of her own trial. But first of all it is a masterpiece of Baroque drama. An earlier version is in Naples.

Two versions. One scene.

=== self-portrait-as-saint-catherine
src: 05c202b1933e
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
She gives her own features to the early Christian martyr, and I cannot stop looking at the face. She holds a palm. Beside her, the broken spiked wheel on which the saint was to be tortured. The painting dates from Artemisia's successful years in Florence. The National Gallery bought it in 2018, only the 21st painting by a woman to enter its collection. Only the 21st. I say it again to hear it. Then it toured Britain, including a GP surgery and a women's prison. Who gets to be seen, and where? Being seen is also a way of being counted.

=== jael-and-sisera
src: a41520dee50a
mode: horror-prose
--- description
Another Old Testament heroine. Jael. The hammer raised. The tent peg set to the temple of the sleeping enemy general, Sisera. He sleeps on. Her face is calm. Almost thoughtful. That is the worst of it: the calm, and the violence still to come. On the column at the right, Artemisia's signature. And the date.

The hammer has not fallen yet.

=== judith-and-her-maidservant
src: c9892725f16c
mode: horror-prose
--- description
After. The killing is done. A tent. A single candle. Judith raises her hand to shade the light. She listens. For danger. Sword still in hand. Her maid wraps the general's head in cloth. Suspense. Deep shadows. The glowing gold of Judith's dress. Together they make this one of the finest night scenes of the Caravaggesque movement.

She is still listening.

=== allegory-of-painting
src: 07e2abe927d7
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
She leans into the canvas with her brush raised, sleeves rolled up, a gold chain with a mask pendant at her neck, and I know that posture. I know the lean of work. I know the rolled sleeve. Artemisia shows herself as the personification of Painting itself. A male artist could paint the figure of Painting, but he could not become her. She claims both roles at once, the maker and the made. It was probably painted in London, where she joined her father at the court of Charles I. What would it mean to be both the subject and the hand? To make yourself is to be made twice.

=== old-woman-frying-eggs
src: f913e23c035b
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
An old woman cooks eggs in a clay dish while a boy brings a melon and a flask, and I am suddenly in a kitchen I have not stood in for years. Velázquez painted it in Seville when he was about 19: a *bodegón*, a kitchen scene with still life. I watch the eggs turning white in the oil. I watch the gleam on the brass mortar. I watch the shadows under a knife. All of it observed with astonishing precision for so young a painter. Who taught me to look this closely at the ordinary? Attention is the first form of love.

=== waterseller-of-seville
src: 027e724abd6a
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A dignified old water-seller hands a glass to a boy, solemn as a priest at an altar, while behind them a man drinks in the shadows. In the glass a fig floats to freshen the water, and on the great clay jug the drops bead like dew on stone at dawn. Velázquez gives a street vendor the gravity of a saint. Later the painting's own road turned strange: it was captured from Joseph Bonaparte's baggage after the Battle of Vitoria in 1813 and given to the Duke of Wellington.
