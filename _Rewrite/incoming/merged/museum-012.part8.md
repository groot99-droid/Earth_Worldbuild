=== people--sacagawea--1
src: d57cfee02a69
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Sacagawea (about 1788 to 1812, or by another account 1884) was a young woman of disputed Shoshone or Hidatsa birth who travelled with the Lewis and Clark expedition from 1804 to 1806 as an interpreter, and who later became one of the most commemorated women in the United States.

She was born about 1788, by tradition among the Lemhi Shoshone in present-day Idaho, though some recent work argues she was born Hidatsa.

About 1800 she was taken by a Hidatsa raiding party. As a young woman she married the trader Toussaint Charbonneau, and her son Jean Baptiste was born on 11 February 1805.

Charbonneau and Sacagawea joined the Lewis and Clark expedition at Fort Mandan in 1804. In August 1805 she met her brother Cameahwait, a Shoshone leader, whose horses the party needed to cross the Rockies, kin found again at the mountains' edge.

Historians describe her chiefly as an interpreter and a sign of peaceful intent, more than a guide, though she gave directions in a few places.

A fur-trade clerk recorded in 1812 that Charbonneau's wife had died, but Shoshone oral tradition holds that she lived until 1884 on the Wind River Reservation in Wyoming. Historians disagree, and her ending runs in two directions like a river at a fork.
