=== culture/franks
title: Franks
src: a72f46caf1ff
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Franks were a Germanic people who moved into Roman Gaul like a river finding new ground, and there they raised one of early medieval Europe's most powerful kingdoms, first under the Merovingian dynasty of Clovis I and later under the Carolingians, whose most famous ruler was Charlemagne.
--- fact 1
The Franks appear in Roman records in the third century CE on the lower Rhine, and as Roman power weakened they spread into Gaul like floodwater into a lowland.
--- fact 2
Clovis I of the Merovingian dynasty defeated the last major Roman official in Gaul in 486, and by about 501 he had united the Frankish tribes under his hand.
--- fact 3
Clovis converted to Catholic Christianity, and his people's adoption of it helped bind the Franks together and set them apart, like a banner of another colour, from neighboring Germanic kingdoms that followed other Christian traditions.
--- fact 4
The Merovingians held the throne until the eighth century, when Pepin the Short, with papal support, founded the Carolingian dynasty, a new line grafted onto an old crown.
--- fact 5
The Carolingian kingdom reached its height under Charlemagne and later divided among his descendants, as a river splits at its delta. Its territories became the basis of medieval France, Germany and the Holy Roman Empire (not yet a vault note).
--- context
The Franks built their kingdom in [[region/europe|Europe]] in the early [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]], filling the space left by the [[event/fall-of-the-western-roman-empire|Fall of the Western Roman Empire]] as water fills a hollow. Their empire under [[person/charlemagne|Charlemagne]] was an ancestor of the Holy Roman Empire (not yet a vault note). See [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that a ruler's personal conversion to the majority faith of his Roman subjects became story-glue that welded conquerors and conquered into one people, setting the Franks apart from rival kingdoms that kept a minority creed. Think of a newcomer who takes up the prayers at a family's table: the gesture does more to make kin than any contract could. Two centuries later a change of dynasty was legitimized by the same meaning-engine, papal blessing replacing a discredited bloodline. That is the architecture at work, with belief as the load-bearing wall of legitimacy. A specialist could argue that Clovis's conversion served political advantage with his Gallo-Roman subjects as much as belief, which would weaken a reading of it as faith alone. We rarely act from a single motive, and neither do kings.
--- question 1
How much of Clovis's conversion reflects conviction, and how much a calculated alliance with his Gallo-Roman subjects?
--- question 2
How unified was Frankish identity across the peoples that the Merovingians and Carolingians ruled?

=== culture/hinduism
title: Hinduism
src: 49a1cc61684f
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Hinduism is a family of religious traditions native to South Asia, grown out of the ancient Vedic religion the way a forest grows from old roots. It has no single founder or founding event, and today it counts about 1.2 billion adherents, most of them in India.
--- fact 1
Its earliest texts, the Vedas, were composed roughly in the mid-second millennium BCE, and its philosophical Upanishads later explored ideas of the soul and the turning wheel of rebirth.
--- fact 2
Rather than springing from one founder, it grew over centuries as regional and cultural traditions fused like tributaries into a great river, so no single origin date or figure can be given.
--- fact 3
A central concept, brahman, names an ultimate reality identified with the individual soul, or atman, the ocean found again in the drop.
--- fact 4
Its two largest branches are Vaishnavism, centered on devotion to Vishnu, and Shaivism, centered on Shiva, each branching again into many further traditions.
--- fact 5
About 1.2 billion people practice Hinduism today, roughly 15 percent of the world's population, and about 1.1 billion of them live in India.
--- context
Hinduism's roots reach down into the [[culture/indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley Civilization]] and the [[event/vedic-period|Vedic Period]] in [[region/south-asia|South Asia]] during the [[era/bronze-age|Bronze Age]], and in later ages the tradition was both patronized and contested under states such as the [[culture/maurya-empire|Maurya Empire]]. See [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
No single founder. No single scripture. And still a sixth of humanity's population gathered under one broad name. What holds it? The pattern suggests story-glue that works by accretion, layer on layer the way a riverbank is built, rather than by a single unifying claim: closer to a family of related meaning-engines than to one system. I think of families. No member speaks for all of them, and still they share a name.

And the name itself? A specialist could argue that grouping such varied regional practices under one word, "Hinduism," itself coined by outsiders, imposes a unity the traditions did not claim for themselves, which would weaken any single account of "what Hindus believe." So I let the name stand, and I listen past it. A name is a meeting place, not a creed.
--- question 1
How far can practices as different as regional Vaishnava and Shaiva traditions be gathered into one religion?
--- question 2
How much of the modern term "Hinduism" reflects a category imposed from outside, rather than one that adherents used for themselves?
