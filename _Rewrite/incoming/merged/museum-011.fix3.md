Christopher Wren (1632 to 1723) was an English mathematician and astronomer, a founder of the Royal Society, who turned architect after the Great Fire of London in 1666. He designed more than fifty City churches and a new St Paul's Cathedral, topped out in 1708 and declared complete by Parliament in 1711, rising from the ash like a mountain.

He was born in East Knoyle, Wiltshire, son of a clergyman. At 25 he was appointed professor of astronomy at Gresham College, then Savilian Professor of Astronomy at Oxford, and in 1662 he was a founding member of the Royal Society.

His work as an architect began with the Sheldonian Theatre in Oxford. The Great Fire of September 1666 destroyed old St Paul's and most of the City.

In 1669 he was appointed Surveyor of the King's Works, and he gave the rest of his working life to the City's parish churches and to St Paul's. He died in 1723, aged 90, and lies in the cathedral crypt.

He joined the classical vocabulary of Inigo Jones and Palladio with French and Italian Baroque elements he studied on a research trip to Paris, the only time he travelled outside England.

The dome of St Paul's is three independent shells nested one inside another, so that the outer profile, the inner ceiling and the structural core could each be shaped for its own purpose.
