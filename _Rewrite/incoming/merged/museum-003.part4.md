=== surrender-of-breda
src: a5624fcb4af6
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
How you win says more about you than the winning. This canvas was painted for the Hall of Realms in the Buen Retiro Palace, and it commemorates a Spanish victory of 1625 in the war with the Dutch. Yet look at what Velázquez chose to show. Not triumph, but courtesy: the Spanish general Ambrogio Spinola lays a consoling hand on the shoulder of the defeated Justinus of Nassau as he hands over the city's keys. That hand is the whole doctrine of the picture. Behind the Spanish, a forest of upright lances gave the painting its popular name, *Las Lanzas*. The lances record the force; the hand records the grace. Victory is a ledger; courtesy is a covenant.

=== rokeby-venus
src: 74a003675a5d
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This is the only surviving female nude by Velázquez, a rare flower in the stern soil of Catholic Spain. Venus reclines with her back to us and gazes into a mirror held by Cupid, and there her face appears only as a soft blur, like a moon seen through mist. In 1914 the suffragette Mary Richardson slashed the canvas with a meat cleaver in protest at the arrest of Emmeline Pankhurst. The wound was skilfully restored, and the goddess reclines still.

=== innocent-x
src: f4abfae4b74c
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
His gaze finds me before I find him: sharp, suspicious, fixed on the viewer, and today the viewer is me. Velázquez painted him during his second trip to Italy, the 75-year-old pope in shimmering red and white. Innocent is said to have remarked that it was *troppo vero*, "too true". I know that flinch. I have met my own likeness and looked away. Three centuries later it obsessed Francis Bacon, who painted dozens of screaming variations on it. What is it about being seen truly that makes us want to scream? The truest likeness is the one we cannot forgive.

=== las-meninas
src: 1911452320a1
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
The little Infanta Margarita stands at the heart of the room like a lamp in a dim hall, gathered about by her maids of honour, a dwarf, a dog and courtiers. At the left Velázquez himself stands at a vast canvas, brush poised, a watcher inside his own tale. On the back wall hangs a mirror, and in it appear the king and queen, standing, it seems, exactly where we stand, as though the glass had dissolved the threshold between their world and ours. Who is looking at whom? Its riddles of seeing and representation have made it perhaps the most analysed painting in Western art.

=== anatomy-lesson
src: 46e012f58d6f
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This was Rembrandt's first big commission in Amsterdam, painted when he was 26. The city's leading anatomist demonstrates the muscles of a dissected arm, and seven surgeons lean in around him like reeds bent toward a single current, each face lit with its own kind of attention. Group portraits had usually been stiff rows of heads, set out like stones along a wall. Rembrandt turned this one into a dramatic scene of learning, and it made his name.

=== night-watch
src: 2744ba9eaa53
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A company of civic guardsmen bursts into motion as their captain gives the order to march, like a river breaking through a winter dam. Muskets are loaded, a drum beats, a dog barks, and among them a glowing girl carries the company's emblem, a chicken's claw, bright as a lantern in the throng. Yet this is no night scene. The name came from darkened varnish, dusk laid over a field of day, and later restorations removed it. Since 2019 the Rijksmuseum has studied and restored it in public, in a glass room.

=== hundred-guilder-print
src: 6ef58ff725a5
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Rembrandt's most ambitious print gathers several episodes from Matthew 19 around a radiant Christ, as a hearth gathers the cold: the sick being brought to him, mothers with children, arguing Pharisees and a rich young man. On the left the plate is densely worked, dark as a forest at dusk; on the right it is left almost bare, pale as morning. Its nickname comes from the very high price it was said to fetch.

=== self-portrait-with-two-circles
src: 5f5f688cba1c
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
He stands before me with palette and brushes, in a plain fur-lined robe and a white cap, looking straight out, and I do not look away. This is one of the last and greatest of Rembrandt's roughly 80 self-portraits. The artist is ageing. He is bankrupt. He is still holding his brushes. Behind him, two mysterious arcs on the wall have been read as symbols of perfection, a map of the world, or a display of the painter's skill. I remember my own bad years. Which circle would I draw around my life? When the rest is gone, the looking remains.

=== jewish-bride
src: 51a404a5a8b9
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
A couple stands in a tender embrace, probably portrayed as the biblical Isaac and Rebecca. His hand rests on her breast. Her hand touches his. I have known that gesture, the hand that answers a hand. The sleeves are built up with thick paint applied with a palette knife, glowing gold and red. Vincent van Gogh said he would give ten years of his life to sit in front of it for a fortnight with only a crust of bread. What would I give? Tenderness is the one thing paint cannot fake.

=== prodigal-son
src: 3e403340fe3d
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
The ragged son had come home at last. He kneels, shaven-headed and barefoot, and presses his face against his old father's chest, as a spent river presses into the sea. The father rests both hands on his back in forgiveness, and the light gathers there like warmth in a hearth. From the right the elder brother watches, cold as a winter stone. It was painted near the end of Rembrandt's life, and it is one of the most moving images of mercy in art.

=== st-pauls-cathedral
src: 948952b350dd
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
What you see is rarely what holds you up. This is Wren's masterpiece, the building he spent 35 years on, rising on the site of a medieval cathedral gutted in the Great Fire of London. Consider its dome, which is really three shells in one. A shallow inner dome, seen from the floor. A hidden brick cone that carries the real structural load. A tall outer dome of timber and lead that gives St Paul's its skyline silhouette. Said plainly: the beauty you admire and the strength that bears the weight are different layers of one architecture. Wren's son laid the final stone in 1710. A life's work can be finished by another's hand.
