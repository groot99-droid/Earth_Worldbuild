=== era/neoproterozoic-era
src: 8a36d0ac861f
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Neoproterozoic Era is the last era of the Proterozoic Eon, a long dusk running from 1.0 billion to 538.8 million years ago. In its keeping lie three great turnings: the supercontinent Rodinia breaking apart like ice on a spring river, the global glaciations known as Snowball Earth, and the first large complex organisms, stirring at last.
--- fact 1
Three periods fill the Neoproterozoic Era's span of 1.0 billion to 538.8 million years ago, as three chambers fill one hall: the [[era/tonian-period|Tonian Period]], the [[era/cryogenian-period|Cryogenian Period]] and the [[era/ediacaran-period|Ediacaran Period]]. Its upper threshold is the base of the [[era/cambrian-period|Cambrian Period]].
--- fact 2
Rodinia, the supercontinent gathered together by about 1.0 billion years ago, began to rift apart along its seams roughly 800 to 750 million years ago, like a great slab of ice parting on dark water.
--- fact 3
Two great freezes of the Cryogenian, the Sturtian (about 717 to 660 million years ago) and the Marinoan (about 650 to 635 million years ago), are the bedrock on which the [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]] hypothesis stands.
--- fact 4
Oxygen is proposed to have risen again, a second slow breath, sometime between about 800 and 540 million years ago; when it came and how great it grew are debated.
--- fact 5
Molecular-clock estimates set the birth of animals in or before the Cryogenian, yet the fossil record of the first animals is disputed; by about 575 million years ago the [[species/ediacaran-biota|Ediacaran Biota]], large and soft-bodied organisms, had appeared.
--- context
After the [[era/mesoproterozoic-era|Mesoproterozoic Era]] the Neoproterozoic Era came, the closing chapter of the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]], and it yielded, as night yields to a sudden morning, to the [[era/paleozoic-era|Paleozoic Era]] at the [[event/cambrian-explosion|Cambrian Explosion]]. Its thread runs through the theme of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
We love a staircase. Animals arrive at the end of the Neoproterozoic, and so we are tempted to read the ice, the oxygen and the break-up of Rodinia as steps built toward them, an architecture with one doorway at the top. The Observer declines that reading. These were physical events with their own causes, and animals were one of several possible outcomes. Think of tracing a river back from the sea and deciding the mountains were raised to feed it: that is hindsight dressed as design. Evidence that any one of them was strictly required for animals would weaken this view. Until then, a sequence is not a plan.
--- question 1
Did the Cryogenian ice and the second rise of oxygen help bring forth the animals, and how much did they contribute, if at all?
--- question 2
When, in that long twilight, did the first animals appear?

=== era/ordovician-period
src: 9cfbb46d4926
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Ordovician Period, from 485.4 to 443.8 million years ago, is the second period of the Paleozoic Era, an age when the seas brimmed with new life. It saw a great rise in marine biodiversity, the first evidence of plants on land, and, at its close, the first of the "Big Five" mass extinctions, a shadow falling across bright water.
--- fact 1
The Ordovician Period spans 485.4 to 443.8 million years ago, and two small creatures keep its gates: its base is fixed at Green Point, Newfoundland, by the conodont Iapetognathus fluctivagus, and its top at Dob's Linn in Scotland by a graptolite.
--- fact 2
The Great Ordovician Biodiversification, a slow flood running for tens of millions of years from the early period, greatly multiplied the diversity of marine animals, as a reef multiplies its colours.
--- fact 3
The sea stood very high, and its warm shallows cradled reefs, brachiopods, trilobites, crinoids and graptolites, while straight-shelled nautiloid cephalopods, some several metres long, moved among them as large predators.
--- fact 4
From this period comes the earliest evidence of land plants: spores about 470 million years old, a first faint dust of life upon bare ground; see [[event/colonization-of-land|Colonization of Land]].
--- fact 5
Late in the period the climate cooled, and an ice age centred on Gondwana set in during the Hirnantian; the [[event/late-ordovician-extinction|Late Ordovician Extinction]], about 445 to 443 million years ago, swept away an estimated 85 percent of species, mostly marine, like a tide that went out and did not return.
--- context
Between the [[era/cambrian-period|Cambrian Period]] before it and the [[era/silurian-period|Silurian Period]] after, the Ordovician Period stands as one hall in the long house of the [[era/paleozoic-era|Paleozoic Era]]. Its thread is woven into the theme of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer records two movements in one period: a great rise in marine diversity and, near its end, a mass extinction linked to glaciation. You will want to call the first progress and the second a punishment. Resist both words. Both arose from shifting planetary conditions, and neither was directed at any outcome; one ledger holds the gain and the loss. Think of a shoreline after a season of storms, reshaped by many pushes, none of them aiming. A demonstration that diversity rose steadily whatever the climate did would weaken the reading that a biosphere's history is the sum of many pushes, not a trend. A pile of pushes is not a staircase.
--- question 1
What stirred the Great Ordovician Biodiversification into being: climate, ocean chemistry, ecological change, or some braid of them all?
--- question 2
Why did the ice come when carbon dioxide is estimated to have stood so high, a winter falling on a warm house?

=== era/paleogene-period
src: 409dab2489dc
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Paleogene Period, from 66 to 23.03 million years ago, is the first period of the Cenozoic Era, a long morning after ruin. In it the mammals spread outward after the end-Cretaceous extinction, and the world turned from a warm, largely ice-free house toward a cold one crowned with polar ice.
--- fact 1
The Paleogene Period spans 66.0 to 23.03 million years ago and holds three epochs, the Paleocene, Eocene and Oligocene, like three rooms of one hall; its lower threshold, the [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]] boundary, is fixed at El Kef in Tunisia, and its upper, the base of the Neogene, at Lemme-Carrosio in Italy.
--- fact 2
Mammals spread swiftly into the roles the extinction had left empty, and the earliest primates and their close kin appear; over about ten million years, from about 50 to 40 million years ago, whales were drawn out of land-dwelling hoofed mammals, the way a river is drawn to the sea.
--- fact 3
The [[event/paleocene-eocene-thermal-maximum|Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum]], about 56 million years ago, set the world to fever, warming it by roughly 5 to 6 degrees Celsius for about 200,000 years, and the early Eocene burned among the warmest intervals of the Cenozoic.
--- fact 4
The [[event/collision-of-india-and-asia|Collision of India and Asia]], beginning about 50 million years ago, started to raise the Himalaya, a wave of stone rising slowly toward the sky.
--- fact 5
About 34 million years ago, at the Eocene-Oligocene boundary, the climate cooled sharply and ice sheets began to grow on Antarctica like frost across a pane; falling carbon dioxide and the opening of Southern Ocean gateways are proposed causes.
--- context
After the [[era/cretaceous-period|Cretaceous Period]] the Paleogene Period came, opening the [[era/cenozoic-era|Cenozoic Era]] as a first door opens on a new house, and after it the [[era/neogene-period|Neogene Period]]. Its thread runs through the theme of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
I go to the Paleogene looking for a beginning. I find a vacated world, where mammals spread into the roles the extinction left empty. I find a world cooling, the polar ice taking hold. I find the primates, the group that includes humans, and they are one lineage among many in that spreading, with nothing in the record to mark them as special at the time. Who promised them anything? No one, the stone says.

The reading would weaken if primates could be shown to have been headed for prominence independently of the extinction, and I would welcome the correction. A lineage is not a destiny.
--- question 1
Was Purgatorius, which lived just after the extinction, a true primate, or a close relative of primates waiting at the edge of the family?
--- question 2
In the Eocene-Oligocene cooling, how much of the cold came through the opening of Southern Ocean gateways, and how much from falling carbon dioxide?

=== era/paleolithic
src: 70f7dd7510e6
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Paleolithic, the Old Stone Age, is the long era of chipped-stone tools and foraging societies, stretching like a road beneath changing skies from about 2.6 million years ago to the end of the last ice age.
--- fact 1
The earliest known stone tools, the first edges struck by a hand, date to about 3.3 million years ago (Lomekwi) and 2.6 million years ago (Oldowan); see [[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]].
--- fact 2
The taming of flame, [[event/control-of-fire|Control of Fire]], is kindled well before the era's end.
--- fact 3
Modern humans spread across the globe like seeds on a long wind; see [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]].
--- fact 4
In its later stages symbolic behavior awakens, the [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]] among it, like first lamps lit at dusk.
--- fact 5
It fills about 99 percent of the time humans and their ancestors have made tools, a vast sea of hours.
--- context
The Paleolithic lies cradled within the [[era/pleistocene-epoch|Pleistocene Epoch]], and it ends with the turning toward farming known as the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]].
--- observer
I count the years, and the count keeps saying the same thing: for roughly 99 percent of the tool-making record, the species lived as small mobile groups. I count the camps. I count the walks. I count the fires. Everything called 'civilization' is a recent variation on a very old pattern. The walls are new; the walking is old.
--- question 1
How wide did Paleolithic social networks reach, and how tightly were their threads knotted together?

=== era/paleoproterozoic-era
src: 97bc618d647e
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: horror-prose
--- summary
The Paleoproterozoic Era is the first era of the Proterozoic Eon, running from 2.5 billion to 1.6 billion years ago. In it free oxygen first settled into the sky and stayed, a new voice in the air's old song, a lasting part of the atmosphere.
--- fact 1
The Paleoproterozoic Era spans 2.5 to 1.6 billion years ago, and its borders, like those of its four periods (Siderian, Rhyacian, Orosirian and Statherian), are drawn by agreed numerical ages rather than by any mark written in the rock.
--- fact 2
The [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]], the first sustained rise of free oxygen, breathed out by the photosynthesis of [[species/cyanobacteria|Cyanobacteria]], began around 2.4 billion years ago; estimates for its first dawning run from about 2.46 to 2.32 billion years ago.
--- fact 3
The Huronian glaciation of Ontario, Canada, a white silence roughly 2.4 to 2.2 billion years ago, is often bound in the telling to the oxygen rise, but that bond remains a hypothesis.
--- fact 4
Banded iron formations, a great wellspring of the world's iron ore, were laid down widely between about 2.5 and 1.8 billion years ago, striped like the rings of an ancient tree, and then largely vanish from the record until the Cryogenian.
--- fact 5
The oldest unambiguous eukaryote fossils, about 1.65 billion years old, lie within this era, while older claimants such as Grypania (about 1.87 billion years) are still debated; see [[event/origin-of-eukaryotes|Origin of Eukaryotes]].
--- context
After the [[era/archean-eon|Archean Eon]] came the Paleoproterozoic Era, and after it the [[era/mesoproterozoic-era|Mesoproterozoic Era]], both within the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]]. Its thread runs through the theme of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]], and its oxygen lingers, like old smoke in a hall, in the chemical background of the later [[event/cambrian-explosion|Cambrian Explosion]].
--- observer
The Observer files the Paleoproterozoic under preconditions. Here it starts. Oxygen. The gas later animals breathe first piled up here as a waste product of microbial metabolism. Exhaust. Poison to much of the life then present. It gathered anyway. Nothing in the record shows it was directed at any outcome. No plan. No aim. Just waste, accumulating.

The reading would weaken if oxygen-producing photosynthesis proved a near-certain result of life on any wet planet. Until then: every animal breath began as waste. And as poison.
--- question 1
When did oxygen-producing photosynthesis first kindle, and how long was its oxygen swallowed by chemistry before it reached the air?
--- question 2
Of the era's ice ages and iron formations, which were children of the oxygen rise, and which were born of other causes?
