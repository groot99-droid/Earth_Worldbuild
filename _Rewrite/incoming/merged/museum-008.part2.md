=== people--frederick-barbarossa--1
src: 9394387f6ccd
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Frederick I, called Barbarossa ("Redbeard"; about 1122 to 1190), was Holy Roman Emperor from 1155 until his death and one of medieval Europe's mightiest rulers, and much of his reign was spent contending for northern Italy.

Elected King of Germany in 1152, he was crowned Holy Roman Emperor by the pope in 1155, and again and again he came down into Italy to assert imperial authority over its wealthy independent city-states.

The Lombard League, an alliance of northern Italian cities backed by the papacy, broke his forces at the Battle of Legnano in 1176, and in the Peace of Constance of 1183 he was made to recognize significant city autonomy.

He arranged the marriage of his son Henry to Constance of Sicily, a match that later brought the Kingdom of Sicily under his family's hand.

In 1190, during the Third Crusade, he drowned while crossing a river in Anatolia, before he reached the Holy Land. With his death much of his army scattered or turned back rather than go on, like a wave breaking before the shore.

=== people--jayavarman-vii--1
src: b273d3375602
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Jayavarman VII (about 1122 to about 1218) was king of the Khmer Empire from about 1181 until his death. He drove out the Cham invaders who had sacked Angkor, and then raised a great building program that included Angkor Thom and the Bayon.

Champa forces sacked Angkor in 1177. Jayavarman VII defeated them and was crowned about 1181; his birth and death years are estimates.

Later, Khmer forces brought parts of Champa under Khmer rule.

He was a Mahayana Buddhist, while most earlier Khmer rulers had favored Hindu cults.

His works include Angkor Thom; the Bayon, its towers carved with large faces, calm as still water; Ta Prohm, dedicated to his mother; and Preah Khan, dedicated to his father.

An inscription of 1186 at Ta Prohm credits him with 102 hospitals, a figure that comes from the king's own inscriptions.

=== people--eleanor-of-aquitaine--1
src: 226ef4b79364
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Eleanor of Aquitaine (about 1124 to 1204, with older sources giving 1122 for her birth) was successively queen of France and queen of England, and one of the wealthiest and most powerful women in 12th-century Europe, because she ruled the Duchy of Aquitaine in her own right. Her power began with land, and that is the key to her story.

She inherited the vast Duchy of Aquitaine in southwestern France in 1137 and married King Louis VII of France that same year. The marriage was annulled in 1152 after producing no sons.

Weeks after the annulment she married Henry of Anjou, who became King Henry II of England in 1154, and her extensive French territories went into the combined "Angevin Empire" that spanned much of France and England.

She supported a rebellion by three of her sons against Henry II in 1173 to 1174. After it failed, Henry kept her in confinement for about fifteen to sixteen years, until his death in 1189.

Freed by her son Richard I ("the Lionheart"), she acted as regent during his crusade and captivity, and later helped secure the throne for her youngest son, John, after Richard's death in 1199. Sovereignty interrupted is not sovereignty lost.

=== people--genghis-khan--1
src: ae4ebf01783c
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Genghis Khan (born Temujin, about 1162 to 1227) united the Mongol tribes in 1206 and founded the Mongol Empire.

He built an army on merit and loyalty rather than clan, a new architecture of allegiance.

His campaigns overran Central Asia, northern China and Persia.

He promoted a written script and a legal code (the Yassa), and he encouraged religious tolerance.

He died in 1227, and the location of his tomb is unknown.
