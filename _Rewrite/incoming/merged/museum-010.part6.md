=== people--pieter-bruegel-the-elder--1
src: 747844e977ac
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Pieter Bruegel the Elder (about 1525 to 1569) was the most important Netherlandish painter of the 16th century. Peasants, proverbs and the turning seasons were his subjects, in crowded panels and sweeping landscapes where the year wheels like a great slow sky. Only about 40 of his paintings survive.

He was probably born between 1525 and 1530, perhaps near Breda in the Duchy of Brabant. In 1551 he became a master in the painters' guild of Antwerp.

In 1552 he travelled to Italy, and his drawings of the Alps on the road home fed the mountain views of his later paintings.

In Antwerp he designed prints for the publisher Hieronymus Cock, some in the manner of Hieronymus Bosch. In 1563 he married Mayken Coecke and moved to Brussels, where he painted most of his masterpieces.

He died in Brussels in September 1569. His sons Pieter Brueghel the Younger and Jan Brueghel the Elder became successful painters and founded a dynasty.

Key works include Netherlandish Proverbs (1559), Children's Games (1560), The Tower of Babel (1563), The Hunters in the Snow (1565) and The Peasant Wedding (about 1567 to 1568).

=== people--philip-ii--1
src: 07c2719510f0
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Philip II (1527 to 1598) was King of Spain from 1556 to 1598, ruling an empire that stretched across the Iberian Peninsula, the Netherlands, parts of Italy, and vast lands in the Americas and the Philippines, which bear his name.

In 1556 he inherited Spain, its Italian territories and the Spanish Americas from his father, Charles V, and through his marriage to Mary I of England he briefly shared the English throne from 1554 to 1558.

He waged a long, costly war to put down a revolt in the Spanish Netherlands, beginning in 1566, that burned on in various phases for decades and was never fully resolved in his reign.

In 1588 he sent the Spanish Armada against England, to support a planned invasion and end English support for Dutch rebels and privateering against Spanish shipping; storms and English naval tactics destroyed much of the fleet, the sea and the wind turning against him.

He presided over Spain's peak territorial extent while driving the crown into bankruptcy again and again, in 1557, 1560, 1575 and 1596, from the cost of near-constant warfare funded partly by American silver.
