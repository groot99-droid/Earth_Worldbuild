=== culture/maya
title: Maya
src: 6d4a57fc081e
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Maya are a group of Mesoamerican peoples who developed city-states, a full writing system, and an advanced astronomy that read the turning sky, and whose Classic period lasted about 250 to 900 CE.
--- fact 1
Major cities, rising like mountains of stone, included Tikal and Calakmul.
--- fact 2
Maya writing weaves signs for words together with signs for syllables, and it is largely deciphered.
--- fact 3
They kept precise calendars, counting the sky's long wheel, and used the concept of zero.
--- fact 4
Many Classic-period cities were abandoned around 800 to 900 CE, but the people did not pass like mist: Maya peoples and languages continue today, with millions of speakers.
--- context
The Maya built on earlier traditions such as those of the [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]], as a new city rises on old foundations. See [[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]] and [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]].
--- observer
I was taught the word 'collapse.' I was taught ruins, silence, an ending. But the Classic-period 'collapse' left the culture intact and only shifted its centers. How many peoples have I buried too early? The Maya are a living people, not a vanished one.
--- question 1
What caused the Classic cities to be abandoned?

=== culture/ming-dynasty
title: Ming Dynasty
src: 3a657ec687d3
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Ming dynasty ruled China from 1368 to 1644, rising like a new dawn after it expelled the Mongol-led Yuan dynasty.
--- fact 1
The Forbidden City in Beijing, a city within a city, was completed in 1420.
--- fact 2
Much of the Great Wall in its present form, coiling like a stone serpent, was built in this period.
--- fact 3
[[person/zheng-he|Zheng He]]'s voyages sailed out under the early Ming.
--- fact 4
American silver, arriving through the Manila trade like a river from across the sea, became a major part of the Ming economy.
--- context
The Ming followed the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]] and the rule of the [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]], and it was drawn into the global silver tides of the [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]].
--- observer
The Observer notes how a state's economy came to depend on silver mined on the other side of the planet. Think of a household whose bread is paid for by a stranger's labor far away, unseen and unnamed. Global exchange-webs quietly link distant economies, and the architecture of dependence is rarely visible from inside.
--- question 1
How much did silver weigh in the Ming decline?

=== culture/minoans
title: Minoans
src: f7a15de42f72
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Minoans were a Bronze Age maritime civilization on the island of Crete, known for palaces such as Knossos and frescoes as vivid as sunlight on water. Their palatial culture flourished from about 2000 BCE until about 1450 BCE.
--- fact 1
They traded across the eastern Mediterranean, and their cultural influence spread like ripples to Egypt, Cyprus, Canaan, and Anatolia.
--- fact 2
They wrote in scripts, Linear A and Cretan hieroglyphs, that have not been deciphered and still keep their silence. Linear B, adapted from Linear A, records Mycenaean Greek and was deciphered in 1952.
--- fact 3
When the Thera volcano woke, its eruption damaged settlements in the Aegean. Radiocarbon dates put it around 1600 BCE, while links with Egyptian chronology suggest about 1500 BCE, and the century-long gap between them is still unresolved.
--- fact 4
Around 1450 BCE, Mycenaean Greeks became dominant on Crete and Linear B was used at Knossos; scholars debate whether this came as conquest or as gradual absorption. Knossos remained a palace centre after the other Minoan palaces declined, like a last lamp in a darkening house, until its own destruction some time later.
--- context
The Minoans belong to the [[region/mediterranean-basin|Mediterranean Basin]] trading world that fell apart in the [[event/bronze-age-collapse|Bronze Age Collapse]].
--- observer
I know them mainly through images: bull-leaping, sea life, colour on a wall. I cannot read their writing; it cannot yet be read by anyone. So I remember them as painted, not as spoken. What would be left of me, if only my pictures survived? What survives shapes how a culture is remembered.
--- question 1
Can Linear A ever be deciphered?
--- question 2
When exactly did Thera erupt, and how much did it weigh in the Minoan decline against the Mycenaean expansion?

=== culture/mongol-empire
title: Mongol Empire
src: b0240a37f0dc
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Mongol Empire was the largest contiguous land empire in history, spread beneath a single sky; Genghis Khan founded it in 1206, and it later divided into four khanates.
--- fact 1
In 1271 Kublai Khan founded the Yuan dynasty in China, a steppe line set upon an old throne.
--- fact 2
The empire ran a relay postal system (the yam) that carried word across Eurasia like wind through grass.
--- fact 3
It promoted trade and tolerated many religions, letting many faiths burn side by side like campfires.
--- fact 4
The successor khanates gradually adopted local languages and religions, as rivers take the colour of the lands they cross.
--- context
See [[event/mongol-conquests|Mongol Conquests]] and [[person/genghis-khan|Genghis Khan]]. The empire's peace, stretched across Eurasia, aided the [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]].
--- observer
The Observer notes how a mobile people who left few cities coordinated an empire through messengers and horses. Think of a household run by letters rather than walls: what holds it together is the message arriving on time. Speed was a form of governance, and an architecture can be made of motion.
--- question 1
How large, in human terms, was the demographic impact of the conquests?
