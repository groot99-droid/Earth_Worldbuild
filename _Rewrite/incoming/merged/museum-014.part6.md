src: 1390074ef5f2

--- description
Antoni Gaudí (25 June 1852 to 10 June 1926) was the leading architect of Catalan Modernisme, the regional cousin of Art Nouveau. Almost all his mature work stands in Barcelona, and it rises to its summit in the still-unfinished Sagrada Família, a forest of stone still growing.

He was born in Reus or possibly the neighbouring Riudoms, Catalonia (sources differ, and from 1915 he himself said Riudoms), the youngest son of a coppersmith. He credited his father's workshop with teaching him to see three-dimensional space. In 1878 he graduated from the Barcelona School of Architecture.

The industrialist Eusebi Güell noticed a display case he designed for a glove-maker at the Paris World's Fair, and became his patron for the rest of his career.

He never married, grew ever more devout, and by his sixties lived an almost monastic life given to the Sagrada Família.

On 7 June 1926 he was struck by a tram while walking to church in shabby clothes. Mistaken for a beggar, he received delayed care, and he died on 10 June. He was buried in the crypt of his unfinished basilica.

He modelled forms on nature, and he hung scale models of chains and sandbags, photographed upside down, to find the ideal shape for a compression-only arch. He revived trencadís, mosaic of broken tile, as a cheap weatherproof skin.
