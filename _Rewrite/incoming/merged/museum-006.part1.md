=== people--enheduanna--1
src: 071013585b74
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Enheduanna (flourishing around 2300 BCE; the span of about 2285 to 2250 BCE is only an estimate) stood as high priestess of the moon god Nanna at the Sumerian city of Ur, a daughter of Sargon of Akkad. She is often called the earliest author known by name, though her dates and the attribution of her texts are debated, like a name half-worn from a stone.

Her father, Sargon of Akkad, appointed her en-priestess of Nanna at Ur, and her dates rise and fall with whichever chronology is used.

In 1927 Leonard Woolley's team lifted a disc from the ground at Ur. On it a figure moves through a ritual scene, identified by inscription as Enheduanna, daughter of Sargon.

Sumerian poems are attributed to her: 42 temple hymns, and hymns to the goddess Inanna such as "The Exaltation of Inanna."

Yet the copies that survive come from schools of the Old Babylonian period, about 1800 BCE, some five to six centuries after her lifetime, carried down like water passed from hand to hand, and scholars disagree about whether she wrote every attributed text.

In "The Exaltation of Inanna" the speaker tells of being driven from office by a usurper, Lugal-ane, and of being restored; whether this happened as told is debated.

=== people--nefertiti--1
src: 3b3f6232fdcc
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Consider Nefertiti (about 1370 to about 1330 BCE), Great Royal Wife of the Egyptian king Akhenaten, and notice how much room the art of his reign gives her: an unusual prominence. Then notice what the record withholds. Her exact birth and death dates and her burial place are uncertain, and the dates given are approximate.

She married Akhenaten (then Amenhotep IV) early in his reign; the exact dates of the marriage are uncertain.

Together the couple promoted the worship of the sun disk Aten and moved the royal capital to a new city, Akhetaten (modern Amarna). That is a change in the whole architecture of a kingdom's devotion, and of its address.

Reliefs and statues show Nefertiti at a scale and in roles usually reserved for the king, including taking part in rites for the Aten. In that visual grammar, scale is a statement of standing.

They had six daughters, including Meritaten and Ankhesenamun, who later married Tutankhamun.

Here you can watch scholarship revise itself. Before 2012 it assumed she vanished from the record around year 12 of the reign, but an inscription dated to year 16 names Neferneferuaten-Nefertiti alongside Akhenaten. Whether she died, changed title, or ruled as co-regent under the name Neferneferuaten is debated. A silence in the record is not always an ending.

=== people--akhenaten--1
src: 4f8589f76664
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Akhenaten was an Egyptian pharaoh (reigned about 1353 to 1336 BCE) who lifted the worship of the sun disk Aten above the traditional gods, one light raised over many.

He built a new capital at Akhetaten (modern Amarna).

The art of his reign moves in a distinctive, naturalistic style.

Soon after his death the traditional religion returned, restored under his successors, Tutankhamun among them.

His wife was Nefertiti.
