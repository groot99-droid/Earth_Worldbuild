=== people--chandragupta-maurya--1
src: fa9bfc8684a6
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Chandragupta Maurya (about 340 to 297 BCE) founded the Maurya Empire, the first political power to gather most of the Indian subcontinent under one hand, after he overthrew the Nanda dynasty of Magadha around 321 BCE, as a river in flood takes the old banks with it.

Guided by the strategist Chanakya (Kautilya), Chandragupta took the throne of Magadha. The Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft traditionally attributed to Chanakya, was long bound to the memory of his rule, but most scholars now date it to roughly the 1st to 3rd centuries CE and doubt that it describes his reign at all.

Between about 305 and 303 BCE he went to war with the Diadoch ruler Seleucus I Nicator. The settlement that followed ceded territory in the northwest to the Maurya and sealed a marriage alliance, and Seleucus received war elephants in return.

The Greek ambassador Megasthenes, sent to Chandragupta's court at Pataliputra, wrote the Indica, a major source on Mauryan India that survives now only in fragments, like light through a broken lattice.

Later Jain tradition holds that Chandragupta laid down his crown, became an ascetic under Bhadrabahu, and died by ritual fasting (sallekhana) at Shravanabelagola in Karnataka, though this account survives mainly in sources written well after his reign.

=== people--basil-ii--1
src: 9081362a75d2
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Basil II (958 to 1025) was Byzantine emperor for nearly fifty years, the longest reign in Roman or Byzantine imperial history, and he is remembered above all for his decades-long war against the First Bulgarian Empire.

He was made co-emperor as a child in 960, yet he did not hold the government in his own hands until about 989, when he was in his thirties, after he had put down the rebellions of powerful aristocratic generals, Bardas Skleros and Bardas Phokas among them.

His long campaigns against Bulgaria rose to the decisive Battle of Kleidion in 1014, and by 1018 Bulgaria had been absorbed into the Byzantine Empire, which earned him the later epithet "Bulgar-slayer" (Boulgaroktonos).

A story recorded by later chroniclers claims that after Kleidion he blinded thousands of Bulgarian prisoners, leaving one man in every hundred with one eye to lead the others home. Modern historians debate how literally this account should be taken.

He left the imperial treasury full and the empire at its widest since the 7th century, like a tide at its highest mark. But he died without an heir, and the strong central rule he had imposed did not outlast his less capable successors.

=== people--timur--1
src: 855cba0d3f06
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Timur (Tamerlane, traditionally dated 1336 to 1405) was a Central Asian conqueror who built the Timurid Empire and made Samarkand its centre.

Even his beginning is contested. 1336 is the traditional date, but many contemporary accounts imply the late 1320s. He was born near Kesh (now Shahrisabz, Uzbekistan).

By about 1370 he had besieged his rival Amir Husayn at Balkh, and after Husayn's assassination he proclaimed himself sovereign of the Chagatai line of khans, with dominion over Transoxiana.

Notice the architecture of his legitimacy. He was not a descendant of Genghis Khan and did not take the title khan. He ruled as amir through a puppet Chaghatayid khan, Suyurghatmish, and took the title güregen (royal son-in-law) through his marriage to Saray Mulk Khanum, of the Chinggisid line.

His armies campaigned in Persia, Iraq, the lands of the Golden Horde, India (Delhi was sacked in 1398), and Anatolia (he defeated the Ottoman sultan Bayezid I at Ankara in 1402).

Later chronicles describe massacres in the cities he conquered. Their casualty figures are contested, and the dead deserve that care in the counting.

=== people--pachacuti--1
src: 1e9b5121e465
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Pachacuti (about 1418 to 1471) was the ninth Sapa Inca, and under him the Inca state grew from a regional power into an empire.

He is credited with rebuilding Cusco and with expanding Inca rule along the Andes.

Machu Picchu is believed to have been built as one of his royal estates.

He organized the state through labor obligations and roads, an architecture of duty and distance.

The sources on him were written after the Spanish conquest, and they blend history and legend. Read them knowing that.

He is usually counted the ninth Sapa Inca, but a set of fourteen portraits of Inca kings painted between about 1750 and 1800, now in the Brooklyn Museum, titles him the tenth Inca. A count, too, can depend on who is keeping it.
