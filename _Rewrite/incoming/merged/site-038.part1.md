=== culture/buddhism
title: Buddhism
src: 39d2e256e9fb
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Buddhism began in northeastern India between the sixth and fourth centuries BCE, rising from the teaching of Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, and it spread across Asia the way rivers spread across a delta, parting into several major traditions. Its path out of suffering was lit by insight and practice, not by a creator god.
--- fact 1
Born a member of a ruling family in the region of present-day Nepal and India, Siddhartha Gautama is said to have reached enlightenment and begun teaching in his mid-thirties, a lamp newly kindled.
--- fact 2
His core teaching, the Four Noble Truths, maps suffering, its root in craving, its ending, and the path that leads there, aiming at release from the turning wheel of rebirth, called nirvana.
--- fact 3
The faith travelled as seeds travel, by missionary activity, by trade routes and by royal patronage, notably under the Mauryan emperor Ashoka.
--- fact 4
It grew into three major living traditions, like branches from a shared root: Theravada, dominant in Sri Lanka and mainland Southeast Asia; Mahayana, dominant in China, Japan, Korea and Vietnam; and Vajrayana, dominant in Tibet, Bhutan and Mongolia.
--- fact 5
Roughly 500 million people follow it today, about 6 percent of the world's population, a lamp still burning in many windows.
--- context
Buddhism rose in [[region/south-asia|South Asia]] during the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] from the teaching of [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]], a current within the broader [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] tide of new philosophical and religious movements, and it spread widely, as a river widens, under patrons such as [[person/ashoka|Ashoka]]. See [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
I watch a meaning-engine cross a continent, and I do not see conquest carrying it, not primarily. I see teaching. I see trade. I see a ruler's patronage. I watch it branch into traditions so different in practice that observers sometimes debate whether to call them one religion at all. What do I do with a name that has to hold so much?

A specialist could argue that Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana differ enough in text, in practice and in cosmology that grouping them under one name obscures more than it reveals, and that would weaken any single account of "Buddhist belief." So I keep the name, and I hold it loosely. A name can be a roof, and a roof is not a house.
--- question 1
How much unity of belief and practice still binds its major traditions today?
--- question 2
How much of its early spread rode on state patronage such as Ashoka's, and how much on independent teaching and trade?

=== culture/celts
title: Celts
src: 4d33f75f6191
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Celts were related peoples scattered across Iron Age Europe like embers from a shared hearth, bound by related languages and, later, by the La Tène style of art, yet never gathered into a single state. Rome absorbed most of the continental Celts, and Celtic language and culture held on longest in Ireland and parts of Britain, the way a tide lingers in the far coves.
--- fact 1
The Celts were many related tribal groups, the Gauls of mainland Europe among them, speaking branches of the Celtic language family such as Gaulish, as a tree speaks through its many boughs.
--- fact 2
The La Tène culture, famed for metalwork that swirls like water around stones, flourished from about 450 BCE until the Roman conquest, rising after the earlier Hallstatt culture.
--- fact 3
Before the Roman conquest, druids held major religious and political influence in many Celtic societies, though their role shifted by region and by period like light moving across a field.
--- fact 4
Rome struck at the Arverni in 125 BCE, and by 50 BCE Julius Caesar had completed the conquest of Gaul, despite resistance under leaders such as Vercingetorix.
--- fact 5
Rome aimed directly at the druids, and the continental and southern British Celts were largely assimilated, while in Ireland and northern Britain Celtic culture burned on for longer.
--- context
The Celts dwelt across much of [[region/europe|Europe]] in the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] until the [[culture/romans|Romans]] conquered them, in an age of widening contact across the ancient world that also held [[event/alexander-s-conquests|Alexander's Conquests]]. See [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/language-and-writing|Language and Writing]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that "Celtic" names a shared language family and a later decorative style across many separate societies, not one people with one story-glue. Think of a surname shared by cousins who have never met: it tells you something real, but it does not tell you who sits at whose table. Rome's targeting of the druids suggests it saw their meaning-engine as a source of resistance, and that tells us how Rome read them. Celtic identity lasted where terrain made conquest harder, in Ireland and the far north; geography, too, is an architecture of survival. A specialist could argue that the umbrella label was applied more consistently by later scholars than by the peoples themselves, which would weaken any reading of the Celts as one self-aware group. A label is a map drawn by the mapmaker, not a vow taken by the mapped.
--- question 1
How much of a shared identity did Celtic-speaking peoples recognize in one another?
--- question 2
What political role did the druids truly hold, and how real was the threat Rome saw in them?
