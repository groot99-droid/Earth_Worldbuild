=== people--malinche--1
src: 9e9249bc4688

--- description
Malinche (Malintzin, called Doña Marina by the Spanish; about 1500 to about 1529) was a Nahua woman who served as interpreter and intermediary for Hernán Cortés during the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire in 1519 to 1521. Her dates are uncertain, and so is much of her story; hold that uncertainty with care.

Her birth is placed around 1500 and her death between about 1527 and 1529; both are estimates.

The chronicler Bernal Díaz del Castillo, writing decades later, says she was born to a noble Nahua family near Coatzacoalcos and passed into slavery as a child. This account is traditional and cannot be checked.

In 1519 she was among women presented to Cortés by people of Tabasco. She spoke Nahuatl and a Maya language, and she learned Spanish. Language was the threshold she stood on, between worlds that did not understand each other.

She interpreted for Cortés, at first with Gerónimo de Aguilar, including at the meetings with Moctezuma II at Tenochtitlan in 1519.

She bore Cortés a son, Martín, about 1522, and later married the Spanish soldier Juan Jaramillo.

=== people--joao-iii--1
src: c51fd87c344a
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
João III (1502 to 1557) was King of Portugal from 1521 to 1557. He presided over the height of Portugal's Asian trading empire, and he also introduced the Inquisition and the formal colonization of Brazil. Keep all three in the same sentence, as the record does.

In his reign Portuguese trading posts and fortresses across the Indian Ocean, from East Africa to Goa, Malacca and Macau, reached their fullest extent, built on the routes opened by earlier explorers such as Vasco da Gama.

He introduced the Portuguese Inquisition in 1536, targeting Jewish converts (New Christians) suspected of secretly practicing Judaism, at the urging of factions at his court and with eventual papal approval. The people it targeted had already been made to convert; suspicion followed them anyway.

From 1534 he divided Brazil into hereditary captaincies to encourage settlement and defense of the colony, a system that had mixed results and was later partly replaced by direct royal government.

He was a patron of Portuguese scholarship, moving the university at Lisbon to Coimbra in 1537. But the enormous cost of maintaining the Asian trading empire strained the crown's finances by the end of his reign. An empire's ledger has two columns.

=== people--andrea-palladio--1
src: af2618a1c77c
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Andrea Palladio (1508 to 1580) was a Renaissance architect of the Republic of Venice whose villas and churches, and an illustrated treatise, turned Roman classicism into a style that could travel. His influence is arguably the widest of any single architect in Western history.

He was born Andrea di Pietro della Gondola in Padua in 1508. He was apprenticed to a stonemasons' guild and moved to Vicenza to work as a carver.

The nobleman Gian Giorgio Trissino gave him a classical education, renamed him "Palladio" after Pallas Athena, and took him to Rome to measure and draw ancient ruins, columns standing like old trees in a field.

His remodelling of the medieval basilica of Vicenza, dated to 1546 in some sources, made his name. For three decades he designed country villas for the Venetian nobility and churches in Venice.

In 1570 he published I Quattro Libri dell'Architettura, part treatise and part illustrated catalogue of his buildings. He died in 1580, at either Vicenza or Maser.

Key works include the Villa Rotonda (1566 to about 1592), the Teatro Olimpico (1580 to about 1585) and San Giorgio Maggiore in Venice (1566 to 1610).
