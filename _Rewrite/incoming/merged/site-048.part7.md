=== person/jan-van-eyck
title: Jan van Eyck
type: person
date: c. 1390 – 1441
themes: art, religion
src: 8af632a57bd2
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Jan van Eyck, born about 1380 or 1390 and dead by 1441, led the painters of the Burgundian Netherlands and helped found Early Netherlandish painting. He refined a method of thin, translucent oil glazes that let him paint light and texture with unprecedented realism.
--- fact 1
Van Eyck was probably born sometime around 1380 or 1390 near Maaseik, in present-day Belgian Limburg. Nothing is known of his training; he appears in records about 1422 at The Hague, working for John of Bavaria.
--- fact 2
From 1425 he served as court painter and valet de chambre to Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, who sent him on confidential missions, including a trip to Portugal in 1428 to 1429 to paint Princess Isabella.
--- fact 3
About 1430 he settled in Bruges. He completed the Ghent Altarpiece, begun by his brother Hubert, in 1432, and died there in July 1441.
--- fact 4
He did not invent oil paint, as Vasari later claimed; instead he built up many thin, transparent glazes, and he often signed and dated his work, sometimes with the motto Als ich can ("As I can").
--- fact 5
Key works include the Ghent Altarpiece (1432), the Arnolfini Portrait (1434), and the Madonna of Chancellor Rolin (about 1435).
--- fact 6
He did not use the mathematical perspective then being developed in Italy, building space instead through observation of light and atmosphere.
--- fact 7
The Ghent Altarpiece survived iconoclasm, fire and Napoleon's looters, was seized by the Nazis, and was recovered from an Austrian salt mine. A restoration begun in 2012 revealed the Lamb's strikingly human face in 2020.
--- context
Van Eyck worked in [[region/europe|Europe]] in the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]], at the time of the [[event/renaissance|Renaissance]]. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]] and [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
I see a patron who pays for close detail and is really buying a signal of status — a jewel-like surface proving he can command rare materials and slow labour. I see candles, fruit, mirrors, all the ordinary things a household owns, quietly tied to belief, so that the same careful looking serves prestige and faith at once.

I see how a whole technique came to be credited to one man's name, the way a whole lineage of tools collapses into a single inventor. What Jan painted and what Hubert painted on the same altarpiece is something I can only partly recover.
--- question 1
How much of the Ghent Altarpiece was painted by Hubert van Eyck rather than Jan?

=== person/jane-austen
title: Jane Austen
type: person
date: 1775 – 1817
themes: art, language, kinship
src: ae16caf7c34f
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Jane Austen, who lived from 1775 to 1817, was an English novelist whose six completed novels of courtship, inheritance and class anxiety among the gentry remain among the most widely read in the language. All were published anonymously in her lifetime.
--- fact 1
Austen was born in 1775 in Steventon, Hampshire, the seventh of eight children of a country clergyman, in a family that encouraged reading, amateur theatre and writing.
--- fact 2
Sense and Sensibility appeared in 1811, credited only to "A Lady", followed by Pride and Prejudice (1813), Mansfield Park (1814), Emma (1815), and, after her death, Northanger Abbey and Persuasion (1817).
--- fact 3
Only close family knew of her authorship. She fell ill, probably with Addison's disease though the cause is debated, and died in 1817 at 41; her authorship became public only through a biographical notice her brother attached to her last two novels.
--- fact 4
She worked within a deliberately narrow social canvas — mainly the English gentry, courtship, income and inheritance — rather than the wider political events of her era.
--- fact 5
Her distinctive technique is free indirect discourse: narrating in the third person while letting the prose absorb a character's own irony or blind spots, so a reader senses a character's error before the character does.
--- fact 6
Her novels have remained continuously in print and are widely adapted for film, television and stage; her reputation grew after her death rather than during her modest, anonymous success.
--- context
Austen wrote in [[region/europe|Europe]] in the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]], during the [[event/napoleonic-wars|Napoleonic Wars]], but kept to the gentry's household world. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]], [[theme/language-and-writing|Language and Writing]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I see a body of fiction where the kin-lattice — marriage, inheritance, income — is the whole plot, where who may marry whom and who inherits what becomes a subject for comedy rather than law. I see an author hidden while she lived, her fame assigned only afterward, by readers she never met.

The claim that she chose a narrow canvas over the wars of her age is only one reading of her silence. A reader could just as easily say the wars are absent because the household itself was always her true subject.
--- question 1
What caused her final illness?
