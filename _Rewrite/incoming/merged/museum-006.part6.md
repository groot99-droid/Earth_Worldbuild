=== people--gorgo--1
src: f98e1ef8b7f6
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Gorgo (born about 518 BCE, last attested around 480 BCE) was queen of Sparta as wife of King Leonidas I, and the historian Herodotus remembers her as unusually sharp-witted and politically perceptive for a woman in his account of Greek affairs, a clear flame in a crowded hall.

She was the daughter of the Spartan king Cleomenes I, and according to Herodotus she warned him as a child not to trust a bribe offered by an Ionian envoy seeking Spartan military aid.

Herodotus credits her with finding a warning about the Persian king Xerxes's invasion plans hidden beneath the wax of a writing tablet sent by the exiled Spartan king Demaratus, when other Spartans could not read its message.

She was the wife of Leonidas I, who died leading the defense of Thermopylae against the Persian invasion in 480 BCE, the same year in which she is last clearly attested in the sources.

Nearly all that is known of her comes from Herodotus's Histories, written decades after her lifetime, and no independent Spartan record of her deeds survives.

=== people--pericles--1
src: 70d93c33aac3
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Pericles (about 495 to 429 BCE) was the leading Athenian statesman for over three decades in the mid-5th century BCE, a season later called the Golden Age of Athens for its building programs, its drama and its democratic institutions.

He rose in Athenian democracy through the office of strategos (general), to which he was re-elected year upon year, shaping policy through his sway over the Assembly rather than through any formal single office.

He directed the building program on the Acropolis, the Parthenon among it, paid for partly from treasury funds first gathered by the Delian League, an alliance of Greek states organized against Persia.

He extended pay for jury service and other public roles to poorer citizens, so that participation in Athenian democracy ran wider than the wealthy.

He championed the aggressive strategy that led Athens into the Peloponnesian War against Sparta in 431 BCE, and he died of plague in 429 BCE in the war's early years, before its outcome was decided, a man taken before the storm he summoned had passed.

=== people--aristotle--1
src: 59dd5e7f2de2
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Aristotle (384 to 322 BCE) was a Greek philosopher and scientist who searched logic, ethics, politics, physics and biology, and who tutored Alexander of Macedon.

He studied under Plato in Athens and later founded a school of his own, the Lyceum.

He wrote on logic, ethics, politics, poetics, physics and biology.

His biological work rested on careful observation of marine life, of what moves beneath the water.

His works were kept and extended in the Islamic world, and later in Latin Europe.

=== people--alexander-the-great--1
src: 9aeb1d9d0262
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Alexander III of Macedon (356 to 323 BCE) was a commander who conquered the Persian Empire and carried Greek influence from Egypt to India like a wind across dry grass.

He became king at about twenty, after the assassination of his father, Philip II.

At Issus and Gaugamela he won great battles against the Persian king Darius III.

At the Hyphasis (Beas) river in India, his army turned back.

He died in Babylon in 323 BCE, and his empire was divided among his generals.

=== people--chandragupta-maurya--1
src: 7ca8161defed
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Chandragupta Maurya (about 340 to 297 BCE) founded the Maurya Empire, the first power to unite most of the Indian subcontinent, after overthrowing the Nanda dynasty of Magadha around 321 BCE.

Guided by the strategist Chanakya (Kautilya), he seized the throne of Magadha. The Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft traditionally attributed to Chanakya, was long linked to his rule, but most scholars now date it to roughly the 1st to 3rd centuries CE and doubt that it describes his reign.

Between about 305 and 303 BCE he fought the Diadoch ruler Seleucus I Nicator; the settlement ceded territory in the northwest to the Maurya and sealed a marriage alliance, and Seleucus received war elephants in return.

The Greek ambassador Megasthenes, sent to Chandragupta's court at Pataliputra, wrote the Indica, a major source on Mauryan India that now survives only in fragments.

Later Jain tradition holds that Chandragupta laid down his crown, became an ascetic under Bhadrabahu, and died by ritual fasting (sallekhana) at Shravanabelagola in Karnataka, though this account survives mainly in sources written well after his reign.

=== people--ashoka--1
src: a5ceeb6b4f83
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Ashoka (about 304 to 232 BCE) was a Mauryan emperor who ruled most of the Indian subcontinent and became a patron of Buddhism after a bloody war.

He conquered the region of Kalinga around 261 BCE at great human cost. His own edicts record about 100,000 killed and 150,000 deported; the count comes from the conqueror himself.

He had edicts inscribed on rocks and pillars across his empire, promoting 'dhamma' (moral conduct).

He supported the spread of Buddhism, including missions abroad.

His lion capital is now a national emblem of India.

=== people--qin-shi-huang--1
src: 04d846592063
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Qin Shi Huang (259 to 210 BCE) was the first emperor of a unified China. He made one measure of writing, currency and weights, and raised a vast tomb complex guarded by the terracotta army.

He became king of Qin at thirteen and completed the unification of China in 221 BCE.

He made uniform the script, the weights and measures, and even the widths of axles.

Later historians, hostile to the Qin, told of book burning and harsh punishments; some of these accounts are debated.

His tomb complex near Xi'an holds thousands of life-size clay soldiers, standing in ranks like a buried army waiting for a dawn.

=== people--augustus--1
src: 23ec98e71c15
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Augustus (63 BCE to 14 CE) was the first Roman emperor. He ended a century of civil war and built the imperial system that endured for centuries.

Born Gaius Octavius, he was the adopted heir of Julius Caesar.

After overcoming his rivals he took the name Augustus in 27 BCE, while keeping the forms of the republic like an old shell around new life.

His reign began the Pax Romana, a long season of relative stability.

He reordered the army, the provinces and taxation.
