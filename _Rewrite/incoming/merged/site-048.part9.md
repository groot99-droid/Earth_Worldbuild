--- summary
Johannes Vermeer, who lived from 1632 to 1675, painted in Delft in the Dutch Republic. About 35 paintings are accepted as his, mostly quiet interiors lit by daylight from a window. Almost forgotten for 200 years after his death, he now ranks among the most famous painters in the world.
--- fact 1
Vermeer was baptised in Delft on 31 October 1632; his father was a silk weaver who also dealt in art and ran an inn. Nothing is known of Vermeer's training.
--- fact 2
In 1653 he married Catharina Bolnes, a Catholic from a wealthier family, and probably converted; that same year he joined Delft's Guild of St Luke, which later elected him one of its leaders several times.
--- fact 3
The couple lived with Catharina's mother and had 15 children, 11 of whom survived. He also worked as an art dealer, and his slow output was bought largely by one local patron, Pieter van Ruijven.
--- fact 4
In 1672, the Dutch Republic's "Disaster Year", France invaded and the art market collapsed. He died in 1675, aged 43, leaving his family deep in debt.
--- fact 5
He used costly natural ultramarine, ground from lapis lazuli, even in shadows. Key works include The Milkmaid (about 1657 to 1658), View of Delft (about 1660 to 1661), and Girl with a Pearl Earring (about 1665).
--- fact 6
Some features of his paintings have led scholars to argue that he used a camera obscura, though the question remains debated.
--- fact 7
In the 1860s the critic Théophile Thoré-Bürger rediscovered him; in the 1940s Han van Meegeren sold a fake Vermeer to Hermann Göring. A 2023 Rijksmuseum exhibition of 28 of his works sold out within days.
--- context
Vermeer worked in Delft, in [[region/europe|Europe]] during the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]]. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
I see the calm of these pictures sitting beside a household of 15 children, a mounting debt, a collapsing market — the serenity was made under real pressure, not away from it. I see two centuries of forgetting, and then a rediscovery, and I understand that fame is something later readers assign, not something a life simply earns on its own.

Whether he used a camera obscura is a question about a tool that still has no settled answer. And a forger's fake, sold to Göring, shows just how high the price of his name eventually climbed.
