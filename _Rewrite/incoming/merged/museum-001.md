=== great-wave
title: The Great Wave off Kanagawa
src: 983cb3a7a4c9
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A wave rears over three fishing boats like a hand of the sea, towering, its foam breaking into claw-like fingers, while far off Mount Fuji sits small and calm, still as a held breath. It is the opening gate of the series *Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji*. The print was made with the newly imported Prussian blue, a pigment carried in from beyond the sea. Thousands of impressions were made, and the wave has broken across the world ever since, until it has become perhaps the most famous Japanese artwork in the world.

=== saturn
title: Saturn Devouring His Son
src: 82274b347d8b
mode: horror-prose
--- description
A farmhouse outside Madrid. Its walls. The aged, deaf Goya painted straight onto them: 14 "Black Paintings", never meant for public view. This is one. The Titan Saturn, afraid his children will overthrow him. A headless body in his grip. He tears into it. His eyes wild with horror. In 1874 the murals came off the walls and were transferred to canvas. Now they are among the most disturbing images in the Prado. Never meant to be seen. Seen.

=== fallingwater
title: Fallingwater
src: 30cfac53a17f
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A house can face a waterfall, or it can stand on one. Fallingwater is a weekend house built directly over a waterfall for the Kaufmann family, its stacked concrete terraces cantilevered dramatically out over the falling water rather than facing it from a safe distance. Take that as the architecture's doctrine, and as yours: do not admire what moves you from the safe bank; step out over it. Widely considered Wright's masterpiece, it was designed when he was nearly 70, in the middle of what is often called his career's second, "Usonian" act. A second act can hold the masterpiece.

=== the-sick-child
title: The Sick Child
src: 805e768008b0
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I stand before a pale, red-haired girl propped on a pillow as she turns toward a grieving woman who bows her head beside the bed. I know the picture recalls the death of Munch's sister Sophie from tuberculosis. I see the scraping, the scratching, the repainting he gave the canvas for a year, until the surface seemed damaged. Critics mocked it as unfinished, but Munch called it a breakthrough, and he returned to the subject all his life. What grief is ever finished? I have scraped and repainted my own for years. Unfinished is not a flaw; it is the shape of mourning.

=== third-of-may
title: The Third of May 1808
src: 904ca84a23ee
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Stand in front of this painting and you stand at a threshold in how war is shown. It is the companion to *The Second of May*. At night, a faceless line of French soldiers aims its muskets at Spanish prisoners. At the centre a man in a white shirt flings his arms wide, lit by a lantern, his pose recalling the crucified Christ. Notice the architecture of it: the soldiers have no faces, and the light falls on the man before the muskets. It is one of the first great paintings to show war as senseless slaughter rather than glory, and it prepared the way for Manet and Picasso's *Guernica*. Once you see the men before the muskets as persons, glory is very hard to paint.

=== girl-with-a-pearl-earring
title: Girl with a Pearl Earring
src: b74579d547e9
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I was told this is not a portrait but a *tronie*, a study of a character or type, and still I want her name. A girl in an exotic blue and yellow turban turns over her shoulder toward me, lips parted, a large pearl gleaming at her ear. I lean in. I lean closer. I find the pearl is just two strokes of paint. How much of what I have loved was two strokes and my own longing? Often called the "Mona Lisa of the North", it inspired a best-selling novel and a 2003 film. I came for a girl and found a type, and I love her anyway.

=== genji-kashiwagi
title: The Tale of Genji — Hakubyō scroll fragment
src: 4cd7bd518f5a
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Here is a scene from a later illustrated copy of the novel, drawn in monochrome ink line, the "hakubyō" manner, fine as frost traced on a winter pane. It belongs to a tradition distinct from the coloured Tokugawa and Gotoh scrolls also shown here, as a river by moonlight is distinct from the same river at noon. It stands as evidence of how continuously artists kept reinterpreting Murasaki's novel in new visual styles over the following centuries, each generation lowering its bucket into the same deep well.

=== sunflowers
title: Sunflowers
src: e78b5b33300f
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Fifteen sunflowers stand in a yellow vase on a yellow table against a yellow wall, some bright and fresh as a summer morning, some wilting and going to seed like suns sinking at the end of their day. Van Gogh painted the canvas in Arles to decorate the room of his friend Paul Gauguin, as a host hangs banners in the hall for an honored guest. He made several versions, and the series became his signature: an exploration of how far one colour could go, a single light driven to the edge of the world, and a symbol of gratitude and friendship.
