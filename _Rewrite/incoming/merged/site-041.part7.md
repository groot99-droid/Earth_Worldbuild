=== event/huronian-glaciation
src: df80f37af7de
mode: horror-prose
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Ice came. Then it came again. The Huronian Supergroup of Ontario, Canada, holds a series of ice ages from the early Paleoproterozoic, about 2.4 to 2.2 billion years ago. One of the oldest ice ages we can still read clearly in stone.
--- fact 1
North of Lake Huron: three glacial deposits. Ramsay Lake. Bruce. Gowganda. Between them, sediment laid down in warmer conditions.
--- fact 2
Not only Ontario. Wyoming too. Finland too. Glacial deposits of similar age, scattered across continents. And the Makganyene Formation in South Africa, about 2.2 billion years old — ice that formed near the equator. That detail supports an early snowball state. Whether it connects to the Huronian at all is debated.
--- fact 3
The glaciation follows the first rise of oxygen. One hypothesis: oxygen ate the atmosphere's methane, a strong greenhouse gas, and cooled the world that let it in. Other explanations point instead to volcanoes. To the shifting of plates.
--- fact 4
How long the ice held on. Whether it wrapped the whole planet. Both still debated.
--- context
The Huronian Glaciation falls in the [[era/paleoproterozoic-era|Paleoproterozoic Era]], soon after the [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]] began. The earliest ice age of the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]], before [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
File the Huronian under a caution about attribution: an ice age and an oxygen rise that share a date invite you to build a causal story between them. That story is plausible, but the record allows other explanations too. The reading would strengthen, or weaken, with tighter dating of the glacial deposits against the first traces of oxygen.
--- question 1
Did the oxygen rise cause the glaciation? Or did the two arise from some common cause?
--- question 2
Was the ice global?

=== event/incense-trade
src: 60b0147bb6eb
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The incense trade carried frankincense and myrrh out of southern Arabia and the Horn of Africa, across desert and season, to Mediterranean and Mesopotamian markets — borne mostly on camel caravan, for well over a thousand years.
--- fact 1
Frankincense weeps from the Boswellia trees that grow in southern Arabia — in Oman, in Yemen — and in the Horn of Africa.
--- fact 2
It was burned in temples and at funerals across the Egyptian, Greek, and Roman worlds, its smoke rising toward gods and the dead alike.
--- fact 3
Caravans moved northward, through western Arabia, toward the Levant.
--- fact 4
Kingdoms such as Saba and the Nabataeans grew rich on the taxing and guiding of these caravans.
--- fact 5
The trade faded in the later Roman period; reduced demand is often named as one cause.
--- context
The trade linked the [[region/arabian-peninsula|Arabian Peninsula]] to the [[region/mediterranean-basin|Mediterranean Basin]] and came before the wider [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]]. It fed the wealth of the [[culture/kingdom-of-saba|Kingdom of Saba]] and the [[culture/nabataeans|Nabataeans]]. Incense Trade sits within the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] and the region of [[region/arabian-peninsula|Arabian Peninsula]], within the theme threads of [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]] and [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
I think of a resin burned to nothing, its smoke gone before the memory of the fire — and I remember how it sustained kingdoms in desert country for centuries. I remember how little a thing needs to be useful to be wanted. What does it mean that so much of exchange was never about survival at all, only about ritual, only about what we needed to believe? A scent that vanished built the wealth that lasted.
--- question 1
How much did this route change with the domestication of the camel?

=== event/indian-ocean-trade-network
src: 9f51a42785e2
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Indian Ocean trade network was a web of sea routes binding East Africa, Arabia, South Asia, Southeast Asia, and China together, and it ran busiest from about the 8th to the 15th century CE.
--- fact 1
The seasonal monsoon winds were the tide beneath it all, carrying ships east in one season and calling them home in another.
--- fact 2
Spices, cotton textiles, ivory, gold, and Chinese porcelain all passed along these lanes.
--- fact 3
Swahili city-states such as Kilwa on the East African coast grew rich upon it; see [[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]].
--- fact 4
Portuguese ships entered these waters in 1498 and began to seize control of the key ports.
--- context
The network ran alongside the overland [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]] and carried Islam and other faiths from port to port. See [[person/zheng-he|Zheng He]].
--- observer
I notice that the wind itself kept the calendar here. I remember how a cycle no one owned let strangers of many faiths run a shared exchange between them, for centuries, with almost no one in charge. Who needs a king when the monsoon returns on time? A pattern you can trust becomes its own kind of law.
--- question 1
How large was the volume of trade compared to overland routes?
