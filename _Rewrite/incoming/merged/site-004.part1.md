=== region/southeast-asia
src: 7aaaae11f7ce
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Southeast Asia is the mainland and island region lying between India and China, peninsula and isle strung between them like beads on a cord. It was a hub of maritime trade, where sails gathered as birds gather at water, and it hosted powerful states that drank from the wells of Indian and Chinese culture.
--- fact 1
Between the 9th and 15th centuries CE, the [[culture/khmer-empire|Khmer Empire]] raised [[place/angkor|Angkor]], stone laid upon patient stone.
--- fact 2
From here the Austronesian-speaking peoples went out into the Pacific like seeds carried on the wind; see [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]].
--- fact 3
The spice trade of the Malay Archipelago drew traders from across Eurasia, as the scent of a feast draws guests from far rooms.
--- context
By sea the region binds [[region/south-asia|South Asia]], [[region/east-asia|East Asia]] and [[region/oceania|Oceania]] together, three shores knotted by one water.
--- observer
I come back to Southeast Asia the way a tide comes back to a harbor. I see a crossing-place, the great intersection zone of the maritime exchange-web. I see a launching point, where the largest human ocean migrations set out. What is a crossroads, if not a place that teaches people to leave? Every harbor is also a door.

=== region/sub-saharan-africa
src: a1be23965adb
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Sub-Saharan Africa is the region south of the Sahara, the old hearth of our kind. It is the birthplace of the hominin lineage and of Homo sapiens, and it holds, as a deep well holds water, the greatest human genetic and linguistic diversity.
--- fact 1
Fossil evidence places the origins of [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]] in Africa about 300,000 years ago, the first light of a long morning.
--- fact 2
Like a river that overruns its banks and follows every valley, the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] carried Bantu languages across much of central, eastern and southern Africa.
--- fact 3
Great states rose here, among them the [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]], the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], and the builders of [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]].
--- fact 4
Trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean trade bound the region to Eurasia, threads drawn across sand and across water; see [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]].
--- context
The region stands at the heart of the [[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]] and the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]], and it is central, too, to the [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]].
--- observer
I think of the longest history the species has lived, and it happened here. I think of every population outside Africa, and in genetic terms each one is a subset of African diversity, a handful drawn from a larger store. How far can any of us travel from the house we were born in? The branch carries the root wherever it goes.

=== species/australopithecus-afarensis
src: b4972c28249c
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Australopithecus afarensis was an early hominin that walked upright across East Africa about 3.9 to 2.9 million years ago, and it is best known from the fossil 'Lucy', one name carried out of deep time to stand for the rest.
--- fact 1
'Lucy' was found at Hadar, Ethiopia, in 1974, a sleeper about 3.2 million years old.
--- fact 2
At Laetoli, Tanzania, footprints about 3.66 million years old record upright walking, a stride pressed into the earth and kept.
--- fact 3
Its brain was small (estimates range from about 380 to 550 cubic centimeters), and its long arms were shaped for climbing, as the branch shapes the hand that holds it.
--- fact 4
It is often considered to stand close to the ancestry of the genus Homo, near the headwaters of that river.
--- context
The species comes after the [[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]] and before [[species/homo-habilis|Homo habilis]], one waystation on a long road.
--- observer
We tell our lineage's story as one of the brain. The Observer reads it the other way round: upright walking came long before big brains. Think of a house whose foundations stood for ages before the upper floors were raised. The large brain, the species' most distinctive trait, was a late addition to a lineage that had been walking for millions of years. A body is built from the ground up.
--- question 1
Was A. afarensis a direct ancestor of Homo, a true root of that tree?
