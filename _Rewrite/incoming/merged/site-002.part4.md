=== era/ediacaran-period
title: Ediacaran Period
src: 2c556a026cef
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Ediacaran Period, from 635 to 538.8 million years ago, is the last period of the Proterozoic Eon, the long dusk before the Cambrian dawn. In its span the first large, complex organisms rise into the fossil record like shapes surfacing from deep water.
--- fact 1
The Ediacaran Period spans 635 to 538.8 million years ago. In 2004 it was formally ratified, the first new geological period added to the time scale in over a century, and its base is set at Enorama Creek in the Flinders Ranges of South Australia.
--- fact 2
The [[species/ediacaran-biota|Ediacaran Biota]] are a strange court of disc-, frond- and quilt-shaped organisms, known from Newfoundland, England, Namibia, the White Sea coast of Russia and the Ediacara Hills of South Australia. The oldest assemblages are about 575 million years old.
--- fact 3
Cholesterol-related lipids found with Dickinsonia fossils have been read as the signature of an animal, though its seat in the animal family tree remains unclear.
--- fact 4
Cloudina, a tubular organism from about 550 million years ago, is among the earliest animals to wear a mineralized skeleton, and some specimens bear borings interpreted as the marks of predation, a whisper of the hunt.
--- fact 5
Late in the period, trace fossils appear where animals moved through sediment, and the classic Ediacaran assemblages fade near the start of the Cambrian; see [[event/cambrian-explosion|Cambrian Explosion]].
--- context
The Ediacaran Period comes after the [[era/cryogenian-period|Cryogenian Period]] and closes both the [[era/neoproterozoic-era|Neoproterozoic Era]] and the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]], giving way to the [[era/cambrian-period|Cambrian Period]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
I look at the quilted shapes and I look for a face I know. This is the first period whose living things were large enough to leave an obvious record, and many of them have no clear living relatives. I look for the road from them to the animals that followed, and the record does not show that it ran straight. I call that contingency. I would call it less if evidence showed that most Ediacaran forms were direct ancestors of later animal groups; that would weaken what I read here. Who says a first attempt must have heirs? Not every beginning is a parent.
--- question 1
Which Ediacaran forms, if any, are the forebears of living animal groups?
--- question 2
What ended the classic assemblages: extinction, rivalry with newer animals, or changing chemistry?

=== era/epipaleolithic
title: Epipaleolithic
src: e2f4bd05b9dc
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Epipaleolithic is the final phase of the Paleolithic in the Near East and North Africa, from about 20,000 to about 10,000 BCE. It was the age of small composite stone tools, of foragers who began to linger in one place, and of the last stage before the fields were sown.
--- fact 1
The Epipaleolithic runs from about 20,000 to about 10,000 BCE in the Levant and the lands around it. The name belongs mainly to the Near East and North Africa; Europe calls the following stage the Mesolithic.
--- fact 2
Toolmakers shaped microliths, small geometric stone pieces such as lunates and triangles, like the teeth of a larger jaw, and fixed them into handles or shafts to make composite tools.
--- fact 3
Ohalo II, a site on the Sea of Galilee in Israel about 23,000 years old, keeps brush huts, hearths and the remains of well over a hundred plant species, wild cereals among them. Some researchers argue that this shows early cultivation; that claim is debated.
--- fact 4
The Natufian culture, about 12,500 to 9,500 BCE, raised semi-permanent villages while still foraging, wielded sickle blades and laid its dead in cemeteries. At Ain Mallaha in Israel a burial about 12,000 years old holds a person and a puppy together.
--- fact 5
Some scholars propose the cold, dry [[event/younger-dryas|Younger Dryas]] as a reason for greater reliance on cultivated plants, a link that is debated. From the Epipaleolithic the road runs on into the Pre-Pottery Neolithic, whose earliest sites include [[place/jericho|Jericho]] and [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]]; see [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]].
--- context
The Epipaleolithic comes after the [[era/upper-paleolithic|Upper Paleolithic]] and gives way to the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]] in the Near East. It closes the long book of the [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]], and its threads run through [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I watch them stay, there in the Epipaleolithic Near East. I watch them store food, raise semi-permanent villages, lay their dead in cemeteries, and I see some steps of the Settling appear before farming does. Why does the order matter to me? Because it suggests that settlement may in places have come first, and agriculture after. I hold this loosely: it would weaken if the Natufian sites were shown to have depended on cultivated crops from the start. First a place to grieve. Then, perhaps, a field.
--- question 1
Did the Natufian people sow their cereals, or only gather the wild ones?
--- question 2
How far did the changing climate of the late Pleistocene bend the path toward farming?

=== era/holocene-epoch
title: Holocene Epoch
src: 2419e8767486
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Holocene Epoch began about 11,700 years ago, as the last ice age loosened its grip, and it is the warm, relatively stable interval in which agriculture, cities and writing all arose, like seeds waking in a thawed field.
--- fact 1
The Holocene is an interglacial, a warm breath between the ice, its climate comparatively stable.
--- fact 2
Every page of recorded history was written within its span.
--- fact 3
Its lower boundary is fixed by a marker sealed in Greenland ice cores.
--- fact 4
Within it, the human host swelled from a few million to about eight billion.
--- context
The Holocene is the geological stage on which the species plays out the ages from the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]] through the [[era/information-age|Information Age]]. See [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]].
--- observer
Notice the striking correlation the Observer points to: the species built farms and cities only after the climate stopped swinging. It is tempting to call the calm the cause. Resist. Whether this is cause, coincidence, or both is a serious open question, and a correlation is a doorway, not a destination.
--- question 1
Did the steady Holocene climate make agriculture possible, opening the door, or did agriculture arise anyway?
