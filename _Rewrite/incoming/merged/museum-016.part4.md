src: aff6bd1c4acc
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Buster Keaton (1895 to 1966) was an American silent-film comedian, writer and director, the deadpan "Great Stone Face," whose comedy stood on engineered physical stunts he performed himself, calm as a statue while the world fell apart around him. Ranked with Charlie Chaplin among silent comedy's defining stars, he made The General (1926), now considered among the greatest films ever made.

He was born in 1895 into a vaudeville family, and from age three he performed in the act The Three Keatons, taking falls that built his later command of his body.

He came to film in 1917 under Roscoe "Fatty" Arbuckle, and by 1920 he was writing, directing and starring in his own comedies, moving into features with Our Hospitality (1923).

Sherlock Jr. (1924) set him inside a shifting film-dream by in-camera measurement rather than editing tricks, still studied for its daring.

The Navigator (1924), about passengers adrift on an ocean liner, was his greatest commercial success of the decade.

The General (1926), built around full-scale trains and a locomotive plunged off a burning bridge, failed on release but is now ranked among cinema's greatest films.

=== people--alan-turing--1
src: 399df12eb68c
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Alan Turing (1912 to 1954) was a British mathematician who formalized the concept of computation, helped break German ciphers in World War II, and proposed a test for machine intelligence.

In 1936 he described the abstract 'Turing machine', a foundation of computer science: the architecture under every device you use.

At Bletchley Park he worked on breaking the Enigma cipher.

In 1950 he published a paper proposing what became known as the Turing test.

He was prosecuted in 1952 for homosexuality, then a crime in Britain, and received a posthumous royal pardon in 2013. A pardon after death is a ledger corrected too late for the person it wronged.
