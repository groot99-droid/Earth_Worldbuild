=== event/peopling-of-europe-by-homo-sapiens
src: ffd0ae06f91d
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Homo sapiens reached Europe by about 45,000 years ago, and perhaps earlier still, stepping into a continent already held by the Neanderthals. The earliest securely dated remains, from Bacho Kiro Cave in Bulgaria and from Ranis in Germany, stand about 45,000 years old.
--- fact 1
At Bacho Kiro Cave in Bulgaria, Homo sapiens fossils dated to about 46,000 to 44,000 years ago lay together with Initial Upper Paleolithic stone tools.
--- fact 2
At Ranis in Germany, bone fragments directly dated in 2024 to about 45,000 years ago came together with leaf-shaped stone points also known from Britain, showing an early reach into the northern latitudes.
--- fact 3
At Grotte Mandrin in France, a child's tooth and distinctive stone points, dated to about 54,000 years ago, were attributed to Homo sapiens in 2022. The occupation seems to have been brief, and some researchers find that attribution unconvincing.
--- fact 4
Neanderthals and Homo sapiens overlapped in Europe for several thousand years, and some of the earliest Europeans had a Neanderthal ancestor only a few generations back; see [[event/neanderthal-extinction|Neanderthal Extinction]] and [[event/interbreeding-of-homo-sapiens-with-neanderthals-and-denisovans|Interbreeding of Homo sapiens with Neanderthals and Denisovans]].
--- fact 5
Proposed routes name the Danube corridor and the Mediterranean coast; the evidence does not settle which one carried them.
--- context
This peopling of Europe follows the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]] and belongs to the [[era/upper-paleolithic|Upper Paleolithic]], with new stone-tool industries such as the Aurignacian and the [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]] seen at [[place/chauvet-cave|Chauvet Cave]]. It overlaps the last of the [[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]] and comes before the [[event/last-glacial-maximum|Last Glacial Maximum]]. See [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I see less a single march into Europe than several small landings, some of them brief, onto ground already held by another human kind. The Mandrin tooth reminds me. The Bacho Kiro jaws remind me. 'First arrival' names the evidence found so far, not the past itself. This reading would weaken if the earliest sites turned out to belong to one continuous, growing population.
--- question 1
Did the 54,000-year-old occupation at Mandrin belong to Homo sapiens?
--- question 2
How much ancestry from the earliest Europeans survives in later Europeans?

=== event/peopling-of-sahul
src: d001359cafc8
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Modern humans reached Sahul, the Pleistocene landmass that joined Australia to New Guinea, at least 50,000 years ago, and perhaps about 65,000 years ago.
--- fact 1
Reaching Sahul meant crossing open sea through the Wallacea island chain, making it one of the earliest known deliberate sea crossings.
--- fact 2
Rock-shelter finds at Madjedbebe in Arnhem Land have been dated to about 65,000 years ago, a date still debated, while about 50,000 years ago is widely accepted.
--- fact 3
Human remains at Lake Mungo are about 40,000 years old.
--- fact 4
Many giant marsupials disappeared within several thousand years of human arrival, and the relative roles of people and climate remain debated.
--- context
This settlement followed the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]] and founded the ancestry of the [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]]. Compare the later [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]].
--- observer
I remember that this species crossed open water tens of thousands of years before it raised anything one could call a city. Boat-building and the planning behind it are far older than farming.
--- question 1
How early did the first boats cross Wallacea?

=== event/peopling-of-southeast-asia-and-wallacea
src: 459d58538fbe
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Homo sapiens reached mainland Southeast Asia by about 86,000 to 68,000 years ago, and the Wallacean islands beyond it, Sulawesi and Timor among them, by about 51,000 to 42,000 years ago. This dispersal supplied both the route and the rough timing for the further crossing into Sahul.
--- fact 1
Fossils from Tam Pà Ling cave in Laos, dated in 2023, push Homo sapiens presence in mainland Southeast Asia back to roughly 86,000 to 68,000 years ago; earlier work at the site had suggested a younger range.
--- fact 2
Human teeth from Lida Ajer in Sumatra are dated to about 73,000 to 63,000 years ago, but only indirectly, from the surrounding sediment and animal teeth rather than the human teeth themselves.
--- fact 3
The Niah Cave 'Deep Skull' in Borneo, once disputed as possibly disturbed, is now dated to about 45,000 to 39,000 years ago by direct dating of the bone itself.
--- fact 4
Within Wallacea, the earliest well-dated evidence is rock art on Sulawesi, at least 51,200 years old, and occupation at Laili Cave and Jerimalai Cave in Timor, about 44,000 to 42,000 years ago.
--- fact 5
These Wallacean dates sit oddly late beside the debated claim that Sahul was reached by about 65,000 years ago, which would require an earlier, still-undiscovered Wallacean crossing; a floor of about 50,000 years ago rests on firmer ground.
--- fact 6
A separate, non-sapiens hominin, Homo luzonensis, lived on Luzon in the Philippines, outside Wallacea proper, and should not be confused with this dispersal.
--- context
This dispersal follows the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]] and feeds into the [[event/peopling-of-sahul|Peopling of Sahul]]. Early modern humans in the region also lived through the [[event/toba-eruption|Toba Eruption]]. [[species/homo-floresiensis|Homo floresiensis]], on Flores, is a separate, older hominin lineage. See [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I think of the crossing into Wallacea as voyage after voyage over open water, made without metal, without writing, without a single domesticated animal, thousands of years before farming existed anywhere. The dates keep moving as the methods improve; I take that as ordinary science, not as a record already settled. This reading would weaken if a securely dated site placed Homo sapiens in Wallacea well before 51,000 years ago.
--- question 1
Was Sahul reached by about 65,000 years ago, and if so, where is the matching early Wallacean evidence?
--- question 2
Did the Tam Pà Ling population leave descendants among later Southeast Asian peoples?

=== event/peopling-of-the-americas
src: f0538815e7c3
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Humans reached the Americas out of northeastern Asia by at least 15,000 years ago, and perhaps much earlier still. The earliest date remains contested.
--- fact 1
Footprints at White Sands, New Mexico, have been dated to about 21,000 to 23,000 years ago. The first dates rested on the seeds of an aquatic plant, which critics said could read too old. A 2023 study radiocarbon-dated terrestrial pollen from the same layers and applied luminescence dating to the sediments, and reported that the original chronology holds up. Some researchers still question the dating.
--- fact 2
The old 'Clovis-first' model, which put the first arrival about 13,000 years ago, is no longer accepted; sites such as Cooper's Ferry in Idaho, about 16,000 years old, predate Clovis.
--- fact 3
Two routes are proposed. The inland ice-free corridor is now thought to have opened only about 13,800 years ago, later than the earliest sites, which makes a Pacific coastal route seem the more likely path for the first arrivals.
--- fact 4
Genetic studies show that Indigenous Americans descend from a mixture of ancestry, related both to ancient North Eurasians in Siberia and to East Asians. Scholars now generally favor several small migrations over one single event.
--- context
This migration follows the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]], occurred around the [[event/last-glacial-maximum|Last Glacial Maximum]], and was the last major expansion into unoccupied continents before the [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]].
--- observer
I notice that within a few thousand years, humans spread from Alaska all the way to Tierra del Fuego. Such a pace suggests a species that carries its environment with it, not in its body, but in what it knows.
--- question 1
When did the first migrants arrive, and by what route? Some proposed sites, such as Chiquihuite Cave in Mexico, about 26,000 years old, are disputed because it is not settled that their stones are truly human-made.

=== event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific
src: 662883401715
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Polynesian navigators settled the last remote islands of the central and eastern Pacific, reaching their furthest point with Aotearoa New Zealand around 1250 to 1300 CE.
--- fact 1
The ancestors of the Polynesians, tied to the Lapita culture, reached Samoa and Tonga about 3,000 years ago.
--- fact 2
A pause of roughly a thousand years came before the final expansion to Hawai'i, Rapa Nui and Aotearoa.
--- fact 3
The voyagers read star paths, ocean swells and the flight of birds, and carried no instruments at all.
--- fact 4
Ancient DNA and language alike link the settlers to Austronesian speakers out of Island Southeast Asia.
--- context
This settlement was the last great human dispersal into unoccupied lands, coming after the [[event/peopling-of-the-americas|Peopling of the Americas]]. See [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]].
--- observer
I regard this as one of this species' most extraordinary feats of knowledge: crossing thousands of kilometers of open ocean with no instrument at all, everything held instead in memory.
--- question 1
Why was there a pause before the final wave?
