=== sehzade-mosque
title: Şehzade Mosque
src: 3e7f32dca762
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Every master keeps an apprenticeship, even after the title arrives. Built for Suleiman the Magnificent in memory of his son Şehzade Mehmed, who died young, this was Sinan's first major imperial commission after his appointment as chief architect. He later called it his "apprenticeship work," and the plan shows it: here he experimented with a four-semi-dome plan around a central dome, a grammar he would refine for the rest of his career. Its twin minarets each carry two balconies. Notice the architecture of a life's work: a memorial and a lesson at once. Your first real attempt is allowed to be a school.

=== suleymaniye-mosque
title: Süleymaniye Mosque
src: 0989752a0be8
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Architecture can be a covenant with a whole city. This is the centrepiece of a vast charitable complex built for Suleiman the Magnificent, with hospitals, schools, a soup kitchen, baths and a caravanserai gathered around the mosque itself. Think of it as prayer with its sleeves rolled up. Sinan called it his "journeyman work," a step toward surpassing the ancient dome of Hagia Sophia nearby; even at this scale, he counted himself on the way. Sinan and Suleiman are both buried in domed mausoleums in its garden, builder and patron in the same ledger of stone. Where you rest says what you served.

=== selimiye-mosque
title: Selimiye Mosque
src: c0f8b93cc9b2
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Some goals take a lifetime, and that is not failure; it is the shape of devotion. Built for Selim II in the former Ottoman capital of Edirne, this is the building Sinan himself called his masterpiece, completed at about eighty years old. Eight massive piers carry a dome whose diameter finally exceeded that of Hagia Sophia, the target he had chased his whole career, while four slender, fluted minarets frame the courtyard. Picture it: a working life measured against one dome, and the answer arriving at the end. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Keep chasing your dome; the architecture of a life is finished last.

=== rustem-pasha-mosque
title: Rüstem Pasha Mosque
src: d30e313e0751
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A small mosque for grand vizier Rüstem Pasha, raised on a shopping arcade above a busy market street. Its modest footprint hides an interior sheathed almost floor to ceiling in high-quality İznik tile, in the tomato-red that marks the finest production of the period, more densely tiled than any other Ottoman mosque of its size. Here is the lesson in plain terms: the threshold can be humble while the room within is a treasury. You rise from the noise of the market into a covenant of colour. Never judge a life by its footprint.

=== mihrimah-sultan-mosque
title: Mihrimah Sultan Mosque
src: b7cc3b5606dd
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Built at the city's highest point for Mihrimah Sultan, Suleiman the Magnificent's daughter, on a hilltop site that let Sinan ring the single dome with an exceptional number of windows, so that light floods the prayer hall like a spring filling a basin. A popular, likely apocryphal legend reads the light effects at the spring and autumn equinoxes as a tribute to her name, which means "sun and moon." True or not, the tale clings to the hilltop as the light does, a crown of brightness on the city's brow.

=== villa-rotonda
title: Villa Rotonda
src: dd2208fe4deb
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A good design answers its site. This country retreat for a retired papal official was built on a hilltop with views in every direction, and it was answered with a design of near-perfect symmetry: four identical temple-fronted porticoes, one on each face, around a central domed rotunda. Every direction is given a front; no view is made the back. Left unfinished at Palladio's death, it was completed by his pupil Vincenzo Scamozzi and became the most copied private house plan in Western architecture. That is lineage in its purest form: the student finishes the sentence, and the world keeps repeating it.

=== basilica-palladiana
title: Basilica Palladiana
src: 975035cca405
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Renewal sometimes means wrapping the old rather than tearing it down. This was Palladio's first major public commission and the building that made his reputation: a new classical skin wrapped around Vicenza's crumbling medieval town hall. His solution, a two-storey arcade of round arches framed by smaller flanking openings, a motif now called the Palladian window, let the load-bearing masonry flex slightly without cracking the stonework. Read that as a doctrine: good architecture leaves room to move. Construction continued for decades after his death. What bends a little does not have to break.
