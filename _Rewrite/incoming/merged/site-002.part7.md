=== era/medieval-period
title: Medieval Period
src: eb012113c17e
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Medieval or post-classical period, about 500 to 1500 CE, was the age when major world religions consolidated, when long-distance trade networks stretched like veins across the continents, and when large steppe and Islamic polities rose and fell. The term 'medieval' is European in origin, but the age had global dimensions.
--- fact 1
The [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]] raised a new political and intellectual world, stretching from Iberia to Central Asia.
--- fact 2
The [[event/mongol-conquests|Mongol Conquests]] forged the largest contiguous land empire in history.
--- fact 3
In the 14th century the [[event/black-death|Black Death]] swept over Eurasia and North Africa and left them devastated.
--- fact 4
Its great states were many: the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]], the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], the [[culture/norse|Norse]], the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]], and, near the age's close, the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]] and [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]].
--- context
The Medieval Period follows [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]] and ends around the voyages that set off the [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]] and the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]].
--- observer
Widen your lens, the Observer advises; a global reading serves this era best. What you see then is a species increasingly connected by roads, caravans and ships, and by shared meaning-engines that crossed political claim-lines. Borders mark who rules. They do not mark where ideas stop.
--- question 1
Along the pre-modern long-distance networks, how much travelled as ideas, and how much as goods?

=== era/mesolithic
title: Mesolithic
src: 9f7015c78ee9
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Mesolithic, the Middle Stone Age, is the period of hunter-gatherer societies that lived between the last ice age and the arrival of farming in Europe and neighbouring regions, from about 10,000 to about 5,000 BCE. Like shorelines, its dates vary widely by region.
--- fact 1
The Mesolithic runs from about 10,000 to about 5,000 BCE, and in parts of northern Europe it lingers until about 4000 BCE. It ends wherever farming arrives, and so its last day falls differently from place to place. The name belongs mainly to Europe; other regions speak of it in other terms.
--- fact 2
As the ice age waned, warmth spread forests across northern Europe like a green tide, and rising seas swallowed coastal lowlands such as [[place/doggerland|Doggerland]].
--- fact 3
People hunted deer, wild boar and aurochs, fished, and gathered shellfish and plants. Dugout canoes such as the Pesse canoe from the Netherlands, about 10,000 years old, are among the oldest known boats. Among the well-studied sites are Star Carr in northern England and the shell middens of the Ertebølle culture in Denmark.
--- fact 4
Farming began in Southwest Asia by about 9600 BCE and came into Europe from about 6500 BCE; ancient DNA shows that early farmers of Anatolian ancestry partly replaced and partly mingled with the local foragers, and the balance varied by region.
--- context
The Mesolithic comes after the [[era/epipaleolithic|Epipaleolithic]] and the [[era/upper-paleolithic|Upper Paleolithic]] and overlaps the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]], for foragers and farmers lived side by side in different regions. It lies in the [[era/holocene-epoch|Holocene Epoch]], and its threads run through [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]]. See [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]].
--- observer
Notice, as the Observer does, that the Mesolithic is defined by what it is not: after the ice age, before farming. Notice too that the label fits some regions better than others. So treat such labels as tools, not as stages every society passed through. The proof is in the living: foragers in some places lived this way for thousands of years after farming had begun elsewhere. A label is a lantern, not a road. The reading would weaken if most societies did follow the same sequence.
--- question 1
How much of the Mesolithic population of Europe was replaced by farmers, and how much was folded in?
--- question 2
Why did some coastal foragers, such as the Ertebølle people, hold back from farming for centuries after contact?

=== era/mesoproterozoic-era
title: Mesoproterozoic Era
src: 0621892f62d3
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Mesoproterozoic Era is the middle era of the Proterozoic Eon, running from 1.6 billion to 1.0 billion years ago. It lies within the interval often called the "Boring Billion", a long hush in which climate, ocean chemistry and life showed little evident change, though how uneventful that hush truly was is debated.
--- fact 1
The Mesoproterozoic Era spans 1.6 to 1.0 billion years ago. Its three periods, the Calymmian, Ectasian and Stenian, and its borders alike are set by numerical ages.
--- fact 2
The "Boring Billion" is an informal name for about 1.8 to 0.8 billion years ago, an age with no well-established ice ages and only slow apparent change in the fossil record; the term itself is debated.
--- fact 3
Atmospheric oxygen is thought to have stayed well below today's level, and parts of the deeper ocean are reconstructed as oxygen-poor or rich in sulfide, a sour and breathless deep, though estimates vary widely.
--- fact 4
[[species/stromatolites|Stromatolites]] still rose in their stony cushions, common in shallow seas. Eukaryotes diversified: Tappania microfossils are about 1.5 billion years old, and the red alga Bangiomorpha, dated in different studies to between roughly 1.05 and 1.2 billion years ago, is often cited as the oldest taxonomically resolved eukaryote.
--- fact 5
The supercontinent Nuna, or Columbia, is proposed to have broken apart about 1.6 to 1.3 billion years ago, and late in the era Rodinia began to gather its scattered lands.
--- context
The Mesoproterozoic Era lies between the [[era/paleoproterozoic-era|Paleoproterozoic Era]] and the [[era/neoproterozoic-era|Neoproterozoic Era]] within the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]], and it rests between the oxygen rise of the [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]] and the glaciations of [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]], a long calm between breath and ice; see [[event/origin-of-eukaryotes|Origin of Eukaryotes]].
--- observer
Treat the quiet as a message, the Observer says: the apparent quiet of the Mesoproterozoic is information. A planet's surface can stay much the same for hundreds of millions of years without heading anywhere in particular. There is no queue in deep time. What arrived later was not overdue. The reading names its own undoing: it would weaken if new records showed that the quiet hides substantial change in eukaryote diversity. Stillness is not the same as waiting.
--- question 1
Was the Boring Billion truly still, or does a sparse record hide its changes?
--- question 2
What kept atmospheric oxygen low for so long after the Great Oxidation Event had passed?
