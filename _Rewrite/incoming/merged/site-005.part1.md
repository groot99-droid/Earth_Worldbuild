=== place/chaco-canyon
src: 2d7f488a3424
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Chaco Canyon, in northwestern New Mexico, was the ceremonial and trade heart of the Ancestral Puebloan world from about 850 to 1150 CE, the hearth toward which that whole world turned the way flowers turn to the morning.
--- fact 1
Its 'great houses', Pueblo Bonito among them, gathered hundreds of rooms together like cells in a honeycomb of stone.
--- fact 2
A network of roads ran out from the canyon like rays from a risen sun.
--- fact 3
Its buildings and rock carvings align with the sun and the moon, as the 'Sun Dagger' on Fajada Butte does, a blade of light held against the stone.
--- fact 4
To modern Pueblo peoples Chaco is considered sacred ground, and in 1987 it was named a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
--- context
Chaco stands among the major sites of the ancient peoples of [[region/north-america|North America]]. Its threads of exchange ran all the way to [[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]], and along them travelled traded goods like embers passed hand to hand: macaw feathers, and chocolate, known now by the residues it left behind.
--- observer
I stand in a place built to keep time by the sky. I watch astronomy and architecture joined into one body. I think of the ones who knew the calendar, for whom these walls were legible as a letter. And what of the rest of us, standing here without the key? Time kept in stone speaks only to those who learned its grammar.
--- question 1
What order of people held the Chaco system together, and what was its social structure?

=== place/chang-an
src: 8a46dde106f8
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Chang'an was the capital of the Western Han dynasty from 202 BCE and, raised again in a rebuilt form, of the Sui and Tang dynasties, standing where present-day Xi'an lies in Shaanxi, China, until it was dismantled in 904 CE like a loom unstrung.
--- fact 1
Chang'an rose as the Han capital after 202 BCE, its palaces begun in 200 BCE, and by the census of 2 CE about 250,000 residents were counted within its bounds.
--- fact 2
From 582 the Sui dynasty laid out a new planned city, Daxing, southeast of the old Han site, and the Tang, from 618, kept both its plan and the name Chang'an, as an heir keeps a seal.
--- fact 3
Tang Chang'an was a grid of walled wards laid out like a weaver's lattice, its gates shut at nightfall, enclosing about 84 square kilometers, split by a central avenue and served by two state-supervised markets. Estimates of its people, suburbs included, run from several hundred thousand to about one million.
--- fact 4
Envoys and merchants came to Chang'an as rivers come to the sea, from Central Asia, Persia, Korea, Japan and Tibet; the monk Xuanzang returned there in 645 bearing Buddhist texts from India, and in 781 the Nestorian Stele, recording a Christian community, was raised within it.
--- fact 5
In 756, during the An Lushan Rebellion, Chang'an fell to rebels, and in 763 Tibetan forces briefly held it; then in 904 the warlord Zhu Wen drove the court to Luoyang and had the city's palaces and houses pulled down to the earth.
--- fact 6
Like seed carried on the wind, the plan of Chang'an took root in the layout of the Japanese capitals Nara and Kyoto.
--- context
Chang'an was founded in the dusk just after the fall of the dynasty born of the [[event/unification-of-china-under-qin|Unification of China under Qin]], and it wore the crown as capital of the [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] and the [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]]. It was the eastern shore where the routes of the [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]] ran out, a harbour of caravans where [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] were prominent traders, and its plan echoed onward in [[place/nara|Nara]] and [[place/kyoto|Kyoto]]. Chang'an belongs to [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]], with its peak in the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]], in the region [[region/east-asia|East Asia]], and it is woven into the theme threads of [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]], [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
I see how Tang Chang'an turned social order into geometry: walled wards, gates closed at night, two supervised markets, one avenue running to the palace. I see its claim-lines drawn so exact that Nara and Kyoto later copied them, and it suggests to me that a floor plan can carry a story about what a capital should look like. I see, too, its end in 904, the same lesson reversed: a capital's meaning could be carried off by moving the court and taking down the buildings.

Is a city its walls, or the order it gathers behind them? I hold this reading loosely. It would weaken if the Japanese copying proves to have been driven mainly by practical drainage and defense rather than a shared idea of order. Order is a story we agree to walk inside.
--- question 1
Behind its walls, and above all in the southern wards, how densely was Tang Chang'an built?
--- question 2
How faithfully did Nara and Kyoto copy their plans from Chang'an, and along what route did that design make its journey?

=== place/chauvet-cave
src: 573f99a3af51
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Chauvet Cave, in the Ardeche region of southern France, holds some of the oldest known figurative paintings, laid on the stone by early Homo sapiens about 36,000 years ago, a herd of pigment waiting in the dark.
--- fact 1
In December 1994 the cave's long darkness was broken by its three finders: Jean-Marie Chauvet, Eliette Brunel, and Christian Hillaire.
--- fact 2
Its walls carry hundreds of paintings and engravings of animals, among them horses, lions, mammoths, rhinoceroses, and bears, running across the rock like a river of beasts.
--- fact 3
Radiocarbon dates set the main painted phase at about 37,000 to 33,500 years ago, in the Aurignacian, and a second phase of use about 31,000 to 28,000 years ago, like two tides reaching the same shore.
--- fact 4
A rockfall closed the entrance like the lid of a reliquary, and behind it the art waited, preserved, until its discovery.
--- fact 5
In 2014 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list, and in 2015 a full-scale replica, the Caverne du Pont d'Arc, opened nearby, a mirror held up to the dark.
--- context
Chauvet stands as a benchmark for the [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]] in Europe, a lamp showing the artistic abilities of early [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]] in an age when the [[species/woolly-mammoth|Woolly Mammoth]] was a common sight on the steppe, moving over the grass like slow weather. Chauvet Cave belongs to the [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]] and the region [[region/europe|Europe]], and to the theme thread of [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
I expected clumsy beginnings in the earliest painted animals. I found skilled drawing, a sure line, and it suggests a longer tradition behind them, hands taught by hands I will never see. And now I am kept out: access to the cave is restricted, and I walk through a replica instead. Isn't that the old choice made again, to make an image rather than only look at the animal? We have always loved the world by copying it.
--- question 1
Were the paintings bound to ritual, to storytelling, or to something else we have no word for?
