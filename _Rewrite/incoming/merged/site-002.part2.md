=== era/cenozoic-era
title: Cenozoic Era
src: a4b1365ab9e2
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Cenozoic Era began 66 million years ago and has not yet ended; we stand inside it still. It is the age of mammals, of grasslands rolling to the horizon, of a climate slowly cooling, and within its span lies all of human history.
--- fact 1
Its three periods: Paleogene, Neogene, Quaternary.
--- fact 2
In the long quiet after the [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]], mammals and birds diversified, branching like rivers across a delta.
--- fact 3
The world's climate cooled overall, and ice sheets crowned the poles, then advanced and withdrew, again and again, like a white tide.
--- fact 4
Primates appeared early in the era, and about 7 million years ago the hominin lineage branched away on its own road. See [[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]].
--- context
Within the Cenozoic lie the [[era/pleistocene-epoch|Pleistocene Epoch]], the [[era/holocene-epoch|Holocene Epoch]], and the proposed [[era/anthropocene|Anthropocene]], nested like rings in a tree still growing.
--- observer
I came to watch one species, and it arrives late, in the final fraction of a percent of this era. I watched most of the era pass in the long cooling that shaped the world the species inherited. What is an inheritance, if not a room made ready by no one? Most of the story is the house. The guest comes last.
--- question 1
Which turns of the climate most shaped hominin evolution?

=== era/classical-antiquity
title: Classical Antiquity
src: ab2cd852e17c
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Classical Antiquity, about 500 BCE to 500 CE, was the age of the great territorial empires, and of the philosophical and religious traditions that still shape the world like rivers running underground.
--- fact 1
Its empires stood like mountain ranges: Persia, Macedon, Rome, Maurya India, and Han China.
--- fact 2
Great currents of thought rise in this age, among them Greek philosophy, Confucianism, Buddhism, and the Hebrew prophetic tradition; see [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]].
--- fact 3
Trade routes bind Eurasia together like threads drawn through a loom; see [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]].
--- fact 4
In the Mediterranean the age closes with the [[event/fall-of-the-western-roman-empire|Fall of the Western Roman Empire]], the setting of a long western sun.
--- context
Classical Antiquity rises from the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] and flows onward into the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]]. Among its great powers were the [[culture/ancient-greeks|Ancient Greeks]], the [[culture/romans|Romans]], the [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] and the [[culture/achaemenid-persians|Achaemenid Persians]].
--- observer
Consider what the Observer notes: several distant societies, largely out of contact, developed comparable ethical and philosophical questions around the same centuries. Separate rooms, similar questions. Minds need not meet to reach the same threshold.
--- question 1
What could explain why the Axial traditions rose near-simultaneously, like fires lit on distant hills?

=== era/cretaceous-period
title: Cretaceous Period
src: ee65eff91b76
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Cretaceous Period, from about 143.1 to 66 million years ago, is the last period of the Mesozoic Era and the longest of the Phanerozoic Eon. It was a warm greenhouse world in which the first flowering plants opened, and it ends as a great house falls, with the extinction of the non-avian dinosaurs.
--- fact 1
The Cretaceous Period spans about 143.1 to 66.0 million years ago, though older references set its start at 145.0 million years ago. Its name is drawn from the Latin word for chalk, and its top, the base of the Paleogene, is fixed at El Kef in Tunisia.
--- fact 2
The world was a warm greenhouse and sea level stood high, spilling shallow seas across the continents, the Western Interior Seaway of North America among them, and laying down thick chalk deposits like snow on a drowned floor.
--- fact 3
[[species/flowering-plants|Flowering Plants]] appear, their oldest widely accepted pollen about 134 million years old, and they diversified rapidly alongside pollinating insects, a bargain struck in colour and dust. Meanwhile the [[event/breakup-of-pangaea|Breakup of Pangaea]] went on as the South Atlantic opened.
--- fact 4
Among the Late Cretaceous [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]] strode Tyrannosaurus and Triceratops in North America; birds diversified, and mosasaurs swam as large marine reptiles. Mammals were mostly small, and whether the modern placental orders arose before or after the end-Cretaceous extinction is debated.
--- fact 5
Then came the [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]], about 66 million years ago: it followed the Chicxulub impact, coincided with the eruption of the Deccan Traps, and killed about three-quarters of species, fire from the sky and fire from below.
--- context
The Cretaceous Period comes after the [[era/jurassic-period|Jurassic Period]] and closes the great book of the [[era/mesozoic-era|Mesozoic Era]], giving way to the [[era/paleogene-period|Paleogene Period]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
Look at how the Cretaceous ends, the Observer says: with dinosaurs at or near their greatest diversity, ended by a rock from space and, probably, volcanic stress. From this the Observer draws a practice. Read long, stable-looking intervals as provisional, since nothing in the record of the last million years before the boundary announces what was about to happen. Stability is a description, not a promise. The Observer also names what would weaken the reading: evidence of a long decline in dinosaur diversity before the impact.
--- question 1
Were the non-avian dinosaurs already fading before the end-Cretaceous impact?
--- question 2
Did the modern orders of placental mammals take root in the Cretaceous, or only after the extinction?
