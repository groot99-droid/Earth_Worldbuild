=== place/mecca
src: f399dad79b97
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Mecca is a city in the Hejaz region of western Saudi Arabia, about 70 kilometers inland from the Red Sea; it is the birthplace of Muhammad and the holiest city of Islam, and its sanctuary around the Kaaba draws the annual Hajj pilgrimage the way a basin gathers every stream.
--- fact 1
The date of Mecca's first settlement is unknown, lost like footprints under drifting sand, and whether it is the place Macoraba in Ptolemy's 2nd-century geography is disputed. Muslim sources written after Muhammad's time describe a sanctuary town governed by the Quraysh in the century before Islam.
--- fact 2
Islamic tradition places Muhammad's birth in Mecca about 570 CE, his journey to Medina in 622, and the city's passing under Muslim control in 630, with little fighting.
--- fact 3
The Hajj is one of the Five Pillars of Islam, required once in a lifetime of every Muslim able to make the journey; in recent decades Saudi authorities have reported attendance in the low millions each year, a tide that returns with the turning year.
--- fact 4
Historians disagree about Mecca's weight in long-distance trade before Islam; some see a regional sanctuary and market town, not a hub where the incense and spice trades converged.
--- fact 5
Mecca was ruled locally by the Sharifs beneath a succession of overlords, the Ottomans among them from 1517, until the Saudi conquest of 1924 to 1925. Non-Muslims are barred from entering the city.
--- fact 6
During the 2015 Hajj, in the crush at Mina near Mecca, the Saudi authorities reported 769 deaths, while counts compiled from other governments' figures exceeded 2,000.
--- context
Mecca is the central place of the [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]], joined by the Hijra to [[place/medina|Medina]], and a waystation in the peninsula's wider [[event/incense-trade|Incense Trade]] setting. It passed under the [[culture/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad Caliphate]] and later the [[culture/ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]], and pilgrims came from far horizons, among them the ruler of the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], Mansa Musa, who made the Hajj in 1324. Mecca stands in the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]] and the region [[region/arabian-peninsula|Arabian Peninsula]], and it is woven into the theme threads of [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]].
--- observer
I see a meaning-engine drawing millions of strangers to one point each year. I see a recurring exchange-web of people, goods and news that reached from West Africa to Southeast Asia. I see pilgrims in plain white garments, for men two unstitched cloths, so that the visible marks of rank grow faint during the rites.

But I remember that pilgrims themselves describe the journey as obedience and devotion, and my reading of its social effects is meant to sit beside their account, not replace it. I remember Mina in 2015, and the crowd disasters that recur as a cost of gathering at this scale; I record them as suffering.

My reading would weaken if pilgrim networks turn out to have carried little trade or news beside their devotional purpose. What gathers us is not always what we carry home.
--- question 1
What does archaeology reveal about pre-Islamic Mecca, when so little excavation has been done in the sacred precinct?
--- question 2
How great a part did Mecca play in long-distance trade before Islam?

=== place/monte-verde
src: 93efe2c595d2
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Monte Verde is an archaeological site in southern Chile that, for decades, was considered the best evidence that people lived in South America about 14,500 years ago, before the Clovis culture of North America kindled its fires; now its age is debated, and the old certainty has thinned to mist.
--- fact 1
From the 1970s, excavations led by Tom Dillehay laid bare what a peat bog had kept like a miser: wooden hut foundations, leather, rope, and plant remains.
--- fact 2
In 1997 a panel of archaeologists came to inspect the site and accepted an age of about 14,500 years.
--- fact 3
The site helped topple the 'Clovis-first' model like an old stone from its plinth; see [[event/peopling-of-the-americas|Peopling of the Americas]].
--- fact 4
A recent study has proposed a much younger age for the site; specialists dispute it, and the question stands unsettled, a scale still swinging.
--- context
Monte Verde is a keystone site for the [[event/peopling-of-the-americas|Peopling of the Americas]], dating from the end of the [[event/last-glacial-maximum|Last Glacial Maximum]], when the ice was loosening its grip.
--- observer
I know how a site's importance can outrun its certainty. I know how a single set of dates can hold up a large theory. I know the theory is only as strong as the dates. What are we standing on, when we stand on a number? Every tower leans on its lowest stone.
--- question 1
How old, truly, is Monte Verde?
