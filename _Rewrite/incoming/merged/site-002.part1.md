=== era/anthropocene
title: Anthropocene
src: 2008986742a5
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Anthropocene is a proposed geological interval, a name for the stretch of time in which human activity is the dominant influence on Earth's systems. Think of it as a signature we did not know we were signing. In March 2024 the International Union of Geological Sciences declined to formalize it as an official epoch, yet the term remains widely used: the ledger was refused, and still we keep writing in it.
--- fact 1
Consider the first candidate threshold: a proposed start date around 1950, tied to the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]] and to markers such as radioactive fallout.
--- fact 2
Other proposed thresholds reach further back, to the beginning of agriculture and to the [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]].
--- fact 3
The ledger of cited signals reads plainly: changes to atmospheric carbon dioxide, plastics, and species extinction rates.
--- fact 4
On 4 March 2024 the Subcommission on Quaternary Stratigraphy voted to reject formalization as an epoch, and on 20 March 2024 the IUGS upheld the result. Yet a vote on the codex is not a verdict on the world: the formal geological decision does not settle the scientific or cultural question.
--- context
Use the Anthropocene as a lens, and the [[era/information-age|Information Age]] and the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]] come into sharper focus through it. For the longer argument, see [[observer-note/the-species-as-a-geological-force|The Species as a Geological Force]].
--- observer
I have read the rock record for fire, for ice, for the slow arrival of shells. Here, as I read it, is the point where the species became legible in the rock record, its behavior pressed into the layers like a palm into wet clay. For the first time, a single species' behavior is a planetary variable. Who taught you to write so large? No one did. That is the weight of it.
--- question 1
If the threshold must be drawn at all, from when should the Anthropocene be dated?

=== era/archean-eon
title: Archean Eon
src: 80003b3422e4
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Archean Eon, about 4.0 to 2.5 billion years ago, was the age in which the first continents stabilized like islands of cooling iron and life first appeared. Its air held almost no free oxygen, a sky still waiting for its breath.
--- fact 1
Among the oldest widely accepted fossil evidence stand the stromatolites of Western Australia, layered stone-cushions about 3.5 billion years old.
--- fact 2
Continental cores, the cratons, formed and grew like the first bones of a body, and plate-tectonic-like processes began their slow grinding at some point in this eon.
--- fact 3
Life in this age was microbial, a kingdom of the unseen. See [[event/origin-of-life|Origin of Life]].
--- fact 4
Before the eon closed, photosynthesis had evolved, the first light-fed labour that set up the [[event/great-oxidation-event|Great Oxidation Event]].
--- context
The Archean is the long, slow overture to the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]], a hall in which the first living things were kindled and the chemistry was laid, stone by patient stone, that later made complex life possible.
--- observer
Imagine you arrived in the Archean with a notebook. You would find a living planet and no obvious candidates for study: no faces to read, no tools to catalogue. That emptiness is the lesson. The Observer treats this eon as the deep root of the entire tree, and a root is still the tree.
--- question 1
When did plate tectonics first stir into motion?
--- question 2
Are the oldest claimed microfossils true witnesses, or tricks of stone?

=== era/bronze-age
title: Bronze Age
src: 8cf31b8e84f5
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Bronze Age, about 3300 to 1200 BCE in the Near East, was the age of bronze metallurgy, when copper met tin in the furnace, when the early states rose, and when the first writing was pressed into being.
--- fact 1
Bronze is a marriage of copper and tin, an alloy wed in fire; see [[technology/bronze-metallurgy|Bronze Metallurgy]].
--- fact 2
Now the first cities rise and the first writing systems wake: [[event/the-urban-revolution|The Urban Revolution]], [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]].
--- fact 3
Its great powers were many-crowned: the [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]], the [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]], the [[culture/indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley Civilization]], the [[culture/minoans|Minoans]] and the [[culture/shang-dynasty|Shang Dynasty]] were among its major states.
--- fact 4
In the eastern Mediterranean the age ends as a lamp gutters, in the [[event/bronze-age-collapse|Bronze Age Collapse]].
--- context
The Bronze Age stands between the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]] and the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] like a bridge of hammered metal. Tin travelled long roads, and that long-distance trade spun the era into wide exchange-webs, like dew-heavy threads strung between distant hands.
--- observer
Notice what arrived together, the Observer says: the first states and the first writing. Writing seems to have started as accounting, not poetry, a ledger before a lyric. We like to believe the word was born to sing; it seems it was first set down to count. The ledger is the oldest architecture of the written word.
--- question 1
How deeply did the long-distance tin trade shape the early states?

=== era/cambrian-period
title: Cambrian Period
src: 852873209e47
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Cambrian Period, from 538.8 to 485.4 million years ago, is the first period of the Paleozoic Era, the dawn in which the seas put on armour. In its span most major animal body plans, and the first animals with hard parts, appear in the fossil record, as though a curtain had been drawn back on a crowded stage.
--- fact 1
The Cambrian Period spans 538.8 to 485.4 million years ago. Its base, which is also the base of the Paleozoic Era, is set in stone at Fortune Head, Newfoundland, by the first appearance of the burrow trace fossil Treptichnus pedum, a small tunnel marking a great door.
--- fact 2
The [[event/cambrian-explosion|Cambrian Explosion]], when most major animal groups step into the fossil record, burns through roughly the first 13 to 25 million years of the period.
--- fact 3
[[species/trilobites|Trilobites]] first appear about 521 million years ago, and in time they become one of the most common animals on the sea floor, a host in segmented mail.
--- fact 4
Soft bodies survive, exceptionally preserved, in the Chengjiang deposits of China, about 518 million years old, and in the Burgess Shale of Canada, about 508 million years old.
--- fact 5
Animals dwelt almost entirely in the sea. Sea level stood high, shallow seas spread across the continents like spilled silver, and the climate was generally warm. The land's record of plants waits until the Ordovician; see [[event/colonization-of-land|Colonization of Land]].
--- context
The Cambrian Period rises from the [[era/ediacaran-period|Ediacaran Period]] and throws open the gates of the [[era/paleozoic-era|Paleozoic Era]]; after it comes the [[era/ordovician-period|Ordovician Period]]. Its thread runs through the great weave of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
Here is a general problem you will meet again, and the Observer reads the Cambrian fossil record as an early case of it: what is known of the past is what happened to be preserved. Think of an archive that kept only the letters written on stone. Hard parts leave records, so a sudden arrival of animals is partly the arrival of animals that fossilize. The practice is humility before the ledger. The Observer holds the reading open: evidence that the fossil sequence closely tracks real diversity would weaken this emphasis on preservation. The archive is not the world; it is the world's survivors.
--- question 1
How much of the Cambrian Explosion is a true blaze of evolution, and how much a blaze of preservation?
--- question 2
What spark lit the forging of skeletons in several animal groups within a few million years?

=== era/carboniferous-period
title: Carboniferous Period
src: 63293f58fdd4
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Carboniferous Period, from 358.9 to 298.9 million years ago, is the fifth period of the Paleozoic Era, and it bears the name of the coal its swamp forests laid down like black treasure in a vault. It is the age of the first amniote vertebrates, and the age in which the supercontinent Pangaea was gathered together.
--- fact 1
The Carboniferous Period spans 358.9 to 298.9 million years ago. Its name means coal-bearing, and in North America its long reign is split in two, the Mississippian and the Pennsylvanian.
--- fact 2
Swamp forests spread over tropical lowlands, ruled by tree-sized club-mosses such as Lepidodendron, and they buried the plant matter that became coal. Why so much coal formed in this period is still debated. Britain's coalfields were laid down in it, and their coal later fed the fires of the [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]] as a major fuel.
--- fact 3
Estimates for late Carboniferous atmospheric oxygen run as high as about 30 percent, against about 21 percent today, though how far to trust them is debated. Giants moved through that air, among them arthropods such as the dragonfly relative Meganeura, whose wings spanned about 70 centimetres.
--- fact 4
The earliest known amniotes appear, bearers of eggs able to develop on land, a sealed sea carried ashore; among them is Hylonomus from Nova Scotia, about 312 million years old.
--- fact 5
In this period the Late Paleozoic Ice Age began, its cold heart centred on Gondwana. About 305 million years ago the coal forests fell as the climate dried, and Gondwana and Euramerica met in collision, raising much of Pangaea from their meeting.
--- context
The Carboniferous Period rises after the [[era/devonian-period|Devonian Period]] and yields to the [[era/permian-period|Permian Period]] within the [[era/paleozoic-era|Paleozoic Era]]. It is woven into the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]], and its forests carry on the long march of [[event/colonization-of-land|Colonization of Land]].
--- observer
I think of forests that never meant to leave anyone anything. I think of the carbon they locked away in the dark, and I think of the species that dug it up about 300 million years later and burned it, returning it to the air. Nothing about those forests was aimed at that result. It is an inheritance received by accident, a gift no one gave. I hold only to the link across time; the debate over why so much coal formed does not touch it. We are warmed by the dead who never knew our names.
--- question 1
Why did so much coal gather in this age and not in others?
--- question 2
How high did atmospheric oxygen rise, and how far did it limit the size of insects?
