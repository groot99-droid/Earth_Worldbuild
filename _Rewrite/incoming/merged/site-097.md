=== species/sheep
src: 1d7c67580b99
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The domestic sheep was bred from the wild mouflon in the region from the northern Zagros Mountains to south-eastern Anatolia between about 9000 and 8500 BCE. It was kept first for meat, milk and skins and only later bred for wool, and its flocks now spread across the world like a pale tide of well over a billion animals.
--- fact 1
Out of the mouflon, a wild sheep of southwestern Asia, the sheep was domesticated in an estimated window between about 9000 and 8500 BCE, in the region from the northern Zagros Mountains to south-eastern Anatolia, and the practice had travelled to Mehrgarh in the Indus Valley by about 7000 BCE.
--- fact 2
The first domestic sheep were kept mainly for meat, milk and skins rather than fleece; archaeological evidence, statuary from Iran among it, suggests that deliberate selection for woolly sheep began around 6000 BCE, some two to three thousand years after the first taming, a turning sometimes called the secondary products revolution.
--- fact 3
The earliest known woven wool garments date to around 4000 BCE, and by about 6000 BCE Neolithic farmers in what is now France, among the Castelnovien people, were already keeping domestic sheep.
--- fact 4
Today sheep graze in great numbers across Australia, New Zealand, the southern regions of South America, South Africa and the British Isles, while China and India also hold very large flocks by absolute count; the world's domestic sheep were estimated at around 1.2 billion in 2019.
--- context
Sheep were among the first animals drawn into [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]], and beside crops such as wheat and barley they stood as one pillar of [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]] in the Fertile Crescent; they were commonly herded with the [[species/goat|Goat]], tamed in a similar place and period. Sheep belong to the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]] and the region [[region/mesopotamia|Mesopotamia]], and to the theme threads of [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]] and [[theme/technology|Technology]].
--- observer
The Observer offers a reading, not a fact. Consider the gap of two to three thousand years between the taming of sheep and the deliberate breeding for wool. It suggests that early herders did not domesticate the animal with fleece already in mind; they discovered a secondary use, and bred for it, only once the animal was already settled in their kin-lattice. The Observer reads this as evidence that a domesticated species' eventual economic role can differ substantially from the reason it was first tamed. Its spread across so much of the pastoral world since then shows a tool-lineage of herding techniques travelling with the species itself. The reading would weaken if evidence of early wool use turns up that predates the currently accepted timeline for woolly-sheep breeding. What we tame for one purpose may teach us another.
--- question 1
Beyond meat, milk and skins, what use did early herders make of sheep in the two to three thousand years before wool breeding began?
--- question 2
How far did sheep domestication in the northern Zagros and south-eastern Anatolia shape, or draw on, the roughly contemporary domestication of goats in the same region?
