=== people--vitus-bering--1
src: c5b44a6902fa
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Vitus Bering (1681 to 1741) was a Danish-born officer in the Russian navy who led two expeditions across Siberia to chart the waters between Asia and North America. The strait and the sea between the continents still carry his name, like a word cut into the edge of the world.

He was baptized on 5 August 1681 in Horsens, Denmark, and joined the Russian navy in 1704 under Peter the Great. He died on 19 December 1741 on the island now named for him.

On the First Kamchatka Expedition (1725 to 1730) he sailed through the strait between Siberia and Alaska in 1728 without sighting the American coast, though some historians think it was visible.

The Great Northern Expedition (1733 to 1743) swelled to about 3,000 people, one of the largest scientific undertakings of its time.

In 1741 his ship, the St. Peter, and a second ship under Aleksei Chirikov, the St. Paul, sailed separately toward America. Chirikov's crew sighted land about a day earlier, on 15 July by the dates given in the sources; Bering's crew sighted Mount Saint Elias on 16 July.

On the voyage home the St. Peter was wrecked on an uninhabited island. Bering died there, a death traditionally put down to scurvy and exposure, though a 1991 analysis of his remains judged heart failure the likely cause; of 77 men aboard, 46 survived to reach Kamchatka the next year.

=== people--tupaia--1
src: 56feb4e69c8c
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Tupaia (about 1725 to 1770) was a priest and navigator of Ra'iatea in the Society Islands who sailed with James Cook on the Endeavour in 1769 and drew a chart of Pacific islands. He died at Batavia in 1770.

He was born about 1725 on Ra'iatea and became a priest and a reader of the sea's roads. When warriors from Borabora took Ra'iatea, he found refuge on Tahiti.

In July 1769 he joined Cook's Endeavour, usually said to be at Tahiti (sources differ on the place), as guide and interpreter, with a young servant, Taiata.

In August 1769 he began a chart of the Pacific with Cook, Joseph Banks and other officers. Sources differ: it is said to show about 130 islands, of which he could name about 74, and it is often described as centered on Ra'iatea, home at the heart of the ocean.

The chart did not match European map conventions, and modern scholars have argued that it follows a Polynesian way of ordering islands by voyages and directions.

When the ship reached New Zealand in October 1769 he could speak with Māori, whose language is closely related to Tahitian, and he interpreted for the crew.

=== people--ajuricaba--1
src: 65498a3d1a7c
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Ajuricaba (died about 1727) was a leader of the Manaos people of the Rio Negro, in the Brazilian Amazon, who resisted Portuguese colonization and the enslavement of Indigenous people in the 1720s. Almost everything known about him comes from Portuguese sources, so read every line as told by the other side.

He led the Manaos (Manaó) people of the Rio Negro valley, in present-day Amazonas, Brazil. His birth year and origins are unclear; the date given is that of his death.

He resisted Portuguese colonization and the enslavement of Indigenous people in the 1720s. Portuguese accounts say he allied with Dutch traders from the Guianas, who were rivals of Portugal.

The governor of Grão-Pará, João da Maia da Gama, sent forces against him. Some accounts say he was captured through the betrayal of allies.

Portuguese accounts say that while he was being taken in chains toward Belém, he and his men rose against their guards, and he threw himself into the river and drowned, in 1727 (some sources say 1728).

He is remembered in Brazil as a symbol of Indigenous resistance, and the city of Manaus takes its name from the Manaos people. A people's name on a city is a memorial that outlasts the record against them.
