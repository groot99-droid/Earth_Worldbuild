--- description
Yermak Timofeyevich (about 1532 to 1585) was a Cossack leader whose expedition across the Urals in 1581 and 1582 overthrew the Khanate of Sibir and began the Russian conquest of Siberia. His early life lies in fog, poorly known.

His birth year is uncertain; about 1532 is often given. Most accounts say he was a Volga Cossack, and some say he was wanted for piracy.

In 1581 he set out from the Ural lands of the Stroganov merchant family with several hundred men armed with firearms, though accounts of the year and the numbers vary.

In 1582 his force defeated the army of Khan Kuchum of Sibir and took the khanate's capital, Qashliq (Isker), on 26 October.

He sent word of the conquest to Tsar Ivan IV, who reportedly pardoned him and sent gifts.

Yermak died in August 1585 (some sources say 1584) in an ambush by Kuchum's forces. Tradition says he drowned in the Irtysh River, dragged down by chain mail sent by the tsar, the gift become a weight in dark water.
