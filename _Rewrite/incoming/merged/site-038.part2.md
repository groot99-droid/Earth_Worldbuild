=== culture/cherokee
title: Cherokee
src: c9331c0e8fc5
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Cherokee are a people native to the southeastern United States. Their nation adopted a written syllabary in the 1820s, survived the forced removal known as the Trail of Tears, and continues today as several federally recognized nations, including the Eastern Band in North Carolina. Hold those threads together, the script, the removal and the continuance, and you have the lineage this note traces.
--- fact 1
Begin with the land: at European contact in the sixteenth century, the Cherokee homeland spanned parts of present-day North Carolina, South Carolina, Georgia, Alabama and Tennessee.
--- fact 2
Consider the patience behind a script: Sequoyah completed an 85-character syllabary for Cherokee in 1821, after twelve years of work, and it enabled mass literacy within a short time.
--- fact 3
In 1838 and 1839, United States troops under General Winfield Scott forcibly removed most Cherokee to territory west of the Mississippi; the journey is remembered as the Trail of Tears, and many died on it.
--- fact 4
Some Cherokee avoided removal by hiding in the North Carolina mountains or by claiming state citizenship. Their descendants formed the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians, whose home is the Qualla Boundary.
--- fact 5
The lineage continues: the Cherokee Nation in Oklahoma and the Eastern Band are among the largest Native American nations in the United States today.
--- context
The Cherokee homeland lay in [[region/north-america|North America]]. [[person/sequoyah|Sequoyah]]'s syllabary gave the nation a written mark-code of its own shortly before the [[event/indian-removal-and-the-trail-of-tears|Indian Removal and the Trail of Tears]], in the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]], and that timing is worth sitting with. See [[theme/language-and-writing|Language and Writing]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I think of a nation given a new mark-code for its own language in the 1820s. I think of the records it kept with it, the petitions it wrote with it, through the removal that tried to end its presence in its homeland. I think of how the story did not end there. One people's story, and after a single act of state violence, two claim-lines: an Eastern Band and a western nation, the same story carried on along both.

Do I want the alphabet to be the hero? I do. A specialist could argue that literacy's role in Cherokee survival is overstated next to land, kinship networks and legal strategy, which would weaken any reading that credits the syllabary alone. So I set the pen down beside the land, the kin and the law. A people is never carried by any single thing it holds.
--- question 1
How much did literacy, set against other factors, shape Cherokee resistance to removal?
--- question 2
How have the histories of the Eastern Band and the Oklahoma nation compared since they were separated?

=== culture/christianity
title: Christianity
src: 9afe81445806
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Christianity began among the followers of Jesus of Nazareth in first-century Roman Judea, and from that small spring it flowed out through the Mediterranean world until it became the most widely practiced religion in the world.
--- fact 1
Jesus of Nazareth was executed by Roman authority in Jerusalem, most likely between 29 and 33 CE. His followers believed he had risen from death, and on that belief, as on a cornerstone, the new movement was built.
--- fact 2
Paul of Tarsus, once a persecutor of the movement, became its most active early missionary, founding communities among non-Jews across the Roman Empire and shaping much of its early theology, as a river shapes its valley.
--- fact 3
Within about a century, Christian communities had taken root across Greece, Syria, Armenia, North Africa and beyond, like seed scattered along every shore.
--- fact 4
The faith split into Eastern Orthodoxy and the Catholic Church in the Great Schism of 1054, and the Reformation of 1517 brought forth Protestant churches, leaving three main branches today.
--- fact 5
Christianity counts about 2.4 billion adherents, roughly a third of the world's population, and the Catholic Church alone holds about 1.3 billion members.
--- context
Christianity arose in the [[region/levant-and-anatolia|Levant and Anatolia]] within the [[culture/romans|Roman Empire]] during [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]]. It later became the state religion of the [[culture/byzantine-empire|Byzantine Empire]], and centuries afterward it shared that same region with the [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]]. See [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
I keep returning to a movement built around one man's death. I keep returning to the convert who had once opposed the movement and then carried it. I keep returning to how, within a single century, its story-glue crossed the ordinary claim-lines of ethnicity within the Roman world. What kind of story does that?

Then 1054. Then 1517. Two splits, the same meaning-engine dividing again over authority and practice, and still its central claims persisted. A specialist could argue that its early rapid growth owed as much to Roman roads and a common language as to the message itself, which would weaken any reading that credits the spread to doctrine alone. So I hold both, the message and the road it walked on. Even a message needs somewhere to walk.
--- question 1
How much of Christianity's early spread reflects the message itself, and how much the infrastructure of the Roman Empire it grew within?
--- question 2
How do its many branches today compare in the ways they trace authority back to the earliest community?
