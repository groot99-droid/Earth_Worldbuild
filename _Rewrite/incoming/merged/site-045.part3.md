=== person/albrecht-durer
title: Albrecht Dürer
type: person
date: 1471 – 1528
themes: art, technology
src: 52466c2eaadf
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Albrecht Dürer (21 May 1471 to 6 April 1528) was a painter and printmaker from Nuremberg who carried the Italian Renaissance north. His woodcuts and engravings, sold across Europe, made him the first artist whose international reputation was built largely on prints.
--- fact 1
Dürer was born in Nuremberg, the son of a Hungarian-born goldsmith, and from 1486 was apprenticed to Michael Wolgemut, whose workshop produced woodcuts for book illustration.
--- fact 2
He travelled to Venice in 1494, and again from early 1506 to 1507, where Giovanni Bellini treated him as an equal; in 1498 he published the Apocalypse, 15 large woodcuts (some counts give 16 designs dated 1498) that made him famous.
--- fact 3
From 1512 Emperor Maximilian I employed him and granted him a pension; on a trip to the Netherlands in 1520 to 1521 to have it confirmed by Charles V, his diary recorded his amazement at Aztec treasures sent from Mexico.
--- fact 4
He sympathised with Martin Luther's reforms, and in his last years published treatises on measurement, fortification and human proportion.
--- fact 5
His 'AD' monogram served as a trademark, and he took action against copyists who used it.
--- fact 6
Key works include Self-Portrait at the Age of Twenty-Eight (1500), Young Hare (1502), Melencolia I (1514) and The Rhinoceros (1515).
--- fact 7
His prints were copied by artists from Italy to Persia and Mughal India, and Raphael exchanged drawings with him; in the 19th century German Romantics made him a national hero.
--- context
Dürer worked across [[region/europe|Europe]] during the [[event/renaissance|Renaissance]], within the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]] and the years of the [[event/protestant-reformation|Protestant Reformation]]. His diary touches the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]], and his prints reached the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]]. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]] and [[theme/technology|Technology]].
--- observer
I see a maker who understood, early, that a picture printed on paper travels farther than one hung on a wall. I see a workshop skill made into a good that could move through trade, and a monogram defended like a claim of ownership. In his self-portraits I find an artist presenting himself as learned, almost god-made, a status he claimed for himself rather than for any patron. And the 19th-century cult that crowned him a German hero — that came later, stitched on after his death, not a truth of his own time.
--- question 1
How far did his prints, rather than his paintings, set the terms of his fame in his own lifetime?

=== person/aristotle
title: Aristotle
type: person
date: 384 – 322 BCE
themes: science, language
src: 6fd81708d375
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Aristotle (384 to 322 BCE) was a Greek philosopher and scientist who studied logic, ethics, politics, physics, and biology, and tutored Alexander of Macedon.
--- fact 1
He studied under Plato in Athens, and later founded his own school, the Lyceum.
--- fact 2
He wrote on logic, ethics, politics, poetics, physics, and biology.
--- fact 3
His biological work drew on careful observation of marine life.
--- fact 4
His works were preserved and extended in the Islamic world, and later in Latin Europe.
--- context
Aristotle worked in [[place/athens|Athens]] and Macedon among the [[culture/ancient-greeks|Ancient Greeks]], and taught [[person/alexander-the-great|Alexander the Great]]. His works framed European and Islamic scholarship for centuries.
--- observer
I see the taming of patterns become a system: classifying, defining, arguing from evidence. And I see, too, how a great authority can slow inquiry, when his errors are mistaken for settled ground.
--- question 1
Which of his writings survive as he wrote them?

=== person/ashoka
title: Ashoka
type: person
date: c. 304 – 232 BCE
themes: religion, war, language
src: 9cdf72ee9b84
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Ashoka (about 304 to 232 BCE) was a Mauryan emperor who ruled most of the Indian subcontinent, and became a patron of Buddhism after a bloody war.
--- fact 1
He conquered the region of Kalinga around 261 BCE at great human cost; his own edicts record about 100,000 killed and 150,000 deported.
--- fact 2
He had edicts inscribed on rocks and pillars across his empire, promoting 'dhamma,' or moral conduct.
--- fact 3
He supported the spread of Buddhism, including missions sent abroad.
--- fact 4
His lion capital now serves as a national emblem of India.
--- context
Ashoka ruled during [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]] in [[region/south-asia|South Asia]], and helped make [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]]'s teaching a regional, and later global, tradition.
--- observer
The Observer notes that Ashoka's edicts stand as an early case of a ruler reflecting publicly on the harm of war. Public inscriptions offered a new way of speaking to a whole population at once.
--- question 1
How much of Ashoka's dhamma was policy and how much personal belief?
