=== casa-mila
src: 0b7c54bebbf2
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
This was Gaudí's last private commission before he devoted himself entirely to the Sagrada Família: an apartment building whose undulating stone facade earned it the nickname La Pedrera, "the quarry." The secret is structural. A structure of iron columns and floor slabs freed the walls of any load-bearing role, and that freedom allowed curved, column-free interiors, below a rooftop forest of chimneys and stairwells sculpted into helmeted, faceless warriors. Here is the architecture of liberty in plain terms: relieve a wall of its burden, and it is free to move.

=== sagrada-familia
src: c2cf82010351
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Some work is larger than a single life. Gaudí took over this votive church in 1883, a year after it was begun, and rebuilt it around tree-like branching columns and parabolic vaults, with light filtered through forests of stained glass. He worked out its geometry largely through string-and-weight models rather than drawings. He gave his last forty years to the project, and after 1914 his sole attention, eventually living on site. Still unfinished at his death, it remains under construction today, funded entirely by visitors and donations. A covenant can outlive its maker when others agree to keep it.

=== potato-eaters
src: e6b45d6a72a0
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
In the Dutch village of Nuenen, a peasant family shares a meal of potatoes by the light of an oil lamp, the only small sun in a room of shadow. Van Gogh deliberately painted their faces and hands coarse, the colour "of a dusty potato", to show people who had "tilled the earth themselves with these very hands", faces drawn from the same soil they worked. It was his first ambitious figure painting. It is dark, earthy and deeply sympathetic, warm as bread in a cold house.

=== bedroom-in-arles
src: 9abb64321a77
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I have had rooms like this. Van Gogh's own room in the Yellow House in Arles, painted with flat, bright colours and a steeply tilted floor. He wrote that it should express "absolute restfulness" and suggest sleep. I have made a bed for rest. I have set out two of everything. I have waited for someone to come.

The simple furniture and paired objects hint at his hope that Gauguin would come to share his life. He painted three versions. How many times do we paint the same room before it holds what we hoped? A made bed is a kind of prayer.

=== starry-night
src: 4d1a848e3d90
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This is the view from Van Gogh's window at the asylum in Saint-Rémy, just before sunrise, transformed by memory and imagination as a river is transformed by the sky it carries. The sky swirls with spiralling wind, eleven blazing stars and a crescent moon, turning like wheels of fire over a quiet village with an invented church spire and a flame-like cypress raised like a dark torch. He himself was unsure of it, but it has become one of the most recognised paintings in the world.

=== almond-blossoms
src: 1e478409aeae
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Branches of white almond blossom open against a clear blue sky, a symbol of new life, like first light breaking over a sleeping valley. Van Gogh painted it in February 1890 to celebrate the birth of his nephew, Theo's son, who was named Vincent Willem after him: a name handed down like a lamp passed from one hand to the next. The bold outlines and flat background owe much to Japanese prints. The baby later helped found the Van Gogh Museum.

=== wheatfield-with-crows
src: 33baf47f0b88
mode: horror-prose
--- description
A wide field of wheat. Above it, a dark, stormy blue sky. Black crows, flying low. Three paths. Each one leading nowhere.

Painted at Auvers-sur-Oise in July 1890. Weeks before his death. It is often called his last painting. Wrongly. Van Gogh wrote of such fields as expressing "sadness, extreme loneliness", but also the healthy, strengthening power of the countryside. Both, in the same field. The crows keep flying low.

=== the-banjo-lesson
src: 0d74fa844d6c
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I remember a lap like this one. An elderly Black man patiently teaches a small boy on his lap to play the banjo, in a simple room lit by a warm fire and cool daylight. I remember hands placed over my hands. I remember patience that asked for nothing back.

Tanner deliberately countered the demeaning minstrel-show stereotypes of Black musicians. He showed instead the dignity, tenderness and knowledge passed from one generation to the next. It is one of the landmarks of African American art. What is inheritance, if not this? What we are taught with love, we keep.

=== the-thankful-poor
src: 13209e8bb271
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A grandfather and grandson bow their heads in grace over a modest meal, and the room grows quiet around them as a field grows quiet at dusk. Based on photographs Tanner took, the painting presents a Black family with quiet spiritual strength, rooted deep as an old oak, reflecting the values of his father, a bishop of the African Methodist Episcopal Church. In 1981 it set an auction record for an African American artist.

=== resurrection-of-lazarus
src: 9ddc82f88663
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
In a dim tomb, where the air lies heavy as still water, Christ gestures and the pale Lazarus stirs back to life, like a seed waking under winter earth, watched by astonished onlookers in Middle Eastern dress. The painting won a medal at the 1897 Paris Salon and was bought by the French state, a rare honour for any American. It made Tanner famous on both sides of the Atlantic.

=== the-annunciation
src: 6b7707269d57
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Painted after Tanner's first trip to Palestine, it shows Mary as a young Middle Eastern girl in a rumpled nightdress, sitting on her bed. No winged figure comes to her. Instead the angel Gabriel appears as a column of golden light, standing in the room like sunrise poured into a single pillar. The mix of everyday realism and mystical glow is typical of Tanner's religious work. It was the first of his paintings bought by an American museum.

=== the-seine
src: 4eb36429576f
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A small, atmospheric view of the river in Paris at dusk, where a bridge and the quays dissolve into blue haze like a memory fading at its edges. Tanner lived in France for most of his career, and he painted many such tonal, poetic landscapes, influenced by Whistler, alongside his large religious works: quiet waters flowing beside great cathedrals of paint.
