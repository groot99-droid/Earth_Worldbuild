=== people--jose-rizal--1
src: c30f263192f7
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
José Rizal (1861 to 1896) was a Filipino physician, writer and reformer whose novels criticized Spanish colonial rule. Spanish authorities executed him in Manila on 30 December 1896, and he is regarded as a national hero of the Philippines. His weapon was the novel, and that is worth remembering.

He was born on 19 June 1861 in Calamba, in the Philippines. He studied in Manila, Madrid, Paris and Heidelberg and became an eye doctor.

He wrote the novels Noli Me Tángere (1887) and El Filibusterismo (1891), which portrayed abuses by Spanish friars and officials; the colonial authorities banned them. When a government bans a novel, it is admitting the novel is true enough to fear.

In 1892 he founded La Liga Filipina, a reform society, and was exiled to Dapitan on Mindanao, where he practiced medicine and taught until 1896.

The Philippine Revolution began in August 1896. Rizal, who had volunteered as a doctor in Cuba, was arrested on his way there, brought back to Manila and convicted of rebellion. He had said he opposed an armed uprising at that time.

He was shot by firing squad on 30 December 1896, aged 35. Before his death he wrote a poem now known as "Mi último adiós."

=== people--gustav-klimt--1
src: 8af6bc292928
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Gustav Klimt (14 July 1862 to 6 February 1918) was an Austrian painter and the leader of the Vienna Secession. His golden, patterned paintings of women and love, The Kiss above all, are icons of turn-of-the-century Vienna, gleaming like treasure in a dim hall.

He was born at Baumgarten near Vienna, second of the seven children of a gold engraver. In 1876 he won a scholarship to the Vienna School of Arts and Crafts and, with his brother Ernst and Franz Matsch, painted murals and ceilings for the Burgtheater and the Kunsthistorisches Museum.

In 1897 he became the first president of the Vienna Secession, a group breaking from the official academy under the motto "To every age its art, to art its freedom".

His ceiling paintings for the University of Vienna, showing Philosophy, Medicine and Jurisprudence, raised a scandal over nudity and pessimism. He withdrew from the commission, and in 1945 the paintings were lost to fire.

He never married. He had a lifelong companionship with the designer Emilie Flöge and fathered several children with models. He upheld younger artists such as Egon Schiele and Oskar Kokoschka. He died in Vienna after a stroke.

In his "golden period" (about 1899 to 1910) he laid on real gold and silver leaf, drawing on the Byzantine mosaics he saw in Ravenna in 1903, and on Japanese and Egyptian art. Key works include The Kiss (1907 to 1908) and the Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I (1907).
