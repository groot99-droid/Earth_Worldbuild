=== event/great-oxidation-event
src: 23ef9654ec80
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Great Oxidation Event was the rise of free oxygen into Earth's air, beginning about 2.4 billion years ago, driven by the quiet work of photosynthetic microbes.
--- fact 1
Oxygen-producing photosynthesis carried out by [[species/cyanobacteria|Cyanobacteria]] slowly filled the chemical sinks that had swallowed it before, sinks such as iron dissolved in the sea.
--- fact 2
Banded iron formations hold that process recorded in stone.
--- fact 3
Oxygen was a poison to much of the anaerobic life that had ruled before it; some scientists infer from this a great turnover of the living world.
--- fact 4
It may be linked to the Huronian glaciation, one of the earliest of the ice ages.
--- context
This event opens the [[era/proterozoic-eon|Proterozoic Eon]]. Oxygen would later sustain complex life, animals among them; see [[event/cambrian-explosion|Cambrian Explosion]].
--- observer
Here is a lesson that recurs across the record: a living population can change its planet so thoroughly that it reshapes its own future. You are the second case of this, not the first.
--- question 1
How long did the transition take?

=== event/green-revolution
src: ea33caec8630
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Green Revolution was the spread, from the 1940s to the 1970s, of high-yield crop varieties, fertilizers, and irrigation, and it greatly increased grain production.
--- fact 1
The plant breeder Norman Borlaug developed high-yield semi-dwarf wheat in Mexico, and IR8 rice was released in 1966.
--- fact 2
India, Pakistan, Mexico, and the Philippines counted among the earliest and largest adopters.
--- fact 3
The revolution is credited with averting large famines, though it also brought heavy fertilizer and water use with it.
--- fact 4
Yields and populations rose together.
--- context
The Green Revolution fed the growth of the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]]. Compare it with [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]].
--- observer
You can read this as a second Settling: another jump in food that let the species grow by billions. The Haber-Bosch process, which fixes nitrogen for fertilizer, now underwrites a large share of your food supply.
--- question 1
What were the long-term ecological costs?

=== event/haitian-revolution
src: 6f67be0e1beb
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Haitian Revolution, from 1791 to 1804, was a rebellion of enslaved people against the French colony of Saint-Domingue, and it created the independent state of Haiti.
--- fact 1
Saint-Domingue was one of the most profitable sugar colonies in the world.
--- fact 2
An uprising began in August 1791, and Toussaint Louverture rose as its most prominent leader.
--- fact 3
Independence was declared on 1 January 1804, making Haiti the first republic founded by formerly enslaved people.
--- fact 4
France later demanded a large indemnity, in 1825, which burdened Haiti's economy for generations after.
--- context
The revolution stands as a central event of the [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]] and a turning point in the history of the [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]].
--- observer
Notice that the language of universal rights, the very language French revolutionaries also used, was taken at its word by people its authors had excluded. Story-glue, once written, can be turned against those who wrote it.
--- question 1
How did the indemnity shape Haiti's later development?

=== event/holocaust
src: e712ba02dbc5
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Holocaust was the persecution of European Jews by Nazi Germany beginning in 1933, and the systematic murder of about six million of them from 1941 to 1945, carried out with the help of allies and collaborators.
--- fact 1
Persecution began after the Nazi rise to power in 1933 and included exclusion, expropriation, and the Nuremberg Laws of 1935.
--- fact 2
Systematic mass murder began in 1941, first through shootings in the occupied Soviet Union, and then in extermination camps such as Auschwitz-Birkenau, Treblinka, and Sobibor.
--- fact 3
About six million Jews were killed.
--- fact 4
The Nazis murdered as well Roma and Sinti people, disabled people, Soviet prisoners of war, and political opponents.
--- fact 5
The word genocide was coined by Raphael Lemkin in 1944, and the UN adopted the Genocide Convention in 1948.
--- context
The Holocaust took place within [[event/world-war-ii|World War II]] and stands as the most thoroughly documented case of [[observer-note/sanctioned-harm|Sanctioned Harm]] by a modern state. Holocaust sits within the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]] and the region of [[region/europe|Europe]], within the theme threads of [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
The Observer records this as an atrocity carried out by an industrial, literate, bureaucratic society, and it declines to explain the fact away. The evidence shows that ordinary institutions, records, and technologies were turned toward extermination, which is why you keep returning to the question of how this occurred.
--- question 1
How did ordinary people come to take part?
