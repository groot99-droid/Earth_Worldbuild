=== person/ibn-khaldun
title: Ibn Khaldun
type: person
date: 1332 – 1406
themes: science, kinship, war
src: ff95b07915b8
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Ibn Khaldun, who lived from 1332 to 1406, was a North African historian whose 'Muqaddimah' (1377) proposed a general theory of how societies and states rise and fall.
--- fact 1
Born in Tunis, he served in several courts across North Africa and Andalusia.
--- fact 2
He argued that group solidarity, asabiyyah, helps found dynasties, and that it weakens as rulers grow comfortable.
--- fact 3
He wrote about economics, urban life, and the role of climate and environment.
--- fact 4
He is sometimes called a founder of sociology and of historiography.
--- context
Ibn Khaldun wrote within the world of [[person/ibn-battuta|Ibn Battuta]] and recorded facts about the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]]; his approach sits close to the Observer's own.
--- observer
The Observer notes that Ibn Khaldun was practicing an "anthropology of the species" six centuries ago, hunting for patterns in how groups rise and fall rather than for the will of individuals — a precursor of the Observer's own method.
--- question 1
How well does his cycle of dynasties fit the historical record?

=== person/ibn-sina
title: Ibn Sina
type: person
date: 980 CE – 1037
themes: science, religion
src: 31430c7f1398
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Ibn Sina, known as Avicenna, lived from 980 to 1037 as a Persian physician and philosopher whose 'Canon of Medicine' became a standard medical text across the Islamic world and Europe for centuries.
--- fact 1
He was born near Bukhara, in Central Asia.
--- fact 2
Completed about 1025, the Canon of Medicine organized medical knowledge into a systematic whole.
--- fact 3
Translated into Latin, it remained in use in European universities into the 17th century.
--- fact 4
His philosophical 'Book of Healing' likewise influenced medieval Europe.
--- context
Ibn Sina drew on the tradition of the [[event/translation-movement-and-the-house-of-wisdom|Translation Movement and the House of Wisdom]] and the wider world of the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]].
--- observer
I think of one book, carried across languages and centuries, organizing how many societies practiced medicine at all. I think of how a single text can become the glue that holds a whole profession together — every physician reading from the same page, without ever meeting.

A shared text can bind strangers tighter than a shared teacher ever could.
--- question 1
How much of the Canon was original versus a synthesis?

=== person/indira-gandhi
title: Indira Gandhi
type: person
date: 1917 – 1984
themes: war, religion
src: 85ff86aa76fb
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Indira Gandhi, who lived from 1917 to 1984, served as prime minister of India for most of the years 1966 to 1984. She led India through the 1971 war that created Bangladesh, imposed emergency rule from 1975 to 1977, and was assassinated by her own bodyguards after ordering an army raid on Sikhism's holiest shrine.
--- fact 1
Born on 19 November 1917 in Allahabad, daughter of Jawaharlal Nehru, India's first prime minister, she became prime minister herself in 1966.
--- fact 2
In 1971 India backed the independence of East Pakistan, which became Bangladesh, following a short war with Pakistan.
--- fact 3
On 25 June 1975 she had a state of emergency declared, suspending civil liberties, censoring the press, and jailing opponents, until elections in 1977 that she lost.
--- fact 4
She returned to power in 1980. Government policy during her years in office also pushed the Green Revolution's higher-yield crops.
--- fact 5
In June 1984 she ordered Operation Blue Star, a military assault on the Golden Temple in Amritsar meant to remove armed Sikh militants; it damaged the shrine and angered many Sikhs.
--- fact 6
On 31 October 1984 two of her Sikh bodyguards assassinated her in New Delhi, and riots afterward killed thousands of Sikhs, mostly in Delhi.
--- context
Indira Gandhi led India in [[region/south-asia|South Asia]] during the [[era/information-age|Information Age]], a state formed after the [[event/partition-of-india|Partition of India]]. See [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that a leader who suspended the young republic's own rules in 1975 was, nine years later, killed for ordering force against a meaning-engine's holiest site — and that the sanctioned harm done to her spread outward into killing among ordinary Sikhs. A specialist could argue that Operation Blue Star addressed an armed occupation of the temple rather than Sikh belief itself, which would weaken any reading of the raid as a simple attack on religion.
--- question 1
How far did the 1975 Emergency reflect a threat to her rule, and how far a choice to entrench it?
--- question 2
What did she foresee for the Sikh community after Operation Blue Star?

=== person/isaac-newton
title: Isaac Newton
type: person
date: 1642 – 1727
themes: science
src: 58ceaae4bf1b
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Isaac Newton, who lived from 1642 to 1727, was an English mathematician and physicist whose laws of motion and universal gravitation formed the basis of classical mechanics.
--- fact 1
His 'Principia' (1687) laid out the laws of motion together with the law of gravitation.
--- fact 2
He developed calculus independently of Leibniz, a parallel discovery that led to a bitter priority dispute.
--- fact 3
He studied optics and showed that white light is composed of colors.
--- fact 4
He also gave much of his time to alchemy and theology.
--- context
Newton completed the work of the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] begun by [[person/galileo-galilei|Galileo Galilei]], and his physics framed science until the time of [[person/albert-einstein|Albert Einstein]].
--- observer
I think of an apple and a planet, obeying the same rule without having met. I think of what it means to find a law that holds everywhere you look, ground and heavens both — proof that we could find laws holding everywhere we looked.

One rule behind two falls: the sky was never as far from the earth as it looked.
--- question 1
How should his interest in alchemy be understood alongside his physics?
