=== place/paris
src: bb4bfa29cf7f
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Paris is the capital of France, on the Seine River. It grew from a settlement of the Gaulish Parisii on the Île de la Cité in about the 3rd century BCE into a Roman town, a medieval capital, a center of revolutions and one of Europe's largest cities: a lineage of reinventions laid along one river.
--- fact 1
Paris began as a settlement of the Parisii on the Île de la Cité in about the 3rd century BCE; Rome took the area in 52 BCE and built the town of Lutetia.
--- fact 2
Clovis made Paris his capital in about 508, and from 987 the Capetian kings made it the center of the French kingdom; Notre-Dame was begun in 1163, and by about 1200 the University of Paris had developed.
--- fact 3
Around 1300 Paris had perhaps 200,000 inhabitants, placing it among the largest cities in Europe, and in 1348 it lost a large share of its people to the Black Death.
--- fact 4
The Bastille fell on 14 July 1789 during the French Revolution, and in the Reign of Terror of 1793 to 1794 thousands of people were executed in Paris.
--- fact 5
In the 1850s and 1860s Baron Haussmann rebuilt central Paris with wide boulevards, sewers and parks, a new architecture laid over the old.
--- fact 6
In 1871 the suppression of the Paris Commune killed many thousands of people in a week of fighting and executions; estimates run from about 10,000 to 20,000.
--- fact 7
Germany occupied Paris from June 1940 to August 1944; in July 1942 French police arrested about 13,000 Jews in the Vel' d'Hiv roundup, and most of them were later deported to Auschwitz.
--- fact 8
In 1991 UNESCO inscribed Paris, Banks of the Seine.
--- context
Paris was the stage of the [[event/french-revolution|French Revolution]] and a center of the [[event/enlightenment|Enlightenment]]; it was founded on ground later ruled by the [[culture/romans|Romans]], and under German occupation in [[event/world-war-ii|World War II]] it was the scene of roundups that fed the [[event/holocaust|Holocaust]]. You will find it in [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]] and the region [[region/europe|Europe]], and it belongs to the theme threads of [[theme/science|Science]], [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]] and [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that Paris has been remade again and again as a display of the state's story-glue, and as a stage for its overthrow. Walk its layers and you see the pattern: a cathedral for the medieval monarchy, revolutionary crowds, and 19th-century boulevards planned for order and circulation. The architecture of a capital is an argument about who rules. The same city organized the roundup of its Jewish residents in 1942 and now commemorates it, and the Observer records the deaths of the Terror, the Commune and the deportations as suffering under different regimes. The reading would weaken if the city's monuments prove to have been shaped more by local building needs than by successive regimes' claims. Remembrance, too, is a kind of architecture.
--- question 1
How large did Paris grow at its medieval height, and how did the Black Death change it?
--- question 2
How have the Commune and the 1942 roundup been held in memory in French public life?

=== place/pasargadae
src: 3d19828c35db
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Pasargadae was the first capital of the Achaemenid Persian Empire, founded by Cyrus the Great in about 550 BCE on the high plateau of Fars in present-day Iran, under a wide sky, and it holds his tomb.
--- fact 1
Pasargadae stands in Fars province, southwestern Iran, about 90 kilometers northeast of Persepolis.
--- fact 2
Cyrus the Great founded Pasargadae in about 550 BCE, after defeating the Median king Astyages, and it served as a royal seat until Darius I and his successors turned their hands to Persepolis and Susa.
--- fact 3
Pasargadae holds an audience hall, residential palaces, a gate where a winged figure stands in relief, and a formal garden threaded with water channels, regarded as an early example of the Persian garden.
--- fact 4
Stonemasons from other corners of the empire, Lydia and Ionia in western Anatolia among them, set their hands to its masonry, and its architecture braids Persian, Elamite, Mesopotamian and Anatolian elements into one.
--- fact 5
The Tomb of Cyrus, a stepped base crowned by a gabled stone chamber, stands at the site; Greek writers report that Alexander came to it and ordered its restoration after it had been robbed.
--- fact 6
Inscriptions naming Cyrus appear at Pasargadae, though some scholars argue that they were added later, under Darius I.
--- fact 7
In 2004 UNESCO inscribed Pasargadae, and the first capital passed into the world's keeping.
--- context
Pasargadae was the first capital of the [[culture/achaemenid-persians|Achaemenid Persians]], and it was later overshadowed by [[place/persepolis|Persepolis]], as a lamp is by the rising sun. Cyrus, its founder, took [[place/babylon|Babylon]] in 539 BCE, and Alexander visited the site during [[event/alexander-s-conquests|Alexander's Conquests]]. It stands in the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] and the region [[region/iranian-plateau|Iranian Plateau]], and it is woven into the theme threads of [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]], [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that Pasargadae shows an empire assembling its style from its subjects: masons from Lydia, motifs from Mesopotamia and Elam, and a garden layout later copied across Persian courts. That is an exchange-web of craftsmen and ideas made visible in stone, and it is worth sitting with: the architecture of power here is a borrowed grammar, spoken with confidence. A founder's tomb that a later conqueror chose to visit and restore suggests that story-glue about kingship could pass across dynasties and even across enemies. The reading would weaken if the building phases prove to belong mostly to Darius I, since the site would then tell more about a later king's claim on Cyrus's legacy. A legacy is something others decide to keep.
--- question 1
Which of Pasargadae's buildings belong to Cyrus, and which to the kings who came after?
--- question 2
What parts did Pasargadae and Persepolis play in the rituals of the court?
