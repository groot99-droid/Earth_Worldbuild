=== people--louis-comfort-tiffany--1
src: f0ef4c980ee5
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Louis Comfort Tiffany (1848 to 1933) was an American designer whose iridescent Favrile glass, stained-glass windows and lamps became the signature look of American Art Nouveau, colour caught in glass like light held in water. Recent scholarship credits many of his studio's best-known designs to staff designers, above all Clara Driscoll.

He was born in New York in 1848, son of the jeweller Charles Lewis Tiffany. He trained as a painter under George Inness before turning to interior design and the decorative arts in the 1870s.

In 1882 his firm redecorated state rooms in the White House for President Chester Arthur; the work was later removed. In 1885 he founded what became Tiffany Studios, and in 1894 he trademarked the name Favrile.

His Byzantine-style chapel for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago won acclaim across nations. He built Laurelton Hall on Long Island as a showcase of his work.

Favrile glass builds iridescent, metallic colour into molten glass through mineral oxides. "Drapery glass" was worked while hot to form folds like fabric.

Financial losses, traced back to the 1920s and running on into the Depression, forced the studio to close. He died in New York in 1933.

=== people--emperor-meiji--1
src: 3949f0e32e23
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Emperor Meiji (1852 to 1912) was the Emperor of Japan from 1867, and in his name the Meiji Restoration turned Japan into a modern industrial state, as a river cuts a new channel to the sea.

He came to the throne in 1867 at the age of about fourteen.

The Charter Oath (1868) was issued in his name, promising reform.

His reign saw a new constitution (1889), industrialization, and Japan's rise as a regional power.

He died in 1912.
