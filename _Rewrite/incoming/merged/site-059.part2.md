--- summary
Trade and economy cover the production, exchange, and taxation of goods, labor, and money, and the institutions built to organize them: markets, credit, coinage, companies, tribute, and forced labor. This hub traces exchange from the long-distance movement of stone and shells in prehistory, through Bronze Age metal trade, the Silk Road, Indian Ocean, and trans-Saharan networks, and the global commerce created after 1492, on to the industrial production and digital economy of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. Trade carried ideas, religions, crops, and diseases right alongside goods, and it also included the coerced trade in people. This hub gathers the vault's notes on routes, trading states, commodities, and economic systems.
--- observer
The Observer names these exchange-webs and notes that they have no owner and no center. Trade repeatedly turns strangers into partners, and transmits pathogens just as readily as products. The record suggests that exchange-webs rest on trust that has to be built, through shared accounts, coinage, law, and other story-glue, and that they sit right beside coercive extraction — tribute, forced labor, and the trade in people — which the Observer records in the same ledger, rather than in a separate one (see [[observer-note/frontiers-of-extraction|Frontiers of Extraction]]). Networks that carried goods also carried ideas, religions, crops, and diseases, so the Observer refuses to treat them as merely economic. This reading would weaken if long-distance trade proves to have been driven mainly by states and elites, with little of the decentralized quality the term implies.
--- question 1
How did early markets and money actually emerge?
--- question 2
How much long-distance exchange before writing can actually be reconstructed from materials alone, and how reliable are those inferences?
--- question 3
Why did some exchange routes last for centuries — the Silk Roads, the Indian Ocean, the trans-Saharan routes — while others collapsed outright, as in the [[event/bronze-age-collapse|Bronze Age Collapse]]?
--- question 4
How did trade, tribute, and coercion actually interact in empires such as the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]], the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]], the [[culture/romans|Romans]], and the [[culture/qing-dynasty|Qing Dynasty]]?
--- question 5
Why did sustained growth in output per person begin exactly when and where it did?
--- question 6
How far did trade itself spread disease, from the [[event/black-death|Black Death]] to the [[event/1918-influenza-pandemic|1918 Influenza Pandemic]] and the [[event/covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 Pandemic]], and how did societies actually respond?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Long-distance exchange of stone and shells runs back tens of thousands of years. The [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] moved obsidian and other goods over long distances across the western Pacific, and the hunter-gatherer-fishers of [[place/poverty-point|Poverty Point]] in Louisiana brought in stone and other materials from hundreds of kilometers away, between about 1700 and 1100 BCE.
- Farming supported denser populations and permanent settlement ([[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]]), and the first cities followed close behind ([[event/the-urban-revolution|The Urban Revolution]], [[place/uruk|Uruk]], [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]]). [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]] began as a way to record goods and obligations, and the earliest tablets from Uruk stand as accounting records. [[technology/bronze-metallurgy|Bronze Metallurgy]] made trade essential, since tin was scarce and had to be carried over long distances from places such as Afghanistan and later Cornwall.
- Bronze Age networks tied together the eastern Mediterranean, Anatolia, Egypt, and the Indus. The [[culture/indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley Civilization]] traded with Mesopotamia and made goods to standard weights, the [[culture/minoans|Minoans]] traded across the eastern Mediterranean, and [[person/hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]] sponsored a trading expedition to Punt. The [[event/bronze-age-collapse|Bronze Age Collapse]] around 1200 BCE ended the palace economies outright, and the breakdown of trade networks stands among the proposed causes.
- Caravan and sea traders grew rich simply as middlemen. The [[event/incense-trade|Incense Trade]] carried frankincense and myrrh from southern Arabia and the Horn of Africa by camel caravan; the [[culture/kingdom-of-saba|Kingdom of Saba]] and the [[culture/nabataeans|Nabataeans]] profited from taxing and guiding the caravans; and the [[culture/phoenicians|Phoenicians]] of the Levant coast founded colonies, including [[place/carthage|Carthage]], and spread a simple alphabet along with their trade.
- The [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]] from the second century BCE linked East Asia, South Asia, Central Asia, and the Mediterranean together. Goods included silk, horses, glass, spices, and paper, and disease traveled the same routes too ([[event/black-death|Black Death]]). The [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] paid the [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]] annual gifts, the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]] connected Rome, Parthia, India, and Han China commercially, and the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] became the leading Silk Road merchants, acting as caravan leaders, interpreters, and bankers all at once.
- Large states built their economies around roads, taxation, and records. The [[culture/romans|Romans]] were tied together by Latin, Greek, and trade, [[person/augustus|Augustus]] reorganized the army, provinces, and taxation, the [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]] controlled Red Sea trade between Rome, Arabia, and India and minted its own coins, and the Persepolis tablets of the [[culture/achaemenid-persians|Achaemenid Persians]] record rations and administration ([[place/persepolis|Persepolis]]).
- The [[event/indian-ocean-trade-network|Indian Ocean Trade Network]] rode the monsoon winds and was busiest from about the 8th to the 15th century, carrying spices, cotton textiles, ivory, gold, and Chinese porcelain. The [[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]], such as Kilwa, which minted its own coins, grew rich from it directly; [[culture/srivijaya|Srivijaya]] controlled shipping through the Strait of Malacca; the [[culture/chola-dynasty|Chola Dynasty]] traded with China and Southeast Asia; and the [[culture/malacca-sultanate|Malacca Sultanate]] became the leading port of Southeast Asia in the 15th century, before the Portuguese took it in 1511 ([[event/portuguese-sea-route-to-india|Portuguese Sea Route to India]]).
- Trans-Saharan trade in gold, salt, and ivory sustained the [[culture/ghana-empire|Ghana Empire]], the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], and the [[culture/songhai-empire|Songhai Empire]] in turn. [[person/mansa-musa|Mansa Musa]]'s pilgrimage in 1324 reportedly disrupted the gold market in Cairo, and [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]] became a real hub of trade and scholarship. Farther south, [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]] traded gold and ivory with the Swahili coast, and the [[culture/kingdom-of-mapungubwe|Kingdom of Mapungubwe]] received glass beads and Chinese celadon through East African harbors.
- East Asia stood as a commercial center in its own right. The [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]]'s capital Chang'an held about a million people; the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]] issued some of the world's first government paper money; American silver, arriving through the Manila trade, became a major part of the [[culture/ming-dynasty|Ming Dynasty]] economy; and the [[culture/tokugawa-shogunate|Tokugawa Shogunate]] limited foreign trade to only Dutch and Chinese merchants at Nagasaki.
- Exchange and tribute in the pre-Columbian Americas took forms unlike anything in Eurasia. [[place/caral|Caral]] combined coastal fishing with inland farming, [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]] had wide trade and political links, [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] was a ceremonial and trade center with roads radiating out from it, the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]] collected tribute from conquered city-states, and the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] exacted labor service (mit'a) rather than money taxes and kept its records on [[technology/quipu|Quipu]].
- After 1492 trade turned genuinely global. The [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]] moved plants, animals, people, and diseases between the hemispheres; the [[event/manila-galleon-trade|Manila Galleon Trade]] (1565 to 1815) carried Chinese goods to the Americas and American silver to Asia, much of it from [[place/potosi|Potosi]], where the Spanish organized labor through the mita; and the [[event/dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]], chartered in 1602, stood among the first companies to issue tradable shares, and had powers to wage war and build forts of its own.
- Coerced labor underpinned much of the Atlantic economy. The [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]] forcibly transported about 12.5 million Africans to the Americas, the [[event/sugar-plantation-complex|Sugar Plantation Complex]] drove much of that demand, the [[event/haitian-revolution|Haitian Revolution]] arose in Saint-Domingue, the largest sugar producer in the world in the late 18th century, and the [[event/abolition-of-slavery|Abolition of Slavery]] followed between 1807 and 1888.
- The [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]] began in Britain about 1760 with the shift to machine production driven by coal and steam, and economic output per person began, for the first time in history, to rise steadily. The [[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]], [[technology/railways|Railways]], the [[technology/telegraph-and-submarine-cables|Telegraph and Submarine Cables]], the [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]], and the [[event/suez-canal|Suez Canal]], which cut the voyage between Europe and India by thousands of kilometers, together reshaped trade across the 19th century.
- Modern economies were shaped just as much by empire as by state policy. The [[event/opium-wars|Opium Wars]] opened China to Western commerce on unequal terms, the [[event/scramble-for-africa|Scramble for Africa]] and the [[event/congo-free-state|Congo Free State]] built forced-labor extraction, and after 1950 the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]] raised output and energy use steeply. [[event/chinese-reform-and-opening|Chinese Reform and Opening]] (from December 1978) brought market reforms and decades of rapid growth, the [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]] spread computing and networks, and the [[event/covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 Pandemic]] disrupted supply chains right across the world.
--- extra 2: Through the Eras
- **Paleolithic:** Stone, shell, and pigment moved between hunter-gatherer groups over long distances, but the evidence stays fragmentary, and this hub has no dedicated note yet on Paleolithic exchange; see [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]].
- **Neolithic:** Farming and herding ([[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]], [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]]) created storable surpluses and traction animals, and [[technology/pottery|Pottery]] let food and liquids be stored and carried.

- **Iron Age:** The [[event/incense-trade|Incense Trade]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-saba|Kingdom of Saba]], the [[culture/phoenicians|Phoenicians]], [[place/carthage|Carthage]], the [[culture/scythians|Scythians]], [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]], the [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]], the [[culture/achaemenid-persians|Achaemenid Persians]], and [[technology/iron-smelting|Iron Smelting]].
- **Classical Antiquity:** The [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]], the [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] and [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]], the [[culture/romans|Romans]] and [[person/augustus|Augustus]], the [[culture/nabataeans|Nabataeans]], the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]], the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]], [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]], the [[culture/maurya-empire|Maurya Empire]], and [[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]], which made calculation easier.
- **Medieval Period:** The [[event/indian-ocean-trade-network|Indian Ocean Trade Network]], the [[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]], [[culture/srivijaya|Srivijaya]], the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]], the [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]] and [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]], the [[culture/ghana-empire|Ghana Empire]], [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], and [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]], [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-mapungubwe|Kingdom of Mapungubwe]], [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]], [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]], the [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]], whose relay postal system sped communication across Eurasia, the [[culture/norse|Norse]], who traded in eastern Europe for silver from the Islamic world, the [[culture/malacca-sultanate|Malacca Sultanate]], [[person/zheng-he|Zheng He]], [[person/ibn-battuta|Ibn Battuta]], and the [[event/black-death|Black Death]].
- **Early Modern Period:** The [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]], the [[event/portuguese-sea-route-to-india|Portuguese Sea Route to India]], the [[event/manila-galleon-trade|Manila Galleon Trade]], [[place/potosi|Potosi]], the [[event/dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]], the [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]], the [[event/sugar-plantation-complex|Sugar Plantation Complex]], the [[culture/ming-dynasty|Ming Dynasty]], the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]], which by 1700 ruled about 100 million people and made India one of the world's largest economies, the [[culture/tokugawa-shogunate|Tokugawa Shogunate]], and the [[event/russian-expansion-into-siberia|Russian Expansion into Siberia]], driven by the fur trade.
- **Industrial Age:** The [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]], [[technology/railways|Railways]], the [[technology/telegraph-and-submarine-cables|Telegraph and Submarine Cables]], the [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]], the [[event/suez-canal|Suez Canal]], the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]], the [[event/opium-wars|Opium Wars]], the [[event/british-raj|British Raj]], the [[event/scramble-for-africa|Scramble for Africa]], the [[event/congo-free-state|Congo Free State]], the [[event/abolition-of-slavery|Abolition of Slavery]], and the [[event/bengal-famine-of-1943|Bengal Famine of 1943]], which Amartya Sen analyzed as an entitlement failure: food supply did not fall sharply, but economic pressures kept some groups from buying it.
- **Information Age:** [[event/decolonization|Decolonization]], the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]], the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]], the [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]], [[event/chinese-reform-and-opening|Chinese Reform and Opening]], and the [[event/covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 Pandemic]].
--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** Gold, salt, and ivory sustained the Sahelian empires and Swahili city-states, and the interior states of Mapungubwe and Great Zimbabwe reached the Indian Ocean; from the 16th century the [[culture/kingdom-of-kongo|Kingdom of Kongo]] was drawn into the Atlantic slave trade ([[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]]), and between 1885 and 1908 the [[event/congo-free-state|Congo Free State]] extracted rubber and ivory through forced labor.
- **[[region/central-asian-oases|Central Asian Oases]] and the [[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]]:** The [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] of the oases stood as the leading Silk Road merchants, and steppe powers such as the [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]] and the [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]] taxed, protected, and raided the routes in turn; see [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]].
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]:** Control of the straits made [[culture/srivijaya|Srivijaya]] and the [[culture/malacca-sultanate|Malacca Sultanate]] rich, [[culture/champa|Champa]] traded across the South China Sea, and later the [[event/manila-galleon-trade|Manila Galleon Trade]] and the [[event/dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]] (which founded Batavia in 1619) tied the region tightly to European and American markets.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]]:** [[place/caral|Caral]] linked coast and highland, the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] ran a state economy on labor service and recorded goods on [[technology/quipu|Quipu]], and after the Spanish conquest the silver of [[place/potosi|Potosi]], worked under a forced-labor draft adapted from an Inca system, supplied world trade.
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]]:** [[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]] made a staple crop for the whole of the Americas, [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]] had wide trade links, and the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]] collected tribute from conquered city-states for [[place/tenochtitlan|Tenochtitlan]].
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** The [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] and later the [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]] moved goods and people across thousands of kilometers of open sea, and the [[event/treaty-of-waitangi|Treaty of Waitangi]] of 1840 was followed by conflict over land and authority.
- **[[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]]:** European demand for furs drove the [[event/russian-expansion-into-siberia|Russian Expansion into Siberia]] between 1582 and 1639, and the [[culture/yakut-sakha|Yakut (Sakha)]] paid yasak, a fur tribute, until 1917; the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] later linked the region to wider markets. See [[observer-note/frontiers-of-extraction|Frontiers of Extraction]].
- **[[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** The Tang and Song economies, the Ming reliance on American silver, Tokugawa restriction of foreign trade, the Opium Wars, and [[event/chinese-reform-and-opening|Chinese Reform and Opening]] all mark shifts between openness and control.
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- The origins of markets and money stay disputed. Whether exchange began as gift-giving, barter, credit, or tribute is argued over, and the earliest written records, such as the tablets of Uruk, show accounting for obligations rather than any simple barter.
- The size and reach of early exchange can only be inferred from scattered goods, and estimates keep changing. The extent and end of the [[culture/ghana-empire|Ghana Empire]] stay debated, and older population estimates for [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]] of 10,000 to 20,000 have since been revised down to no more than about 10,000.
- What even counts as trade is contested, because exchange, tribute, and coercion overlap so closely. The mita at [[place/potosi|Potosi]], yasak in Siberia, forced rubber collection in the [[event/congo-free-state|Congo Free State]], and the [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]] are all extraction, and scholars disagree about how much slave-based commerce actually contributed to industrialization.
- Why Britain and Europe industrialized first, and why sustained growth began exactly when it did, stays debated, and the [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]] note does not settle the causes either.
- This hub's coverage stays uneven. It has one note on a company ([[event/dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]]) and none yet on coinage, paper money, credit, or double-entry bookkeeping as institutions in their own right, and it has few notes on the internal economies of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas.
