--- description
Leonardo da Vinci (15 April 1452 to 2 May 1519) was an Italian painter, engineer and anatomist. He left few finished paintings, counts differing but fewer than 25 attributed major works, some of them unfinished, the Mona Lisa and The Last Supper among them, and thousands of notebook pages of studies, a sea of ink with few harbours.

He was born on 15 April 1452 near Vinci in Tuscany, the illegitimate son of a notary, Ser Piero, and a young woman named Caterina. In Florence he was apprenticed to Andrea del Verrocchio.

About 1482 he moved to Milan. In a letter to Duke Ludovico Sforza he set his skills as a military engineer before his art. In Milan he painted The Last Supper.

After the French invaded Milan in 1499 he moved between Mantua, Venice and Florence, served briefly as a military engineer for Cesare Borgia, and in Florence began the Mona Lisa.

In 1516 King Francis I invited him to France and gave him the manor of Clos Lucé near Amboise. He died there on 2 May 1519. His pupil Francesco Melzi inherited his paintings, tools, library and personal effects, and by tradition his notebooks.

His notebooks hold thousands of pages of drawings and mirror-writing, studies of anatomy, flight, water and light: hydraulics and optics. He dissected corpses to understand muscles and bones.

=== people--behzad--1
src: 54fc00df43e0
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Kamāl ud-Dīn Behzād (also spelled Bihzad, born about 1455 to 1460 and died about 1535) is the most celebrated painter in the Persian tradition. He led the royal manuscript workshops at Herat and later Tabriz, and many works are attributed to him on grounds of style, so learn to read the style.

He was probably born about 1455 to 1460 in Herat, capital of the Timurid dynasty founded by Timur. Orphaned young, he was brought up by the painter Mirak Naqqash, head of the royal library, and was protected by the poet and minister Mir Ali-Shir Nava'i. Talent, here, needed guardians.

Under Sultan Husayn Bayqara, who ruled Herat from 1469 to 1506, he became the leading artist of the royal kitabkhana, the library workshop. His most famous surviving works date from this period, including paintings in a Bustan of Sa'di (1488) and a Khamsa of Nizami (1494 to 1495).

After Herat fell to the Uzbeks in 1506 and then to the Safavid Shah Ismail I in about 1510, he moved to Tabriz, where in 1522 Ismail appointed him director of the royal library. He died about 1535.

A legend says that during the Battle of Chaldiran in 1514 the shah hid Behzād in a cave, in some tellings together with a famous calligrapher, because he valued him more than his army.

His figures have individual faces and gestures and are often busy with everyday tasks. He often hid his signature discreetly in the architecture of a painting. That is the practice worth keeping: sign your work where only an attentive eye will find it.

=== people--gitarja--1
src: 88d9e258e353
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Tribhuwana Wijayatunggadewi, known as Gitarja, was the third monarch of the Majapahit empire, reigning in Java from about 1328 to 1350. Her birth is placed before 1309 and her death about 1372 to 1375, though both dates are uncertain, like shores seen through sea-haze.

She became queen after the assassination of her half-brother Jayanegara in 1328 (the Nagarakretagama gives 1329 for her accession), ruling in the name of her mother, Gayatri Rajapatni, who had formally withdrawn to a Buddhist convent.

Her chief minister, Gajah Mada, is credited with a famous oath to forgo certain pleasures until he had brought the surrounding archipelago under Majapahit's authority.

In her reign Majapahit forces conquered Bali in 1343, defeating the kingdoms of Pejeng and Bedahulu, a great step in the empire's spread across the Indonesian archipelago.

She stepped down in 1350 after her mother's death, and her son, Hayam Wuruk, followed her; under him Majapahit reached its greatest territorial extent.
