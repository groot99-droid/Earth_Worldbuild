=== people--j-m-w-turner--1
src: 2743811f6d63
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Joseph Mallord William Turner (April 1775 to 19 December 1851) was the greatest British landscape painter and a bold colourist. He began as a precise topographical watercolourist, and his late paintings dissolve ships, trains and cities into light, mist and weather, as if the world were melting back into the sky.

He was born in Covent Garden, London, son of a barber and wigmaker. At 14 he entered the Royal Academy Schools, and at 15 he exhibited his first watercolour. In 1802 he was elected a full Royal Academician, at 26 or 27 depending on the source.

He travelled without rest through Britain, the Alps, France, Germany and Italy, above all Venice, filling hundreds of sketchbooks.

He never married, but he had two daughters with Sarah Danby. John Ruskin championed him in Modern Painters (1843). He died in Chelsea and was buried in St Paul's Cathedral.

By the 1830s he was painting the natural forces themselves: storms, fire, sunlight and steam. He laid thin washes over white grounds, scraped and rubbed his canvases, and built paint up thickly with knives.

He painted steamships and railways alongside Venetian lagoons. Rain, Steam and Speed (1844) is one of the first great paintings of modern technology.

=== people--simon-bolivar--1
src: 7bb9c1a1ad95
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Simón Bolívar (1783 to 1830) was a Venezuelan military and political leader who led the independence of several South American countries from Spain and tried to unite them as Gran Colombia. Winning independence and holding a union together turned out to be different tasks.

He was born in Caracas to a wealthy family.

He and Sucre led the campaigns that freed present-day Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru and Bolivia.

He met José de San Martín at Guayaquil in 1822.

Gran Colombia split apart within seven years, and Bolívar died in 1830.

=== people--shaka--1
src: 64e3e1ffdd3a
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Shaka kaSenzangakhona (about 1787 to 1828) was the founder of the Zulu Kingdom, who remade its military and political order.

He came to power around 1816 and widened the Zulu state swiftly, like a grass fire before the wind.

He is credited with new weapons and tactics, among them the short stabbing spear and the 'buffalo horns' formation.

In 1828 he was assassinated by his half-brothers.

Much of what is told of him comes from colonial-era European accounts and oral tradition, and is debated.
