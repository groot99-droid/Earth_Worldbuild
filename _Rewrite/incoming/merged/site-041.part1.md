=== event/devonian-forests-and-the-rise-of-trees
src: 3474fe159fa6
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Devonian forests were the first forests Earth ever wore, rising about 385 million years ago and spreading like a slow green tide through the rest of the period. Trees were not born once but again and again, in several plant lineages that reached the same tall shape by different roads, and their roots and fallen litter remade soils, rivers and the carbon cycle as surely as rain remakes stone.
--- fact 1
At Gilboa in New York lies a fossil forest about 385 million years old, keeper of some of the oldest known fossil trees, among them cladoxylopsids whose trunks stood hollow as flutes.
--- fact 2
Archaeopteris, woody of trunk and fern-like of leaf, rose more than ten metres toward the light and spread its forests across several continents in the Late Devonian.
--- fact 3
The tall form was found again and again: through the Devonian and Carboniferous, trees arose independently in several plant lineages, among the club-mosses, the relatives of horsetails and the ancestors of seed plants.
--- fact 4
Deep roots quickened the chemical weathering of rock and built soils grain by grain, and atmospheric carbon dioxide ebbed over the Devonian, an ebb that may have helped bring the later cooling.
--- fact 5
Some researchers trace a dark thread from the spreading forests to nutrient runoff and to seas losing their oxygen at the time of the [[event/late-devonian-extinction|Late Devonian Extinction]]; that thread is debated.
--- context
In the long saga of the [[event/colonization-of-land|Colonization of Land]], the Devonian forests stand at the heart, and from their shade the story walks on into the [[event/carboniferous-coal-forests|Carboniferous Coal Forests]]. They belong to the [[era/devonian-period|Devonian Period]] and are woven into the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that unrelated plant groups each arrived at the tree. Think of strangers in different cities who, facing the same crowded skyline, all build upward: the problem, here competition for light, draws the architecture, and the lineage only holds the pen. This suggests that some solutions are strongly favoured by the problem itself and are not tied to one lineage. Hold the reading with an open hand, though: it would weaken if the different tree forms proved to share a single evolutionary origin. Sometimes the problem is the architect.
--- question 1
How great was the forests' sway over Devonian carbon dioxide and climate?
--- question 2
How much of the Late Devonian extinction was the forests' doing?

=== event/digital-revolution
src: 42a3a9025c5e
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Digital Revolution is the spread of digital computing and networks from the mid-20th century on, and it includes the internet (from 1969) and the World Wide Web (from 1989 to 1991). Think of it as a new architecture of connection laid over the old world.
--- fact 1
In 1969 the first message was sent on ARPANET, a precursor of the internet; that was the threshold.
--- fact 2
Tim Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web in 1989, and in 1991 it opened to the public.
--- fact 3
From about 2007, smartphones put networked computers in billions of pockets, and the architecture of the network came to rest in your hand.
--- fact 4
More than five billion people now use the internet: that is the size of the communion today.
--- context
For its lineage, see [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]] and [[person/alan-turing|Alan Turing]]. This is the defining process of the [[era/information-age|Information Age]], the grammar in which that age is written.
--- observer
The Observer regards this as memory-outsourcing at scale. Consider what you did today: the messages, the payments, the photographs. Nearly every human utterance, transaction and image can now be stored and searched; the ledger has left the mind for the machine. A record of the species is being written faster than any observer could read. Memory, once kept, now keeps us.
--- question 1
How will we preserve the digital record, and how will we interpret it?

=== event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage
src: f023e1f00765
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The hominin lineage, the line that leads to humans, parted from the line leading to chimpanzees and bonobos between roughly 6 and 8 million years ago in Africa, as one river divides around a stone into two streams.
--- fact 1
The genetic clocks that time the parting run on assumed mutation rates and generation times: one 2006 study set it no earlier than about 6.3 million years ago, while studies that assume longer generation times imply earlier dates.
--- fact 2
Among the early candidates to stand on the hominin side of the divide is [[species/sahelanthropus-tchadensis|Sahelanthropus tchadensis]], from about 7 million years ago.
--- fact 3
Upright walking, bipedalism, is a key early trait, the first stride of the long road; see [[species/australopithecus-afarensis|Australopithecus afarensis]].
--- fact 4
A 2006 genetic study proposed that the parting was gradual, the two lineages still interbreeding for a time like streams braiding before they part, though this model is disputed.
--- context
The divergence unfolds within the [[era/cenozoic-era|Cenozoic Era]], and it raises the biological stage for the [[event/origin-of-homo-sapiens|Origin of Homo sapiens]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the species' closest relatives are two other apes. Consider how many human stories rest on a wall between 'them' and 'us', a whole architecture of difference. The line between the two is thinner than most of those stories suggest. Kinship runs closer than the walls we build around it.
--- question 1
Did Sahelanthropus walk upright, and did it stand on the hominin side of the parting?
