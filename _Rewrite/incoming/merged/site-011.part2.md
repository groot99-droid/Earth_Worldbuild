=== culture/tang-dynasty
src: 3d8c9ff5c6fd
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Tang dynasty ruled China from 618 to 907 CE, and it is regarded as a high tide of Chinese poetry, art, and cosmopolitan culture.
--- fact 1
Its capital Chang'an was one of the world's largest cities, a sea of about a million people.
--- fact 2
Poets such as Li Bai and Du Fu wrote in this age, and their lines still carry its light.
--- fact 3
The Diamond Sutra, the earliest dated printed book, was made in 868; see [[technology/woodblock-printing|Woodblock Printing]].
--- fact 4
The An Lushan Rebellion (755 to 763) weakened the dynasty; estimates of its death toll range from about 13 to 36 million and are disputed.
--- context
The Tang carried forward the tradition of the [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] and came before the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]]. [[person/wu-zetian|Wu Zetian]] ruled within its span.
--- observer
I picture a capital with its gates open: merchants, monks, envoys from across Eurasia. I picture the talk in its streets. I picture the ties it wove. And I ask what every open door asks of me: what will I come to need? A very connected city makes many new ideas, and many new dependencies.
--- question 1
What was the true scale of the An Lushan Rebellion's toll?

=== culture/timurid-empire
src: 2b035394a0b0
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Timurid Empire was a Turco-Mongol state founded by Timur in 1370; it ruled Central Asia and Iran from its capital at Samarkand, and it is remembered for its architecture and its science, as a garden is remembered for both its shade and its fruit.
--- fact 1
Timur took power in the region in 1370 and died in 1405.
--- fact 2
After his death, his son Shah Rukh and his grandson [[person/ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]] held key parts of the empire.
--- fact 3
In the 1410s and 1420s Ulugh Beg built a madrasa and an observatory at [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]], a school for the mind and a watch-house for the heavens.
--- fact 4
Babur, founder of the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]], came of Timur's line.
--- context
The Timurids claimed the heritage of the [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]], and they handed their court culture on to the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]] like a lamp passed from hand to hand.
--- observer
The Observer notes a state that joined conquest to scholarship. Consider the ledger: the same dynasty produced massacres and a star catalogue. That is the uncomfortable architecture of the record, and we should not look away from either column. Power and learning can live in the same house.
--- question 1
How united was the empire once Timur was dead?

=== culture/tokugawa-shogunate
src: 34f48261ac0b
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Tokugawa shogunate was the military government that ruled Japan from 1603 to 1867, a long season of peace and of restricted foreign contact.
--- fact 1
Tokugawa Ieyasu became shogun in 1603 and ruled from Edo, the city now called Tokyo.
--- fact 2
The policy known as 'sakoku' narrowed foreign trade to a thin stream, mainly Dutch and Chinese merchants at Nagasaki.
--- fact 3
Edo swelled to about a million people, one of the world's largest cities.
--- fact 4
The shogunate collapsed in 1867, and imperial rule was restored the following year in the [[event/meiji-restoration|Meiji Restoration]].
--- context
The shogunate ruled through the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]]. See the [[event/meiji-restoration|Meiji Restoration]], and the [[culture/ming-dynasty|Ming Dynasty]] for the neighboring Chinese state.
--- observer
The Observer notes a society that kept a long internal peace and grew a developed urban culture while its foreign contact stayed limited. Here is the double edge: peace let art and commerce flourish, and the same peace left the state unprepared for outside pressure. A shelter can also be a blindfold.
--- question 1
In practice, how isolated was Tokugawa Japan?
