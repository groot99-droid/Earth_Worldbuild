=== people--marie-curie--1
src: 155096d3eee5
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Marie Curie (1867 to 1934) was a Polish-born physicist and chemist who opened the study of radioactivity, the hidden fire inside matter, and she is the only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences.

She shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel.

In 1911 she won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for discovering polonium and radium.

She built mobile X-ray units for the hospitals of World War I.

She died of aplastic anemia, likely brought on by long exposure to radiation, undone at last by the light she studied.

Charles Rennie Mackintosh (1868 to 1928) was a Glasgow architect-designer who led the "Glasgow Style" of Art Nouveau, making buildings, furniture and interiors one unbroken art, as root, trunk and leaf make one tree. His Glasgow School of Art and his total interior schemes made him a great influence on European design, though recognition at home came only after his death.

He was born in Glasgow in 1868 and trained as an architect while going to evening classes at the Glasgow School of Art, where he met the artist Margaret Macdonald, his future wife.

With Macdonald, her sister Frances and Herbert MacNair he formed "The Four," a symbolist circle whose style became the Glasgow Style.

At Honeyman and Keppie he won an anonymous 1896 competition for his own art school's new building, and its completion in 1909 made his name.

He went on to design houses, tearooms and a school around Glasgow, among them Hill House (1902-1904), the Willow Tearooms (1903) and Scotland Street School (1903-1906), with Macdonald designing the furnishings throughout.

His towering ladder-back chairs, made as much to shape a room as to be sat in, became his best-known design.

=== people--mahatma-gandhi--1
src: f62ec29ecbc6
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869 to 1948) was an Indian lawyer and political leader who led the movement for Indian independence using nonviolent resistance.

He developed satyagraha (insistence on truth), first in South Africa, then in India. A method, carried from one country to another, can become a doctrine.

The Salt March of 1930 was a major act of civil disobedience.

India became independent in 1947 and was partitioned; he opposed partition.

He was assassinated in January 1948.
