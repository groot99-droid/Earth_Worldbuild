=== event/fish-to-tetrapod-transition
src: 28e117ac4d22
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Fish-to-Tetrapod Transition was the slow reshaping of fin into limb — four-limbed creatures rising from lobe-finned fishes across the Middle and Late Devonian, roughly 385 to 360 million years ago. The unfolding sequence is well documented, though where it truly begins is debated.
--- fact 1
Tetrapods descend from the lobe-finned fishes, a lineage whose fins carry within them a central skeleton of bone.
--- fact 2
Among the fossils that bridge the gap stand Panderichthys from Latvia, about 380 million years old, [[species/tiktaalik|Tiktaalik]] from the Canadian Arctic, about 375 million years old, and Acanthostega and Ichthyostega from Greenland, about 365 million years old. These last two bore limbs with digits, yet lived still mostly in the water.
--- fact 3
Limbs probably first evolved for moving through shallow water thick with plants, and only later found use upon the land; this reading remains a hypothesis.
--- fact 4
Trackways at Zachełmie in Poland, dated to about 395 million years ago, have been read as the footprints of tetrapods — which would set limbed creatures earlier than any known body fossil, though the interpretation is debated.
--- context
The Fish-to-Tetrapod Transition falls within the [[era/devonian-period|Devonian Period]] and forms one thread of the [[event/colonization-of-land|Colonization of Land]]. It comes before the [[event/late-devonian-extinction|Late Devonian Extinction]]. It belongs to the theme threads of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and [[theme/science|Science]].
--- observer
Here is a lesson worth carrying with you: a feature's later use need not match its origin. Limbs seem to have arisen for moving through water and only afterward found their walk on land. Notice, too, how some of these fossils were found — researchers predicted the rock age and setting where transitional animals should occur, and then went and looked, an unusually testable case within the whole record. The reading would weaken if the earliest limbs proved to have served mainly for walking on land after all.
--- question 1
How old is the origin of limbed tetrapods, given the Zachełmie trackways?
--- question 2
Which living fish groups, if any, preserve traits of the first limbed animals?

=== event/formation-of-the-oceans
src: 5efb9a8d4d0e
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Formation of the Oceans marks the time, probably between about 4.4 and 4.0 billion years ago, when liquid water first gathered on the face of the cooling young Earth. The date itself is uncertain, resting only on evidence read at a distance.
--- fact 1
The oxygen-isotope ratios held in some Jack Hills zircons, dated to about 4.4 billion years ago, have been read as a sign that their parent magma once touched liquid water; that reading is contested. See [[place/jack-hills-zircons|Jack Hills Zircons]].
--- fact 2
Earth's water came partly from the very material that built the planet, and partly from later impacts of water-rich bodies out of the dark. Asteroids of the carbonaceous chondrite type match the hydrogen isotopes of ocean water closely, but how much each source contributed is debated.
--- fact 3
The young Sun gave only about 70 percent of the light it gives now, so liquid water at the surface needed the shelter of a strong greenhouse, built from gases such as carbon dioxide.
--- fact 4
The oldest rocks that plainly formed in water — sediments and pillow-shaped lavas at Isua in Greenland — are about 3.7 to 3.8 billion years old, so the oceans already existed by then.
--- fact 5
How deep the early oceans ran, and how much land stood above them, are both debated.
--- context
The Formation of the Oceans falls within the [[era/hadean-eon|Hadean Eon]], after the [[event/theia-impact-and-the-formation-of-the-moon|Theia Impact and the Formation of the Moon]] and around the time of the [[event/late-heavy-bombardment|Late Heavy Bombardment]]. The oceans became the setting for the [[event/origin-of-life|Origin of Life]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
File the oceans under preconditions: everything that comes later in this record assumes liquid water was already there. That the planet had it at all depended on its size, its distance from the Sun, and the contingent delivery of water from elsewhere — the Observer treats this as favourable circumstance, not design. The reading would weaken if water turned out to be a near-inevitable product of planet formation at this distance from a star.
--- question 1
How much of Earth's water was present at formation and how much arrived later?
--- question 2
When did stable, long-lived oceans first exist?

=== event/formation-of-the-solar-system-and-earth
src: 5f823dffe55b
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Solar System was born about 4.57 billion years ago, out of a cloud of gas and dust collapsing upon itself, and Earth gathered from that same material over tens of millions of years.
--- fact 1
The Sun formed at the heart of the collapsing cloud, while a disk of material spinning around it clumped, piece by piece, into planets.
--- fact 2
The oldest solids yet dated in the Solar System are calcium-aluminum-rich inclusions found within meteorites, about 4.567 billion years old.
--- fact 3
Earth's age of about 4.54 billion years is read from the radiometric dating of meteorites and of the planet's own oldest lead.
--- fact 4
Earth's early interior grew hot and separated, settling into a metal core wrapped in a mantle of stone.
--- context
Earth's formation opens the [[era/hadean-eon|Hadean Eon]]. The Moon-forming impact, the [[event/theia-impact-and-the-formation-of-the-moon|Theia Impact and the Formation of the Moon]], followed not long after.
--- observer
Consider this: every atom in you heavier than helium was forged inside earlier stars, long before the Sun existed. The 'birth' of Earth, seen this way, is a rearrangement of old material, not a beginning from nothing.
--- question 1
How quickly did Earth's core form?
