--- summary
Science is the disciplined study of the natural world through observation, measurement, mathematics, and testing, together with the habits and institutions that let people check and extend one another's claims. Its practices run from the counting, calendar-keeping, and sky-watching of prehistoric peoples, through the mathematics, astronomy, and medicine of the early literate states and the scholarly centers of the medieval world, on to the experimental sciences of Europe from the sixteenth century and the genomics, computing, and Earth-system research of the twenty-first. Traditions of careful observation arose independently in many regions, and modern science grew out of several of them, not from just one. This hub gathers the vault's notes on scientists, institutions, instruments, and discoveries, and on the events that changed how knowledge of nature was made and passed on.
--- observer
The Observer names this pattern-taming: the species' long effort to find regularities in nature, record them, and use them to predict. Counting, calendars, and careful observation appear in many places long before the modern period, so what looks genuinely distinctive about the modern form is its institutionalized doubt, in which strangers agree to check one another's claims and publish what they find. That habit rests on memory-outsourcing (writing, paper, printing) and on exchange-webs that carried instruments, texts, and people, and it works directly against ancestor-weight, since the older authority is often the very first claim put to the test. This reading would weaken if institutionalized doubt turns out to have been just as old and as widespread as counting itself, or if it turns out to depend on something other than writing and travel.
--- question 1
How did the culture of open criticism first emerge and spread, and where did it already exist in institutions before the seventeenth century?
--- question 2
How much of the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] actually rested on knowledge that moved from the Islamic world, India, and China, and how can that debt even be measured?
--- question 3
What can actually be recovered of scientific traditions carried only by oral teaching, songlines, and cord records?
--- question 4
Why did centers of learning such as the [[place/library-of-alexandria|Library of Alexandria]], Baghdad, and [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] decline at all, and what was lost when they did?
--- question 5
Why do major discoveries so often arise independently, in more than one mind or place at once, as with the calculus and natural selection?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Counting, calendars, and sky-watching stand among the oldest organized knowledge of all. The axis of [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] aligns with the solstice sunrise and sunset, buildings and rock carvings at [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] align with the sun and moon, and the [[culture/maya|Maya]] kept remarkably precise calendars.
- Writing turned raw observation into records that others could check for themselves. [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]] began in [[region/mesopotamia|Mesopotamia]] as accounting, and Mesopotamian scribes, from the time of the [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]], worked in a base-60 number system that still survives in the division of the hour and the circle.
- The [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]] left behind medical papyri, such as the Edwin Smith papyrus of about 1600 BCE, that set out the examination and treatment of injuries case by case; see [[region/nile-valley|Nile Valley]].
- The [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] brought entirely new traditions of reasoned inquiry in Greece, India, and China; see [[person/confucius|Confucius]] and the [[culture/ancient-greeks|Ancient Greeks]].
- Greek natural philosophy went looking for general causes. [[person/aristotle|Aristotle]] studied physics and biology, including careful observation of marine life; the [[place/library-of-alexandria|Library of Alexandria]] supported scholars such as Eratosthenes, who estimated the circumference of the Earth; and [[person/hypatia|Hypatia]] taught mathematics and astronomy in Alexandria. See also [[place/athens|Athens]].
- The decimal place-value system with zero developed in India during the first millennium CE and then spread west; see [[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]] and the [[culture/gupta-empire|Gupta Empire]]. The age of the earliest zero, in the Bakhshali manuscript, stays contested.
- Under the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]], the [[event/translation-movement-and-the-house-of-wisdom|Translation Movement and the House of Wisdom]] rendered Greek, Persian, Syriac, and Indian works into Arabic and then extended them further. The arithmetic and algebra of [[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]] gave later languages the very words algorithm and algebra, and [[person/ibn-sina|Ibn Sina]]'s Canon of Medicine was used in European universities right into the 17th century. [[culture/al-andalus|Al-Andalus]] became another center of learning in its own right.
- Observatories, libraries, and manuscript collections all grew up along the Silk Roads and the Saharan routes. [[person/ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]]'s observatory at [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] produced a star catalogue of at least 1,018 stars, and [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]] still holds hundreds of thousands of historical manuscripts.
- Pacific navigators crossed thousands of kilometers by reading star paths, swells, and bird flight, without instruments of any kind; see [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]] and [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]].
- Instruments then extended what could be measured and shared: the [[technology/magnetic-compass|Magnetic Compass]] in China by about the 12th century, the [[technology/mechanical-clock|Mechanical Clock]] (Su Song's astronomical clock tower of 1088; weight-driven clocks in European towns about 1300), and the [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]] of about 1440.
- The [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] (roughly 1543 to 1687) put experiment, mathematics, and observation at the very center; see [[person/galileo-galilei|Galileo Galilei]] and [[person/isaac-newton|Isaac Newton]]. It drew, in turn, on earlier work in the Islamic world, India, and China.
- The [[event/enlightenment|Enlightenment]] carried this preference for evidence straight into public life. In 1796 Edward Jenner showed that cowpox inoculation protected against smallpox, the start of [[technology/vaccination|Vaccination]]; smallpox was declared eradicated in 1980.
- The 19th and early 20th centuries brought evolution by natural selection ([[person/charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]]), radioactivity ([[person/marie-curie|Marie Curie]]), relativity ([[person/albert-einstein|Albert Einstein]]), and what is often regarded as the very first published computer algorithm ([[person/ada-lovelace|Ada Lovelace]]); the [[event/meiji-restoration|Meiji Restoration]] and the [[event/tanzimat-reforms|Tanzimat Reforms]] stood as state programs built to acquire the new sciences and their institutions.
- Late 20th-century science gave the world computation ([[person/alan-turing|Alan Turing]], [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]]), the structure of DNA ([[person/rosalind-franklin|Rosalind Franklin]], [[event/genomic-revolution|Genomic Revolution]]), and spaceflight ([[event/space-age|Space Age]], [[event/moon-landing|Moon Landing]]); [[person/rachel-carson|Rachel Carson]], [[event/modern-climate-change|Modern Climate Change]], the [[place/jack-hills-zircons|Jack Hills Zircons]], and the [[event/tunguska-event|Tunguska Event]] together show Earth-system science at work.
--- extra 2: Through the Eras
- **Paleolithic:** Working knowledge of stone, fire, animals, and plants steadily accumulates ([[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]], [[event/control-of-fire|Control of Fire]], [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]]). [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] managed the landscape with fire and passed on ecological knowledge through oral traditions and songlines. Notched bones from this era may be tallies, though their true purpose stays debated.
- **Neolithic:** Farmers select seed and stock across generations ([[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]], [[event/domestication-of-rice|Domestication of Rice]], [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]]), and builders align their monuments with the sun ([[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]]).
- **Bronze Age:** Writing lets scribes keep records, tables, and observations ([[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]], [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]], [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]]); Mesopotamian arithmetic runs on base 60, and Egyptian medical texts describe cases and their treatments.
- **Iron Age:** Reasoned inquiry into nature and human affairs appears in the [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] in Greece, India, and China ([[person/confucius|Confucius]], [[culture/ancient-greeks|Ancient Greeks]]); Polynesian voyagers steadily build up knowledge of stars and swells ([[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]]).
- **Classical Antiquity:** [[place/athens|Athens]] and [[person/aristotle|Aristotle]], the [[place/library-of-alexandria|Library of Alexandria]] and [[person/hypatia|Hypatia]], and in India [[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]] under the [[culture/gupta-empire|Gupta Empire]]. The [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] kept careful astronomical records, and its scholar Zhang Heng built a seismoscope in 132 CE. The [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]] moved ideas right alongside goods.
- **Medieval Period:** The [[event/translation-movement-and-the-house-of-wisdom|Translation Movement and the House of Wisdom]], [[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]], [[person/ibn-sina|Ibn Sina]], and [[culture/al-andalus|Al-Andalus]]; the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]]'s [[technology/magnetic-compass|Magnetic Compass]] and [[technology/gunpowder|Gunpowder]]; the [[culture/maya|Maya]] calendar and astronomy; [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]]; [[person/ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]] and the [[culture/timurid-empire|Timurid Empire]]; [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]]; [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]]'s theory of how states rise and fall; the [[technology/mechanical-clock|Mechanical Clock]]; the [[event/renaissance|Renaissance]].
- **Early Modern Period:** The [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] ([[person/galileo-galilei|Galileo Galilei]], [[person/isaac-newton|Isaac Newton]]), the [[event/enlightenment|Enlightenment]], and [[technology/vaccination|Vaccination]] (1796).
- **Industrial Age:** The [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]] created a whole new demand for measurement and engineering. [[person/charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]], [[person/ada-lovelace|Ada Lovelace]], [[person/marie-curie|Marie Curie]], [[person/albert-einstein|Albert Einstein]], [[technology/antibiotics|Antibiotics]], [[technology/nuclear-fission|Nuclear Fission]], and the [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]] all belong here, as do state modernization programs such as the [[event/meiji-restoration|Meiji Restoration]] and [[event/tanzimat-reforms|Tanzimat Reforms]].
- **Information Age:** [[person/alan-turing|Alan Turing]] and the [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]], [[person/rosalind-franklin|Rosalind Franklin]] and the [[event/genomic-revolution|Genomic Revolution]], the [[event/space-age|Space Age]] and [[event/moon-landing|Moon Landing]], [[person/rachel-carson|Rachel Carson]] and [[event/modern-climate-change|Modern Climate Change]], the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]], and the [[event/covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19 Pandemic]], in which mRNA vaccines were developed and authorized within about a year.
--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/mesopotamia|Mesopotamia]] and the [[region/nile-valley|Nile Valley]]:** The earliest written mathematics, astronomy, and medicine run back to temple and palace administration; see [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]] and [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]].
- **[[region/south-asia|South Asia]] and the Islamic world:** Place-value notation with zero arose in India and was carried west by scholars of the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]] and [[culture/al-andalus|Al-Andalus]]; Latin translations of Arabic texts fed European scholarship in the 12th century.
- **[[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** Paper, printing, the compass, and gunpowder were all documented first in China, and the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]] stood as a center of technical and commercial innovation.
- **Central Asia:** The oases were a crossroads for learning. [[person/ibn-sina|Ibn Sina]] was born near Bukhara, [[person/ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]] built his observatory at [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]], and the [[culture/timurid-empire|Timurid Empire]] patronized it; see [[region/central-asian-oases|Central Asian Oases]].
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** Polynesian voyagers navigated without instruments ([[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]]), the [[place/kuk-early-agricultural-site|Kuk Early Agricultural Site]] preserves at least 7,000 years of wetland gardening, and the [[place/jack-hills-zircons|Jack Hills Zircons]] in Western Australia hold the oldest known pieces of Earth's crust, about 4.4 billion years old.
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]] and the [[region/andes|Andes]]:** The [[culture/maya|Maya]] kept precise calendars and used the concept of zero, and the Andean [[technology/quipu|Quipu]] recorded numbers in a decimal system on knotted cords.
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** Scholarship in [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]] and much of the fossil evidence for human origins ([[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]], [[event/origin-of-homo-sapiens|Origin of Homo sapiens]]) come from this region.
- **[[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]]:** The [[event/tunguska-event|Tunguska Event]] of 1908 was first studied on site by an expedition in 1927, and Arctic peoples such as the Inuit built up detailed knowledge of ice, weather, and animal behavior for survival ([[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]]).
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- Whether star-lore, navigation, and Indigenous ecological knowledge deserve the name science, or only the experimental and mathematical tradition of recent centuries does, stays disputed. This hub uses a broad definition and treats the narrower one as a later development.
- Historians debate whether the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] was really a single revolution at all, and how much it actually owed to earlier work in the Islamic world, India, and China. The exact nature of the House of Wisdom is debated too, and the [[place/library-of-alexandria|Library of Alexandria]] declined over centuries rather than in any one fire.
- Credit stays contested in many cases: the earliest zero ([[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]]), the calculus, which [[person/isaac-newton|Isaac Newton]] developed independently of Leibniz, natural selection, which [[person/charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]] and Alfred Russel Wallace reached quite separately, the use of [[person/rosalind-franklin|Rosalind Franklin]]'s X-ray data in the double-helix model, and Lise Meitner's exclusion from the 1944 Nobel Prize for [[technology/nuclear-fission|Nuclear Fission]].
- Science carried only by oral teaching, songlines, and cord records left behind few written texts, so it is all too easy to undercount.
- This hub's science notes lean toward Europe, the Islamic world, and named individuals. It has few notes on scientists in Africa south of the Sahara, Oceania, the Andes, and Mesoamerica, and none yet on Indian astronomy or Chinese mathematics as topics in their own right.
