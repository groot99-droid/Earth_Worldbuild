=== people--truganini--1
src: 55c76223936b
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Truganini (about 1812 to 1876) was a Tasmanian Aboriginal woman of the Nuenonne people who grew up on Bruny Island, lived through the British colonization of Tasmania, and died in Hobart. She was later widely called "the last Tasmanian Aboriginal," a label that Tasmanian Aboriginal people reject. Start there, because the label erases the living.

She was born about 1812, reportedly at Recherche Bay in southern Tasmania, as a member of the Nuenonne people, and she grew up on Bruny Island.

Accounts say members of her family were killed or abducted by colonists during the Black War of the 1820s and early 1830s.

In the 1830s she worked with George Augustus Robinson, a government-appointed conciliator who persuaded surviving Aboriginal people to move to Flinders Island, where disease and hardship were severe.

She later lived at Oyster Cove, south of Hobart, and died on 8 May 1876.

Her skeleton was exhumed in 1878 and displayed in the Tasmanian Museum from 1904 until 1947, despite her reported fear of mutilation. Dignity owed in life is owed after it too.

=== people--harriet-tubman--1
src: fc14b2b5f36d
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Harriet Tubman (about 1822 to 1913) was an American abolitionist who escaped slavery in Maryland in 1849 and then guided others to freedom on the Underground Railroad. She later served the Union Army in the Civil War. Her life teaches that freedom, once reached, can be turned into a road for others.

She was born Araminta Ross into slavery in Dorchester County, Maryland. Her birth year is uncertain, with sources giving dates from 1815 to 1825, and about 1822 the most favoured.

As a teenager she suffered a severe head injury when an overseer threw a metal weight, and she had seizures and headaches for the rest of her life.

She escaped to Pennsylvania in 1849 and afterward made about 13 trips back to Maryland, guiding roughly 70 people to freedom, according to her biographers. Every return was a choice to cross the same threshold again.

During the Civil War she served the Union Army as a cook, nurse, scout and spy. In June 1863 she helped guide the Combahee River Raid in South Carolina, which freed more than 700 enslaved people, though some sources give more than 750.

After the war she lived in Auburn, New York, where she cared for her family and for elderly people, and she worked for women's suffrage.
