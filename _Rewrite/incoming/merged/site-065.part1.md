--- summary
This entry underwrites her reign dates and royal titles: the war of 25 to 22 BCE, the raid on Syene, the sack of Napata, the Samos embassy that ended further tribute, and the 1910 discovery of the Meroe Head.

=== source/wikipedia-on-amina-of-zazzau
title: Wikipedia on Amina of Zazzau
src: f642b286af1f
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
This source backs the 1576-1610 reign dating, the tradition of queen versus princess, her absence from the Chronicle of Abuja's regnal lists, the Kano Chronicle's synchronism with Muhammad Dauda, and the attributed conquests, trade routes and earthen walls.
--- extra 1: Caveats
A general encyclopedia article internally inconsistent on the date of Infaq al-Maysur (1812 versus about 1836), and it gives an infobox birth of 1533 and death of 1610 that the text itself treats as disputed.

--- summary
This source supports Khusrau's 1253 birth at Patiyali, a Turkic father and Indian mother, service under more than seven Delhi Sultanate rulers, an October 1325 death six months after Nizamuddin Auliya, and the contested sitar and tabla attributions.

=== source/wikipedia-on-anacaona
title: Wikipedia on Anacaona
src: 1a6340273218
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
This source backs Anacaona's birth c. 1474, her kinship to Bohechío, her 1500 accession, her areíto compositions, and the 1503 massacre at Jaragua, then her imprisonment and hanging c. 1504.
--- extra 1: Caveats
A general encyclopedia article that dates her death c. 1504 and Caonabo's capture to 1496, both of which vary in other sources, so these dates should be treated with caution.
