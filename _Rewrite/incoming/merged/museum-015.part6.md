=== people--lumiere-brothers--1
src: 5435dedab68c
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Auguste Lumière (1862 to 1954) and Louis Lumière (1864 to 1948) were French makers of photographic plates who patented the Cinématographe in 1895 and staged the first paying public screening of projected motion pictures, light thrown across a dark room and caught on a wall. The dates here run from Auguste's birth to his death.

The brothers were born in Besançon and grew up in Lyon, where their father Antoine kept a portrait studio and later a factory making dry photographic plates.

After their father saw a demonstration of Edison's single-viewer Kinetoscope in 1894, they set out to build a device that could cast moving images before a seated audience. Louis is generally credited with the key mechanical insight, borrowing the sewing machine's intermittent claw movement to advance the film.

In February 1895 they patented the Cinématographe, one device that could shoot, print and project. Their first paying public screening was on 28 December 1895 in the Salon Indien of the Grand Café in Paris, traditionally described as a basement on the Boulevard des Capucines: ten short films for roughly thirty-five to forty people, though accounts differ.

Their "actuality" films were usually a single still, unedited shot of under a minute. L'Arroseur arrosé is a rare staged gag, in effect cinema's first fiction film. Key works include Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory (1895) and The Arrival of a Train at La Ciotat (1896).

They sent camera operators across the world, and within a year or two projected cinema had reached India, Japan, Australia and the Americas.

Edvard Munch (12 December 1863 to 23 January 1944). Norwegian painter. Printmaker. Illness, grief, love, fear: he turned them into images of inner states. The Scream became an emblem of the modern psyche. He is a founding figure of Expressionism.

Born in Løten, Norway. Grew up in Kristiania (now Oslo). His mother died of tuberculosis when he was five. His sister Sophie, of the same disease, when he was 13. Another sister was later hospitalised for mental illness.

The 1890s: mostly Berlin and Paris. The Frieze of Life cycle. His 1892 exhibition in Berlin was closed after a week, amid scandal. It made him famous in Germany.

1908: a breakdown. Treatment in Copenhagen. From 1916, his estate, Ekely, outside Oslo. In 1937 the Nazis labelled his work "degenerate" and removed it from German museums.

Simplified forms. Flowing outlines. Flat, expressive colour. Influenced by Van Gogh, Gauguin and Symbolism. His woodcuts and lithographs as important as his paintings.

The Sick Child (1885 to 1886). The Scream (1893). Madonna (1894). The Dance of Life (1899 to 1900). The losses of childhood, painted again and again.

=== people--edvard-munch--2
src: 1e8a6100ada4
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Edvard Munch (12 December 1863 to 23 January 1944) was a Norwegian painter and printmaker who turned illness, grief, love and fear into images of inner states. The Scream became an emblem of the modern psyche, and he is a founding figure of Expressionism. His work teaches that what wounds you can become what you have to say.

He was born in Løten, Norway, and grew up in Kristiania (now Oslo). His mother died of tuberculosis when he was five and his sister Sophie of the same disease when he was 13. Another sister was later hospitalised for mental illness.

In the 1890s he lived mostly in Berlin and Paris and developed the Frieze of Life cycle. His 1892 exhibition in Berlin was closed after a week amid scandal, which made him famous in Germany. A scandal can be a door.

He suffered a breakdown in 1908 and was treated in Copenhagen. From 1916 he lived at his estate, Ekely, outside Oslo. In 1937 the Nazis labelled his work "degenerate" and removed it from German museums.

He simplified forms into flowing outlines and flat expressive colour, influenced by Van Gogh, Gauguin and Symbolism. His woodcuts and lithographs are as important as his paintings.

Key works include The Sick Child (1885 to 1886), The Scream (1893), Madonna (1894) and The Dance of Life (1899 to 1900).
