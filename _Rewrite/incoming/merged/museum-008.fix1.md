I stand in the room of Rumi (Jalal ad-Din Muhammad Rumi, 1207 to 1273), the Persian-language poet and Sufi teacher who spent his adult life in Konya, in Anatolia, whose Masnavi and lyric poems are among the most widely read works of Persian literature.

I read that he was born in 1207 in Balkh or nearby Wakhsh, in present-day Afghanistan or Tajikistan, and that the sources differ. I read that his family moved west, and that by about 1228 he lived in Konya, in the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum. I read that he trained as a scholar and preacher like his father, Baha ad-Din Walad, and taught there.

In 1244 he met a wandering mystic, Shams of Tabriz. Their friendship changed his life, and he began to write poetry. Shams disappeared about 1248; accounts differ, some saying he was killed, others that he simply moved on.

He wrote the Masnavi, a long poem in six books, and thousands of lyric poems collected in the Divan of Shams, mostly in Persian. The Masnavi runs to some 25,000 to 27,000 couplets, counts differing by source, composed over his last decade or so at the request of his disciple Husam al-Din Chalabi, who wrote the verses down as Rumi recited them. Some later readers called it "the Quran in Persian".

Who has not been changed by one meeting? I have, and I did not write a single couplet.
