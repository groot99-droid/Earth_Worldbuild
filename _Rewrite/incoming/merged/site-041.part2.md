=== event/domestication-of-maize
src: a9f2274ae8e4
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Maize, or corn, was coaxed from a wild grass called teosinte in southwestern Mexico about 9,000 years ago, and from that small beginning it grew into a staple crop across the Americas, as a spring swells into a river.
--- fact 1
Genetic studies follow maize back to its wild source, the teosinte Zea mays subsp. parviglumis, as one follows a river to its spring.
--- fact 2
Five major differences of form divide maize from teosinte, and regions scattered throughout the genome each lent a hand to the change. Early maize bore tiny cobs; selection over thousands of years swelled them into large ears.
--- fact 3
The Maya and Aztecs held maize sacred, and cooking it with lime, the art called nixtamalization, unlocks the nutrients sealed within the grain.
--- fact 4
Today maize stands among the world's most-produced crops by volume, a humble grass grown into a giant.
--- context
Maize was the bedrock on which [[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]]n states were raised, the [[culture/maya|Maya]] and the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]] among them. Later it went out to the rest of the world through the [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]].
--- observer
The Observer treats this as a second, independent run of the Settling: the same experiment begun again, with a different plant. Think of what that means. A grass with a few seeds was reshaped by human selection into a crop that could support cities. That is the architecture of domestication: patience, applied to a seed, can become a city.
--- question 1
How swiftly did the grain travel north and south from its cradle?

=== event/domestication-of-rice
src: 69552b242299
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Rice (Oryza sativa) was drawn out of wild rice in the Yangtze River region of China beginning around 9,000 to 7,000 years ago, not in a single stroke but over a long process, slow as silt settling in a river's bend.
--- fact 1
The wild ancestor populations of rice are usually named as Oryza rufipogon, the untamed kin of every paddy.
--- fact 2
Early whispers of rice use and cultivation rise from Yangtze valley sites such as Shangshan, where rice phytoliths date to about 9,000 years ago, and after it from Kuahuqiao and Hemudu.
--- fact 3
Domestication traits such as seed heads that no longer shattered came gradually, over thousands of years, like a tide creeping up a shore.
--- fact 4
The japonica subspecies is generally traced to the Yangtze region; the indica subspecies is thought to have been born of later crossing with other rice populations, though that origin is debated.
--- fact 5
Rice is a staple for more than half of the world's population, a river of grain that never runs dry.
--- context
Rice stood among the great founder crops of [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]], a sister to wheat and barley in Southwest Asia and to maize in [[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]]. Its paddies, mirrors of the sky, became central to East Asian societies. The domestication of rice belongs to the [[era/neolithic|Neolithic]] and to the region of [[region/east-asia|East Asia]], and is bound into the theme threads of [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I keep returning to the fields. Farming was invented independently on several continents, with different plants, and each time it gave back denser settlement and stored surplus. I remember the grain was different every time. I remember the ending was not.

How often must a pattern repeat before I stop calling it chance? The sameness across different crops is what makes it hard to dismiss as accident. What is sown differently can still be reaped the same.
