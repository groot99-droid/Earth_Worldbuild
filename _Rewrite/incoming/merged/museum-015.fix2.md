--- description
I stand before José Rizal (1861 to 1896), Filipino physician, writer and reformer, whose novels criticized Spanish colonial rule, whom Spanish authorities executed in Manila on 30 December 1896, regarded as a national hero of the Philippines.

I read that he was born on 19 June 1861 in Calamba, in the Philippines; that he studied in Manila, Madrid, Paris and Heidelberg and became an eye doctor. I read that he wrote the novels Noli Me Tángere (1887) and El Filibusterismo (1891), which portrayed abuses by Spanish friars and officials, and that the colonial authorities banned them. I read that in 1892 he founded La Liga Filipina, a reform society, and was exiled to Dapitan on Mindanao, where he practiced medicine and taught until 1896.

The Philippine Revolution began in August 1896. Rizal, who had volunteered as a doctor in Cuba, was arrested on his way there, brought back to Manila and convicted of rebellion. He had said he opposed an armed uprising at that time. He was shot by firing squad on 30 December 1896, aged 35. Before his death he wrote a poem now known as "Mi último adiós."

An eye doctor, convicted of an uprising he had said he opposed. What would I write, knowing it was the last thing? He wrote a farewell, and it still carries its name.
