=== zodiac
title: Zodiac
src: 6544c0e018fe
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Think of the frame around something you love, and how rarely you look at it. This began as a calendar design for a printing house and was later reissued on its own as a decorative panel: a woman in profile, crowned by a jewelled halo that bears the twelve zodiac signs. Yet it was the ornamental frame, rather than the figure itself, that became the design's most imitated element, reproduced on countless later calendars, biscuit tins and packaging. That is the quiet architecture of influence. The border can travel further than the face.

=== the-seasons
title: The Seasons
src: 29a0aeef206b
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Beauty has a price, and here the price came down. This is a set of four decorative panels personifying Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter as women set against stylised landscapes, sold as affordable prints for middle-class homes rather than as advertising. Notice the threshold being crossed: series like this made Mucha's decorative style available well beyond the theatre-poster market that had launched him. A style that reaches the home has found its widest room.

=== cycles-perfecta
title: Cycles Perfecta
src: 4ef7938a09c0
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A style becomes a grammar when everyone begins to speak it. This is an advertisement for a French bicycle brand, and look at the rider's windswept hair: it has been turned into the same decorative, flowing arabesques Mucha had developed for theatre posters years earlier. The poster shows how thoroughly his ornamental vocabulary had by then become the default look of French commercial advertising. What begins as one voice can end as the common tongue.

=== vanishing-lady
title: The Vanishing Lady
src: a2ae345ad739
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
It was one of Méliès's earliest trick films, made within months of a discovery that came by accident, the way a spring breaks unbidden from stone: stopping and restarting his camera mid-shot could make an object or person vanish and reappear. Here a woman is gone from a chair as a candle flame is gone when pinched; a skeleton sits where she sat; and then she returns. This was the basic "stop trick", the seed from which his illusions grew, and it launched his career as cinema's first great illusionist.

=== a-trip-to-the-moon
title: A Trip to the Moon
src: 5779d62793ec
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This was cinema's first true science-fiction film, loosely adapted from Jules Verne and H. G. Wells. In it, astronomers fire themselves to the Moon by cannon, a bright seed flung into the dark, and there battle its insect-like inhabitants, the Selenites. Its image of the capsule lodged in the Man in the Moon's eye, like a thorn in a pale and patient face, is among the most reproduced in film history. Méliès hand-painted colour prints frame by frame in his own studio, laying colour on each small pane the way dawn lays it on water: one frame, then the next.

=== kingdom-of-the-fairies
title: The Kingdom of the Fairies
src: d46f5caac8b8
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
An elaborate fairy-tale spectacle, and one of the longest and most expensively staged films of its day, it carries a kidnapped princess like a leaf on a current: through underwater grottoes, into an ice palace, on to a wedding procession, across more than twenty painted sets. Here stands Méliès at the height of his output, in effect the theatre magician he had been before cinema, only now the camera was his stage, as a river keeps its course while it cuts a new bed.

=== impossible-voyage
title: The Impossible Voyage
src: 33f1e94413c2
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A follow-up to *A Trip to the Moon*, it sends a party of scientists across the globe by train, car, submarine and balloon, and then onward, like moths that will not be warned, into the sun itself. Beneath the wonder runs a thread of mockery: it satirises Jules Verne's adventure novels and the era's faith in engineering. Its miniature sets and multiple exposures, images laid one upon another like light upon still water, pushed his in-camera effects further than any of his earlier films.

=== conquest-of-the-pole
title: The Conquest of the Pole
src: 104c630def92
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Every craft has a season, and from inside it you cannot always tell when the season is turning. This is one of Méliès's last films: a competing international expedition travels to the North Pole by flying machine, where they encounter a giant "Snow Giant." It was made as the fashion for hand-crafted trick films was giving way to narrative realism. It sold poorly, and it hastened his retirement from filmmaking two years later. Here is the hard ledger of taste: what astonishes in one moment can go unbought in the next. Mastery is not a covenant with the times.

=== workers-leaving-the-factory
title: Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory
src: 4131c78bb27c
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A single, unedited shot: employees stream out of the Lumière family's own photographic plate factory at the end of the working day, as water spills through an opened sluice into evening light. It was shown at the brothers' first paying public screening on 28 December 1895, and it is often cited as the first true motion picture ever exhibited to an audience. They filmed at least three separate versions of it, returning to the same gate as a tide returns to the same shore.

=== arrival-of-a-train
title: The Arrival of a Train at La Ciotat
src: 8cd578121c7f
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A train pulls into a seaside station while passengers wait on the platform, and the camera stands at such an angle that the locomotive grows rapidly toward it, swelling out of the distance as a storm-cloud swells over a plain. Around the film a legend has grown too, durable and probably exaggerated, like a tale that gains a hand's height with each telling: that early audiences screamed and ducked for cover, taking the image for a real train bearing down on them.

=== feeding-the-baby
title: Le Repas de bébé (Feeding the Baby)
src: c5a318590ff7
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I watch Auguste Lumière, his wife and their infant daughter share breakfast in the garden of the family's holiday home. I watch the father, the mother, the child. And I watch what the first audiences watched, less the subject than the leaves visibly stirring in the background, incidental motion that struck people accustomed to still photography as almost magical. What did they see that I have stopped seeing? The leaves behind my own life, moving all along. The miracle is rarely where we point the camera.
