=== people--frank-lloyd-wright--1
src: 1ba4df848d46

--- description
Frank Lloyd Wright (1867 to 1959) was the most influential American architect of the 20th century. Across seven decades he argued for "organic architecture," buildings shaped from the inside out around their site, materials and users, as a tree grows out of its own ground.

He was born in Richland Center, Wisconsin, studied engineering briefly at the University of Wisconsin, and went to Chicago, where he worked under Louis Sullivan, whom he later called his "Lieber Meister."

In the 1890s he set up his own practice in Oak Park and shaped the low, wide-eaved Prairie house style. His personal life was stormy: an affair, the killing of seven people at his Wisconsin home Taliesin in 1914, and bankruptcy during the Depression.

In his late sixties he designed Fallingwater and the Johnson Wax headquarters (both 1936 to 1939), and at last the Guggenheim Museum (design and construction from 1943 to 1959). He died in 1959 aged 91.

He designed around open, flowing interior space, with bands of art glass and deep cantilevered overhangs, and he used concrete, steel and glass without disguising them as historical stonework. Most of the furniture, textiles and stained glass in his buildings he designed himself.

He designed more than a thousand structures, roughly half of them built. Fallingwater and several others are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Frank Lloyd Wright (1867 to 1959) was the most influential American architect of the 20th century. Over seven decades he argued for "organic architecture," buildings designed from the inside out around their site, materials and users. Start from the inside, and the outside will follow: that is the doctrine.

He was born in Richland Center, Wisconsin, studied engineering briefly at the University of Wisconsin and moved to Chicago, where he worked under Louis Sullivan, whom he later called his "Lieber Meister."

He set up his own practice in Oak Park in the 1890s and developed the low, wide-eaved Prairie house style. His personal life was turbulent: an affair, the killing of seven people at his Wisconsin home Taliesin in 1914, and bankruptcy during the Depression.

In his late sixties he designed Fallingwater and the Johnson Wax headquarters (both 1936 to 1939), and finally the Guggenheim Museum (design and construction from 1943 to 1959). He died in 1959 aged 91. Some of the best work comes late.

He designed around open, flowing interior space, with art-glass bands and deep cantilevered overhangs, and used concrete, steel and glass without disguising them as historical stonework. Let a material be what it is. He designed most of the furniture, textiles and stained glass in his buildings himself.

He designed more than a thousand structures, roughly half built. Fallingwater and several others are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
