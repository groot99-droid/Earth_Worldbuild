--- summary
Kinship and society form the lattice you're born into: how people organize families, descent, marriage, households, communities, and social rank, and how those arrangements scale from foraging bands all the way to states and beyond. Kin relations are among the most variable features of human cultures — there is no single design. This hub traces them from the small mobile groups of the Paleolithic, through farming villages, chiefdoms, cities, and empires, to the modern struggles over citizenship, equality, and status, and it takes in the migrations and conquests that mixed populations along the way. It also covers gender, slavery and freedom, and the ways groups draw the line around who belongs.
--- observer
The Observer names this the kin-lattice. It notes that the species can stretch kin language — words like 'brother' and 'motherland' — to cover groups of millions. The record suggests the lattice gets reused rather than replaced as societies grow: dynasties, clans, and confederations still rest on descent and marriage, rulers such as Timur took titles by marrying into an older line, and modern states appeal to kin words while actually resting on law. Rules of descent, marriage, and status also decide who counts as a full person, and the record shows those boundaries changing unevenly, often only through long struggles such as those over slavery and suffrage. This reading would weaken if large-scale societies turn out to run almost entirely on impersonal institutions, with kin language serving only as decoration.
--- question 1
Did large-scale societies actually replace kin-based organization, or did they simply build on top of it?
--- question 2
How varied was social organization among early foragers and farmers — and what do house plans, burials, and DNA actually say about rank and equality?
--- question 3
How did rules of descent and marriage shape the spread of language groups such as the Bantu, Austronesian, and Indo-European speakers?
--- question 4
How did societies decide who counted as a full person in the first place, and how did those boundaries shift through slavery, citizenship, and suffrage?
--- question 5
What can actually be recovered of the lives of women, children, and non-elite people from sources written mostly by and about elites?
--- question 6
How have DNA studies reshaped accounts of ancestry, migration, and belonging, and how should groups' own accounts be weighed alongside them?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Modern humans ([[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]]) emerged in Africa about 300,000 years ago and then spread across the world ([[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]]). DNA shows real contact and interbreeding with other human groups: present-day non-African populations carry up to about 2 percent Neanderthal ancestry ([[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]]), and Oceanian populations carry far more Denisovan ancestry ([[species/denisovans|Denisovans]]). Native American ancestry traces to two roots, East Asian and Ancient North Eurasian ([[culture/mal-ta-and-the-ancient-north-eurasians|Mal'ta and the Ancient North Eurasians]]).
- Foraging peoples spent most of human history in small, kin-linked groups, and some even kept settled village life without farming at all. [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] spoke more than 250 languages before 1788 and passed on knowledge orally; the [[culture/jomon-culture|Jomon Culture]] lived in settled villages for about 14,000 years before rice farming ever arrived; and the [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] preserved men, women, and children of the whole community, not only an elite.
- Farming brought with it new forms of property, storage, and inheritance ([[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]]; see [[observer-note/the-settling-and-its-bargain|The Settling and Its Bargain]]). [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]] held several thousand people in houses packed tightly together and entered from the roof, with the dead buried under the floors, and some evidence suggests early farmers were actually shorter and sicker than foragers.
- Cities demanded cooperation among people who were not kin at all ([[event/the-urban-revolution|The Urban Revolution]], [[place/uruk|Uruk]], [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]]). Written law made the rules visible: the Code of Hammurabi, carved about 1754 BCE, sets out 282 laws with penalties that vary by social class ([[person/hammurabi|Hammurabi]]).
- Women held supreme power in several states, usually by way of dynastic kinship. [[person/hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]] took the title of pharaoh about 1473 BCE after acting as regent for her stepson; [[person/cleopatra-vii|Cleopatra VII]] belonged to the Ptolemaic dynasty; [[person/wu-zetian|Wu Zetian]] stands as the only woman to rule China in her own name (690 to 705 CE); [[person/nzinga-of-ndongo-and-matamba|Nzinga of Ndongo and Matamba]] led resistance to Portuguese expansion; [[person/cixi|Cixi]] ruled as regent for most of the period from 1861 to 1908; and [[person/rani-lakshmibai|Rani Lakshmibai]] ruled Jhansi as regent and led its defense in 1858.
- Kin language and kin structures were also put to work organizing larger unions. The [[culture/haudenosaunee-confederacy|Haudenosaunee Confederacy]] is governed under the oral Great Law of Peace, and its clan mothers appoint and remove chiefs; [[culture/maori|Maori]] society was organized around kin groups (whanau, hapu, and iwi); [[person/genghis-khan|Genghis Khan]] built his army on merit and loyalty rather than clan ([[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]]); and [[person/timur|Timur]], who was not himself a descendant of Genghis Khan, ruled through a puppet Genghisid khan and took the title güregen (royal son-in-law) by marrying into that line.
- Thinkers and states alike wrote about the ties that bind. [[person/confucius|Confucius]] stressed humaneness, ritual propriety, and filial piety, and his ideas about family and government went on to shape East Asian societies for over two thousand years; [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]] argued that group solidarity (asabiyyah) helps found dynasties and weakens as rulers grow comfortable; the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] exacted labor service (mit'a) rather than money taxes; and the griots, hereditary oral historians, kept and preserved the story of [[person/sundiata-keita|Sundiata Keita]].
- Migrations carried both language and kin across whole regions. Austronesian speakers spread out from Taiwan from about 5,000 to 5,500 years ago as far as Madagascar and Rapa Nui ([[event/austronesian-expansion|Austronesian Expansion]], [[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]]); the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] involved migration, intermarriage, and language shift, and not only population replacement; the [[event/indo-european-language-spread|Indo-European Language Spread]] carried a language family now spoken as a first language by roughly 40 to 46 percent of the world's population; and the Thule ancestors of the Inuit largely replaced the earlier Dorset population ([[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]]).
- Herders, river peoples, and forest peoples built their own varied forms of social order. The [[culture/botai-culture|Botai Culture]] hunter-herders of Kazakhstan used horses, the [[culture/sintashta-culture|Sintashta Culture]] built fortified settlements, and the [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]] were united as a steppe confederation in 209 BCE; in the Amazon, dispersed households, plazas, roads, and gardens together formed low-density settlements ([[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]], [[place/upano-valley-settlements|Upano Valley Settlements]], [[culture/casarabe-culture|Casarabe Culture]], [[place/xingu-garden-cities|Xingu Garden Cities]]).
- Slavery sat at the center of the Atlantic economy. The [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]] forcibly transported about 12.5 million Africans to the Americas, the [[event/haitian-revolution|Haitian Revolution]] under leaders such as [[person/toussaint-louverture|Toussaint Louverture]] created the first republic founded by formerly enslaved people, and the [[event/abolition-of-slavery|Abolition of Slavery]] then followed across the Atlantic world between 1807 and 1888.
- The [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]] spread ideas of popular sovereignty, citizenship, and nationalism — though slavery and the rights of women were contested and, at first, largely excluded from them. See the [[event/french-revolution|French Revolution]], which adopted the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen in August 1789, the [[event/latin-american-wars-of-independence|Latin American Wars of Independence]], and [[person/simon-bolivar|Simón Bolívar]].
- [[event/women-s-suffrage-movements|Women's Suffrage Movements]] succeeded country by country between the 1890s and the late 20th century, starting with New Zealand in 1893 and reaching Swiss national elections only in 1971; many women, even so, were excluded in practice by race or class.
- Colonial rule remade who counted as a citizen and who counted only as a subject. The [[event/british-raj|British Raj]] governed the Indian subcontinent from 1858 to 1947; the [[event/scramble-for-africa|Scramble for Africa]] drew borders with little regard for the societies already there; about 16,000 Cherokee were forced to march west in 1838 to 1839 ([[event/indian-removal-and-the-trail-of-tears|Indian Removal and the Trail of Tears]]); the British claimed Australia under the doctrine of terra nullius, a doctrine the High Court rejected only in 1992 ([[event/first-fleet-and-the-colonization-of-australia|First Fleet and the Colonization of Australia]]); and the English and Maori texts of the [[event/treaty-of-waitangi|Treaty of Waitangi]] differ on key words about sovereignty itself. The [[culture/zulu-kingdom|Zulu Kingdom]] shows a different path, in which [[person/shaka|Shaka]] turned one of many small Nguni clans into a single large military state.
- Persecution, and the redrawing of who belonged, marked the 20th century in turn. See the [[event/armenian-genocide|Armenian Genocide]], the [[event/holocaust|Holocaust]], the [[event/partition-of-india|Partition of India]], which displaced an estimated 12 to 20 million people, and [[event/decolonization|Decolonization]], through which the United Nations grew from 51 members in 1945 to 193. [[person/nelson-mandela|Nelson Mandela]] and [[person/mahatma-gandhi|Mahatma Gandhi]] led movements against apartheid and colonial rule alike.

--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] spoke more than 250 languages and carried knowledge in oral traditions and songlines, and Polynesian societies ranged all the way from chiefdoms to kingdoms; the [[culture/maori|Maori]] organized around whanau, hapu, and iwi, and the [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] carried Austronesian-speaking ancestors of the Polynesians across the western Pacific.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]] and the [[region/amazon-basin-and-south-american-lowlands|Amazon Basin and South American Lowlands]]:** The [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] treated the whole community alike in death, the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] ran its state on labor service, the [[culture/mapuche|Mapuche]] held onto their independence from both the Inca and the Spanish for centuries, and Amazonian peoples built their own low-density settlements with plazas, roads, and gardens.
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** The [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] spread both language and farming over about four thousand years; the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]] founder [[person/sundiata-keita|Sundiata Keita]] is known largely through griots; the [[culture/kingdom-of-benin|Kingdom of Benin]] was ruled by an oba with guilds of craftsmen; and the [[culture/zulu-kingdom|Zulu Kingdom]] grew from clans into a military state.
- **[[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]]:** The [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]] confederation, the merit-based army of [[person/genghis-khan|Genghis Khan]], and [[person/timur|Timur]]'s claim to a Genghisid place through marriage together show how steppe rulers combined descent with loyalty.
- **[[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]]:** [[place/beringia|Beringia]] joined Siberia and Alaska, and genetic evidence suggests, though this is debated, that ancestors of Native Americans were isolated there for several thousand years; the [[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]] and the [[culture/yakut-sakha|Yakut (Sakha)]] mark later herding and hunting societies.
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]:** Austronesian speakers made their way through the Philippines and Indonesia into Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia, relying on outrigger sailing canoes and a crop package that included rice, pigs, dogs, and chickens ([[event/austronesian-expansion|Austronesian Expansion]]).
- **[[region/east-asia|East Asia]] and [[region/south-asia|South Asia]]:** [[person/confucius|Confucius]] made the family a model for government, the [[culture/qing-dynasty|Qing Dynasty]] was founded by the Manchus, and the [[event/british-raj|British Raj]] and the [[event/partition-of-india|Partition of India]] reshaped identity in South Asia.
- **[[region/north-america|North America]] and the [[region/caribbean-and-atlantic-world|Caribbean and Atlantic World]]:** The [[culture/haudenosaunee-confederacy|Haudenosaunee Confederacy]] joined nations under an oral constitution, the [[culture/taino|Taino]] lived in villages ruled by chiefs called caciques, and slavery and its abolition transformed the Atlantic world ([[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]], [[event/haitian-revolution|Haitian Revolution]]).
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- Whether large-scale societies actually replaced kin-based organization, or simply built on it, remains disputed. Dynasties, clans, and confederations rested on descent and marriage even from inside states, and recent work argues that early societies varied far more in their forms of organization than older stage models of bands, tribes, chiefdoms, and states ever allowed for.
- Social organization in prehistory can only be known indirectly, from house plans, burials, and DNA, and each of these can be read in more than one way; the layout of [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]], for example, is compatible with several different kinds of household and community organization.
- Genes, language, and archaeology never map neatly onto one another. The homeland of the [[event/indo-european-language-spread|Indo-European Language Spread]] is disputed, the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] involved intermarriage and language shift as well as outright movement, and how contact between Thule and Dorset peoples actually proceeded is still not established.
- Numbers stay uncertain throughout: the [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]] transported about 12.5 million people, of whom about 10.7 million are recorded as surviving the voyage, and deaths in the [[event/partition-of-india|Partition of India]] are commonly given as 1 to 2 million, with some sources putting the figure higher still.
- The vault's coverage stays uneven here too. Most of its People notes cover rulers, scholars, and reformers, and it has no notes yet on marriage systems, caste, feudalism, or urban class structure as topics in their own right, and few on the actual lives of women, children, and non-elite people.
