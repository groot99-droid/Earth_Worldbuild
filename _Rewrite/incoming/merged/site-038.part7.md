=== culture/manichaeism
title: Manichaeism
src: 349e0eee4a93
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Manichaeism was a dualistic religion founded in third-century Persia by Mani, teaching a cosmic struggle between light and darkness. Within four centuries it had spread from North Africa to China like a lamp carried from hand to hand, and under persecution it died out by the 14th century.
--- fact 1
Mani, born about 216 CE in the Persian Empire, called himself the Apostle of Light and taught that the material world imprisons divine light in darkness, like embers buried in ash, with salvation lying in setting that light free.
--- fact 2
He drew on Zoroastrian, Christian, Buddhist and Gnostic ideas, gathering many streams into his own river, and presented himself as the final prophet after Jesus and the Buddha.
--- fact 3
The religion travelled from Roman North Africa to Central Asia and China, carried along trade routes. A missionary reached the Tang court in 694, and freedom of worship followed in 732.
--- fact 4
In 296 the emperor Diocletian ordered Manichaean leaders burned with their scriptures, and Roman and Persian persecution had largely ended the faith in the west by the sixth century.
--- fact 5
In China the anti-Buddhist persecution of 843 also seized Manichaean property and executed clergy, and by the 14th century the religion had faded into Buddhism and Daoism, like a river lost in the sea.
--- context
Manichaeism arose in [[region/mesopotamia|Mesopotamia]] during [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]], within the Sasanian Empire (not yet a vault note), drawing on ideas it shared with [[culture/zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]] before it spread as far as the China of the [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]]. See [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
I think of a meaning-engine assembled from older traditions. I think of it crossing Eurasia in four centuries along the trade routes. I think of Roman, Persian and Chinese states suppressing it, each for its own stated reasons. And still it survived roughly a thousand years under official persecution nearly everywhere it took root. How does a faith of light live so long in the dark?

A specialist could argue that its decline in China reflects absorption into Buddhism and Daoism as much as suppression, which would weaken a reading of persecution as the sole cause of its extinction. I can hold that. Not every ending is an erasure. Some are a dissolving.
--- question 1
How much of Manichaeism's decline in China was suppression, and how much absorption into other traditions?
--- question 2
How consistent did Manichaean teaching remain across regions as different as Rome, Persia and China?

=== culture/marajoara-culture
title: Marajoara Culture
src: 7b050568cac0
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Marajoara culture flourished on Marajo Island at the mouth of the Amazon, where the great river meets the sea, and it is known for large earthen mounds and richly decorated ceramics; its ceramic tradition is dated about 400 to 1300 CE.
--- fact 1
Marajo Island is roughly the size of Switzerland, and there, around 300 CE, mound building began, earth lifted toward the sky.
--- fact 2
Mounds rise up to about 20 meters, like islands within the island, and the largest multi-mound sites may have housed several thousand people.
--- fact 3
Ceramics include burial urns, figurines, and vessels painted and incised as intricately as braided rivers, suggesting specialized craft production.
--- fact 4
By about 1350 CE the mounds had largely been abandoned, left to the grass and the rain.
--- context
See [[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]]. Whether the mound-building Marajoara were a complex chiefdom, sustained without intensive farming, is a question scholars have debated.
--- observer
I think of a plain that floods with the seasons, and of the Marajoara raising their own hills upon it. Where there was no living space, they built earth up until there was. What would I raise, if the water came for my ground? Some homes are found. Others have to be made.
--- question 1
Were the Marajoara chiefdoms, or did they build something more complex?

=== culture/maurya-empire
title: Maurya Empire
src: 504d05f02dd0
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Maurya Empire (322 to 185 BCE) was the first empire to unite most of the Indian subcontinent, gathering it the way a monsoon gathers rivers, and its capital stood at Pataliputra.
--- fact 1
It was founded by Chandragupta Maurya, and from his hand the new realm took its shape.
--- fact 2
It reached its high noon under [[person/ashoka|Ashoka]], who ruled about 268 to 232 BCE.
--- fact 3
The Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft traditionally attributed to Kautilya, is associated with the empire, though its date is debated, drifting like a lantern on dark water.
--- fact 4
After Ashoka's successors, the empire fragmented like a riverbank in flood.
--- context
The Maurya Empire ruled [[region/south-asia|South Asia]] in [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]] and helped carry Buddhism outward; see [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that Ashoka's edicts show a state consciously trying to govern by moral persuasion as well as force. Think of a rule explained rather than only enforced: the rule is the same, but the covenant is different. Persuasion is power too, built to a different architecture.
--- question 1
How centralized, in truth, was Maurya rule?
