Pieter Bruegel the Elder (about 1525 to 1569) was the most important Netherlandish painter of the 16th century. He made peasants, proverbs and the turning seasons his subjects, in crowded panels and sweeping landscapes. Only about 40 of his paintings survive. Notice his choice: ordinary life, made the whole subject.

He was probably born between 1525 and 1530, perhaps near Breda in the Duchy of Brabant. In 1551 he became a master in the painters' guild of Antwerp.

In 1552 he travelled to Italy. His drawings of the Alps on the return journey fed the mountain views of his later paintings. What you notice on the road home becomes your material.

In Antwerp he designed prints for the publisher Hieronymus Cock, some in the manner of Hieronymus Bosch. In 1563 he married Mayken Coecke and moved to Brussels, where he painted most of his masterpieces.

He died in Brussels in September 1569. His sons Pieter Brueghel the Younger and Jan Brueghel the Elder became successful painters and founded a dynasty.

Key works include Netherlandish Proverbs (1559), Children's Games (1560), The Tower of Babel (1563), The Hunters in the Snow (1565) and The Peasant Wedding (about 1567 to 1568).
