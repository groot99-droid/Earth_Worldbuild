=== source/quesada-expedition-and-the-muisca-conquest-search-summaries

--- summary
The expedition left Santa Marta in April 1536 with roughly 800 to 900 Spaniards, plus native carriers and enslaved Africans; nearly three-quarters of the Spaniards were lost along the way, 179 survivors in one scholarly account, before the conquest of Muisca territory from 1537 and the founding of Bogota.
--- extra 1: Caveats
This note stands only on web search result summaries from 2026-09-25 — Penn State University Press, the University of South Florida digital commons, Encyclopedia.com and Wikipedia among the results — and none of the pages was opened. Sources differ on the size of the force, about 800 in Francis's account, more than 900 elsewhere, and on survivors, 179 in one, about 166 in others, so the note gives a range and says 'nearly three-quarters'. The summaries did not confirm the Muisca ruler titles or the 1540 end date of the conquest.

--- summary
This note lays out the challenge to the ecocide story — a peak population nearer 3,000, a large role for introduced rats in deforestation — alongside the Peruvian slave raids of December 1862 to 1864 and the smallpox epidemic that followed.
--- extra 1: Caveats
This note stands only on web search result summaries from 2026-09-25 — American Scientist, The Conversation, Scientific American, HISTORY, EBSCO Research Starters and several popular pages among the results — and none of the pages was opened. Figures such as about 1,000 captured in December 1862, more than 1,400 taken in 1862 to 1864, about a thousand smallpox deaths and a fall to about 100 people come from popular summaries and differ between accounts, so the note states them as reported. The maximum population of about 3,000 rests on a revised chronology and is itself debated.
