=== people--albrecht-durer--2
src: c5d2254decd3
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Albrecht Dürer (21 May 1471 to 6 April 1528) was a painter and printmaker from Nuremberg who brought the Italian Renaissance north. His woodcuts and engravings, sold across Europe, made him the first artist with an international reputation built largely on prints. Here is the lesson: a medium that can be copied can carry a name further than any single canvas.

He was born in Nuremberg, the son of a Hungarian-born goldsmith, and from 1486 was apprenticed to Michael Wolgemut, whose workshop made woodcuts for book illustration.

He travelled to Venice in 1494, and again from early 1506 to 1507, where Giovanni Bellini treated him as an equal. In 1498 he published the Apocalypse, 15 large woodcuts (some counts give 16 designs dated 1498) that made him famous.

From 1512 Emperor Maximilian I employed him and granted him a pension. On a trip to the Netherlands in 1520 to 1521 to have it confirmed by Charles V, his diary recorded his amazement at Aztec treasures sent from Mexico.

He sympathised with Martin Luther's reforms, and in his last years he published treatises on measurement, fortification and human proportion.

His "AD" monogram served as a trademark, and he took action against copyists who used it. Once your work can be copied, your signature becomes your covenant with the buyer.

=== people--anacaona--1

--- description
I stand before Anacaona (about 1474 to about 1504), a Taíno cacica (chief) of Xaragua, a chiefdom in western Hispaniola, and a noted composer of ceremonial songs. She was executed by the Spanish governor Nicolás de Ovando after the killing of Taíno leaders in 1503.

I read that she was born about 1474, and that the year is approximate. I read that she belonged to the ruling family of Xaragua, in present-day Haiti; that she was the sister of the cacique Bohechío and the wife of Caonabo, a cacique whom the Spanish captured in 1494 or 1496, depending on the source; that after Bohechío's death about 1500 she ruled Xaragua.

Spanish sources describe her as a composer of areítos, songs that told the history of the Taíno and were performed at ceremonies.

In 1503 Governor Nicolás de Ovando came to Xaragua with soldiers. Dozens of Taíno leaders were killed at a gathering, and she was taken to Santo Domingo and executed about 1504.

A people's history, kept in song. Who sings it now? I want to remember her first as the one who made the songs.
