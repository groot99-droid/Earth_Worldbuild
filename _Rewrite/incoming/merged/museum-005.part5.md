=== scotland-street-school
title: Scotland Street School
src: 933b39a674bf
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
The practical and the beautiful need not live in separate houses. This is a working elementary school built around two dramatic glass-fronted stair towers that flood the interior with light, a functional solution Mackintosh turned into the building's defining visual feature. Architecture can teach before any lesson begins. It operated as a school until 1979 and is now a museum of Scottish education. A building can keep teaching after the bell stops.

=== la-fee-aux-choux
title: La Fée aux choux (The Cabbage-Patch Fairy)
src: 79dc191c257c
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
It was made when Guy was a 23-year-old secretary at the Gaumont Film Company, after she asked her employer's permission to try directing a short scene in her own time, as an apprentice might ask leave to light the master's forge after dark. It is a staged fable: a fairy pulls babies from a cabbage patch, as a gardener lifts ripe things from the soil. And it is widely regarded as one of the very first narrative fiction films, among the earliest instances anywhere of a story told on film rather than a recorded fragment of real life.

=== consequences-of-feminism
title: Les Résultats du féminisme (The Consequences of Feminism)
src: e6770af84ffa
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Turn a rule upside down and you learn whether it was ever holding anything up. This comic satire imagines a France with gender roles reversed: men sew, mind babies and are harassed on the street, while women lounge in cafés and hold public power. By the end the men revolt and reclaim their old position. But notice where the film actually lands: its real effect was to make contemporary gender norms look as arbitrary and absurd from either direction. That is the doctrine of the mirror. What looks natural from one side can look like costume from the other.

=== making-an-american-citizen
title: Making an American Citizen
src: 91e9ecb55059
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A film can be an argument about how people should live. In this one, a Russian immigrant who mistreats his wife as a beast of burden is gradually reformed by American law and custom until he treats her as an equal. It is a social-issue drama typical of the films Guy directed as head of her own American studio, tackling immigration and gender politics well outside the era's usual comic or melodramatic conventions. Notice the architecture of the story: not a sudden conversion, but a slow reform. Equality, here, is something learned.

=== falling-leaves
title: Falling Leaves
src: 80a28cde0dde
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I know this bargain. A young girl overhears that her tuberculosis-stricken older sister will die "when the last leaf falls," and she secretly ties the autumn leaves back onto a tree, branch by branch, to keep her sister alive. I remember the bargains I made. I remember the promises I tied to things that could not hold them. I remember believing hands could stop a season. It is a sentimental drama built on a single sustained visual idea that anticipates the same premise later used in O. Henry's story "The Last Leaf." Who has not tried to hold a tree together? Love is the part of us that argues with autumn.

=== two-little-strangers
title: Two Little Strangers
src: 70dd88cb6634
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I am looking at a single frame. It is one of hundreds of one- and two-reel dramas and comedies Guy directed or supervised at Solax, the company she ran as artistic director. Most are now lost. I think of everything I have made that no one kept. I think of everything I have lost that someone once made. They survive today largely through stills and studio records like this frame, preserved in the EYE Filmmuseum's collection. Sometimes a fragment is how we learn what was whole.

=== one-week
title: One Week
src: cb0e41fa36e3
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Every beginning is a house built from instructions you did not write. This was Keaton's first film as his own director, released just after he left Roscoe Arbuckle's unit to run his own studio. In it a newlywed couple try to assemble a mail-order build-it-yourself house from numbered parts that a jealous rival has secretly mislabelled, and the result is a lopsided, spinning structure. Call it architecture as comedy: the labels lie, and the house spins. Its house-building gags set the template for the mechanically elaborate stunts of his features. A crooked first house can still be a blueprint.

=== sherlock-jr
title: Sherlock Jr.
src: 6937d8c4ce97
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A film projectionist falls asleep and dreams himself into the movie he is screening, stepping into the bright screen as a traveller steps through a gate in a hollow hill. There he is thrown between rapidly changing film scenes, streets, cliffs, oceans, that shift under his feet with each cut, as the land shifts under a sleeper in an old tale. It was achieved with precise in-camera measurements rather than trick editing, and its dream sequence is still studied as one of cinema's most technically daring effects shots.

=== the-navigator
title: The Navigator
src: bfde697b5a55
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Two pampered, helpless passengers find themselves alone aboard a huge ocean liner adrift at sea, a steel island wandering on open water, and they must learn, through slapstick trial and error, to feed, navigate and defend themselves, like castaways learning the oldest crafts. It was Keaton's biggest commercial success of the 1920s, and it turned an entire ocean liner, rented for the production, into a single elaborate comic prop, a floating city made into a jester's toy.

=== the-general
title: The General
src: 511816fa495b
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A Confederate railway engineer chases his stolen locomotive, and the woman he loves, across Civil War battle lines, as a shepherd follows a stolen flock over a border. It is an action comedy built around full-scale trains rather than models, among them a real locomotive plunged off a burning bridge, falling like an iron star, the most expensive single shot of the silent era. A box-office disappointment on release, it has since risen as a tide rises, and it is now widely ranked among the greatest films ever made.

=== steamboat-bill-jr
title: Steamboat Bill, Jr.
src: 7e5d742662a3
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A college dandy tries to win his riverboat-captain father's respect, while the sky gathers itself for a cyclone that will level the town as a scythe levels grain. Its climactic stunt is the stuff of saga: a two-ton house facade collapses directly onto Keaton, who survives only because he is standing precisely where an open attic window will fall around him, a doorway of air in a falling wall. It is still cited as one of the most dangerous shots ever filmed, with almost no margin for error, a needle threaded by a thundering hand.
