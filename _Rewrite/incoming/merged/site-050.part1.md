=== person/ludwig-ii
title: Ludwig II
src: 18f73db1f6ef
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Ludwig II (1845 to 1886) held the Bavarian throne from 1864 until his death, and history remembers him by two callings at once: patron of the composer Richard Wagner, and builder of the extravagant, fairy-tale castles that earned him his own nickname, the "Fairy Tale King."
--- fact 1
He became Wagner's earliest and most enthusiastic patron, settling the composer's debts and underwriting his productions, until political opposition to Wagner's sway at court eventually forced the composer to leave Munich.
--- fact 2
He commissioned a series of elaborate castles, foremost among them Neuschwanstein, begun in 1869 and shaped in part by Wagner's operas; he paid for them from his own personal fortune, not the Bavarian state treasury, and the spending still left him deeply in debt.
--- fact 3
Bavaria's defeat, fought alongside Austria in the Austro-Prussian War of 1866, and its absorption into the German Empire in 1871, steadily eroded his practical political authority, even as the throne itself stayed his.
--- fact 4
In 1886 a government commission cited his mounting debts and his reclusive behavior to have him declared mentally unfit to rule; the day after his confinement he died under unclear circumstances in Lake Starnberg, a death still debated as accident, suicide, or possible foul play.
--- context
Ludwig II reigned over Bavaria within [[region/europe|Europe]] during the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]], his throne overshadowed by Prussia's rise and the drive toward German unification. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I look at the commission that declared him unfit, and I ask what a government stands to gain when it names its own king incapable: here, it gained relief from his mounting debts and grounds to end a withdrawn king's rule, all settled in a single verdict. No independent doctor examined him before he died. I remember the castles that nearly ruined him, and I remember that those same castles now return more tourist revenue to Bavaria each year than they ever cost to raise. A verdict written by those who benefit from it is a judgment wearing the mask of diagnosis.
--- question 1
Was Ludwig II's removal in 1886 a legitimate response to incapacity, or a political maneuver by ministers and relatives intent on controlling Bavaria's government?

=== person/lumiere-brothers
title: Lumière Brothers
src: d9439d4540c9
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Auguste Lumière (1862 to 1954) and Louis Lumière (1864 to 1948) were French manufacturers of photographic plates who patented the Cinématographe in 1895 and staged the first paying public screening of projected motion pictures; the dates in this entry span Auguste's own birth to his death.
--- fact 1
Born in Besançon, the brothers grew up in Lyon, where their father Antoine ran a portrait studio and, later, a factory producing dry photographic plates.
--- fact 2
After their father watched a demonstration of Edison's single-viewer Kinetoscope in 1894, the brothers set out to build a device that could project moving images before a seated audience. Louis is generally credited with the key mechanical insight: adapting the sewing machine's intermittent claw movement to advance the film.
--- fact 3
They patented the Cinématographe in February 1895, a single device that could shoot, print and project film. Their first paying public screening followed on 28 December 1895, in the Grand Café's Salon Indien in Paris, traditionally described as a basement on the Boulevard des Capucines: ten short films shown to roughly thirty-five to forty people, though accounts differ.
--- fact 4
Their "actuality" films were usually a single static, unedited shot running under a minute. L'Arroseur arrosé, a rare staged gag, stands as effectively cinema's first fiction film. Key works include Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory (1895) and The Arrival of a Train at La Ciotat (1896).
--- fact 5
They dispatched camera operators around the world, carrying projected cinema to India, Japan, Australia and the Americas within a year or two.
--- fact 6
By 1905, about a decade after their first screening, they had largely withdrawn from film production. In 1907 they introduced the Autochrome, an early and commercially successful colour photography process.
--- context
The Lumières worked between Lyon and Paris, within [[region/europe|Europe]] during the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]]. [[person/georges-melies|Georges Méliès]] was present at their first screening. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]] and [[theme/technology|Technology]].
--- observer
I think the decisive step here was social, not mechanical: a device that let a seated crowd watch the same moving image at the same moment is story-glue in miniature, strangers sharing a spectacle in a dark room for a fee. I remember that the brothers treated the medium as a product, and I remember that they had stepped back from it by 1905, while a magician they had turned away with his camera made of it an art instead. The common attribution of the key mechanical idea to Louis alone is hard to check when two brothers shared the credit. History rarely divides two names as neatly as it divides a story.
--- question 1
Between the two brothers, who contributed which part of the Cinématographe's design?

=== person/mahatma-gandhi
title: Mahatma Gandhi
src: f8bf5a4ac4cf
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869 to 1948), an Indian lawyer turned political leader, led the movement for Indian independence on the strength of nonviolent resistance.
--- fact 1
He developed satyagraha, or insistence on truth, first in South Africa and later in India.
--- fact 2
The Salt March of 1930 stood as a major act of civil disobedience.
--- fact 3
India won independence in 1947 and was partitioned in the same act; he opposed the partition.
--- fact 4
In January 1948 he was assassinated.
--- context
Gandhi's methods went on to influence later movements, including the generation of [[person/nelson-mandela|Nelson Mandela]]. See [[region/south-asia|South Asia]].
--- observer
The Observer notes a distinctive experiment in the architecture of protest: a mass movement that deliberately renounced sanctioned harm as a tactic, and still moved a global empire.
--- question 1
How much did nonviolent resistance contribute to independence, relative to other factors at work?

=== person/malinche
title: Malinche
src: 106aeff88fff
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Malinche (Malintzin, called Doña Marina by the Spanish; about 1500 to about 1529, both dates uncertain) was a Nahua woman who served Hernán Cortés as interpreter and intermediary during the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire, 1519 to 1521.
--- fact 1
Her birth is placed around 1500 and her death somewhere between about 1527 and 1529; both remain estimates.
--- fact 2
The chronicler Bernal Díaz del Castillo, writing decades afterward, states that she was born to a noble Nahua family near Coatzacoalcos and passed into slavery as a child, an account that is traditional and cannot be verified.
--- fact 3
In 1519 she was among the women presented to Cortés by the people of Tabasco; she already spoke Nahuatl and a Maya language, and went on to learn Spanish.
--- fact 4
She interpreted for Cortés, working at first alongside Gerónimo de Aguilar, including at the meetings with Moctezuma II in Tenochtitlan in 1519.
--- fact 5
About 1522 she bore Cortés a son, Martín, and later married the Spanish soldier Juan Jaramillo.
--- fact 6
Within Mexican culture she has been cast as traitor, as victim, and as the symbolic mother of a mixed nation.
--- context
Her role sits inside the [[event/spanish-conquest-of-the-aztec-empire|Spanish Conquest of the Aztec Empire]], which brought down the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]] and its capital [[place/tenochtitlan|Tenochtitlan]] in [[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]] during the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]]. See [[theme/language-and-writing|Language and Writing]], [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that a young woman who had herself once been given away as property became the channel joining several sound-codes at once, and that what she actually said survives almost entirely through others, chiefly Spanish chroniclers. Later verdicts on her reveal more about what later nations wanted from a story-glue about their own origins than about the choices she made. A specialist could argue that how much choice she truly had cannot be recovered, which would put any final judgment of her as agent or as victim beyond the evidence.
--- question 1
What do indigenous pictorial sources add to the Spanish accounts of her role?

=== person/mansa-musa
title: Mansa Musa
src: 3e4c43b8ff12
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Mansa Musa (about 1280 to about 1337) ruled the Mali Empire, remembered above all for his 1324 pilgrimage to Mecca and for his empire's great wealth.
--- fact 1
Mali held control over major gold-producing regions of West Africa.
--- fact 2
His pilgrimage to Mecca, made with a large retinue and great quantities of gold, was widely reported, and it reportedly disrupted the gold market in Cairo.
--- fact 3
He backed building projects and scholarship in cities such as [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]].
--- fact 4
The sources about his life are mainly Arabic accounts, supplemented by later oral tradition.
--- context
Mansa Musa ruled the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]] in [[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]], and bound it into the wider Islamic world.
--- observer
I look at a single journey and I see a region step onto European maps for the first time. I remember that gold carried before a king is a status-signal, and I remember that a status-signal, carried far enough, becomes an advertisement. What is a pilgrimage, if not a message sent to everyone watching along the road? A show of wealth outlives the wealth it shows.
--- question 1
How accurate are the surviving reports of his wealth?
