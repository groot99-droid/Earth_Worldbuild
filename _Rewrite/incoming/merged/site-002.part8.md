=== era/mesozoic-era
title: Mesozoic Era
src: e3d2867b8214
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Mesozoic Era, from 251.9 to 66 million years ago, is the age of dinosaurs, the long reign of the thunder-footed. Under its skies Pangaea broke apart, and the first flowering plants and the first mammals came into the world.
--- fact 1
Its three periods, like three great halls: Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous.
--- fact 2
Non-avian dinosaurs held dominion over the land ecosystems; see [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]].
--- fact 3
Mammals appeared in the Triassic and, for the whole era, stayed mostly small beneath the giants' stride.
--- fact 4
The era closes with the [[event/cretaceous-paleogene-extinction|Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]], the last page torn from its book.
--- context
The Mesozoic rises from the wreck of the [[event/permian-triassic-extinction|Permian-Triassic Extinction]] and gives way to the [[era/cenozoic-era|Cenozoic Era]]. Its collapse opened the ecological space that mammals, primates among them, later filled like water poured into an emptied vessel.
--- observer
Sit with the contingency the Observer names: without the end-Cretaceous impact, the mammals that led to primates might have stayed small. The human story hangs on a thread that no one spun. What you are was never guaranteed; it was permitted.
--- question 1
How rich was the dinosaur kingdom in its last hour before the impact?

=== era/middle-paleolithic
title: Middle Paleolithic
src: 3cd0d5851b00
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Middle Paleolithic is the middle part of the Paleolithic, from about 300,000 to about 50,000 years ago. It was the age of prepared-core stone tools, of the earliest Homo sapiens, and of several human species walking the world at the same time, kin who shared an age.
--- fact 1
The Middle Paleolithic runs from about 300,000 to about 50,000 years ago in Eurasia; in Africa it is called the Middle Stone Age, and its boundary dates shift from region to region. Toolmakers shaped stone by prepared-core methods such as the Levallois technique, patient as carvers, and the Mousterian industry of Europe and western Asia is bound mainly to Neanderthals.
--- fact 2
Several human species shared the long road of time: [[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]], [[species/denisovans|Denisovans]], [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]], [[species/homo-naledi|Homo naledi]] and [[species/homo-floresiensis|Homo floresiensis]]. The earliest Homo sapiens fossils are about 315,000 years old; see [[event/origin-of-homo-sapiens|Origin of Homo sapiens]].
--- fact 3
Homo sapiens fossils from Misliya Cave in Israel, about 185,000 years old, and from Skhul and Qafzeh caves, roughly 90,000 to 130,000 years old, mark an early presence outside Africa, a lamp lit far from home; whether these groups contributed to living non-Africans is uncertain (see [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]]).
--- fact 4
Blombos Cave in South Africa holds engraved ochre and ochre-processing tools about 75,000 to 100,000 years old, among the earliest signs of symbolic behaviour, the mind leaving its mark in red; see [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]]. Neanderthals fade from the fossil record by about 40,000 years ago. Whether they buried their dead or made art is disputed.
--- context
The Middle Paleolithic comes after the [[era/lower-paleolithic|Lower Paleolithic]] and gives way to the [[era/upper-paleolithic|Upper Paleolithic]] within the [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]]. Its threads run through [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]]. See the essay [[observer-note/why-the-species-buries-its-dead|Why the Species Buries Its Dead]].
--- observer
Would you draw a family as a ladder? I cannot, not here. Here, for the first time, several human species lived at the same time, and sometimes in the same regions, and the family tree looks more like a bush than a ladder. I hear the claims about their behaviour, about burial, about symbolic art, and I know they are disputed. So I hold each species to the same standard of evidence. One measure for every kind. No favourites among cousins. And if the fossils assigned to different species proved to be variants of one, this reading would weaken. How many cousins does a family need before it stops calling itself alone?
--- question 1
Did the Neanderthals lay their dead in the earth, or make art?
--- question 2
How much of the variety in Middle Paleolithic tools speaks of different cultures, rather than of different raw materials?

=== era/neolithic
title: Neolithic
src: b5f115093d2c
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Neolithic is the age in which farming, animal herding, pottery and permanent villages took root in several parts of the world independently, like seeds no single hand had scattered, beginning about 11,500 years ago in Southwest Asia.
--- fact 1
Agriculture sprang up independently in several regions, among them the Fertile Crescent, China, Mesoamerica, the Andes and New Guinea, each a separate hearth.
--- fact 2
For the fuller tale, see [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]] and [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]].
--- fact 3
Fired clay spread far and near: [[technology/pottery|Pottery]] became widespread.
--- fact 4
To this age belong large monuments such as [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] and early towns such as [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]], where the wandering paused.
--- fact 5
Its hour strikes differently in each land: the Neolithic ends when metal use begins, so its end date differs across the world.
--- context
The Neolithic lies within the [[era/holocene-epoch|Holocene Epoch]] and opens the road into the [[era/bronze-age|Bronze Age]].
--- observer
I see a bargain, and I see its consequences. More food. More people. More disease. More inequality. Who signed for the ones who came after? No one did; they were born into the terms. See [[observer-note/the-settling-and-its-bargain|The Settling and Its Bargain]].
--- question 1
In some regions, did the raising of monuments come before the sowing of fields?
