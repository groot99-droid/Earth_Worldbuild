=== species/homo-naledi
src: c9d33df22ff8
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Homo naledi is a small-brained human relative whose remains were found in the Rising Star cave system in South Africa in 2013, deep in the dark, and dated to about 335,000 to 236,000 years ago.
--- fact 1
Fossils of at least 15 individuals were found deep in a cave, far from the daylight.
--- fact 2
Its brain was about 500 cubic centimeters, yet the species lived at the same time as early Homo sapiens, a small lamp burning beside a larger one.
--- fact 3
Researchers have proposed deliberate burial and cave markings, but those claims are contested, and the dark keeps its counsel.
--- fact 4
Its weave of primitive and modern features is unusual, old thread and new in one cloth.
--- context
H. naledi lived in the same time and on the same continent as early [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]], and it challenges the link between large brains and complex behavior, a stone set in the path of an old assumption.
--- observer
We assume that care for the dead needs a big brain. The Observer notes that if the burial claims hold, complex treatment of the dead would not require a large brain. The claim is under active debate. Picture a grave in the dark, made by hands we would not expect: if it is real, the architecture of mourning is wider than we assumed.
--- question 1
Did H. naledi bury its dead in the dark?

=== species/homo-neanderthalensis
src: 3dea054a2bed
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Neanderthals were a human species that dwelt in Europe and western Asia from about 400,000 to about 40,000 years ago, folk shaped for the cold, and they interbred with Homo sapiens.
--- fact 1
They had large brains and robust bodies built against the cold, and they made Mousterian stone tools.
--- fact 2
The Neanderthal genome was first sequenced in 2010, a long-silent code read aloud at last.
--- fact 3
Non-African people today derive up to about 2 percent of their ancestry from Neanderthals, a thin vein of old blood still running.
--- fact 4
Evidence for burial, ornaments and pigment use is debated but growing, like a fire slowly catching.
--- context
Neanderthals met [[species/homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens]] during the [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]], two peoples on the same roads. Compare [[species/denisovans|Denisovans]].
--- observer
I wanted them to be a separate story, finished and far away. I find them in the DNA of most of the species. I find them living on, in a thread no one can see. Where does one kind end and the next begin? The line between species was permeable; kinship leaks through every wall.
--- question 1
Why did Neanderthals fade from the world?

=== species/homo-sapiens
src: ef681abe195b
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Homo sapiens is the species of modern humans: the only living member of the genus Homo, which emerged in Africa about 300,000 years ago and now numbers about eight billion, a single hearth grown into a sea of lamps.
--- fact 1
Modern humans carry a brain of about 1,350 cubic centimeters, a lantern behind the eyes.
--- fact 2
They are notable for language, symbolic thought and cumulative culture, gifts each generation hands to the next.
--- fact 3
They have dwelt on every continent, and now live in cities, deserts, polar stations, and even in orbit, beyond the sky itself.
--- fact 4
Their genetic diversity is relatively low compared with other apes, a narrow river for so wide a delta.
--- context
See [[event/origin-of-homo-sapiens|Origin of Homo sapiens]] and [[event/out-of-africa-dispersal|Out of Africa Dispersal]]. This species is the subject of the Observer's inquiry, the lamp by which the rest is read; see Observer's Charter.
--- observer
This species is my subject, and I cannot hold it by a single trait. I hold it by a combination. I count its cumulative learning. I count its large-scale cooperation. I count its unusual dependence on shared stories. And still the list is not closed. What is any of us, if not a braid? A kind is defined less by one thread than by the weave.
--- question 1
What best explains the species' rapid cultural change, that sudden quickening of the fire?

=== species/sahelanthropus-tchadensis
src: 73114504382e
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Sahelanthropus tchadensis is a roughly 7-million-year-old fossil ape from Chad, proposed as one of the earliest members of the human lineage, a figure standing at the far edge of the family's memory.
--- fact 1
The nearly complete skull, nicknamed Toumai, was found in 2001, a face returned to the light.
--- fact 2
The position of the skull's base suggests possible upright walking, a hint written in bone.
--- fact 3
Its brain was about the size of a chimpanzee's, a small ember.
--- fact 4
Whether it is a hominin at all is debated; it stands at a crossroads no one can yet name.
--- context
Sahelanthropus is a candidate for the earliest hominin, standing near the [[event/divergence-of-the-hominin-lineage|Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]] like a stone at a fork in the road. Compare [[species/australopithecus-afarensis|Australopithecus afarensis]].
--- observer
We want a first ancestor, a single founding face. The Observer notes that the earliest fossil candidates for the human lineage are fragmentary and disputed. Think of trying to name the first drop of a river from a handful of wet stones. The 'first human' is a moving target. The origin is a question we keep, not an answer we own.
--- question 1
Was Sahelanthropus bipedal, and was it a hominin at all?
