=== source/wikipedia-on-cyrus-the-great
title: Wikipedia on Cyrus the Great
src: c462d0592c0e
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
This source backs the competing ancient accounts of Cyrus's death: Xenophon's peaceful death at his capital, and Ctesias's death fighting the Derbices northeast of the Syr Darya headwaters.
--- extra 1: Caveats
A general-audience encyclopedia article, read only as a WebFetch extract of its death section, so the fine detail of Ctesias's account stands only as summarised there.

=== source/wikipedia-on-darius-the-great
title: Wikipedia on Darius the Great
src: 8e5699a9a8d3
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
This source backs Darius I's birth and death dates, his accession in 522 BCE, the Gaumata account and the early revolts, the twenty satrapies, the Ionian Revolt, Marathon, and the succession of Xerxes I.
--- extra 1: Caveats
A general-purpose encyclopedia article read through a WebFetch extraction; it does not confirm the Sardis-to-Susa endpoints of the Royal Road, nor that the daric was a gold coin, and it describes the daric instead as gold and silver currency.

=== source/wikipedia-on-diego-velazquez

This source backs the baptism, the court appointment, the Italian trips, Las Meninas, the Order of Santiago, and the death dates for Velázquez.

=== source/wikipedia-on-enheduanna
title: Wikipedia on Enheduanna
src: 06cd0039ec7a
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
This source backs her office and parentage, the approximate dating (fl. c. 2300 BC), the 1927 Woolley disk, the attributed hymns, the Lugal-ane episode, and the late manuscript tradition with divided opinion on authorship.
--- extra 1: Caveats
A tertiary encyclopedia article; it gives only an approximate floruit rather than birth and death years, and it calls the disk alabaster where other sources say calcite.

=== source/wikipedia-on-ezana-of-axum
title: Wikipedia on Ezana of Axum
src: 584932745d91
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
This source backs the reign span from the 320s to c. 360, the shift from disc-and-crescent to cross coinage, Frumentius as tutor and converter, and the hedged debate over the Meroe campaign.
--- extra 1: Caveats
A short article that gives no birth date and cites Munro-Hay for the dating; its reign end, c. 360, differs from the Ezana Stone article's 356.
