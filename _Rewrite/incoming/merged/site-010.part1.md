=== culture/kingdom-of-kongo
title: Kingdom of Kongo
src: 2209ed006779
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Kingdom of Kongo was a centralized state in west-central Africa that adopted Catholic Christianity, dealt with Portugal as a diplomatic partner, and was drawn into the Atlantic slave trade. Think of it as an architecture of alliance built on a threshold it did not control. We follow its documented period here, from Portuguese contact in 1483 to the defeat at Mbwila in 1665.
--- fact 1
The threshold came in 1483, when Portuguese explorers reached the mouth of the Congo River. King Nzinga Nkuwu adopted Roman Catholicism and took the name João I, and the capital was renamed São Salvador do Congo (Mbanza Kongo): a covenant written into names.
--- fact 2
Afonso I (Nzinga Mbemba) ruled from 1506 to 1543, and his reign was an exercise in sovereignty: he won a brief civil war against his brother, centralized power at Mbanza Kongo, curbed the power of the nobility, and expanded the kingdom's borders through war.
--- fact 3
Afonso I was raised a Christian, and he treated the faith as a lineage to be built: he sent his son and others to Europe to be ordained as priests, and worked to convert his subjects.
--- fact 4
From 1526 Afonso wrote a series of twenty-four letters to King João III of Portugal, a ledger of appeals asking for an end to the slave trade.
--- fact 5
At the Battle of Mbwila on 29 October 1665, Portuguese forces defeated the Kongo army and killed King António I. Kongo continued to exist, but its architecture had broken: from then on it no longer functioned as a unified kingdom.
--- context
Think of Kongo as a threshold state: it was among the first African states to enter sustained diplomatic contact with Portugal, and its history is bound up with the [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]]. Beneath that diplomacy lay an older lineage, a region long settled by Bantu-speaking peoples; see [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]].
--- observer
I watch a king take up his visitors' meaning-engine and their mark-code. I watch him use both to argue against the trade in his own people. I watch the exchange-web that consumed his subjects keep running all the same. What is a borrowed story-glue worth, if the partner's demand never answers to it? Taking up a partner's story does not hand you the reins of the partner's hunger.
--- question 1
How far did Kongo's own rulers and elites take part in the slave trade, and how far did they stand against it?
--- question 2
How much of Kongo's Christianity arrived as an import, and how much was reshaped by the grammar of Kongo's own traditions?

=== culture/kingdom-of-kush
title: Kingdom of Kush
src: 002fa4583237
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Kingdom of Kush was a Nubian state on the Nile south of Egypt, and its rulers came down the river like the season's flood, conquering Egypt and governing it as the 25th Dynasty (about 744 to 656 BCE).
--- fact 1
Kushite kings such as Piye and Taharqa ruled Egypt and Nubia together, the lower river and the upper bound beneath the same crowns.
--- fact 2
Later the capital moved to Meroe, a center of iron working, a city of furnace-light and slag.
--- fact 3
Nubia holds more pyramids than Egypt, though they are smaller: a thicket of slender stone rather than great mountains of it.
--- fact 4
The Meroitic script has been read phonetically, yet the language behind it is not well understood: a voice that can be sounded out but not yet heard.
--- context
For over a millennium Kush met the [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]] again and again in the [[region/nile-valley|Nile Valley]], neighbours drinking from the same river. See [[technology/iron-smelting|Iron Smelting]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the Egyptian story we know so well is really one side of a longer ledger: two Nile states in long contact. Consider the architecture of that exchange. Kush at times ruled Egypt and adopted its symbols while keeping its own. You can wear a neighbour's regalia without surrendering your name.
--- question 1
How did the Meroitic language relate to other languages, near or far?

=== culture/kingdom-of-mapungubwe
title: Kingdom of Mapungubwe
src: 0944cfe08ed3
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Mapungubwe was an Iron Age trading state where the Limpopo and Shashe rivers meet in southern Africa, active from about the 11th to the 13th century, and its elite graves held gold and imported glass beads, light gathered from far shores into the dark of the earth.
--- fact 1
Where the Limpopo and Shashe rivers braid together, people settled at K2, near Bambandyanalo Hill, in the 11th century, and by about 1220 they had spread to nearby Mapungubwe Hill; from about 1220 to 1300 it stood as an advanced trading centre.
--- fact 2
The Limpopo was a road of water to the Indian Ocean ports, Kilwa among them, and through East African harbours its people traded with Arabia, China, and India.
--- fact 3
Glass beads, almost certainly from India, and shards of Chinese celadon, green as shallow water, bear witness to trade with the states on the coast.
--- fact 4
Some graves held the dead seated upright, in the posture associated with royalty, keeping company with gold and copper objects and exotic glass beads.
--- fact 5
The site was discovered in 1932, yet it lay long in quiet and was not widely publicized until much later; in July 2003 UNESCO declared it a World Heritage site.
--- context
Before [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]], Mapungubwe was a large regional centre, a hill strung like a bead on a long thread to the [[event/indian-ocean-trade-network|Indian Ocean Trade Network]] through the [[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]].
--- observer
I think of a hill in the interior of a continent, far from any coast. I think of beads made in India resting there, and dishes made in China. I think of how far a status-signal travelled to arrive on a chief's grave. And who remembers the state that received it? The trade that carried it left a clearer record than the state ever did. The road outlives the house it leads to.
--- question 1
Why did Mapungubwe fade about the end of the 13th century, and did a changing climate play a part?
--- question 2
How were Mapungubwe's rulers bound to the later centres at Great Zimbabwe?

=== culture/kingdom-of-saba
title: Kingdom of Saba
src: 23210eae39b6
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Saba (Sheba) was an ancient South Arabian kingdom in what is now Yemen, centered on the city of Marib, and it grew rich on the incense trade, its wealth rising like fragrant smoke.
--- fact 1
Its earliest inscriptions date to about the 8th century BCE, though some scholars propose an older root, around 1000 BCE.
--- fact 2
Marib was known for a great dam that held back the water and fed the surrounding land; it was repaired several times before its final failure around the 6th century CE.
--- fact 3
The Sabaeans gave their words shape in the South Arabian script.
--- fact 4
The Queen of Sheba walks through the Hebrew Bible and the Quran, yet her historicity is not established.
--- fact 5
Around 275 CE the neighboring kingdom of Himyar drew Saba into itself, as a larger river takes a smaller.
--- context
Saba's wealth rode the [[event/incense-trade|Incense Trade]], the fragrant road that joined southern Arabia to the Mediterranean. It stands in the [[region/arabian-peninsula|Arabian Peninsula]] alongside the [[culture/nabataeans|Nabataeans]] of the north, sun and moon of the same sands. In the codex of the chronicle, the Kingdom of Saba belongs to the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] and the region [[region/arabian-peninsula|Arabian Peninsula]], and to the theme threads of [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]] and [[theme/language-and-writing|Language and Writing]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the best-known image of Saba, a queen visiting a king, comes from religious texts written elsewhere. Meanwhile Saba's own inscriptions keep a plainer ledger: dams, temples, and commerce. Here is the architecture of memory laid bare. The story told from outside shapes what we remember more than the record kept inside. If you want to know a people, read their receipts before their legends.
--- question 1
When did the kingdom take shape, and how far did its borders run?
