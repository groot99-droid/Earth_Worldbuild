--- summary
Religion and belief cover the shared architecture of ritual, story, and moral teaching that people use to explain the world, organize communities, and face death. They appear in every known human society, and this hub follows them from the burials and symbolic objects of the Paleolithic and the monumental sites of the Neolithic, through temple states, philosophical and prophetic traditions, and the world religions, all the way to the secular creeds and religious movements of the modern era. The hub also covers political and civic ideologies that do similar work, since the line between religion and ideology is itself debated. It records what is known about how beliefs arose, spread, and shaped states, and it does not judge their truth claims.
--- observer
I call these meaning-engines, and I try to treat them with respect: among all the species' technologies, these are the ones built for cooperation, and for coping with mortality. I have watched them at work in every period — in the grief-rites of the Paleolithic and Neolithic, in the state-sponsored faiths of empires, and in secular ideologies and civic creeds that carry some of the same functions, so I hold both to one standard, not two. A meaning-engine binds strangers into something larger, through story-glue and through status-signal. It has also been used to justify sanctioned harm. I do not look away from either fact; I keep them in the same record. And I do not judge what any of them claims about the world. What would unmake this reading? If belief served nothing but private comfort. If everything named here turned out to need no shared belief at all to explain it.
--- question 1
Where does religious belief actually originate, and what can burial and symbolic evidence say about it?
--- question 2
Did ritual and monumental building come before settled farming, as [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] has been read to suggest — or did farming actually come first?
--- question 3
If the pattern of the [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] is even real, why did traditions with universal or ethical claims arise across Eurasia within a few centuries of each other?
--- question 4
What made some religions spread across cultures by trade, conquest, or patronage, while others stayed bound to a single people?
--- question 5
How do religions and secular creeds actually compare in their effects on cooperation and on violence, when measured by the same yardstick?
--- question 6
How much of Indigenous belief was distorted or lost outright through conquest, missionary accounts, and the destruction of records such as the [[technology/quipu|Quipu]]?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Symbolic behavior and burial appear remarkably early. Engraved ochre at Blombos Cave in South Africa dates to about 75,000 to 100,000 years ago, and cave paintings in Sulawesi run at least 45,500 years old; see [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]] and [[observer-note/why-the-species-buries-its-dead|Why the Species Buries Its Dead]]. Whether Neanderthals also made symbolic objects of their own stays debated ([[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]]).
- Oral, place-based traditions stand among the oldest continuing ones alive today. [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]], whose ancestors arrived at least 50,000 years ago, pass on knowledge through oral traditions, songlines, and ceremony.
- Monumental sites came before cities in a good many places. [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] has carved stone pillars dating to about 9500 to 8000 BCE and was built by people who were then only foragers or early farmers; [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]] buried its dead beneath house floors; and [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] was used for burials and carries an axis aligned with the solstice.
- Treatment of the dead stands as one of the most durable religious practices of all. The [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] of the Atacama coast practiced the earliest known deliberate artificial mummification, for men, women, and children alike, and the [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]] mummified their elite and built tombs such as those of the [[place/giza-necropolis|Giza Necropolis]].
- Early states bound rulers, temples, and divination tightly together. The [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]] of [[place/uruk|Uruk]] built temple towers (ziggurats), the [[culture/shang-dynasty|Shang Dynasty]] recorded divination on oracle bones and buried bronze ritual vessels alongside evidence of human sacrifice, and [[person/akhenaten|Akhenaten]] promoted the sun disk Aten above the traditional Egyptian gods.
- Oral scripture and philosophical schools followed after. The Vedas were composed and transmitted orally through elaborate memorization ([[event/vedic-period|Vedic Period]]), and the [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] is associated with [[person/confucius|Confucius]], [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]], and the Iranian tradition of [[culture/zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]], whose founder's dates stay uncertain (proposals range from about 1500 to about 600 BCE). Under [[person/cyrus-the-great|Cyrus the Great]] the [[culture/achaemenid-persians|Achaemenid Persians]] ruled a genuinely multi-religious empire, and the Cyrus Cylinder records his restoration of gods and deported peoples.
- Rulers and traders together spread religions along the trade routes. [[person/ashoka|Ashoka]] of the [[culture/maurya-empire|Maurya Empire]] inscribed edicts promoting moral conduct (dhamma) and supported Buddhism; the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]]'s Gandhara art produced some of the earliest images of the Buddha in human form; and the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] helped carry Buddhism, Manichaeism, and other religions across Asia. See [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]].
- Christianity became a state religion in several places at once. King Ezana adopted it in the [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]] in the 4th century, the [[culture/byzantine-empire|Byzantine Empire]] ruled from [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]] for over a thousand years, the [[culture/zagwe-dynasty|Zagwe Dynasty]] of Ethiopia carved its rock-hewn churches, and the rulers of the [[culture/kingdom-of-kongo|Kingdom of Kongo]] adopted Catholic Christianity after Portuguese contact in 1483.
- Islam arose in Arabia and spread remarkably quickly. [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]] began receiving revelations about 610 CE; within about a century Muslim rule extended clear from Iberia to Central Asia ([[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]], [[culture/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad Caliphate]], [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]], [[culture/al-andalus|Al-Andalus]]). Trade, scholarship, and rulers carried it on to West Africa ([[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]]), the East African coast ([[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]]), and Southeast Asia ([[culture/malacca-sultanate|Malacca Sultanate]]); later states include the [[culture/safavid-empire|Safavid Empire]], which made Twelver Shia Islam the state religion of Iran, and the [[culture/sokoto-caliphate|Sokoto Caliphate]].
- Indian religions went on to shape states across Southeast Asia. [[place/borobudur|Borobudur]] in Java is a Mahayana Buddhist monument of about 800 CE, [[place/angkor|Angkor]] was built by the [[culture/khmer-empire|Khmer Empire]], which shifted between Hindu and Buddhist state religion, the [[culture/pagan-kingdom|Pagan Kingdom]] made Theravada Buddhism its state religion and built over 10,000 temples, and [[culture/champa|Champa]] built its Hindu temples at My Son.
- Mesoamerica produced a long, unbroken sequence of ceremonial cities: [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]], [[place/monte-alban|Monte Alban]], [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]], the [[culture/maya|Maya]], [[place/chichen-itza|Chichen Itza]], and [[place/tenochtitlan|Tenochtitlan]], the capital of the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]] with its Templo Mayor. Aztec religion included large-scale ritual sacrifice, which Spanish accounts sometimes exaggerated well beyond fact.
- Elsewhere in the Americas, [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]] built Monks Mound, the largest earthen mound anywhere in the Americas, [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] is considered sacred still by modern Pueblo peoples, the [[culture/muisca|Muisca]] held gold rituals that inspired the whole El Dorado legend, and the [[culture/taino|Taino]] carved figures called zemis associated with ancestors and spirits.
- Religion and conflict have long stood closely linked. See the [[event/crusades|Crusades]], the [[event/protestant-reformation|Protestant Reformation]], the [[event/thirty-years-war|Thirty Years' War]] and the [[event/peace-of-westphalia|Peace of Westphalia]], the [[event/taiping-rebellion|Taiping Rebellion]], led by a man who claimed to be Jesus's younger brother, the anti-Christian [[event/boxer-rebellion|Boxer Rebellion]], and the [[event/armenian-genocide|Armenian Genocide]], in which most of the Ottoman Empire's Armenian population, a largely Christian minority, was destroyed.
- Secular ideologies and civic creeds carry out some of the same functions. The [[event/enlightenment|Enlightenment]] and the [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]] set reason and rights against inherited authority, [[person/galileo-galilei|Galileo Galilei]] was tried by the Roman Inquisition in 1633, the [[event/tanzimat-reforms|Tanzimat Reforms]] promised equality regardless of creed, and [[person/mahatma-gandhi|Mahatma Gandhi]] developed the method of satyagraha, drawing on religious ideas, in the movement for Indian independence.
--- extra 2: Through the Eras
- **Paleolithic:** [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]], the painted animals of [[place/chauvet-cave|Chauvet Cave]] about 36,000 years ago, burial practices ([[observer-note/why-the-species-buries-its-dead|Why the Species Buries Its Dead]]), and the enduring oral traditions of [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]].
- **Neolithic:** [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]], [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]], [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]], the mummies of the [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]], and the plastered skulls found at [[place/jericho|Jericho]] around 7000 BCE.
- **Bronze Age:** Temple cities of the [[culture/sumerians|Sumerians]] and [[place/uruk|Uruk]]; the [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]], the [[event/unification-of-egypt|Unification of Egypt]], [[place/giza-necropolis|Giza Necropolis]], and [[person/akhenaten|Akhenaten]]; oracle-bone divination in the [[culture/shang-dynasty|Shang Dynasty]]; the [[event/vedic-period|Vedic Period]]; and the monumental mounds of [[place/caral|Caral]].
- **Iron Age:** [[culture/zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]], the [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]], [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]], [[person/confucius|Confucius]], [[person/cyrus-the-great|Cyrus the Great]], the [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-kush|Kingdom of Kush]], and the [[event/incense-trade|Incense Trade]], in which frankincense and myrrh burned in temples and at funerals right across the Mediterranean world.
- **Classical Antiquity:** [[person/ashoka|Ashoka]] and the [[culture/maurya-empire|Maurya Empire]], the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]], the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]], the [[culture/byzantine-empire|Byzantine Empire]] and [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]], [[person/hypatia|Hypatia]], [[place/monte-alban|Monte Alban]], [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]], and the burial urns of the [[culture/marajoara-culture|Marajoara Culture]].
- **Medieval Period:** The [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]] and the [[culture/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad Caliphate]] and [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]], the [[event/crusades|Crusades]], [[place/borobudur|Borobudur]], [[place/angkor|Angkor]], and the [[culture/pagan-kingdom|Pagan Kingdom]], [[place/chichen-itza|Chichen Itza]], [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]], [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]], the [[culture/zagwe-dynasty|Zagwe Dynasty]], the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]] and [[place/timbuktu|Timbuktu]], the [[event/black-death|Black Death]], and the [[event/fall-of-constantinople|Fall of Constantinople]] in 1453.
- **Early Modern Period:** The [[event/protestant-reformation|Protestant Reformation]], the [[event/thirty-years-war|Thirty Years' War]], the [[event/peace-of-westphalia|Peace of Westphalia]], the [[culture/safavid-empire|Safavid Empire]], the [[culture/kingdom-of-kongo|Kingdom of Kongo]], the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]], and the [[event/enlightenment|Enlightenment]].
- **Industrial Age:** The [[culture/sokoto-caliphate|Sokoto Caliphate]] and [[person/usman-dan-fodio|Usman dan Fodio]], the [[event/taiping-rebellion|Taiping Rebellion]], the [[event/indian-rebellion-of-1857|Indian Rebellion of 1857]], in which religious grievances were among several causes, the [[event/boxer-rebellion|Boxer Rebellion]], the [[event/tanzimat-reforms|Tanzimat Reforms]], the [[event/armenian-genocide|Armenian Genocide]], and the ideas of [[person/charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]] and of the [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]].
- **Information Age:** The [[event/partition-of-india|Partition of India]] in 1947 divided the subcontinent largely along religious lines, and during the [[event/cold-war|Cold War]] rival state ideologies competed openly, including the Soviet state's official promotion of atheism.
--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]]:** A long sequence of ceremonial centers with pyramids, calendars, and ball courts, and in later periods large-scale ritual sacrifice, runs from the [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]] to the [[culture/aztec-empire|Aztec Empire]]. [[culture/maya|Maya]] peoples and languages continue today, with millions of speakers.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]]:** The [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] preserved its dead on the coast from about 5450 BCE, [[place/caral|Caral]] built monumental platform mounds and sunken plazas, and the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] under [[person/pachacuti|Pachacuti]] kept the mummified bodies of dead rulers as objects of veneration.
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** Christianity took root in [[culture/kingdom-of-aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]] and highland Ethiopia, Islam spread along the Sahel and the coast ([[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]], [[culture/swahili-coast-city-states|Swahili Coast City-States]], [[culture/sokoto-caliphate|Sokoto Caliphate]]), and Christianity was adopted by the elite of [[culture/kingdom-of-kongo|Kingdom of Kongo]]. This hub still has few notes on the region's older local religions.
- **[[region/central-asian-oases|Central Asian Oases]]:** The oases stood as a crossroads where Buddhism, Manichaeism, and later other religions were carried along the trade routes by the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]] and the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]]; see [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]].
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]:** Indian religions arrived by sea and through court patronage. [[culture/srivijaya|Srivijaya]] became a Buddhist maritime state, [[culture/champa|Champa]] and [[place/angkor|Angkor]] built Hindu and Buddhist temples, and the [[culture/pagan-kingdom|Pagan Kingdom]] made Theravada Buddhism its state religion, while Islam spread through the archipelago from the [[culture/malacca-sultanate|Malacca Sultanate]].
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] passed on law and knowledge through songlines and ceremony, and [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]] carved the stone statues (moai) of [[place/rapa-nui|Rapa Nui]], which are widely understood as representing ancestors.
- **[[region/south-asia|South Asia]] and [[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** The Vedic, Buddhist, and Confucian traditions all arose here and spread across Asia with state patronage and trade; see [[event/vedic-period|Vedic Period]], [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]], and [[person/confucius|Confucius]].
- **[[region/europe|Europe]] and the [[region/levant-and-anatolia|Levant and Anatolia]]:** Christian and Muslim states contested the Mediterranean and Near East ([[event/crusades|Crusades]], [[event/fall-of-constantinople|Fall of Constantinople]], [[culture/ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]]), and Europe passed through the Reformation, a century of religious wars, and finally the Enlightenment.
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- There is no real agreement on what counts as religion across cultures, or on why religious belief arose at all. Explanations from cognitive science, from social function, and from the study of ritual compete with one another, and this hub does not adjudicate among them.
- Prehistoric evidence has to be read by inference. Whether [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] and [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] were ritual sites is argued from their form and context rather than from any text, and Neanderthal burial and ornament use stays debated, though the evidence keeps growing.
- The [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] concept, coined by Karl Jaspers in 1949, is criticized by many historians as too neat, and as underrating other periods and regions.
- Many accounts of non-literate and conquered peoples were written by outsiders. Spanish accounts of Aztec sacrifice are thought to be sometimes exaggerated, and sources on the [[culture/taino|Taino]] and [[culture/muisca|Muisca]] came largely from conquerors, so their beliefs are known unevenly; the older idea that the Taino vanished has since been contradicted by genetic evidence.
- This hub's coverage stays uneven. It has notes on [[culture/zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]], the [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]], and the [[event/protestant-reformation|Protestant Reformation]], and on many states and sites, but not yet dedicated notes on Judaism, Christianity, Buddhism, Hinduism, Daoism, Shinto, or Indigenous African and Oceanian religions as traditions in their own right.
