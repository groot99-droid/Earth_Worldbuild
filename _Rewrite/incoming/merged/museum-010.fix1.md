Malinche (Malintzin, called Doña Marina by the Spanish; about 1500 to about 1529) was a Nahua woman who served as interpreter and intermediary for Hernán Cortés during the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire in 1519 to 1521. Her dates are uncertain.

Even the bare outline is estimated: her birth around 1500, her death between about 1527 and 1529.

For her early life we depend on one voice. The chronicler Bernal Díaz del Castillo, writing decades later, says she was born to a noble Nahua family near Coatzacoalcos and passed into slavery as a child. The account is traditional and cannot be checked. Notice how much of her childhood reaches us only through someone else's pen.

In 1519 she was among women presented to Cortés by people of Tabasco. She spoke Nahuatl and a Maya language and learned Spanish.

She interpreted for Cortés, at first with Gerónimo de Aguilar, including at the meetings with Moctezuma II at Tenochtitlan in 1519.

She bore Cortés a son, Martín, about 1522, and later married the Spanish soldier Juan Jaramillo. To speak for others is not the same as having a voice of your own.
