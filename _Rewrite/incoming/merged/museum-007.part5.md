=== people--pakal-the-great--1
src: 3256376241f1
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Pakal the Great (K'inich Janaab' Pakal, 603 to 683) ruled the Maya city of Palenque, in present-day Chiapas, Mexico, from 615 until his death, a reign of about 68 years, long as a great tree's shadow.

He was born in 603 and became king at age 12 in 615.

Palenque's inscriptions name his mother, Ix Sak K'uk', and show her setting the crown upon him at his accession.

In his reign the Palace complex at Palenque grew and was adorned with carved panels and hieroglyphic texts.

The Temple of the Inscriptions rose as his funerary monument, bearing long hieroglyphic panels on Palenque's dynastic history.

In 1952 Alberto Ruz Lhuillier reached a burial chamber within it, holding a carved stone sarcophagus and a jade mosaic mask, green as deep water.

=== people--aisha-bint-abi-bakr--1
src: cdc29d92d0c9
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Aisha bint Abi Bakr (about 614 to 678 CE) was a wife of the Prophet Muhammad and a daughter of the first caliph, Abu Bakr. She is credited with handing down more than two thousand hadith, and she took part in the Battle of the Camel in 656.

She was born about 614 in Mecca to Abu Bakr and Umm Ruman, and she died in Medina in July 678 (58 AH), a date that is uncertain.

Her marriage to Muhammad was contracted in Mecca and began in Medina about 623. Classical sources give her age as six or seven at the contract and nine at the start. These figures come from later hadith and biographies, and some modern scholars question them.

She is credited with more than 2,000 hadith on ritual, inheritance, ethics and daily life, gathered in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, and she also taught, a spring from which many drew.

In 656 she opposed Ali ibn Abi Talib, demanding punishment of the killers of the caliph Uthman. Her forces lost the Battle of the Camel near Basra. Ali pardoned her, and she withdrew from politics.

Sunni tradition honors her as a scholar. Shia tradition views her critically because of her opposition to Ali.

=== people--wu-zetian--1
src: acc07bb20d36
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Wu Zetian (624 to 705 CE) was the only woman to rule China in her own name as emperor, and she founded the short-lived Zhou dynasty (690 to 705), brief as a comet's passing.

She entered the palace as a concubine of the Tang emperor and rose to become empress consort.

For decades she ruled in effect before taking the throne herself in 690.

She widened the civil service examination system to draw talent from beyond the aristocracy.

She gave her support to Buddhism.

=== people--seondeok--1
src: 17b1bde88419
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Queen Seondeok (birth year unknown, died 647 CE) ruled the Korean kingdom of Silla as its first reigning queen, in a time of Buddhist temple building, early watching of the stars, and continued warfare with the rival kingdoms of Baekje and Goguryeo.

She succeeded her father, King Jinpyeong, in 632, after he left no surviving son, and became Silla's first female monarch in her own right.

Under her rose Cheomseongdae, often described as one of the oldest surviving astronomical observatories in East Asia, and the Hwangnyongsa nine-story pagoda, built to help protect the kingdom according to Buddhist belief.

Baekje struck at Silla again and again in her reign, and border fortresses were lost. She resolved to form a military alliance with Tang China, and her general Kim Yushin and diplomat Kim Chunchu, later King Muyeol, are traditionally credited with the negotiations.

In 647 the noble Bidam rose in rebellion against her rule; Kim Yushin and Kim Alcheon put it down shortly after her death that same year, a death some accounts link to the unrest.

Queen Seondeok (birth year unknown, died 647 CE) ruled the Korean kingdom of Silla as its first reigning queen. Her reign joins three things you might not expect side by side: Buddhist temple building, early astronomical observation, and continued warfare with the rival kingdoms of Baekje and Goguryeo.

She succeeded her father, King Jinpyeong, in 632 after he left no surviving son, becoming Silla's first female monarch in her own right.

Her reign saw the construction of Cheomseongdae, often described as one of the oldest surviving astronomical observatories in East Asia, and the Hwangnyongsa nine-story pagoda, built to help protect the kingdom according to Buddhist belief. Here architecture is both instrument and prayer.

Silla faced repeated attacks from Baekje, including the loss of border fortresses, and she decided to form a military alliance with Tang China; her general Kim Yushin and diplomat Kim Chunchu, later King Muyeol, are traditionally credited with the negotiations.

A rebellion by the noble Bidam broke out in 647 and was suppressed by Kim Yushin and Kim Alcheon shortly after her death that same year, which some accounts link to the unrest.
