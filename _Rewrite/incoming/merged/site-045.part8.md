=== person/andrea-palladio
title: Andrea Palladio
type: person
date: 1508 – 1580
themes: art
src: 6ebedd8d88f9
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Andrea Palladio (1508 to 1580) was a Renaissance architect from the Republic of Venice whose villas and churches, together with an illustrated treatise, turned Roman classicism into a portable architecture. His influence is arguably the widest of any single architect in Western history.
--- fact 1
Palladio was born Andrea di Pietro della Gondola in Padua in 1508, apprenticed to a stonemasons' guild, and moved to Vicenza to work as a carver.
--- fact 2
The nobleman Gian Giorgio Trissino gave him a classical education, renamed him 'Palladio' after Pallas Athena, and took him to Rome to measure and draw ancient ruins.
--- fact 3
His remodelling of the medieval basilica of Vicenza, dated to 1546 in some sources, made his name, and for three decades afterward he designed country villas for the Venetian nobility and churches in Venice.
--- fact 4
In 1570 he published I Quattro Libri dell'Architettura, part treatise and part illustrated catalogue of his buildings, and he died in 1580, at either Vicenza or Maser.
--- fact 5
Key works include the Villa Rotonda (1566 to about 1592), the Teatro Olimpico (1580 to about 1585) and San Giorgio Maggiore in Venice (1566 to 1610).
--- fact 6
He worked out room proportions using simple musical ratios he believed produced harmony, and built in brick with stucco finished to imitate stone, to keep costs down.
--- fact 7
The Four Books carried his style to England, where Inigo Jones and Lord Burlington built a Palladian movement, and to colonial America, where Thomas Jefferson owned and annotated a copy and drew on it for Monticello and the rotunda of the University of Virginia.
--- fact 8
Vicenza's historic centre and the Palladian villas of the Veneto are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
--- context
Palladio worked in [[region/europe|Europe]] during the [[event/renaissance|Renaissance]], in the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]]. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
I watch a stonemason become an architect through a patron's gift — a classical education, a new name — and I watch a book carry his manner farther than his buildings ever could. Print detached a way of building from any one place. In England, in colonial America, the style became a mark of status for landed families, a Roman past borrowed to dignify present ownership. His belief in musical ratios as a source of harmony was his own conviction; it does not follow that anyone standing in the room feels what he felt.
--- question 1
How far did the Palladian movement follow his own buildings, and how far his book?

=== person/antoni-gaudi

type: person

mode: essay-self-help

--- summary
Antoni Gaudí (25 June 1852 to 10 June 1926) was the leading architect of Catalan Modernisme, the regional cousin of Art Nouveau. Almost all his mature work stands in Barcelona, and it culminates in the still-unfinished Sagrada Família.
--- fact 1
Gaudí was born in Reus, or possibly the neighbouring Riudoms, Catalonia — sources differ, and from 1915 he himself said Riudoms — the youngest son of a coppersmith; he credited his father's workshop with teaching him to see three-dimensional space, and graduated from the Barcelona School of Architecture in 1878.
--- fact 2
The industrialist Eusebi Güell noticed a display case he designed for a glove-maker at the Paris World's Fair, and became his patron for the rest of his career.
--- fact 3
He never married, grew increasingly devout, and by his sixties lived an almost monastic life devoted to the Sagrada Família.
--- fact 4
On 7 June 1926 he was struck by a tram while walking to church in shabby clothes; mistaken for a beggar, he received delayed care and died on 10 June, and was buried in the crypt of his unfinished basilica.
--- fact 5
He modelled forms on nature, and hung scale models of chains and sandbags, photographed upside down, to find the ideal shape for a compression-only arch; he revived trencadís, mosaic of broken tile, as a cheap weatherproof skin.
--- fact 6
Key works include Casa Vicens (1883 to 1885), Palau Güell (1886 to 1888), Park Güell (1900 to 1914), Casa Batlló (about 1904 to 1906) and Casa Milà (about 1906 to 1912; sources differ, and one gives both as built between 1904 and 1910).
--- fact 7
Modernist critics dismissed him after his death, while Salvador Dalí and the Surrealists became early champions; seven of his works are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the Sagrada Família, funded by ticket sales and donations, has its main towers targeted for completion around the centenary of his death.
--- context
Gaudí worked in Barcelona in [[region/europe|Europe]] in the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]]. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]] and [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
I watch a builder let gravity do the drawing: hanging chains, photographed upside down, turning a law of physics into a tool of design, so the shape of an arch came from the world itself rather than from a pattern book. He gave his last decades to a building he knew he would not finish — a relay passed across generations, paid for by visitors and donors rather than any state treasury. The critics who dismissed him, and the champions who later claimed him, show me how quickly a judgment can reverse.
--- question 1
Which of his design intentions for the Sagrada Família are documented, and which are reconstructions by later builders?
