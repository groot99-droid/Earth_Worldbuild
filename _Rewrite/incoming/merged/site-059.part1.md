--- summary
Technology is the making and use of tools, techniques, and systems built to extend what people can do. It is cumulative by nature: each generation builds on the tools of the last, and the record runs from the first chipped stones, at least 2.6 million years ago, through fire, pottery, farming, metals, writing, and printing, on to engines, electric grids, and digital networks. This hub covers materials, energy, transport, information, food, medicine, and the built environment, and it links the vault's technology notes to the events, places, and peoples through which each technique arose and spread. Many techniques arose independently in more than one region, and others crossed whole continents within a few centuries.
--- observer
The Observer names this the tool-lineage: an inheritance in which each tool makes the next one possible. The lineage branches rather than running in any single line, and it sometimes breaks outright when a technique dies with its makers, yet similar solutions such as farming and writing appear in unconnected branches, which suggests that some steps are shaped by shared needs and materials, and not only by chance. Memory-outsourcing made the lineage far easier to carry across generations, and ancestor-weight can keep a working technique in use long after real alternatives exist. The lineage is now old enough that no single person understands the whole of it anymore. The Observer would revise this reading if apparent independent origins prove to be diffusion after all, or if the loss of techniques turns out to be far more common than the record shows.
--- question 1
Do technologies actually follow a predictable order, or is the whole sequence contingent?
--- question 2
Why did some techniques, such as farming and writing, arise independently in several regions at once, while others, such as wheeled transport, were never used in the Americas before contact?
--- question 3
How much perishable technology — wood, cord, cloth, basketry — is simply missing from the record, and how can its true scale even be estimated?
--- question 4
Where did the pace of change actually shift decisively: with fire, farming, writing, the steam engine, or the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]]?
--- question 5
How were techniques actually carried between cultures — by traders, craftspeople, captives, missionaries, and armies — and which traditional stories, such as papermaking at [[event/battle-of-talas|Battle of Talas]], survive real scrutiny?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Stone tools begin at least 2.6 million years ago in Africa; see [[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]] and [[species/homo-habilis|Homo habilis]], and, later still, the handaxes made by [[species/homo-erectus|Homo erectus]].
- [[event/control-of-fire|Control of Fire]] stands as an early energy technology in its own right. Evidence at Wonderwerk Cave in South Africa dates to about one million years ago, habitual use is well established by about 400,000 years ago, and cooked food may well have supported larger brains.
- [[technology/pottery|Pottery]] appears in East Asia at least 20,000 years ago, well before farming ever did; see [[culture/jomon-culture|Jomon Culture]].
- Farming and herding arose independently in several regions at once, and each brought new techniques with it: see [[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]], [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]], [[event/domestication-of-rice|Domestication of Rice]] in the Yangtze region, [[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]] in Mexico, and the drained wetland gardens of the [[place/kuk-early-agricultural-site|Kuk Early Agricultural Site]] in New Guinea.
- Wheeled vehicles appear around 3500 to 3000 BCE ([[technology/the-wheel|The Wheel]]). Bronze, an alloy of copper and tin, was first widely produced in the Near East around 3300 BCE, and tin itself had to be traded over long distances ([[technology/bronze-metallurgy|Bronze Metallurgy]]). On the steppe, the [[culture/botai-culture|Botai Culture]] managed horses and the [[culture/sintashta-culture|Sintashta Culture]] built the earliest known spoke-wheeled chariots.
- Recording systems arose in several places quite independently. [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]] happened independently at least three or four times, first in Mesopotamia about 3400 to 3200 BCE; the [[technology/alphabet|Alphabet]] (Proto-Sinaitic, about 1800 BCE) gave rise to most alphabets still in use today; and in the Andes knotted cords served as records ([[technology/quipu|Quipu]], with possible early relatives at [[place/caral|Caral]]).
- Iron smelting began on a limited scale in Anatolia in the early 2nd millennium BCE and then spread widely from about 1200 BCE ([[technology/iron-smelting|Iron Smelting]]). Ironworking in Africa, including at Meroe in the [[culture/kingdom-of-kush|Kingdom of Kush]] and among Bantu-speaking peoples ([[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]]), remains a case where independent invention is debated.
- Paper was developed in China somewhere between about the 2nd century BCE and the 2nd century CE ([[technology/paper|Paper]], [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]]). [[technology/woodblock-printing|Woodblock Printing]] produced the Diamond Sutra of 868, the oldest dated printed book, and movable type appeared in China about 1040 and in Korea (metal type) in 1377, well before [[person/johannes-gutenberg|Johannes Gutenberg]]'s [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]] in Mainz about 1440.
- Under the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]] gunpowder became a military technology and the compass came into use for navigation at sea; see [[technology/gunpowder|Gunpowder]] and [[technology/magnetic-compass|Magnetic Compass]]. Su Song built an astronomical clock tower in 1088, and weight-driven mechanical clocks with escapements appeared in European towns about 1300 ([[technology/mechanical-clock|Mechanical Clock]]).
- Ocean and polar travel demanded their own techniques: [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]] settled the remote Pacific in double-hulled canoes using non-instrument navigation ([[event/polynesian-settlement-of-the-pacific|Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]]); the Thule ancestors of the Inuit used dog sleds, skin boats, and toggling harpoons ([[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]]); and the [[culture/norse|Norse]] crossed the North Atlantic, reaching L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland by 1021.
- Large-scale landscape engineering appears in many regions at once: the reservoirs and canals of [[place/angkor|Angkor]] and the [[culture/khmer-empire|Khmer Empire]], the earthworks and enriched soils of [[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]] (see [[place/upano-valley-settlements|Upano Valley Settlements]] and [[place/xingu-garden-cities|Xingu Garden Cities]]), the road system of the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]], roughly 30,000 to 40,000 kilometers long, and the dry-stone walls of [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]].
- Steam power ([[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]]: Newcomen 1712, Watt's separate condenser patented 1769) drove the [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]] and made [[technology/railways|Railways]] possible in turn; the [[technology/telegraph-and-submarine-cables|Telegraph and Submarine Cables]] then carried messages across oceans, and works such as the [[event/suez-canal|Suez Canal]] (1869) and the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] (1891 to 1916) followed close behind.
- The [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]] began with the first commercial power station in 1882. [[technology/nuclear-fission|Nuclear Fission]], discovered in 1938, led on to reactors and weapons alike ([[event/nuclear-age|Nuclear Age]]). [[technology/vaccination|Vaccination]] (1796), [[technology/antibiotics|Antibiotics]] (1928), and the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]] together changed health and food supply.
- The [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]] (ENIAC, 1945) then shrank with the transistor (1947) and the integrated circuit (about 1958). The [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]] brought the internet (1969), the World Wide Web (1991), and smartphones (about 2007), and the [[event/space-age|Space Age]] began with Sputnik in 1957; see [[person/alan-turing|Alan Turing]].
--- extra 2: Through the Eras
- **Paleolithic:** [[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]], [[event/control-of-fire|Control of Fire]], the handaxes of [[species/homo-erectus|Homo erectus]], [[technology/pottery|Pottery]] about 20,000 years ago, open-sea crossings all the way to Sahul at least 50,000 years ago ([[event/peopling-of-sahul|Peopling of Sahul]]), and the wooden hut foundations, leather, and rope preserved at [[place/monte-verde|Monte Verde]].
- **Neolithic:** Farming and herding ([[event/the-agricultural-revolution|The Agricultural Revolution]], [[event/animal-domestication|Animal Domestication]], [[event/domestication-of-rice|Domestication of Rice]], [[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]]), stone building at [[place/jericho|Jericho]], where a wall and tower date to roughly 8000 BCE, pottery-making hunter-gatherers ([[culture/jomon-culture|Jomon Culture]]), and artificial mummification from about 5050 BCE ([[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]]).
- **Bronze Age:** [[technology/the-wheel|The Wheel]], [[technology/bronze-metallurgy|Bronze Metallurgy]], [[event/invention-of-writing|Invention of Writing]], the [[technology/alphabet|Alphabet]], and cities ([[event/the-urban-revolution|The Urban Revolution]]); planned streets, drainage, and standard weights in the [[culture/indus-valley-civilization|Indus Valley Civilization]]; spoke-wheeled chariots ([[culture/sintashta-culture|Sintashta Culture]]); large stone construction at [[place/giza-necropolis|Giza Necropolis]] and platform mounds at [[place/caral|Caral]].
- **Iron Age:** [[technology/iron-smelting|Iron Smelting]], ironworking at Meroe in the [[culture/kingdom-of-kush|Kingdom of Kush]], farming and ironworking carried by the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]], and Pacific voyaging by the [[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]].
- **Classical Antiquity:** [[technology/paper|Paper]] under the [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]]; the standardization of writing, weights, measures, and currency in the [[event/unification-of-china-under-qin|Unification of China under Qin]]; roads, aqueducts, and concrete in the [[culture/romans|Romans]]' empire; [[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]] in India; and, from about 500 BCE, the settlement networks of [[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]] and the [[place/upano-valley-settlements|Upano Valley Settlements]].
- **Medieval Period:** [[technology/woodblock-printing|Woodblock Printing]], [[technology/gunpowder|Gunpowder]], the [[technology/magnetic-compass|Magnetic Compass]], the [[technology/mechanical-clock|Mechanical Clock]], the [[technology/quipu|Quipu]], and the [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]]; water works at [[place/angkor|Angkor]]; stone building at [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]]; Norse and Thule seafaring ([[culture/norse|Norse]], [[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]]); and the Inca royal estate at [[place/machu-picchu|Machu Picchu]].
- **Early Modern Period:** The first commercially successful [[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]] (1712); ocean routes opened by ships armed with cannon ([[event/portuguese-sea-route-to-india|Portuguese Sea Route to India]]); crops and animals crossing the Atlantic ([[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]]); instruments and print in the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]]; and [[technology/vaccination|Vaccination]] in 1796.
- **Industrial Age:** The [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]], [[technology/railways|Railways]] (1825), the [[technology/telegraph-and-submarine-cables|Telegraph and Submarine Cables]] (1844), the [[technology/electrical-power-grid|Electrical Power Grid]] (1882), the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]], the [[event/suez-canal|Suez Canal]], [[technology/antibiotics|Antibiotics]] (1928), and [[technology/nuclear-fission|Nuclear Fission]] (1938). States such as Egypt under [[person/muhammad-ali-of-egypt|Muhammad Ali of Egypt]], Japan in the [[event/meiji-restoration|Meiji Restoration]], and Ethiopia under [[person/menelik-ii|Menelik II]] sought to acquire industrial and military technology.
- **Information Age:** The [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]], the [[event/digital-revolution|Digital Revolution]], the [[event/nuclear-age|Nuclear Age]], the [[event/green-revolution|Green Revolution]], the [[event/space-age|Space Age]] and [[event/moon-landing|Moon Landing]], the [[event/genomic-revolution|Genomic Revolution]] (CRISPR gene editing, 2012), and the [[event/great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]], in which energy and fertilizer use have risen steeply since about 1950.
--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** Pottery, paper, printing, gunpowder, and the compass were all documented first here. Several reached the west along the Silk Roads and through the [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]]; see [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]].
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** The earliest known stone tools come from here ([[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]]), as does much of the debate over African ironworking; the [[event/bantu-expansion|Bantu Expansion]] carried farming and iron right across the continent, and [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]] shows dry-stone building at real scale.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]]:** The [[technology/quipu|Quipu]] recorded numbers on knotted cords, the [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] practiced the earliest known deliberate artificial mummification, and the [[culture/inca-empire|Inca Empire]] built a road system running tens of thousands of kilometers. The wheel was not used for transport in the Americas before contact ([[technology/the-wheel|The Wheel]]).
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]]:** Maize was domesticated here, and cooking it with lime (nixtamalization) makes its nutrients available ([[event/domestication-of-maize|Domestication of Maize]]). [[place/tenochtitlan|Tenochtitlan]] was fed in part by chinampas, and the [[culture/maya|Maya]] developed a full writing system.
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** People crossed open sea to Sahul at least 50,000 years ago ([[event/peopling-of-sahul|Peopling of Sahul]]), cultivated drained wetlands in New Guinea ([[place/kuk-early-agricultural-site|Kuk Early Agricultural Site]]), and later settled the far Pacific ([[culture/polynesians|Polynesians]]).
- **[[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]]:** Horse management at [[culture/botai-culture|Botai Culture]] sites and spoke-wheeled chariots at [[culture/sintashta-culture|Sintashta Culture]] settlements both come from the steppe.
- **[[region/siberia-and-the-arctic|Siberia and the Arctic]]:** [[culture/inuit-and-the-thule-expansion|Inuit and the Thule Expansion]] shows cold-climate technology (dog sleds, skin boats, toggling harpoons), [[culture/yakut-sakha|Yakut (Sakha)]] herders adapted horses and cattle to one of the coldest inhabited regions on Earth, and the [[technology/trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] later crossed the region.
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]] and the [[region/amazon-basin-and-south-american-lowlands|Amazon Basin and South American Lowlands]]:** Both regions show large-scale water and soil engineering, from the reservoirs of [[place/angkor|Angkor]] to the dark earths and earthworks of [[event/amazonian-urbanism-and-dark-earths|Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]].
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- Independent invention versus diffusion stays disputed in many cases. Writing arose independently at least three or four times, but scholars are divided on whether African ironworking was independent of the Near East, and the spread of papermaking and printing across Eurasia can only be traced in part.
- Single-inventor stories usually compress much longer processes than they let on. Movable type was used in China and Korea before Gutenberg, paper fragments predate the traditional date credited to Cai Lun, and the [[technology/steam-engine|Steam Engine]] of Newcomen preceded Watt's improvements.
- The record stays biased toward durable materials. Wood, cord, cloth, and basketry rarely survive at all, Spanish colonial authorities banned or destroyed many quipus ([[technology/quipu|Quipu]]), and earlier stone tools than the Oldowan, such as reports from Lomekwi in Kenya of about 3.3 million years ago, remain under study.
- Whether technologies drive social change, or are themselves shaped by it, stays contested, and this hub does not try to settle it.
- This hub's coverage stays uneven. It has no notes yet on optics, sanitation, aviation, semiconductors, or GPS as technologies in their own right, and few on non-Western techniques such as crucible steel, porcelain, qanats, chinampas, or outrigger canoes.
