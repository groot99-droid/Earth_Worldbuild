=== people--nzinga-of-ndongo-and-matamba--1
src: c6f04ebb9fa5
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Nzinga (about 1583 to 1663) was a ruler of the kingdoms of Ndongo and Matamba in present-day Angola who resisted Portuguese expansion and the slave trade for decades.

In 1622 she negotiated with the Portuguese as an ambassador for her brother. Diplomacy was her first instrument.

She later took power and led the resistance, including an alliance with the Dutch (1641 to 1648).

She used both diplomacy and war, and she took in people escaping from slavery. Sovereignty, for her, included giving refuge.

She is a symbol of resistance in Angola today.

--- description
I stand in the room of Artemisia Gentileschi (1593 to about 1654), Italian Baroque painter, known for powerful heroines painted with Caravaggesque drama, the first woman admitted to Florence's Academy of the Arts of Drawing.

I read that she was born in Rome on 8 July 1593, the eldest child of the painter Orazio Gentileschi, a friend and follower of Caravaggio, that she trained in his workshop, and that she painted Susanna and the Elders by the age of 17.

I read that in 1611 she was raped by Agostino Tassi, a painter working with her father. I read that at the seven-month trial in 1612 she was tortured with thumbscrews to test her testimony. I read that Tassi was convicted and sentenced to exile from Rome, that the exile was never carried out, and that he spent less than a year in prison.

She married the Florentine painter Pierantonio Stiattesi and moved to Florence, where she won the patronage of Grand Duke Cosimo II de' Medici, befriended Galileo Galilei, and in 1616 was admitted to the Accademia delle Arti del Disegno. She learned to read and write in these years. From about 1630 she settled in Naples; about 1638 she travelled to London to join her father at the court of Charles I. She is thought to have died in Naples about 1654, though some have speculated she died in the Naples plague of 1656.

She specialised in strong women from the Bible and ancient history: Judith, Susanna, Jael, Cleopatra and Lucretia. She often lent her own features to saints and allegories.

Who tests the one who tells the truth? I keep reading her heroines, and I keep seeing who they answer.
