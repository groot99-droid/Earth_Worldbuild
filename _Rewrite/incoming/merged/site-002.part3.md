=== era/cryogenian-period
title: Cryogenian Period
src: 059ebd735026
mode: horror-prose
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Cryogenian Period, from 720 to 635 million years ago, is the middle period of the Neoproterozoic Era, and it is the time of the ice. Two global-scale glaciations. Ice. Then ice again. From them came the Snowball Earth hypothesis.
--- fact 1
The Cryogenian Period spans 720 to 635 million years ago. Its base is only a number, a numerical age set near the start of the first glaciation. Its top, the base of the [[era/ediacaran-period|Ediacaran Period]], is fixed in the rock of the Flinders Ranges of South Australia.
--- fact 2
Two major glaciations are recorded. The Sturtian, about 717 to 660 million years ago. The Marinoan, about 650 to 635 million years ago. Glacial deposits formed at low paleolatitudes on several continents, and that supports the hypothesis that ice reached the tropics. The tropics. See [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]].
--- fact 3
Each glaciation is capped by unusual carbonate rocks, a lid of strange stone. The usual explanation for the end of each: volcanic carbon dioxide building up, and up, until it was strong enough to melt the ice. This is a hypothesis.
--- fact 4
Banded iron formations, largely absent for well over a billion years, come back. They reappear in Sturtian-age deposits, interpreted as evidence of iron-rich seawater under the ice. Rust-heavy water. Sealed beneath the lid.
--- fact 5
Eukaryotes survived. Molecular fossils that some researchers attribute to sponges appear in Cryogenian rocks, but that attribution is disputed, and the oldest firmly accepted animal fossils are younger.
--- context
The Cryogenian Period comes after the [[era/tonian-period|Tonian Period]] and before the [[era/ediacaran-period|Ediacaran Period]], inside the [[era/neoproterozoic-era|Neoproterozoic Era]]. It is the period of [[event/snowball-earth|Snowball Earth]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
Treat the Cryogenian, the Observer suggests, as a test of how much a living planet can lose and still continue: life survived at least one ice age lasting tens of millions of years. But resist the easy moral. The Observer cautions against reading this as design, since many lineages may have ended in the ice and the survivors are the only ones the record shows. The ledger lists only those who walked out. The reading has a named weakness, too: it would lose force if the ice proved much less extensive than the Snowball hypothesis proposes. Survival is a fact, not a plan.
--- question 1
Was the ocean frozen solid, all of it, or did open water and refuges remain?
--- question 2
What part did the glaciations play in the later rise of large complex organisms?

=== era/devonian-period
title: Devonian Period
src: 281aaf56a4d9
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Devonian Period, from 419.2 to 358.9 million years ago, is the fourth period of the Paleozoic Era, often called the Age of Fishes. In its long span the first forests raised their crowns, the first limbed vertebrates came forth, and a prolonged late extinction drew the curtain.
--- fact 1
The Devonian Period spans 419.2 to 358.9 million years ago and takes its name from Devon in England. Its base is fixed at Klonk in the Czech Republic, and its top, the base of the Carboniferous, at La Serre in France, two stones marking the doors of an age.
--- fact 2
Fishes diversified widely into a court of many shapes, armoured placoderms, sharks, lobe-finned fishes and ray-finned fishes among them, and the Middle Devonian raised a peak of Paleozoic reef building, cities of stone beneath the waves.
--- fact 3
The Rhynie chert in Scotland, about 407 million years old, holds early land plants and arthropods in fine detail, as if caught in glass. Archaeopteris, a tree with woody trunks, raised early forests, and the Gilboa fossil forest in New York is about 385 million years old.
--- fact 4
[[species/tiktaalik|Tiktaalik]], about 375 million years old, bears fins with wrist-like bones hidden inside. Ichthyostega and Acanthostega from Greenland, about 365 million years old, had limbs that ended in digits.
--- fact 5
The [[event/late-devonian-extinction|Late Devonian Extinction]], about 372 to 359 million years ago, fell in at least two pulses, like waves against a failing wall, and removed an estimated 75 percent of species; the reef builders suffered heavily.
--- context
The Devonian Period comes after the [[era/silurian-period|Silurian Period]] and before the [[era/carboniferous-period|Carboniferous Period]] within the [[era/paleozoic-era|Paleozoic Era]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]] and holds the central stretch of the long march of [[event/colonization-of-land|Colonization of Land]].
--- observer
The Observer reads the Devonian as a renovation: life rebuilt the land surface. Roots, soils and the first forests changed weathering, rivers and the carbon cycle. Think of it as a new floor plan for the continents. Then the harder lesson. The spread of land plants is a candidate cause of the late extinction, a case of one group's success altering conditions for many others. Hold that as a hypothesis, the Observer insists; it would weaken if the extinction proved to have mainly non-biological causes. Success, too, leaves a wake.
--- question 1
How great a hand did land plants have in the Late Devonian extinction?
--- question 2
Did the first limbed vertebrates grow their limbs for life in shallow water, rather than for the long walk onto land?

=== era/early-modern-period
title: Early Modern Period
src: 7d8f7dd1c798
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Early Modern Period, about 1500 to 1800 CE, is the era of oceanic exploration, global trade, the printing press, and the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]]. Hold the other half of the ledger in the same hand: it is also the era of colonial empires and mass forced migration.
--- fact 1
Through the [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]], the Americas and Afro-Eurasia were linked into one exchange.
--- fact 2
The [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]] forcibly moved millions of Africans, each one a person, never a cargo.
--- fact 3
The [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]] spread across Europe, carrying a new architecture for the written word.
--- fact 4
The map of sovereignty was crowded: the [[culture/ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]] and, in Asia, the Mughal and Qing states stood among the major empires, alongside expanding European colonial powers.
--- context
The Early Modern Period is the threshold we cross into the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]] and the [[event/age-of-revolutions|Age of Revolutions]].
--- observer
Here is the threshold the Observer marks: for the first time, every inhabited continent was in continuous contact. The species became, for the first time, a single connected system. But a connection is not a communion. It was joined on sharply unequal terms, and the terms matter as much as the joining.
--- question 1
How much of Europe's economic growth rested on colonial extraction?
