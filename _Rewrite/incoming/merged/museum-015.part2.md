=== people--georges-melies--1
src: 0d496e0d050a
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Georges Méliès (1861 to 1938) was a Paris stage magician who became a filmmaker and invented cinema's first special effects. Over roughly 500 films in under twenty years, including A Trip to the Moon (1902), he turned the new camera from a device for recording reality into a machine for staging the impossible. A tool is only as narrow as the imagination of the person holding it.

He was born in Paris to a prosperous shoe-manufacturing family and wanted to study painting, but his father would not support him as an artist, and he supervised machinery at the family factory. In about 1888 he bought the Théâtre Robert-Houdin, a famous conjuring theatre.

After attending the Lumière brothers' first public film screening in December 1895, he tried to buy a camera from them and was refused. He acquired one from a British inventor and built a studio at Montreuil with a glass-walled stage. A refusal is not the end of a road.

About 1896 he discovered, by accident when his camera jammed mid-shot, that stopping and restarting filming could make objects vanish or transform. This became the "stop trick" behind his trick films. Pay attention to your accidents.

His techniques included stop-motion substitution, multiple exposure, dissolves, painted sets built to force perspective, and hand-tinted colour done frame by frame by a team of women colourists.

American piracy of his films, the rise of narrative studio filmmaking and the disruption of the First World War ruined his business. He was reduced to running a toy stall in a Paris train station.
