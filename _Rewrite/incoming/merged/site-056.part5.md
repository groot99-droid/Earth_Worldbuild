=== theme/art-and-ritual
title: Art and Ritual
src: 5719dfb38818
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Art and ritual name a wide architecture of symbolic and expressive practice: painting, carving, ornament, music and dance, storytelling, monumental building, and ceremony, including the rites held for the dead. You find them in every human society, and they reach back at least to the Paleolithic, evidenced by engraved ochre, beads, and cave paintings tens of thousands of years old. The vault follows this lineage from cave painting and prehistoric monuments, through the temple and court arts of ancient empires and medieval kingdoms, to Renaissance and Mughal building, and onward to objects taken in conquest, such as the Benin Bronzes. It treats art and ritual as evidence of what societies valued, and of how they marked power, belonging, and mortality.
--- observer
I look at art and ritual and find behavior with no obvious use for survival, and yet it swallows enormous time and enormous resources in every culture I study. This is one of the deepest puzzles of the species, and I do not pretend to have solved it. I read three roles, again and again: story-glue that binds strangers together, a status-signal for rulers and for groups, and a shape given to the grief-rite. I have stood before a single monument that did all three at once. What is a monument, if not a species talking to itself across time? I would revise this reading if the cost of art and ritual could be shown to buy some hidden advantage, cooperation, or shared memory the record now keeps from me; then the puzzle would dissolve rather than deepen.
--- question 1
What adaptive role, if any, does art and ritual actually serve?
--- question 2
When did symbolic art begin, and did other human species, such as Neanderthals, make it too?
--- question 3
Did ritual and monumental building come before settled life, as [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] has been read to suggest, or only after it?
--- question 4
How can perishable arts, such as music, dance, textiles, and wood carving, be studied at all where none survive?
--- question 5
How did patronage by courts, temples, and merchant families shape which art actually survives?
--- extra 1: Key Threads
- Symbolic behavior reaches back early. Engraved ochre at Blombos Cave in South Africa dates to about 75,000 to 100,000 years ago; a warty-pig painting in Sulawesi is at least 45,500 years old; and the painted animals of [[place/chauvet-cave|Chauvet Cave]], made about 36,000 years ago, include horses, lions, mammoths, rhinoceroses, and bears. See [[event/emergence-of-symbolic-art|Emergence of Symbolic Art]]. Whether Neanderthals also made symbolic objects remains debated ([[species/homo-neanderthalensis|Homo neanderthalensis]]).
- Ice Age peoples worked in bone, tusk, and clay. Humans hunted the [[species/woolly-mammoth|Woolly Mammoth]] and used its bones and tusks for shelters and art, [[technology/pottery|Pottery]] appears in East Asia at least 20,000 years ago, and the [[culture/jomon-culture|Jomon Culture]] made cord-marked pottery and clay figurines called dogu.
- Oral tradition and performance carry knowledge and identity forward. [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] pass on knowledge through oral traditions, songlines, and ceremony, and Australian rock art includes some of the oldest known paintings; griots perform the epic of [[person/sundiata-keita|Sundiata Keita]] and preserve the history of the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]].
- Monumental building came before cities in some places. [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] has carved pillars up to about 5.5 meters tall, [[place/catalhoyuk|Çatalhöyük]] has painted walls, [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] was raised and rebuilt over several centuries from about 3000 BCE, and [[place/poverty-point|Poverty Point]] in Louisiana was built by hunter-gatherer-fishers, with six concentric earthen ridges and mounds.
- Egypt bound art to kingship and to the afterlife. The [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]] built pyramids and temples and mummified their elite, and tombs such as those of the [[place/giza-necropolis|Giza Necropolis]] show just how many resources were committed. [[person/akhenaten|Akhenaten]]'s reign carries a distinctive, naturalistic style, and many images of [[person/hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]], whose mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari stands as a major monument, were later removed or defaced.
- Craft traditions carried identity across wide areas. The [[culture/minoans|Minoans]] painted vivid frescoes in palaces such as Knossos, the [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] decorated pottery with fine stamped geometric patterns, the [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]] carved colossal heads from basalt more than three meters tall, the [[culture/scythians|Scythians]] made gold work and left textiles and tattoos preserved in frozen tombs at Pazyryk, and Polynesian carvers made the moai of [[place/rapa-nui|Rapa Nui]].
- Classical and Byzantine art served city and empire alike. [[place/athens|Athens]] built the Parthenon between 447 and 432 BCE and produced drama and philosophy ([[culture/ancient-greeks|Ancient Greeks]]); [[place/rome|Rome]] completed the Colosseum in 80 CE; the Apadana reliefs of [[place/persepolis|Persepolis]] show delegations from across the empire bringing gifts; the [[culture/nabataeans|Nabataeans]] cut tombs and temples into the cliffs of Petra; and the [[culture/byzantine-empire|Byzantine Empire]] built the Hagia Sophia under Justinian I.
- Buddhist, Hindu, and court arts flourished across South and East Asia. Gandhara art under the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]] blended Greek and Indian styles and produced some of the earliest images of the Buddha in human form; the [[culture/gupta-empire|Gupta Empire]] is associated with a flowering of the arts, though "Golden Age" is a later label; the [[culture/chola-dynasty|Chola Dynasty]] made celebrated bronzes, including the dancing Shiva, and built the Brihadisvara Temple about 1010; the [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]] marks a high point of Chinese poetry and art; and the [[culture/ming-dynasty|Ming Dynasty]] completed the Forbidden City in 1420.
- Southeast Asian states raised vast religious monuments. [[place/borobudur|Borobudur]] in Java has 2,672 relief panels and originally 504 Buddha statues, [[place/angkor|Angkor]] holds Angkor Wat, the largest religious monument in the world by area, built by the [[culture/khmer-empire|Khmer Empire]], and the [[culture/pagan-kingdom|Pagan Kingdom]] built thousands of temples. [[culture/champa|Champa]] built temples at My Son.
- Islamic and Central Asian courts patronized architecture and learning both. [[culture/al-andalus|Al-Andalus]] produced the Alhambra, built mainly in the 13th and 14th centuries; the [[culture/timurid-empire|Timurid Empire]] and [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] are remembered for architecture and science under [[person/timur|Timur]] and [[person/ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]]; the [[culture/safavid-empire|Safavid Empire]] made Isfahan known for its mosques, squares, and gardens; and the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]] produced the [[place/taj-mahal|Taj Mahal]], built from about 1632 to 1653 by Shah Jahan for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, with about 20,000 workers.
- African states built in stone and cast in bronze. [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]] has dry-stone walls up to about 11 meters high, the [[culture/zagwe-dynasty|Zagwe Dynasty]] carved churches from living rock, the [[culture/kingdom-of-benin|Kingdom of Benin]] organized craft guilds, and the elite graves of the [[culture/kingdom-of-mapungubwe|Kingdom of Mapungubwe]] held gold and imported glass beads. In 1897, British forces removed the Benin Bronzes and sold them to defray the costs of their expedition, during the age of the [[event/scramble-for-africa|Scramble for Africa]].
- The Americas built ceremonial centers at a large scale. [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]] laid out the Pyramids of the Sun and Moon along the Avenue of the Dead, [[place/chichen-itza|Chichen Itza]] has El Castillo, where the equinox sun casts a serpent-like shadow, [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]] built Monks Mound, and the Inca built [[place/machu-picchu|Machu Picchu]] about 1450. The [[culture/marajoara-culture|Marajoara Culture]] made richly decorated ceramics and burial urns, and the [[culture/muisca|Muisca]] held gold rituals.
- The [[event/renaissance|Renaissance]] shows art shaped directly by patronage. Brunelleschi developed linear perspective in Florence through experiments of about 1415 to 1420, and Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, and Raphael worked under the patronage of wealthy families and the Church, with the Medici financing artists through banking.
- Rites for the dead shaped much of the art that survives. The [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] reconstructed bodies with clay, wigs, and mineral pigments; the Egyptians mummified their elite; the dead of Çatalhöyük lay under house floors; and burial goods appear in graves across cultures. See [[observer-note/why-the-species-buries-its-dead|Why the Species Buries Its Dead]].
- Named individual artists appear once art becomes a named profession rather than an anonymous craft, from [[person/murasaki-shikibu|Murasaki Shikibu]]'s court novel and [[person/giotto|Giotto]]'s frescoes through the workshops of the [[event/renaissance|Renaissance]] and the Mughal court ([[person/behzad|Behzad]], [[person/basawan|Basawan]]) to European academies and, by the 19th century, an international gallery and print market. Painting, architecture, design, film, and literature all appear among these named figures ([[person/katsushika-hokusai|Katsushika Hokusai]], [[person/mimar-sinan|Mimar Sinan]], [[person/william-morris|William Morris]], [[person/georges-melies|Georges Méliès]], [[person/jane-austen|Jane Austen]]), and images could travel by mass reproduction, as [[person/utagawa-hiroshige|Utagawa Hiroshige]]'s and [[person/raja-ravi-varma|Raja Ravi Varma]]'s prints did, long before broadcast or digital media.

--- extra 3: Regional Patterns
- **[[region/oceania|Oceania]]:** [[culture/aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australians]] made some of the oldest known paintings, the [[culture/lapita-culture|Lapita Culture]] decorated pottery with stamped patterns, and Polynesian carvers made the moai of [[place/rapa-nui|Rapa Nui]], about 95 percent of the island's monolithic statues carved from the volcanic tuff of a single quarry.
- **[[region/andes|Andes]] and the [[region/amazon-basin-and-south-american-lowlands|Amazon Basin and South American Lowlands]]:** The [[culture/chinchorro-culture|Chinchorro Culture]] reconstructed and preserved its dead, the Inca built in stone at [[place/machu-picchu|Machu Picchu]], the [[culture/muisca|Muisca]] held gold rituals, and the [[culture/marajoara-culture|Marajoara Culture]] made painted and incised ceramics.
- **[[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]]:** Dry-stone building at [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]], rock-hewn churches under the [[culture/zagwe-dynasty|Zagwe Dynasty]], guild-made bronzes in the [[culture/kingdom-of-benin|Kingdom of Benin]], and the oral art of griots in the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]].
- **[[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]] and [[region/central-asian-oases|Central Asian Oases]]:** The [[culture/scythians|Scythians]] made gold ornaments and preserved textiles, and the oases of [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] under the [[culture/timurid-empire|Timurid Empire]] became a center of monumental architecture; Gandhara art under the [[culture/kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]] joined Greek and Indian styles.
- **[[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]] and [[region/north-america|North America]]:** The [[culture/olmecs|Olmecs]] carved colossal heads, [[place/teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]] and [[place/chichen-itza|Chichen Itza]] built pyramids, and Chichen Itza has the largest known Mesoamerican ball court; in North America, [[place/poverty-point|Poverty Point]] and [[place/cahokia|Cahokia]] raised earthworks at large scale, and [[place/chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] built great houses with hundreds of rooms.
- **[[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]]:** [[place/borobudur|Borobudur]], [[place/angkor|Angkor]], the [[culture/pagan-kingdom|Pagan Kingdom]], and [[culture/champa|Champa]] show Buddhist and Hindu art carried by sea and by court patronage.
- **[[region/south-asia|South Asia]] and [[region/east-asia|East Asia]]:** Gandhara, Gupta, and Chola art in the south, and Tang poetry and Ming palace building in the east, show state and temple patronage on a large scale; the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]] added the [[place/taj-mahal|Taj Mahal]].
- **[[region/europe|Europe]], the [[region/mediterranean-basin|Mediterranean Basin]], and the [[region/levant-and-anatolia|Levant and Anatolia]]:** From [[place/chauvet-cave|Chauvet Cave]] and [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] to Athens, Rome, Byzantium, and the Renaissance, art in these regions is tied to city, church, and court.
--- extra 4: Debates and Limits
- The categories of art and ritual are themselves modern, not universal. Many objects in the vault, such as pillars, urns, masks, and statues, were made for religious, political, or social purposes rather than as art in the modern sense, so this hub treats art broadly.
- The meaning and function of prehistoric art are inferred, never known outright. Whether [[place/gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] and [[place/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] were ritual sites is argued from their form and context, the purposes of cave paintings remain debated, and the dates of the oldest art keep being revised, as in the 2024 report of Sulawesi narrative scenes at least 51,200 years old.
- The record itself is biased toward durable materials. Stone, pottery, and metal survive, while music, dance, wood carving, and most textiles rarely do, except in exceptional finds such as the frozen tombs at Pazyryk and in living traditions such as songlines and griot performance.
- Ownership, attribution, and erasure all shape what is known. The Benin Bronzes were removed and sold in 1897, attribution of the Taj Mahal's chief architect remains debated, images of [[person/hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]] were defaced, and colonial-era writers denied that [[place/great-zimbabwe|Great Zimbabwe]] was built by Africans, a claim archaeology has since overturned.
- The vault's coverage stays uneven. It holds 53 named artists (painting, sculpture, architecture, design, film, and literature, added from a compiled source, [[source/art-talk-artist-profiles|Art-Talk (Artist Profiles)]], not yet independently fact-checked), but still has no notes on music, dance, or theater as topics, none on textiles, and almost none on art after 1945; the artists themselves also skew toward Europe and toward painting, since the source selected only public-domain-licensed figures.
