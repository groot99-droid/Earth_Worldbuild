=== composition-vii
title: Composition VII
src: 2008e2860681
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Considered the summit of Kandinsky's pre-war work, this huge canvas swirls with intersecting shapes and colours around a dark central vortex, as a sea churns around a whirlpool's eye. It draws on the themes of resurrection, the Last Judgment and the Garden of Love. He made more than 30 preparatory studies, gathering like clouds before weather, and then painted it in about four days, the storm breaking all at once.

=== composition-viii
title: Composition VIII
src: 1e0787be9657
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Painted at the Bauhaus, it shows the change in Kandinsky's style after his years in revolutionary Russia, as a river that once roared through gorges runs clear and level across a plain. Instead of stormy colour, crisp geometric circles, triangles, grids and lines float on a pale ground in precise balance, like stars fixed in a winter sky. He called circles "the synthesis of the greatest oppositions".

=== several-circles
title: Several Circles
src: c20f0a77e79e
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Translucent coloured circles, large and small, overlap and float on a dark, cosmic ground, like moons drifting through a night that has no floor. They look like planets, cells or musical notes. It is one of Kandinsky's purest and most serene works, still as a pool under starlight, reducing painting to the interplay of a single shape and colour.

=== robie-house
title: Robie House
src: 9af848b71985
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Constraint is where architecture shows its character. This is the most fully resolved of Wright's Prairie School houses, built for a bicycle manufacturer on a narrow urban lot. Look at what it does on that narrow ground: long, low rooflines cantilever dramatically beyond their brick walls with almost no visible support, echoing the flat horizon of the Midwestern prairie, while art-glass windows dissolve the boundary between rooms and garden. The lesson is plain. When you cannot have more ground, you can still reach for the horizon.

=== unity-temple
title: Unity Temple
src: b10f4f7d6ec8
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Every threshold teaches before the room does. This is a Unitarian church built to a tight budget, and Wright's first major public building, cast almost entirely in raw concrete left exposed rather than faced with stone. Worshippers enter through a low, compressed passage before the space opens dramatically upward into a top-lit, cube-shaped sanctuary. Think of the breath you hold in a narrow hallway and the breath you release when a ceiling lifts away: that is the architecture of arrival, a sequence of compression and release Wright reused throughout his career. Humility first; then the light.

=== johnson-wax-headquarters
title: Johnson Wax Headquarters
src: 319c2e6e7377
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Sometimes you have to prove what you can already see. This is an open-plan office hall lit not by conventional windows but by bands of glass tubing between slender, mushroom-capped concrete columns that widen toward the ceiling like tree trunks and leaves, an architecture of the forest brought indoors. Wright had to build a test column and load it with sandbags to prove to sceptical building inspectors that the unusually thin supports would hold. A vision is a claim; the sandbags are the evidence. Carry both.

=== guggenheim-museum
title: Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum
src: 9051b1ca5a58
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Some works take a whole season of a life. This was Wright's last major work, completed the year of his death after sixteen years of design and dispute with the client and the city. Its architecture refuses the usual grammar of the museum: a continuous spiral ramp, rather than a sequence of separate rooms, lets visitors descend past artworks along one unbroken gallery wound around an open, skylit central well. You do not move from room to room here; you move through a single sentence. Continuity is its own kind of argument.

=== glasgow-school-of-art
title: Glasgow School of Art
src: b44e9369acc7
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A first commission can become a life's masterpiece. This is Mackintosh's masterpiece, and at the time it was an almost unknown young architect's first major commission, won through an anonymous competition. Its severe stone facades and soaring, timber-lined library interior fused Scottish baronial tradition with an entirely original Art Nouveau vocabulary: a lineage honoured and a new grammar spoken in the same breath. Then the ledger turned. Two catastrophic fires, in 2014 and 2018, destroyed much of the original library and interior, a loss still being assessed and, in part, rebuilt. What a building teaches can outlast what a fire takes.

=== hill-house
title: Hill House
src: fe1175c78d87
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
An experiment is a covenant with the future, and the future collects. This is a private house for the publisher Walter Blackie, designed down to its furniture, light fittings and stencilled wall decoration as a single unified scheme. Considered Mackintosh's finest domestic building, it is now protected under a giant steel "box" and rain shield while conservators address decades of water damage to its experimental cement render. Think of it as a house wearing a coat over its own skin. What we try boldly, others must later tend with patience.

=== willow-tearooms
title: Willow Tearooms
src: d8ba9079c2ef
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A room can be designed so completely that you stop noticing where design ends. This one was made for the entrepreneur Catherine Cranston down to the cutlery and waitress uniforms, and its "Room de Luxe" salon is lined with silvered high-backed chairs and leaded mirror glass. It is the most complete surviving example of the total interior environments Mackintosh and his wife, the artist Margaret Macdonald, designed together for Cranston's chain of tearooms. That is architecture as communion: two designers, one room, every spoon accounted for. The whole is the argument.

=== high-backed-chair
title: High-backed chair
src: d5664efc92fa
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
A chair is never only a chair. This is an elongated ladder-back chair whose towering rear rail was designed as much to define and divide a room visually as to be sat in comfortably. Think of it as a small wall you can sit in: furniture doing the work of architecture. Variants of this silhouette became Mackintosh's most widely recognised design and one of the signature furniture forms of the Glasgow Style. What shapes a room can also shape a name.
