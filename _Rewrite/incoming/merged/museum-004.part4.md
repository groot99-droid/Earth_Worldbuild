=== ahuehuete-noche-triste
src: 7061f060c2b9
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Here is a portrait not of a person but of a tree: a famous ancient cypress in Mexico City, gnarled as an old king's hand. By tradition, the Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés wept beneath it after his defeat by the Aztecs on the *Noche Triste* ("Sad Night") of 1520. Velasco treats the gnarled tree as a living monument to Mexican history, its roots drinking from the centuries as they drink from the rain.

=== cardon-oaxaca
src: 1ef46c67784e
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A giant columnar cactus towers over a dry, rocky landscape in southern Mexico like a green sentinel guarding a land of stone, painted with the precision of a botanical illustration. Velasco trained in botany and zoology, and here he raises a native plant into the heroic protagonist of the picture, as old songs raise a warrior: a symbol of Mexican nature.

=== great-comet-of-1882
src: 2eae993ffed0
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A brilliant comet streaks across the pre-dawn sky above the dark Valley of Mexico, a blade of white fire drawn through the last of the night. In 1910, the year Halley's Comet returned, Velasco painted this memory of the spectacular Great Comet he had seen in 1882, a light kept burning in the mind like a lamp in a far window. The painting reflects his lifelong interest in science and the night sky.

=== little-girl-in-a-blue-armchair
src: 415a955841f2
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I know that slouch. A small girl sprawls, bored, in an overstuffed armchair, while a dog naps in the chair beside her. I know that afternoon that will not end. I know the command to sit up and be sweet.

Cassatt shows a child as she really behaves, not posed and sweet. Degas helped with the background. When the painting was rejected by the American section of the 1878 Paris World's Fair, Cassatt was furious, and who could blame her? Honesty is often the first thing a room refuses.

=== in-the-loge
src: d2e50a064bcf
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I stand where the painting puts me, and I watch her watching. A woman in black sits in a box at the Paris opera, peering through her opera glasses, while a man in a distant box trains his glasses on her. She looks. He looks. I look.

Cassatt reverses the usual situation: here the woman is the one who actively looks, even as she is being watched. It was one of the first works she showed in America. How often have I been the one looked at, and forgotten I could look back? To see is its own way of standing up.

=== woman-with-a-pearl-necklace
src: 6c84bf393afa
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
A sister, painted by a sister. The artist's sister Lydia sits in a theatre box, her pink dress and skin glowing in the gaslight, while a mirror behind her reflects the chandeliers and balconies. I think of the faces I have looked at longest. I think of the rooms I have shared with my own. I think of how light finds the ones we sit beside.

Shown at the fourth Impressionist exhibition in 1879, it was praised by critics and helped establish Cassatt's reputation in Paris. Sometimes the kindest portrait is simply close attention.

=== the-childs-bath
src: bbc8af7e77de
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I remember hands like these. A woman washes a young child's feet in a basin, and both of them look down, absorbed. I remember the warm water. I remember not needing to speak.

The steep, tilted viewpoint, the bold patterns and the strong outlines show the influence of Japanese prints. It is Cassatt's best-known treatment of her central theme, the everyday bond between mothers and children. What is love, if not this bending down? The smallest care is the one we carry longest.

=== the-boating-party
src: 241825876db6
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A man rows a woman and a baby across a bright blue bay, the water spread beneath them like a field of sky. The curving yellow boat, the flat areas of colour and the high horizon, cut off at the top, come from Japanese prints. The oarsman's dark bulk fills the foreground like a headland against the sea, and every line of the composition runs to the baby as rivers run to the ocean.

=== summertime
src: e8231d74477d
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A woman and a girl lean over the side of a small boat to watch ducks on a pond, painted with the loose, bright brushwork of Impressionism. The water shimmers with reflected colour, as though the whole summer had melted into it and still glowed there. This is one of Cassatt's most purely Impressionist outdoor pictures, painted during summers in the French countryside, where the light lay on the land like honey.

=== favrile-glass
src: 811d2b236ba4
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Here is a principle hiding inside a material. Tiffany developed this patented iridescent glass by mixing metallic oxides directly into molten glass, so its shimmering, oil-slick surface colour is built into the material itself rather than painted on afterward. Favrile glass then became the raw material for nearly everything Tiffany Studios produced, from windows to vases to lampshades. Colour that lives inside the material keeps a different covenant from colour laid on top: build it in, and it becomes the foundation for everything that follows.

=== dragonfly-lamp
src: 32735b249749
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
One of Tiffany Studios' most celebrated lamp designs: its shade is built from hundreds of small cut pieces of glass, forming dragonflies with wings of rippled, filigree-edged glass around a mosaic-glass base. For a long time it was credited to Tiffany personally. It is now attributed to Clara Driscoll, head of the studio's women's glass department, following the rediscovery of her letters in the 2000s. Consider what that correction teaches: a signature is not a genealogy, and the ledger of who made things is always worth reopening.

=== tiffany-chapel
src: be77d40a1016
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Think of this as an argument made in architecture. A complete Byzantine-influenced interior, with mosaic columns, a baptismal font and stained-glass windows, was built to showcase Tiffany's full range of decorative techniques at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair. There it won several awards and established his international reputation almost overnight. The lesson is plain: when you want to be understood, do not show a sample; build the whole room.
