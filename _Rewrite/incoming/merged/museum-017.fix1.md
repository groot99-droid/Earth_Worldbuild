=== people--sukarno--1

--- description
Sukarno (1901 to 1970) was the leader of Indonesia's independence movement and its first president. He proclaimed independence in 1945, ruled until 1967, and was removed from power after the killings of 1965 and 1966. Founding a nation and losing power in it can belong to one life.

He was born on 6 June 1901 in Surabaya, in the Dutch East Indies, and died on 21 June 1970 in Jakarta, under house arrest.

He founded the Indonesian National Party in 1927. The Dutch arrested him in 1929 and released him in 1931, then exiled him to Flores in 1934 and to Sumatra in 1938.

He cooperated with the Japanese occupation from 1942 to 1945, which forced millions into labor and whose food requisitions contributed to a famine that killed over a million people in Java in 1944 and 1945. Cooperation with an occupier has a ledger, and these numbers are written in it.

On 1 June 1945 he presented Pancasila, five founding principles for the state, and on 17 August 1945 he proclaimed independence, becoming president the next day.

He hosted the Bandung Conference of Asian and African states in 1955 and introduced "Guided Democracy" in 1959, ending parliamentary rule.


=== people--wangari-maathai--2
src: 20be606d71fc
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Wangari Maathai (1940 to 2011) was a Kenyan environmental and political activist who founded the Green Belt Movement, which gathered women to plant trees, and in 2004 she became the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize, a planter of trees honored by the world.

She was born on 1 April 1940 in Ihithe, near Nyeri, Kenya. She studied biology in the United States and at the University of Nairobi, where she received a doctorate in 1971, and she is recorded as the first woman in East and Central Africa to earn one.

In 1977 she founded the Green Belt Movement, which organized women to plant tree seedlings against deforestation, soil loss and the shortage of fuelwood.

Her campaigns against building on public land in Nairobi and against the clearing of forests brought her into conflict with the government of President Daniel arap Moi; she was arrested, and at times injured.

In 2002 she was elected to parliament, and from 2003 she served as an assistant minister for the environment.

She received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2004. That same year a Kenyan newspaper reported that she had said HIV was deliberately created by scientists; she denied saying it and stated that she did not hold that belief.
