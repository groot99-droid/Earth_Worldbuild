=== culture/ghana-empire
title: Ghana Empire
src: 84c6bd9a1a00
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Ghana Empire, called Wagadu by its own people, was a Soninke state in the western Sahel whose wealth rested on the trans-Saharan trade in gold, salt and ivory, carried over the sands like light over dark water. Its exact extent, its end, and the role of outside conquest are all debated.
--- fact 1
The empire lay in what is now southeastern Mauritania, western Mali and eastern Senegal. Most scholars accept that its roots reach back to the 8th or 9th century.
--- fact 2
The Soninke people were its foundation stone. Its wealth was bound to the growth of regular trans-Saharan trade in gold, salt and ivory, a stream of riches that fed larger towns and pushed the realm outward to control the trade routes.
--- fact 3
The capital is believed to have stood at Koumbi Saleh, on the edge of the Sahara, where the sand meets the living land. The description left by al-Bakri in 1067 or 1068 says the capital was two towns joined by continuous habitations, twin lamps linked by a string of lights.
--- fact 4
Some accounts say the capital was sacked about 1076 by the Almoravids of North Africa, perhaps in retaliation for Ghana's attempt to control trade held by Saharan commercial centres.
--- fact 5
In the 12th century the empire waned like a moon, as competing trade routes opened further east and a prolonged dry spell withered its agriculture. Tradition says the city was destroyed by Sundiata in 1240, and what remained was absorbed into the Mali Empire.
--- context
Ghana was the first of the large Sahelian states, the eldest in a line that the [[culture/mali-empire|Mali Empire]] and later the [[culture/songhai-empire|Songhai Empire]] would follow. Its tale is woven with the spread of Islam through the Sahara, a thread that leads back to the [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]].
--- observer
We imagine empires falling to a single blow: a sacked city, a date in a chronicle. The Observer notes something slower, an exchange-web whose wealth was carried on paths and rainfall, and which, on these accounts, came apart through shifting routes and drought more than through one decisive strike. Think of the architecture beneath a kingdom: roads, rains, the seasons of the harvest. A network that looks like a state is only as fixed as the roads and climate under it.
--- question 1
Did the Almoravids truly conquer Ghana, or is that a later simplification of a slower decline?
--- question 2
How much of what we know about the capital comes from outside observers, and how much from excavation?

=== culture/gupta-empire
title: Gupta Empire
src: dbb0f3c610cd
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Gupta Empire ruled much of northern India from about 320 to 550 CE and is often associated with a flowering of mathematics, astronomy and the arts, a garden of the mind in full bloom.
--- fact 1
The astronomer and mathematician Aryabhata (born 476) worked in this age and gave an accurate value of pi, measuring the circle as if he had walked its rim.
--- fact 2
In India the decimal place-value system was developed with zero as a digit, an empty vessel that holds every number; its tale is told in [[technology/zero-and-place-value-notation|Zero and Place-Value Notation]].
--- fact 3
Nalanda, a major Buddhist center of learning, flourished like a lamp-lit hall in the night.
--- fact 4
'Golden Age' is a later label, a crown set on the era's head long afterward, and historians debate how far it fits.
--- context
The Gupta period belongs to [[region/south-asia|South Asia]] in the age of [[era/classical-antiquity|Classical Antiquity]]. Its mathematics travelled westward like a river finding the sea, through scholars such as [[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]].
--- observer
I think of an idea born in one place and carried far. I think of a small mark, a symbol for 'nothing', passing from hand to hand. I think of how it changed how numbers work across Eurasia. How can nothing weigh so much? The smallest sign can move the widest world.
--- question 1
How much of 'Gupta science' belonged to the empire itself, and how much to the wider Indian tradition?

=== culture/gokturk-khaganate
title: Göktürk Khaganate
src: fabe48890e96
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Göktürk Khaganate was a Turkic steppe empire, founded about 552 CE by Bumin Khagan, that spread across Inner Asia like wind over grass and left behind the earliest known Turkic writing.
--- fact 1
The First Turkic Khaganate endured from 552 to 603; a Second Khaganate rose in its turn from about 682 to 744.
--- fact 2
The Orkhon inscriptions, raised between 732 and 735 in Mongolia to honor Kul Tigin and his brother Bilge Khagan, are the oldest known Turkic writing, voices set in stone to outlast the wind.
--- fact 3
They are written in a runic-like Old Turkic script, and they kept their silence until they were discovered in 1889.
--- fact 4
Sogdian served as a court language for writing documents, a borrowed pen in the khagans' hall.
--- context
The Göktürks walked in the [[culture/xiongnu|Xiongnu]] tradition of steppe empire and dealt with the [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]] and the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]]. In later ages Turkic peoples spread across Asia and Anatolia, like seeds scattered on a long wind.
--- observer
Imagine being written about for a long time and never getting to answer. The Observer notes a people who carved their history in stone to speak to the future, and in their own language, after long being described by others. That is sovereignty over one's own story. The inscriptions are a self-portrait of a steppe empire. To write yourself down is to take back the pen.
--- question 1
How were the two Khaganates related to each other?

=== culture/han-dynasty
title: Han Dynasty
src: 076ef26b4fab
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Han dynasty ruled China from 206 BCE to 220 CE and set firm the imperial system begun under Qin, so that 'Han' is still the name of the majority ethnic group, a dynasty's name worn by a people.
--- fact 1
It pushed westward and opened contact with Central Asia, a door swung wide onto far lands; the tale runs on in [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]].
--- fact 2
A census in 2 CE recorded about 58 million people, a sea of households under one throne.
--- fact 3
Confucian learning became the bedrock of state education.
--- fact 4
Under the Han, paper and other inventions came into being, quiet gifts to later ages.
--- context
The Han built upon the [[event/unification-of-china-under-qin|Unification of China under Qin]] and upon the ideas of [[person/confucius|Confucius]], as a tower rises on older stone. The tale of [[technology/paper|Paper]] runs beside theirs.
--- observer
Think about how an institution becomes a self. The Observer notes that the dynasty gave a name and a canon to a whole cultural sphere. Habits of administration, repeated long enough, stop feeling like policy and start feeling like who you are. A state that lasts four centuries turns its administrative habits into identity.
--- question 1
Set beside Rome, how large was the Han state?
