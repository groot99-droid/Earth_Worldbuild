=== people--nezahualcoyotl--1
src: 99a0d5cefd24
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Nezahualcoyotl (1402 to 1472) was the tlatoani, or ruler, of the Acolhua city-state of Texcoco in the Valley of Mexico, and a founder of the Triple Alliance with Tenochtitlan and Tlacopan. He is remembered as lawgiver, builder and poet, though much of what is said of him comes from later writers.

He was born in 1402 in Texcoco, son of the ruler Ixtlilxochitl I and a Mexica princess. When the Tepanec state of Azcapotzalco conquered Texcoco and killed his father, he lived in exile.

With Tenochtitlan and Tlacopan he fought Azcapotzalco, and its defeat, traditionally dated about 1428, forged the Triple Alliance. He ruled Texcoco from about 1431 until 1472, when his son Nezahualpilli followed him.

Later sources credit him with a law code and public works, among them a dike guarding Tenochtitlan from the floodwaters and the gardens at Tetzcotzingo.

About 34 poems are attributed to him in sixteenth-century manuscripts. Whether he composed them is debated, since "his song" can mean a song about him, an echo that may belong to the singer or the sung.

Accounts that he opposed human sacrifice and favored a single creator deity come largely from his descendant Fernando de Alva Ixtlilxochitl, writing in the early seventeenth century, and are treated with caution.

=== people--pachacuti--1
src: 8b2d60832d1d
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Pachacuti (about 1418 to 1471) was the ninth Sapa Inca, under whom the Inca state grew from a regional power into an empire.

He is credited with rebuilding Cusco and expanding Inca rule along the Andes.

Machu Picchu is believed to have been built as one of his royal estates.

He organized the state through labor obligations and roads, the architecture that holds an empire together.

Sources on him were written after the Spanish conquest and blend history and legend, so read them as both.
