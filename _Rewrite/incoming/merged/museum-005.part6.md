=== strike
title: Strike
src: a3584fd525e9
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Some films make their argument with a cut. This was Eisenstein's first feature, a dramatisation of a factory strike brutally crushed by police and company spies in Tsarist Russia, made with a cast largely of non-professional workers chosen for their faces and bearing rather than acting experience. Its climactic massacre is intercut with shots of cattle being slaughtered: an early, blunt demonstration of his theory that colliding images could generate meaning neither contained alone. Notice the grammar at work, two images set side by side, saying together what neither says alone. But a theory of montage is never only a theory; behind it stand the people whose deaths it depicts. The cut makes the argument. The dead keep their weight.

=== battleship-potemkin
title: Battleship Potemkin
src: 013e9fbb0e6b
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Anniversaries are a kind of architecture: we build them to hold memory. This film was made to mark the twentieth anniversary of the failed 1905 Revolution, and it dramatises a real mutiny aboard a Black Sea battleship. Its centrepiece is the Odessa Steps sequence, in which Tsarist soldiers massacre civilians on a monumental staircase, a runaway pram bouncing down the steps among them. That passage is among the most studied, quoted and parodied in film history. Here is the hard thing to hold: a scene of civilians killed has become a lesson, a quotation, even a joke. Study it, then, but do not let the quotation outlive the grief.

=== october
title: October (Ten Days That Shook the World)
src: 491bc8c5ba18
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
It was commissioned to mark the tenth anniversary of the October Revolution, and it raised the storming of the Winter Palace again with thousands of extras, many of whom had taken part in the actual events a decade earlier, as a river returns to an old channel. Eisenstein used the film to push "intellectual montage" furthest, cutting to unrelated images, a mechanical peacock, religious icons, purely for ironic or conceptual effect, like strange lanterns hung among the scenes and lit for irony rather than for light.

=== alexander-nevsky
title: Alexander Nevsky
src: 93f446776ffd
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
This was Eisenstein's return to official favour after years of suppressed and abandoned projects, as a river long locked under ice breaks into the open. It is a patriotic historical epic about the 13th-century prince who defeated invading Teutonic knights on a frozen lake, and it was released as tensions with Nazi Germany were rising, like a storm-front gathering. Its "Battle on the Ice" sequence, scored by Sergei Prokofiev, choreographs mass combat with the same rhythmic montage Eisenstein had developed for crowds in his silent films, ranks moving like tides to a drum.

=== ivan-the-terrible
title: Ivan the Terrible
src: 9f26e048ddbc
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
It is a stylised, operatic two-part biography of the 16th-century tsar, shot with sharply angled lighting that cuts its shadows like blades, and stark, mask-like performances closer to Kabuki theatre than naturalistic acting. Part I won Eisenstein a Stalin Prize. But Stalin personally banned Part II for what he read as a veiled critique of his own purges, and the film lay sealed like a letter no one was permitted to open: it was not released until 1958, a decade after Eisenstein's death. A planned Part III was never completed, a road that ends in snow.

=== self-portrait-1930
title: Self-Portrait (Self-Portrait 7)
src: b8e2cf7206f0
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I stand before a young woman painted in Paris when she was 17, one of some 19 self-portraits she made in Europe. She leans toward me, hair loose and shoulders bare, confident and playful. I remember that age. I remember leaning toward the world like that. I remember not yet being afraid to be looked at. The painting shows how quickly she absorbed the lessons of Post-Impressionism and the European tradition of self-portraiture. How quickly did I learn to look back? Some of us learn our faces by painting them.

=== three-girls
title: Three Girls (Group of Young Girls)
src: f7b7bc6bebaf
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I see three young Indian women sit close together, silent and withdrawn. I see their faces, sombre. I see their clothes glowing in deep reds, greens and ochres, brighter than anything they say. It was painted soon after her return to India, and it expresses the waiting and melancholy she saw in the lives of Indian women. How long have I waited in a room, dressed in colour, saying nothing? It won the gold medal of the Bombay Art Society in 1937. The brightest cloth cannot speak for the one who wears it.

=== brides-toilet
title: Bride's Toilet
src: 39971a4bd9e9
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
Women prepare a young bride for her wedding. I watch one comb her hair. I watch another bring a tray. I watch a child sit nearby. And I watch the bride, whose expression is quiet and uncertain. Was I ever that uncertain at a threshold, with every hand around me busy? It is part of her "South Indian trilogy", painted after her visit to the Ajanta caves, and it combines the flat colour and flowing lines of ancient Indian murals with a modern, psychological intensity. Other hands can dress us; only we can wear the day.

=== brahmacharis
title: Brahmacharis
src: 1008b0bb98c2
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A group of young Brahmin students, bare-chested with sacred threads, sit on the ground in solemn contemplation, still as stones in a riverbed. Dark skin tones set against white cloth, like earth against first snow, and monumental, simplified figures show the influence of the Ajanta frescoes, as a far spring still flavours the river far downstream. It is another painting from the South Indian trilogy.

=== village-scene
title: Village Scene
src: 390f5e7cf68f
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Women sit talking beside a long, whitewashed house with red-barred windows, while a lone figure wrapped in a yellow shawl stands in the dusky courtyard near a ladder, like a lamp not yet lit. The flat, glowing earth colours and still, simplified figures recall the Indian miniature painting that Sher-Gil studied closely after 1937, as a song recalls the older song it was learned from. When it came up for auction in 2006, it sold for $1.6 million, then one of the highest prices ever paid for an Indian painting.

=== haldi-grinders
title: Haldi Grinders
src: 88ec1280819e
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Women in red and orange grind turmeric (*haldi*) under the shade of trees in a village courtyard, their colours glowing like embers in a cool hearth of green. It was painted at Saraya in Uttar Pradesh, where she lived in her last years, and it carries the decorative patterning and bright, flat colour of Indian miniatures, laid now upon the everyday labour of rural women, as the same sun falls on every field.
