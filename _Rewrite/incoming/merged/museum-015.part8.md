=== people--wassily-kandinsky--1
src: 7ca341b0d314
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Wassily Kandinsky (16 December 1866 to 13 December 1944) was a Russian painter and theorist, one of the first in Europe to make purely abstract works. He believed that colours and forms, set free from depicting objects, could move the viewer as directly as music moves the air.

He was born in Moscow and grew up in Odessa. He studied law and economics and began an academic career. In 1896, having seen one of Monet's Haystacks and heard Wagner's Lohengrin, he left the law behind and went to Munich to study painting.

In 1911 he and Franz Marc founded Der Blaue Reiter (The Blue Rider), and he published On the Spiritual in Art. About then he painted his first fully abstract works.

The First World War drove him back to Russia, where he took part in Soviet cultural institutions after the 1917 Revolution. He returned to Germany in 1920 or 1921 (sources differ) and taught at the Bauhaus from 1922 to 1933.

When the Nazis closed the Bauhaus he moved to Neuilly-sur-Seine near Paris, became a French citizen in 1939, and died there.

He sorted his works into Impressions, Improvisations and Compositions, and made only ten Compositions over 30 years. He probably experienced synaesthesia and gave colours musical voices: yellow like a trumpet, blue like a deep cello.
