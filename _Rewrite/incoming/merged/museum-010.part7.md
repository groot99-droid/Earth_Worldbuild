--- description
I stand in the room of Sofonisba Anguissola (about 1532 to 1629), Italian portrait painter from Cremona, one of the first women in Europe to build a professional career as a painter, who spent years at the Spanish court, traditionally put at about 14, and won international fame.

I read that she was born about 1532 in Cremona, the eldest of seven children of Amilcare Anguissola, a minor nobleman, who gave all his daughters a humanist education, and that several became painters. I read that she studied with Bernardino Campi and Bernardino Gatti. I read that as a noblewoman she could not study anatomy from the nude or take large public commissions, so she made portraiture her field.

Her father sent her drawings to Michelangelo, who reportedly challenged her to draw a crying boy; she answered with Boy Bitten by a Crayfish, a drawing that circulated widely. In 1559 she was invited to Madrid to serve Queen Elisabeth of Valois, wife of Philip II, as lady-in-waiting and painting teacher. She painted the royal court for a period traditionally given as about 14 years, though sources differ, and the Spanish court arranged her marriage to a Sicilian nobleman, Fabrizio de Moncada.

After his death she married a Genoese sea captain and lived in Genoa and Palermo. In 1629 Anthony van Dyck visited her in Palermo (an earlier reading gave 1624) and recorded her advice. She died there in 1629 (older sources say 1625), in her mid-to-late nineties.

Doors closed, and she painted the faces in front of her. What wall have I called the end of the road? She made it the subject.
