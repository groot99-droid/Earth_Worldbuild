=== ejiri-in-suruga
src: 8276bf410127
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A sudden gust sweeps across a marshy road, and the world bows before it. Travellers bend double and clutch their hats, and a stream of papers flies from a woman's kimono into the sky like a flight of startled white birds. Fuji is reduced to a simple white outline in the background, the ghost of a mountain. Hokusai captures an invisible force, wind, through its effects alone, as one knows a spirit by the doors it opens. Photographer Jeff Wall restaged the scene in 1993.

=== kirifuri-waterfall
src: acfccee6fcd1
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
It comes from the series *A Tour of the Waterfalls of the Provinces*. Here the falls pour down the rock face in branching, finger-like streams of blue and white, like the hand of some vast creature reaching down the mountain, while tiny pilgrims gaze up in wonder. Hokusai treats falling water as a living, almost animal form. The series shows his lifelong fascination with water in every state, a fascination that ran through his years like a river through a valley.

=== hannibal
src: 959b6b570055
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A vast black vortex of storm cloud swirls over Hannibal's army like a great eye opening in the sky, and below it his troops are almost lost in the chaos, small as leaves in a flood. Turner is said to have based the sky on a thunderstorm he watched over Yorkshire. He painted it as Napoleon's armies marched on Russia, and it carries a warning about the fate of empires, which gather like storms and scatter like them. Its whirling composition anticipates his later work.

=== burning-of-parliament
src: 6cad0ef11a64
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Turner watched from a boat on the Thames as fire destroyed the old Palace of Westminster, and he sketched furiously, as if his hand could race the blaze. In this version, flames and smoke pour upward into the night sky like a river flowing the wrong way, and the river beneath answers with their mirrored glow, while crowds watch from Westminster Bridge. Fire and water, light and darkness: these became his great subjects, the old quarrel of the elements made into paint.

=== fighting-temeraire
src: f812743e7624
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
The *Temeraire*, a veteran warship from the Battle of Trafalgar, is towed up the Thames by a small, smoke-belching steam tug, bound to be broken up for scrap. The sailing ship glides pale and ghostly against a blazing sunset, as an old king might walk to his grave in borrowed light. It is an elegy for the age of sail giving way to steam, one tide ebbing as another floods in. Turner called it his "darling" and refused to sell it. In 2005 it was voted Britain's greatest painting, and it appears on the Bank of England's £20 note.

=== slave-ship
src: 4838994ad0f4
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Some paintings ask you to look at what a ledger tried to hide. A ship sails into a blood-red storm, and in the churning sea below, shackled bodies sink, attacked by fish and gulls. These were people. Turner was inspired by the *Zong* massacre of 1781, when enslaved Africans were thrown overboard so the owners could claim insurance. Say it plainly, because the history demands plainness: people were killed for an insurance claim. He showed the painting during the 1840 campaign to end slavery worldwide. John Ruskin owned it and called it the noblest sea Turner ever painted. Admire the sea if you must; never let it outweigh the people in it.

=== snow-storm-steam-boat
src: ee224ff60af1
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
At the centre of a vortex of snow, spray and smoke, a steamboat is barely visible, a spark swallowed by a whirlpool of white. Turner claimed he had himself tied to a ship's mast for four hours during the storm in order to experience it. The story is probably invented, a legend wound about the painting like rope about a mast, but the painting's dizzying energy seems to make it believable. One critic dismissed it as "soapsuds and whitewash".
