=== people--jose-maria-velasco--2
src: 14fee25fa47e
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
José María Velasco (6 July 1840 to 26 August 1912) was the most important Mexican painter of the 19th century, known for panoramic views of the Valley of Mexico. He was also a trained naturalist who illustrated scientific publications on botany, zoology and geology. Art and science were not two careers for him; they were one discipline of looking.

He was born in Temascalcingo, in the State of Mexico, and his family moved to Mexico City in 1849. In 1858 he entered the Academy of San Carlos, where the Italian painter Eugenio Landesio taught him to observe nature closely and paint outdoors. He also studied botany, zoology, anatomy, physics and mathematics.

In 1877 he succeeded Landesio as professor of landscape painting. Among his students was Diego Rivera.

He worked as a scientific illustrator for the National Museum and on expeditions, and painted archaeological sites such as Teotihuacan.

He won a medal at the Paris Universal Exposition of 1889 and exhibited at the 1893 Chicago World's Columbian Exposition. One view of the Valley of Mexico was given to Pope Leo XIII and is now in the Vatican Museums.

He worked from outdoor sketches to large studio compositions. His panoramas set a detailed foreground of cacti and rocks against a luminous distance, and often include railways, bridges and haciendas alongside nature and ruins. Old and new share one frame: that is his grammar of a country.

=== people--yaa-asantewaa--1
src: 959f03f00791
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Yaa Asantewaa (about 1840 to 1921) was the queen mother of Ejisu in the Asante kingdom, in present-day Ghana, who led an Asante uprising against British rule in 1900. She died in exile in the Seychelles, far across the sea.

She was born about 1840 and was queen mother (ohemaa) of Ejisu, a title held by a woman who advises and helps choose the ruler.

The British exiled the Asante king Prempeh I in 1896. In March 1900 the governor, Frederick Hodgson, demanded the Golden Stool, the Asante symbol of national unity, so that he could sit on it.

According to tradition she then called on the Asante chiefs at a council to resist, and she became a leader of the uprising sometimes called the War of the Golden Stool.

Asante forces besieged the British fort at Kumasi for several months in 1900. British reinforcements relieved it, and the fighting ended that year with a British victory.

She was captured and exiled to the Seychelles in 1901, and she died there on 17 October 1921.

=== people--wilfrid-laurier--1
src: 48c03ffa2491
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Wilfrid Laurier (1841 to 1919) was the first French-Canadian Prime Minister of Canada, serving from 1896 to 1911 during a period of rapid western settlement and economic growth. His career is a study in the work of balancing.

A lawyer from Quebec, he became Liberal Party leader in 1887 and prime minister in 1896, holding the office for over fifteen years, then the longest unbroken term in Canadian history.

His government promoted immigration and settlement of the Canadian prairies, a policy associated with his minister Clifford Sifton, which brought large numbers of European immigrant farmers to Western Canada.

He worked to balance relations between English- and French-speaking Canada, though his support for Canadian participation in Britain's Boer War strained relations with Quebec nationalists who opposed it. Every balance tips somewhere.

He lost the 1911 election over a proposed reciprocity (free trade) agreement with the United States, which opponents attacked as a threat to Canada's ties with Britain.
