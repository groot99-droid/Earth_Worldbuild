=== technology/woodblock-printing
title: Woodblock Printing
src: 9b6ffa7ba5d2
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Woodblock printing, the architecture of carving a whole page into a block of wood, inking it, and pressing it onto paper, took shape in East Asia by the 8th century CE.
--- fact 1
The Diamond Sutra, dated 868, is the oldest dated printed book known.
--- fact 2
Early printing served largely to reproduce Buddhist texts and calendars.
--- fact 3
The technique spread onward to Korea, Japan, and Vietnam.
--- fact 4
Bi Sheng invented movable type in China around 1040.
--- context
Woodblock printing depended on [[technology/paper|Paper]], developed under the [[culture/tang-dynasty|Tang Dynasty]], and came well before the [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]] of Europe.
--- observer
I notice printing rose in more than one place, and rose first inside a religious setting. I think of hands copying scripture for merit, not wages, and understand: that hunger for merit alone could drive copying at scale. What devotion repeats itself, if repetition counts as prayer?
--- question 1
How far did printing reshape literacy across East Asia?

=== technology/x-ray-imaging
title: X-ray Imaging
src: f7608740c03a
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
X-rays are penetrating electromagnetic radiation, identified in 1895 by Wilhelm Conrad Roentgen. Within months, they let physicians see bones and foreign objects inside a living body without any surgery at all.
--- fact 1
On 8 November 1895, working with cathode-ray tubes at the University of Wurzburg, Roentgen saw a glow appear on a fluorescent screen.
--- fact 2
He named them X-rays because their nature was unknown, and published his findings in December 1895 with an image of his wife's hand.
--- fact 3
Photographs began circulating from January 1896, and medical use followed within weeks across Europe and North America.
--- fact 4
Early operators suffered skin burns and other injuries before anyone understood the hazards.
--- fact 5
Henri Becquerel reported radioactivity in uranium salts in 1896, following a lead opened by the new rays.
--- fact 6
Roentgen received the first Nobel Prize in Physics in 1901, and took out no patent on his discovery.
--- context
X-ray imaging grew out of university physics in [[region/europe|Europe]] during the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]]. Becquerel's 1896 discovery of radioactivity, prompted by the new rays, led onward to the research of [[person/marie-curie|Marie Curie]]. It shows a result of [[theme/science|Science]] becoming a clinical tool almost immediately. Thread: [[theme/technology|Technology]].
--- observer
For the species, X-rays extended the tool-lineage by making the body's own interior visible, and the images spread fast partly because a photograph is such an easy thing to show around. Early enthusiasm may reflect status-signal as much as medical need, since public demonstrations proved popular in their own right. This reading would weaken if adoption records showed clinical demand alone explains the 1896 spread. An alternative reading credits existing networks of instrument makers and hospitals instead.
--- question 1
Did earlier cathode-ray experimenters glimpse this same effect without ever recognizing it?
--- question 2
How quickly did practitioners link the injuries to exposure itself?

=== technology/zero-and-place-value-notation
title: Zero and Place-Value Notation
src: 7c30f71c7625
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The decimal place-value system, an architecture in which zero serves as both digit and placeholder, developed in India during the first millennium CE and spread from there across the world.
--- fact 1
The Bakhshali manuscript, found in what is now Pakistan, uses a dot as its placeholder zero. It is a composite of birch-bark leaves from different periods: Oxford radiocarbon dating in 2017 gave ranges of 224 to 383 CE, 680 to 779 CE, and 885 to 993 CE for three folios, and a 2024 revision widened the overall range to 799 to 1102 CE, moving the oldest tested folio to 931 to 1032 CE. The claim that it holds the earliest zero is, therefore, contested.
--- fact 2
The Indian mathematician Brahmagupta set down rules for arithmetic with zero in 628 CE.
--- fact 3
Scholars in the Islamic world, among them [[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]], carried the system westward.
--- fact 4
Fibonacci's 1202 book carried it into popular use across Europe.
--- context
The system emerged in [[region/south-asia|South Asia]], possibly during the [[culture/gupta-empire|Gupta Empire]], and now underlies all modern arithmetic as well as the binary numbers used in the [[technology/digital-computer|Digital Computer]].
--- observer
The Observer notes how a mere symbol for absence turned out to be one of the most productive ideas in history. Cultures without it could still do arithmetic, only not nearly as easily.
--- question 1
When was zero first used as a number in its own right, rather than only a placeholder?
