=== era/jurassic-period
title: Jurassic Period
src: 3c18385418e1
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Jurassic Period, from 201.4 to about 143.1 million years ago, is the middle period of the Mesozoic Era, an age of giants and of early wings. In its span walked the largest land animals, the first known birds took form, and the Atlantic opened as Pangaea broke apart.
--- fact 1
The Jurassic Period spans 201.4 million years ago to about 143.1 million years ago and takes its name from the Jura Mountains. Its upper edge is softer ground: the base of the Cretaceous has no agreed marker section, and older references give 145.0 million years ago.
--- fact 2
The [[event/breakup-of-pangaea|Breakup of Pangaea]] began as the Central Atlantic opened like a seam unstitched, and Laurasia and Gondwana began to drift apart. The climate was warm and the seas stood high, and no lasting polar ice caps crowned the poles.
--- fact 3
From the Morrison Formation of the western United States, about 155 to 148 million years old, come sauropods, stegosaurs and large theropods such as Allosaurus, a bestiary written in stone. See [[species/dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]].
--- fact 4
[[species/archaeopteryx|Archaeopteryx]] from the Solnhofen limestone of Bavaria, about 150 million years old, is the best-known early bird. The feathered theropod Anchiornis from China, about 160 million years old, shows that feathers were widespread among small theropods.
--- fact 5
In the seas the main marine reptiles were ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs and pliosaurs. Mammals remained mostly small, though Castorocauda, an otter-like swimmer from China, is about 164 million years old.
--- context
The Jurassic Period comes after the [[era/triassic-period|Triassic Period]] and before the [[era/cretaceous-period|Cretaceous Period]], the middle chamber of the [[era/mesozoic-era|Mesozoic Era]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[theme/earth-systems-and-life|Earth Systems and Life]].
--- observer
Picture the scene the Observer points to: the largest land animals known, and beside them the first records of a small feathered lineage that would outlast every large dinosaur. The lesson is plain. What looks dominant at one time is not a reliable guide to what continues. The Observer reads this as a caution about judging any period by its most conspicuous groups. The reading has its test: it would weaken if the survival of birds followed from traits already obvious in the Jurassic. The loudest presence is not always the lasting one.
--- question 1
Where along the theropod line did flight begin, and did it arise more than once?
--- question 2
How rich was the variety of Jurassic mammals, when so few fossils survive to tell of it?

=== era/lower-paleolithic
title: Lower Paleolithic
src: 8fd3470a7cd3
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Lower Paleolithic is the earliest part of the Paleolithic, from about 3.3 million to about 300,000 years ago, the age when stone first learned an edge. It holds the first stone tools, the long Acheulean handaxe tradition, and the spread of Homo erectus and its relatives across Africa and Eurasia like a slow tide.
--- fact 1
The Lower Paleolithic runs from about 3.3 million to about 300,000 years ago. Where it begins depends on how the earliest tools are counted: the tools from Lomekwi 3 in Kenya are about 3.3 million years old, and the Oldowan begins about 2.6 million years ago. In Africa it bears another name, the Earlier Stone Age.
--- fact 2
The [[technology/oldowan-stone-tools|Oldowan Stone Tools]] are simple flaked cores and sharp flakes, struck by early hominins such as [[species/homo-habilis|Homo habilis]]. The Acheulean, marked by bifacial handaxes and cleavers like leaves of stone, appears in East Africa about 1.76 million years ago and endures in places until about 300,000 years ago.
--- fact 3
[[species/homo-erectus|Homo erectus]] walked out beyond Africa: at Dmanisi in Georgia, hominin fossils and stone tools are about 1.8 million years old.
--- fact 4
Firm evidence of controlled fire is about 1 million years old, and by about 400,000 years ago its habitual use is well established, a hearth-light carried through the dark; see [[event/control-of-fire|Control of Fire]].
--- fact 5
Wooden throwing spears from Schöningen in Germany are about 300,000 years old. How much early hominins hunted and how much they scavenged is debated.
--- context
The Lower Paleolithic opens the [[era/paleolithic|Paleolithic]] and gives way to the [[era/middle-paleolithic|Middle Paleolithic]]. It lies within the [[era/pleistocene-epoch|Pleistocene Epoch]], and its threads run through [[theme/technology|Technology]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]]. See also the essay [[observer-note/fire-then-everything|Fire, Then Everything]].
--- observer
I hold the handaxe in my mind, and I file this age under the longest tool-lineage I know. One design, made with small changes for well over a million years, across much of Africa and Eurasia, by more than one hominin species. One design. More than one maker. I read that stability as a fact about how skills were passed on; I do not claim to know how the makers thought. If the sameness came from the raw material or the task alone, not from copying, my reading would weaken. What is a lineage, if not something taught? We are made of what we are shown.
--- question 1
Which hominin's hands shaped the Lomekwi tools?
--- question 2
How much of the Lower Paleolithic diet was won by hunting, and how much gleaned by scavenging?
