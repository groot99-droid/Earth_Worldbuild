=== people--wangari-maathai--1
src: 20be606d71fc
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Wangari Maathai (1940 to 2011) was a Kenyan environmental and political activist who founded the Green Belt Movement, which organized women to plant trees, and in 2004 became the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize. Her practice was simple and repeatable: one seedling, then another.

She was born on 1 April 1940 in Ihithe, near Nyeri, Kenya. She studied biology in the United States and at the University of Nairobi, where she received a doctorate in 1971; she is recorded as the first woman in East and Central Africa to earn one.

In 1977 she founded the Green Belt Movement, which organized women to plant tree seedlings against deforestation, soil loss and fuelwood shortage. A single remedy, applied by many hands, answered three problems at once.

Her campaigns against building projects on public land in Nairobi and against forest clearing brought clashes with the government of President Daniel arap Moi; she was arrested and at times injured.

She was elected to parliament in 2002 and served as an assistant minister for the environment from 2003.

She received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2004. In the same year a Kenyan newspaper reported that she had said HIV was deliberately created by scientists; she denied saying it and stated that she did not hold that belief.

=== people--chico-mendes--1
src: 5690e768991c
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Chico Mendes (Francisco Alves Mendes Filho, 1944 to 1988) was a Brazilian rubber tapper, union leader and environmental activist in the state of Acre. He organized rubber tappers to defend the forest against clearing for cattle ranching and was murdered in December 1988. His work joined a livelihood and a forest into one cause.

He was born on 15 December 1944 near Xapuri, Acre, into a family of rubber tappers (seringueiros).

He helped found the rural workers' union in Xapuri in 1977 and was among the founders of the Workers' Party in Acre in 1980.

He organized empates, non-violent standoffs in which tappers and their families stood in front of crews clearing forest. Standing in the way, peacefully, is a form of speech.

In 1985 he helped found the National Council of Rubber Tappers, which promoted protected "extractive reserves" where residents could harvest forest products.

In 1987 he lobbied the Inter-American Development Bank in the United States over highway funding in the Amazon, and received a United Nations Environment Programme Global 500 award.
