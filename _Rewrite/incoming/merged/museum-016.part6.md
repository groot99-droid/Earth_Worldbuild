--- description
Amrita Sher-Gil (1913 to 1941) was a Hungarian-Indian painter, born in Budapest to a Sikh father and a Hungarian-Jewish mother, who trained in Paris before coming back to India to paint its people, its women above all. Often called India's first great modern woman artist, she fused European Post-Impressionist technique with the Ajanta murals and Mughal-Rajput miniature painting across barely a decade of work, a short flame burning very bright.

She was born in Budapest on 30 January 1913 to a Sikh father and a Hungarian-Jewish mother, moved to India in 1921, and began painting as a child in Shimla.

She studied art in Paris from about 1929 (sources date her attendance at Parisian art schools from 1930), and in 1933 she became the youngest associate, and the only Asian, elected to the Grand Salon.

She returned to India in 1934, and a 1937 journey through South India and the Ajanta murals remade her style into simplified, monumental figures in flat colour.

Three Girls (1935) won the Bombay Art Society's gold medal in 1937, the year she also painted Bride's Toilet and Brahmacharis; later works such as Village Scene (1938) and Haldi Grinders (1940) drew on Indian miniature painting to show the lives of rural women.

In 1938 she married her Hungarian cousin, Victor Egan; the couple later settled at Saraya in Uttar Pradesh before moving to Lahore in 1941.
