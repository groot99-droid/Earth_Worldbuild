=== people--giotto--1
src: 80f08f596943
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Giotto di Bondone (about 1267 to 1337) was a Florentine painter who gave medieval figures weight, feeling and space, as if light had at last found bodies to fall on. Many call him the father of Western painting, and his frescoes in the Scrovegni Chapel in Padua are his firmest work.

He was born about 1267, traditionally in the Mugello valley north of Florence, though his birthplace is disputed and some recent research points to Florence itself. Vasari's story of the shepherd boy discovered by Cimabue is legend, but he was almost certainly trained in Florentine workshops.

He was traditionally credited with the Legend of St Francis frescoes at Assisi in the late 1290s, but it is now generally accepted that four different hands can be told apart there and that they came from Rome, so his authorship is doubtful; his early career is still debated.

He painted the Scrovegni Chapel in Padua about 1303 to 1305 for the banker Enrico Scrovegni. It is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

He worked in Rome, in Santa Croce in Florence, and at the court of King Robert of Anjou in Naples from about 1328 to 1333.

In 1334 Florence named him master of works for the cathedral, and he designed its bell tower. He died in January 1337 and was buried with public honours.

=== people--robert-the-bruce--1
src: 5711a45af721
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Robert the Bruce (1274 to 1329) was King of Scotland from 1306 to 1329. He led Scottish resistance to English rule and won recognition of Scottish independence shortly before his death.

He claimed the Scottish throne in 1306 after killing a rival claimant, John Comyn, in a church at Dumfries, a deed that brought his excommunication by the pope.

Early in his reign he suffered several defeats and spells as a fugitive, and according to a later popular legend he drew perseverance from watching a spider try again and again to build its web.

At the Battle of Bannockburn in 1314 he decisively defeated a much larger English army under Edward II, the most significant Scottish military victory of the wars of independence.

In the Treaty of Edinburgh-Northampton in 1328 England formally recognized Scottish independence and Robert's kingship, a year before his death in 1329, reportedly from leprosy.

=== people--ibn-khaldun--1
src: 8275da7f78aa
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Ibn Khaldun (1332 to 1406) was a North African historian whose 'Muqaddimah' (1377) proposed a general theory of how societies and states rise and fall. It is a theory you can still test against your own century.

He was born in Tunis and served in several courts in North Africa and Andalusia.

He argued that group solidarity (asabiyyah) helps found dynasties, and that as rulers grow comfortable it weakens. Comfort, in his architecture of history, is where decline begins.

He wrote about economics, urban life, and the role of climate and environment.

He is sometimes called a founder of sociology and historiography.
