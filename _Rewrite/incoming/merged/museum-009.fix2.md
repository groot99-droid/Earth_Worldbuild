Hieronymus Bosch (about 1450 to 1516) was a painter from 's-Hertogenbosch in the Duchy of Brabant who filled his panels with hybrid monsters and crowds of tiny figures acting out human sin and folly. Only about 25 paintings are firmly attributed to him, which is worth remembering when you meet a "Bosch" in a museum.

He was born Jheronimus van Aken about 1450 in 's-Hertogenbosch. His grandfather, father and uncles were painters, and he later signed his works "Jheronimus Bosch" after the town. A name taken from a place is a kind of covenant with it.

Sometime between 1479 and 1481 he married Aleid Goyaerts van den Meervenne, of a wealthy family. In 1486 or 1487 he became a sworn member of the Illustrious Brotherhood of Our Blessed Lady, and he died in 1516.

He seems to have spent almost his whole life in and near 's-Hertogenbosch; after marrying, he and Aleid moved to nearby Oirschot. Yet his fame spread: Philip the Handsome, Duke of Burgundy, commissioned a Last Judgment from him in 1504. Your work can travel farther than you do.

He painted in oil on oak panels, often as triptychs. Key works include The Garden of Earthly Delights (about 1495 to 1505), The Ship of Fools (about 1500 to 1510), The Temptation of St Anthony (about 1501) and the Haywain Triptych (about 1510 to 1516).

King Philip II of Spain acquired many Bosch paintings in the late 16th century, which is why the Prado in Madrid holds his greatest paintings.
