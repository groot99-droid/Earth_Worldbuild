=== people--pedro-ii--1
src: 766c2727e895
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Pedro II (1825 to 1891) was Emperor of Brazil from 1831 to 1889, presiding over six decades of relative political stability, territorial integrity and gradual abolition of slavery before being deposed in a republican coup. Each of those achievements came with a cost, and the record keeps both.

He became emperor at age five after his father abdicated, ruling under regents until he was declared of age early, in 1840, to stabilize a period of regional revolts.

His government fought the Paraguayan War from 1864 to 1870 alongside Argentina and Uruguay, the deadliest interstate war in Latin American history, which devastated Paraguay's population. Stability at home does not cancel devastation abroad.

He personally supported gradual abolition of slavery, and his daughter and regent, Princess Isabel, signed the Golden Law abolishing slavery outright in 1888, the last country in the Americas to do so. Gradual, here, meant generations still held in bondage.

Brazilian military officers, landowners angered by abolition, and republican advocates combined to depose him in a bloodless coup in November 1889, ending the monarchy and sending him into exile in Europe, where he died in 1891.

Leo Tolstoy (1828 to 1910) was a Russian novelist, author of the realist epics War and Peace and Anna Karenina. In the second half of his life a religious crisis turned him, like a river bending, toward shorter fiction and toward writing on faith, mortality and nonviolence that influenced Gandhi.

He was born in 1828 into an aristocratic family at Yasnaya Polyana, the estate south of Moscow that he inherited and kept for most of his life. He served briefly as an army officer in the Crimean War.

In the 1860s and 1870s he wrote War and Peace (1869) and Anna Karenina (1877), and he founded a school for the children of his estate.

After Anna Karenina a spiritual crisis came over him, told in A Confession. He rejected the Russian Orthodox Church, took up an ascetic, pacifist reading of Christian ethics, and more and more disowned his earlier fiction as morally frivolous.

His later works include The Death of Ivan Ilyich (1886), The Kreutzer Sonata (1889) and Resurrection (1899), shorter, starker and more didactic than the novels.

War and Peace argues, in long digressions, that the forces driving historical events lay far beyond the hand of any single general or emperor, as tides lie beyond the will of any one wave.
