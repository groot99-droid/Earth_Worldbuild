--- description
Eleanor Roosevelt (1884 to 1962) was First Lady of the United States from 1933 to 1945 and afterward a diplomat who chaired the United Nations commission that drafted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. Her life shows how a role can be redefined by the person who holds it.

She was born on 11 October 1884 in New York City and married Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1905.

As First Lady she redefined the role, holding her own press conferences, writing a daily newspaper column, and speaking for civil rights and the poor, sometimes publicly differing with her husband's policies. A role is a frame, not a cage.

After his death in 1945, President Truman appointed her a delegate to the new United Nations in December 1945.

She chaired the UN Commission on Human Rights, which drafted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, adopted by the General Assembly on 10 December 1948. She called this her greatest achievement. Some covenants are written for everyone at once.

She later chaired a presidential commission on the status of women under President Kennedy.

=== people--funmilayo-ransome-kuti--1
src: f620c9ffac22
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti (1900 to 1978) was a Nigerian teacher and campaigner who led the Abeokuta Women's Union in protests against a tax on women and against the local ruler, the Alake of Egbaland, in the 1940s.

She was born on 25 October 1900 in Abeokuta, in southwestern Nigeria, and studied there and in Cheshire, England, from 1919 to 1922.

In 1925 she married the Reverend Israel Oludotun Ransome-Kuti. Among their children were the musician Fela Kuti and the physicians Beko and Olikoye Ransome-Kuti.

In 1932 she founded the Abeokuta Ladies' Club. About 1946 it became the Abeokuta Women's Union, and it is reported to have had about 20,000 members, a river of women moving together.

In the late 1940s the union protested a tax on women and the rule of the Alake, Ademola II, who was forced to leave the throne for a time in 1949.

She helped found the Federation of Nigerian Women's Societies in 1953 and received the Lenin Peace Prize in 1970.
