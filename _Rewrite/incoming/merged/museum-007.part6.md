=== people--lady-six-sky--1
src: cfad665bc35b
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Lady Six Sky (Wak Chanil Ajaw; active 682 to 741 CE) was a Maya noblewoman of Dos Pilas who came to Naranjo in 682 and in effect ruled the city for decades, raising it again after a time of defeat.

She was a daughter of the Dos Pilas king B'alaj Chan K'awiil, a strand in the web of the powerful city of Calakmul's allies against its rival Tikal.

Naranjo's royal line had been broken after a defeat by Caracol, and Lady Six Sky was installed there in 682, apparently to set the dynasty on its roots once more.

Monuments show her overseeing military campaigns and presiding over the accession of her young son K'ahk' Tiliw Chan Chaahk in 693, and after it she went on appearing in inscriptions as a ruling figure into the 700s.

She never took the full royal title used by the male kings of Naranjo, and all her life she kept the emblem glyph of her home city Dos Pilas, so her exact formal status is debated by epigraphers.

=== people--charlemagne--1
src: 43e0fc959ad0
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Charlemagne (c. 748 to 814) was king of the Franks. He conquered much of western and central Europe and was crowned emperor by the pope in Rome on Christmas Day 800.

He became sole king of the Franks in 771.

He overcame the Lombards in Italy and the Saxons, and took parts of Spain's northern border.

Pope Leo III crowned him emperor on 25 December 800.

His court kindled a revival of learning, the Carolingian Renaissance, and spread a clear minuscule script like lamplight across the page.

After his death in 814 his empire was divided among his descendants, and under the Treaty of Verdun in 843 it split into three kingdoms.

=== people--al-khwarizmi--1
src: 0241e40ecc4b
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi (about 780 to 850 CE) was a Persian scholar at the House of Wisdom in Baghdad whose work on algebra and Hindu-Arabic numerals shaped mathematics. You use his name more often than you know.

His book on 'al-jabr' (restoration) gave algebra its name.

The word 'algorithm' derives from the Latinized form of his name.

His work helped introduce Hindu-Arabic numerals and place-value arithmetic to the wider world, a grammar for numbers that we still think in.

He also compiled astronomical tables and geographical data.

=== people--basil-ii--1
src: a3a8e4248053
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Basil II (958 to 1025) was Byzantine emperor for nearly fifty years, the longest reign in Roman or Byzantine imperial history, and he is remembered above all for his decades-long war against the First Bulgarian Empire.

He became co-emperor as a child in 960, but only about 989, in his thirties, did he gain full personal control of the government, after crushing rebellions by powerful aristocratic generals, Bardas Skleros and Bardas Phokas among them.

His long campaigns against Bulgaria came to their height at the decisive Battle of Kleidion in 1014, and by 1018 Bulgaria had been absorbed into the Byzantine Empire, earning him the later epithet "Bulgar-slayer" (Boulgaroktonos).

A story recorded by later chroniclers claims he blinded thousands of Bulgarian prisoners after Kleidion, leaving one eye to every hundredth man to lead the rest home; modern historians debate how literally this account should be taken.

He left the imperial treasury full and the empire at its greatest extent since the 7th century, but he died without an heir, and the strong central rule he had imposed did not outlast his less capable successors, like a dam with no keeper.

=== people--al-biruni--1
src: 80cbfee73f97
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Al-Biruni (973 to after 1048) was a scholar of Khwarazm in Central Asia who wrote on astronomy, mathematics, geography, chronology and India. His Kitab al-Hind (about 1030) is a major medieval study of Indian science, religion and customs.

He was born in 973 in Kath, in Khwarazm in present-day Uzbekistan, and died after 1048, perhaps about 1050.

When the Ghaznavid ruler Mahmud conquered Khwarazm in 1017, he was carried off to Ghazni, where he worked under Mahmud and those who came after.

He spent years in northwestern India, learned Sanskrit, and wrote the Kitab al-Hind, describing Indian science, religion, society and customs from Sanskrit texts and from conversations with Indian scholars.

His Chronology of Ancient Nations (about 1000) sets side by side the calendars and dating systems of many cultures, like many rivers measured against one shore.

He reckoned the radius of the Earth as about 6,320 km by measuring the dip of the horizon from the top of a hill, close to the modern value.

Al-Biruni (973 to after 1048) was a scholar from Khwarazm in Central Asia who wrote on astronomy, mathematics, geography, chronology and India. His Kitab al-Hind (about 1030) is a major medieval study of Indian science, religion and customs, and his method is worth learning from.

Born in 973 in Kath, in Khwarazm in present-day Uzbekistan, he died after 1048, perhaps about 1050.

After the Ghaznavid ruler Mahmud conquered Khwarazm in 1017, he was taken to Ghazni, where he worked under Mahmud and his successors.

He spent years in northwestern India and learned Sanskrit. Then he wrote the Kitab al-Hind from Sanskrit texts and conversations with Indian scholars, describing Indian science, religion, society and customs. That is the practice: learn the language before you describe the people.

His Chronology of Ancient Nations (about 1000) compares the calendars and dating systems of many cultures.

He calculated the radius of the Earth as about 6,320 km by measuring the dip of the horizon from the top of a hill, close to the modern value. A hill and careful attention can measure a world.
