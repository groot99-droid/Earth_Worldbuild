=== people--muhammad-ali-of-egypt--1
src: 8290d14731cd
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Muhammad Ali, an Ottoman officer of Albanian family, became governor of Egypt in 1805, destroyed the old Mamluk elite, built a conscript army and a state-run economy, and founded the dynasty that ruled Egypt until 1952. Modernization and massacre stand side by side in his record; read both.

He was born in Kavala, in what is now Greece, to an Albanian family from the Korçë region. His father was a tobacco merchant and an Ottoman military commander.

He rose to power after the French withdrawal from Egypt in 1801. In 1805 prominent Egyptians, including the religious scholars, demanded his appointment as governor (wali), and the Ottomans accepted.

In 1811 he invited the leading Mamluks to a celebration at the Cairo Citadel, where his forces ambushed and killed them. More Mamluks were then hunted down in Cairo and elsewhere, for a total of perhaps 3,000. An invitation turned into a trap: that is how this power was cleared.

He introduced long-staple cotton, built an army of conscripts trained by European officers, and set up a state-run industrial economy.

He founded the dynasty that ruled Egypt until 1952.

=== people--toussaint-louverture--1
src: a46702af2d9d
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Toussaint Louverture was a formerly enslaved man who became the principal military and political leader of the Haitian Revolution, and he died in a French prison in 1803. Hold the whole arc in view: from bondage to command, and then to a cell.

He was born into slavery on Saint-Domingue around 1743 (20 May 1743 in one account) and was freed in 1776 or 1777, depending on the source, before the revolution began in 1791.

He began as a lieutenant in the 1791 uprising and at first allied with the Spanish of neighboring Santo Domingo. In 1794 he switched to the French Republic, which had abolished slavery, and in 1798 he negotiated the withdrawal of British forces under a treaty with General Thomas Maitland. Notice the principle behind the switch: he went to the side that had ended slavery.

On 7 July 1801 he promulgated a constitution asserting his authority over the whole island of Hispaniola, which made him governor-general for life.

Napoleon sent an expedition in 1802; Toussaint was arrested by deception and deported to France, where he died at Fort de Joux in April 1803.

Jean-Jacques Dessalines proclaimed Haiti's independence on 1 January 1804. A leader can be taken; a revolution can still be finished.

Napoleon sent an expedition in 1802; Toussaint was arrested by deception and deported to France, where he died at Fort de Joux in April 1803. He did not live to see the proclamation. Who finishes what I begin? Freedom is a relay, and he carried it far.
