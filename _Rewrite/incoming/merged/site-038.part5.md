=== culture/islam
title: Islam
src: 2b1947b2269d
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Islam is the religion founded by Muhammad in seventh-century Arabia, centered on the Quran as the word of God and on the practices set out in its five pillars, and today, the way a spring becomes a river, it is the second-largest religion in the world.
--- fact 1
Muslims hold that Muhammad, an Arabian merchant, began receiving revelations from God around 610 CE, later compiled as the Quran, and that before his death in 632 he united the tribes of Arabia under the new faith, as scattered streams gather into a single channel.
--- fact 2
A dispute over his successor split his followers soon after his death, like a river parting around a stone: most accepted the elected caliphs (becoming Sunni), while a smaller group held that leadership belonged to his family through his son-in-law Ali (becoming Shia).
--- fact 3
The faith spread rapidly under the early caliphates and then under empires such as the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates, a tide running from Spain to Central and South Asia within a few centuries.
--- fact 4
Sunni Muslims make up roughly 85 percent of the world's estimated 1.9 billion Muslims, and Shia Muslims about 15 percent, with Shia forming the majority in Iran, Iraq, Bahrain and Azerbaijan.
--- context
Islam began in the [[region/arabian-peninsula|Arabian Peninsula]] with [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]] at the dawn of the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]], part of the [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]], and it spread through empires that included the [[culture/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad Caliphate]] and the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]]. See [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
I think of a single kin-lattice question: who should lead? I think of how a succession dispute was settled within a generation of the founder's death, and of the claim-line it drew, Sunni against Shia, that fourteen centuries of later history have not erased. I think of the empires built around it, and of how the question has outlasted them all.

Is it a single grievance, carried unbroken? A specialist could argue that many local and political factors, not the original dispute alone, kept the split alive in specific regions, which would weaken a reading of the divide as a single continuous grievance. I know how an old question stays alive: each age asks it again, for its own reasons.
--- question 1
How much of the lasting Sunni-Shia distinction reflects the original succession dispute, and how much later political history?
--- question 2
What let the faith spread so swiftly across such different regions within its first century?

=== culture/jainism
title: Jainism
src: d5f4072b373c
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Jainism is an ancient Indian religion centered on non-violence toward all living things. It was taught by a line of teachers called tirthankaras and is traditionally traced to Mahavira in the sixth century BCE, though the tradition itself holds its teaching to be eternal. Think of it as an ethic built to the smallest scale: a whole religious architecture resting on the refusal to harm.
--- fact 1
Consider how the tradition frames its own beginning: Mahavira, traditionally dated about 599 to 527 BCE, is remembered as the twenty-fourth and final tirthankara of the current cosmic cycle, and is sometimes called the founder of Jainism as practiced today. Jain tradition itself considers its teaching timeless rather than founded by any one person.
--- fact 2
Mahavira reportedly renounced a noble upbringing at about 30 and practiced severe asceticism for over a decade before reaching what Jains call perfect knowledge, a threshold crossed only after long discipline.
--- fact 3
Its central principle, ahimsa, non-violence toward all living beings, is practiced to an unusual degree by Jain monks and nuns, some of whom sweep their path and cover their mouths to avoid harming small creatures: doctrine carried all the way into the body.
--- fact 4
By about the fourth century CE the tradition had split into two main branches, Digambara and Svetambara, which differ over practices such as monastic clothing.
--- fact 5
Roughly four to five million Jains live today, the great majority in India, with smaller communities in East Africa, North America and Europe.
--- context
Jainism developed in [[region/south-asia|South Asia]] during the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]], in the same broad period as [[person/siddhartha-gautama|Siddhartha Gautama]]'s teaching and the wider [[event/axial-age|Axial Age]] of new religious movements, and the two are worth reading side by side. See [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]].
--- observer
I think of a small community. I think of over two thousand years. I think of the smallest creature, and of how harm to it is treated as morally weighty here, in one of the most demanding versions of nonviolence recorded among the world's meaning-engines. Could I walk that carefully? Could anyone?

A specialist could argue that Mahavira's dates, and the very idea of Jainism as a distinct tradition beginning with him, are themselves later constructions laid over a longer, less clearly bounded history of ascetic teaching, which would weaken any precise founding date. I do not need the date to be exact. A practice kept this long outlasts the question of when it began.
--- question 1
How much of the traditional account of Mahavira's life reflects history, and how much later hagiography?
--- question 2
How consistently has ahimsa been practiced across Jain communities and periods?
