=== people--rani-lakshmibai--1
src: 0b5a1f70f57b
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi (about 1828 to 1858) was an Indian queen who rose as a leader of the 1857 rebellion and became a national symbol of resistance, a name carried like a torch.

She ruled Jhansi as regent after her husband's death, and the British refused to recognize her adopted heir.

In 1858 she led the defense of Jhansi against British forces.

On 1 June 1858 she and other rebels seized the fortress of Gwalior.

She fell in battle on 17 June 1858.

=== people--savitribai-phule--1
src: 4583248411cd
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Savitribai Phule (1831 to 1897) was an Indian educator, poet and social reformer in Maharashtra who, with her husband Jyotirao Phule, opened one of the first schools for girls in Pune in 1848. She died of plague in 1897 after nursing patients. Her life is a lesson in teaching as an act of courage.

She was born on 3 January 1831 in Naigaon, Maharashtra, and married Jyotirao Phule in 1840 as a child. He taught her to read, and she turned that gift outward.

In 1848 the couple opened a school for girls in Pune. She is often called India's first woman teacher; earlier girls' schools existed, so the "first" claim is contested, and historians also credit Fatima Sheikh as a co-worker. A movement is rarely the work of one name.

Accounts say she was harassed with stones and dung on her way to teach. She kept going.

She and Jyotirao later opened further schools, including for children of lower castes, and in 1863 they founded a home for the prevention of infanticide.

She published a book of poetry, Kavya Phule, in 1854.

=== people--sitting-bull--1
src: 6d5d722f9e32
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Sitting Bull (Tȟatȟáŋka Íyotake, born between 1831 and 1837, died 1890) was a Hunkpapa Lakota leader and holy man who resisted United States expansion onto Lakota lands and was in the camp at the Battle of the Little Bighorn in 1876. Native police killed him in 1890.

He was born sometime between 1831 and 1837, traditionally on the Grand River in present-day South Dakota (a 2007 family oral tradition places his birth on the Yellowstone River instead), and became a leading Hunkpapa Lakota war leader and holy man.

Begin with the covenant that was broken. The Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868) guaranteed the Black Hills to the Lakota. After gold was found there in 1874 the United States sought to take the land, and war followed in 1876.

On 25 and 26 June 1876 Lakota, Northern Cheyenne and Arapaho warriors destroyed the force of Lt. Col. George Custer at the Little Bighorn. Sitting Bull was in the large camp; accounts describe his role as spiritual leader and organizer rather than field commander.

After the battle he led his followers to Canada in 1877, and he surrendered at Fort Buford in July 1881.

On 15 December 1890, at Standing Rock, Native police sent to arrest him amid tension over the Ghost Dance killed him in a struggle. The Wounded Knee massacre followed on 29 December. A treaty is only as strong as the will to keep it.
