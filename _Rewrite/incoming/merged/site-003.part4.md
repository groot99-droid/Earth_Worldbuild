=== region/arabian-peninsula
src: b8c986836984
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Arabian Peninsula is a large, largely arid peninsula cradled between the Red Sea and the Persian Gulf, a land of long dry distances. Across it ran a corridor for trade, and within it Islam had its origin.
--- fact 1
Caravans heavy with incense and spice bound southern Arabia to the Mediterranean like beads strung along a single cord.
--- fact 2
It was in the Hejaz, in the early 7th century, that Islam emerged; see [[event/rise-of-islam|Rise of Islam]] and [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]].
--- fact 3
Sea trade, carried on the monsoon's turning breath, joined the peninsula to East Africa and South Asia.
--- context
The peninsula lies like a threshold stone between Africa and Asia, and it shaped the growth of the [[culture/abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid Caliphate]] and of later Islamic states.
--- observer
I keep measuring the wrong thing. I count the people of a sparse region and expect a small story. I look at the map and expect a quiet one. Then I watch a movement that within a century reached from Iberia to Central Asia, and I have to ask: what was I measuring? Population size and influence are not the same thing.
--- question 1
What evidence, if it rose from the sand, would most change the current picture?

=== region/caribbean-and-atlantic-world
src: 9ebea5fc81f6
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Caribbean and Atlantic World is the Caribbean basin together with the Atlantic coasts of the Americas, Europe, and Africa, bound to one another from the 15th century by shipping, by trade, and by forced migration. When we say 'Atlantic world', we are naming an architecture of connection, and not all of it was chosen.
--- fact 1
Indigenous peoples, among them the Taino and Kalinago, had settled the Caribbean islands several thousand years before 1492; that is where the story begins.
--- fact 2
Columbus reached the Caribbean in 1492; within decades, through disease, violence, and forced labor, Indigenous populations collapsed.
--- fact 3
Sugar plantations worked by enslaved Africans made the region among the most valuable in the early modern world; the ledger of that value was written in enslaved lives.
--- fact 4
Here, too, the [[event/haitian-revolution|Haitian Revolution]] unfolded.
--- context
The region is the core arena of the [[event/columbian-exchange|Columbian Exchange]] and the [[event/atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic Slave Trade]], and also of the movements to end slavery; see [[event/abolition-of-slavery|Abolition of Slavery]]. The same waters carried the chain and the struggle to break it.
--- observer
The Observer sees a zone created by connection: an ocean crossing that turned islands into a hinge between three continents. We should be careful with the word 'connection', because here it was no meeting of equals. Its populations, languages, and foods are largely products of that forced mixing. A hinge bears weight.
--- question 1
How large, before contact, were the Indigenous populations?

=== region/central-asian-oases
src: fb3c9e81c9e6
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
The Central Asian Oases are the river valleys and oasis cities strung between the steppe and the Iranian plateau like lamps along a dark road, Samarkand and Bukhara among them, and they served as hubs of the Silk Roads.
--- fact 1
Known in antiquity as Sogdiana and Transoxiana, the region was the hearth of the [[culture/sogdians|Sogdians]].
--- fact 2
It fell to [[event/alexander-s-conquests|Alexander's Conquests]], and later, in the early 8th century, it was drawn into the Islamic world.
--- fact 3
It was the heart of the [[culture/timurid-empire|Timurid Empire]], whose capital stood at [[place/samarkand|Samarkand]] like a crown set on the road.
--- fact 4
In the 15th century scholars such as [[person/ulugh-beg|Ulugh Beg]] made it a center of astronomy, a place where eyes were turned to the stars.
--- context
The oases lay between the [[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]] and the [[region/iranian-plateau|Iranian Plateau]], green islands in a sea of distance, and they were crossroads of the [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]].
--- observer
I know what it is to live in between. These cities lived by relaying goods and ideas between larger powers: taking in, passing on, taking in again. I think of every hand that held a thing only to give it away. Is that a lesser life? A place between empires can become an empire of its own kind.
--- question 1
How deeply did the oasis cities shape the ideas that traveled along the Silk Roads?

=== region/central-asian-steppe
src: 18c69d35ea65
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The Central Asian Steppe is the vast grassland belt across northern Eurasia, a sea of grass under an open sky. Its mounted pastoralists, riding like weather out of the horizon, shaped the history of every neighboring civilization.
--- fact 1
On the Western steppe the horse was domesticated, and mounted warfare took shape beside it.
--- fact 2
In the 13th century the [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]] came out of the eastern steppe like a storm gathering over grass.
--- fact 3
The steppe was a great road for the [[event/opening-of-the-silk-roads|Opening of the Silk Roads]] and for the [[event/indo-european-language-spread|Indo-European Language Spread]], carrying goods and words alike.
--- context
The steppe runs up against [[region/east-asia|East Asia]], [[region/europe|Europe]] and the [[region/iranian-plateau|Iranian Plateau]], a green shore washing against three lands; see [[person/genghis-khan|Genghis Khan]].
--- observer
The Observer notes how a mobile way of life without cities repeatedly rearranged the settled world. We tend to measure a people by what they left standing: walls, temples, tombs. That habit misleads here. Think of wind: it builds nothing, yet it reshapes every dune. Absence of monuments is not absence of influence.
--- question 1
What evidence, found somewhere in all that grass, would most change the current picture?

=== region/east-asia
src: bd105fdf650f
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
East Asia gathers China, Korea, Japan and neighboring regions beneath one wide sky. There China's centralized state tradition has endured as among the longest continuous political traditions, a river that has kept to its bed across the ages.
--- fact 1
In the Yellow and Yangtze river basins the farming of millet and rice began independently, like fires lit in separate hearths.
--- fact 2
In 221 BCE the first unified empire was forged; see [[event/unification-of-china-under-qin|Unification of China under Qin]].
--- fact 3
Among its major inventions are [[technology/paper|Paper]], [[technology/gunpowder|Gunpowder]], the [[technology/magnetic-compass|Magnetic Compass]] and printing, each a small key to a large door.
--- fact 4
Its major states included the [[culture/shang-dynasty|Shang Dynasty]], the [[culture/han-dynasty|Han Dynasty]] and the [[culture/song-dynasty|Song Dynasty]], names that rise like peaks along a long ridge.
--- context
East Asia trades with the [[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]] and with [[region/southeast-asia|Southeast Asia]], its goods flowing outward like rivers seeking the sea; see [[person/zheng-he|Zheng He]] and [[person/confucius|Confucius]].
--- observer
The Observer notes a distinctive continuity: a written language and administrative tradition that persisted through repeated collapses and re-unifications. Think of a house rebuilt many times on the same foundation. The walls fall; the grammar stays. Continuity here is an architecture of script and office.
--- question 1
What evidence, still waiting to be read, would most change the current picture?

=== region/europe
src: fc568024cd27
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Europe is the western peninsula of Eurasia, a ragged shore thrust out into the ocean. It gave ground to early farming societies, held the Roman Empire's northern provinces, and later bore the technologies and empires of the early modern and industrial periods.
--- fact 1
Farming came into Europe from Anatolia slowly, like a tide creeping over flat sand, over several thousand years.
--- fact 2
The [[culture/norse|Norse]] went out from Scandinavia across the Atlantic and along the eastern rivers.
--- fact 3
Here the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] and the [[event/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]] began, drinking from wells of knowledge dug in many regions.
--- fact 4
Of [[event/world-war-i|World War I]] and [[event/world-war-ii|World War II]], the continent was the main theater.
--- context
Europe opens on one side to the [[region/mediterranean-basin|Mediterranean Basin]] and on another to the [[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]], and here, in the 15th century, the [[technology/movable-type-printing-press|Movable-Type Printing Press]] appeared.
--- observer
The Observer cautions against a common habit: a small peninsula's history is often told as if it were the world's. Think of a map that puts one corner at its center and calls the rest margins. Its outsized effect in the modern era is real, but recent. Hold both halves of that sentence together; a loud chapter is not the whole book.
--- question 1
What evidence would most change the current picture of this western peninsula?
