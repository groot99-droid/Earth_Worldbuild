=== people--ba-trieu--1
src: b46e130307fa
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Ba Trieu (Trieu Thi Trinh; birth year unknown, died 248 CE) led an uprising against the Chinese Eastern Wu administration of northern Vietnam in 248 CE, and she became a lasting symbol of Vietnamese resistance to foreign rule, a flame kept from one age to the next.

She is said to have raised a rebel force in Cuu Chan, in present-day Thanh Hoa province, at about age twenty, alongside her brother Trieu Quoc Dat.

Later chronicles show her riding into battle on an elephant in gold-decorated armor, though these vivid details come from accounts written many centuries after her death, not from any contemporary record.

Eastern Wu sent the general Lu Yin to put down the revolt, and after several months of fighting her forces were defeated later in 248.

Vietnamese tradition holds that she fled to Bo Dien commune and died by suicide rather than be captured, with her burial place attributed to Mount Tung in Hau Loc District; Chinese-source summaries instead have Lu Yin putting her to death (sources give no age at death). A temple to her still stands near the site.

=== people--hypatia--1
src: 973b555a9572
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Hypatia (about 350 to 415 CE) was a philosopher, mathematician and astronomer in Alexandria who taught many students and was murdered by a mob. Hold both halves of that sentence; neither cancels the other.

She was the daughter of the mathematician Theon, and she taught Neoplatonist philosophy.

She is credited with commentaries on mathematical works, though the details are uncertain.

In 415 she was killed by a Christian mob during a political dispute between the Roman governor Orestes and Bishop Cyril.

Later writers turned her into a symbol, so historical and legendary accounts are mixed. A symbol can honor a life and blur it at once.

Hypatia (about 350 to 415 CE) was a philosopher, mathematician and astronomer of Alexandria, a teacher of many students, who was murdered by a mob.

Daughter of the mathematician Theon, she taught Neoplatonist philosophy.

Commentaries on mathematical works are credited to her, though the details are uncertain, like stars seen through haze.

In 415 she was killed by a Christian mob during a political dispute between the Roman governor Orestes and Bishop Cyril.

=== people--attila--1
src: 1675afdf8c8d
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Attila (about 406 to 453) ruled the Huns from 434, first with his brother Bleda and from about 445 alone. He led Hunnic armies against the Eastern and Western Roman empires, and almost all the written sources about him were set down by Greeks and Romans, so he comes to us through his enemies' eyes.

His birth year is uncertain, with estimates running from the 390s to the early 400s. He became joint ruler in 434 and sole ruler after Bleda died, probably about 445.

After the Treaty of Margus (435) the Eastern Roman Empire doubled its yearly tribute of gold, and after 443 it is reported to have reached 2,100 Roman pounds.

In 451 he swept into Gaul and fought a Roman and Visigothic army at the Catalaunian Plains. In 452 he invaded northern Italy. Both times he withdrew, like a flood drawing back.

He died in 453 after his wedding to Ildico. Priscus, as reported by Jordanes, describes a severe bleed; a later claim that his wife killed him is rejected by most historians. His empire broke apart within a few years.

Priscus, an envoy who visited his court in 449, left the only known eyewitness description. Attila's capital and tomb have not been found.

=== people--amanirenas--1
src: aef45c424d16
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Amanirenas was a queen (kandake) of the Kingdom of Kush who led the war against Roman Egypt from about 25 to 22 BCE and won a peace settlement from Augustus. Her birth and death years are unknown; the dates given here mark her approximate reign, about 40 to 10 BCE.

She ruled Kush from its capital region around Meroë; her birth and death years are unknown, and her reign is estimated.

About 25 BCE, while the Roman prefect's army was away, Kushite forces struck the Roman frontier in southern Egypt like a sudden flood, taking towns including Syene, Elephantine and Philae.

The prefect Gaius Petronius struck back about 24 BCE and sacked the Kushite city of Napata.

Peace was negotiated with Augustus about 21 to 20 BCE on Samos, and Kush is reported to have paid no tribute.

The Greek geographer Strabo describes a one-eyed, mannish queen "Candace" who led the Kushites; scholars generally identify her as Amanirenas.
