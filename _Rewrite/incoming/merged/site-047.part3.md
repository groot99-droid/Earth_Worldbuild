=== person/frederick-barbarossa
title: Frederick Barbarossa
type: person
date: c. 1122 – 1190
themes: war, religion, kinship
src: ead8b3bae4d7
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Frederick I, called Barbarossa, "Redbeard" (about 1122 to 1190), reigned as Holy Roman Emperor from 1155 until his death, and stood among medieval Europe's most powerful rulers, spending much of his reign contesting control of northern Italy.
--- fact 1
Elected King of Germany in 1152, he was crowned Holy Roman Emperor by the pope in 1155, then repeatedly invaded Italy to assert imperial authority over its wealthy independent city-states.
--- fact 2
The Lombard League, an alliance of northern Italian cities backed by the papacy, broke his forces at the Battle of Legnano in 1176, forcing him to recognize significant city autonomy in the Peace of Constance of 1183.
--- fact 3
He arranged the marriage of his son, Henry, to Constance of Sicily, a match that later drew the Kingdom of Sicily under his family's control.
--- fact 4
He drowned in 1190 while crossing a river in Anatolia during the Third Crusade, before reaching the Holy Land; his death led much of his army to disband or turn back rather than continue the campaign.
--- context
Frederick Barbarossa ruled the Holy Roman Empire in [[region/europe|Europe]] during the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]], contesting authority against both the papacy and the Italian city-states. See [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]], [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that Barbarossa's defeat at Legnano, a rare and decisive loss for an emperor to city militias rather than a rival monarch, shows the limits of imperial authority once wealthy urban communities organized collectively. His accidental death crossing a river, not a battle, then ended a Crusade that no strong start could save from being cut short by chance.
--- question 1
Would the Third Crusade's outcome for the German contingent have differed meaningfully had Barbarossa survived to reach the Holy Land?

=== person/frida-kahlo
title: Frida Kahlo
type: person
date: 1907 – 1954
themes: art, kinship
src: e12781d092b6
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Frida Kahlo (1907 to 1954) worked as a Mexican painter, best known for small, vivid self-portraits made in Mexico City. She painted through years of pain after a bus accident in 1925 and became a global cultural icon only after her death.
--- fact 1
Kahlo was born on 6 July 1907 in Coyoacán, now part of Mexico City, though she often gave 1910, the year the Mexican Revolution began, as her birth year.
--- fact 2
A bus accident on 17 September 1925 broke her spine, pelvis and right leg, ending her plan to study medicine; she began painting during her recovery.
--- fact 3
She married the muralist Diego Rivera in 1929, divorced him in 1939, and remarried him in 1940.
--- fact 4
Estimates of her output run from fewer than 150 to about 200 paintings, most of them self-portraits, including The Two Fridas, 1939, and The Broken Column, 1944.
--- fact 5
Her first solo show opened at the Julien Levy Gallery in New York in 1938. In 1939 the Louvre bought her painting The Frame, the first work by a Mexican artist to enter its collection.
--- fact 6
She died in Mexico City on 13 July 1954, officially of a pulmonary embolism; her work stayed little known until art historians rediscovered it in the late 1970s.
--- context
Frida Kahlo made her work in [[region/mesoamerica|Mesoamerica]] during the [[era/industrial-age|Industrial Age]], in the decades that followed the [[event/mexican-revolution|Mexican Revolution]]. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I see a painter who made her own body the subject of nearly everything she made, and who moved her own birth year to 1910, binding her life to a nation's revolution. Since the late 1970s I have watched her image hold together movements that did not exist while she lived.

A specialist could argue that this later fame flattens a private, uncertain woman into an emblem she never asked to be, which would weaken any reading of the icon as what she intended. What icon was ever exactly what the person intended?
--- question 1
How much of her public persona did she shape herself, and how much was added after 1954?
--- question 2
What was the exact cause of her death?

=== person/fu-hao
title: Fu Hao
type: person
date: c. 1250 – 1200 BCE
themes: war, religion, kinship
src: 9e69de0d18da
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Fu Hao, who died about 1200 BCE, served as a consort of the Shang king Wu Ding and is named in oracle bone inscriptions as both a military commander and a ritual leader. Her intact tomb, found in 1976, ranks among the richest of the Shang period; her birth year is unknown.
--- fact 1
Fu Hao is named in oracle bone inscriptions as a wife of the Shang king Wu Ding, whose reign is dated about 1250 to 1192 BCE, though dates vary by chronology; she is thought to have died about 1200 BCE.
--- fact 2
The inscriptions record her both leading military campaigns against neighboring groups and directing ritual sacrifices.
--- fact 3
Her tomb, M5, at Yinxu, the Shang capital site near Anyang in Henan, was found undisturbed in 1976 by a team led by Zheng Zhenxiang.
--- fact 4
It held 468 bronze objects and 755 jade objects, along with hundreds of bone objects and thousands of cowry shells.
--- fact 5
Inscriptions on many bronzes name her, as Fu Hao or as "Mother Xin," which is the basis for identifying the tomb with the woman of the oracle bones.
--- fact 6
Sixteen people and six dogs were buried alongside her, interpreted as sacrifices.
--- context
Fu Hao lived under the [[culture/shang-dynasty|Shang Dynasty]] in [[region/east-asia|East Asia]] during the [[era/bronze-age|Bronze Age]]; her tomb reveals the scale of [[technology/bronze-metallurgy|Bronze Metallurgy]] achieved by the Shang state. See [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]], [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
The Observer notes that the earliest written evidence for a woman commanding armies comes from divination records her husband made for other purposes, records that also ask about her illness and her childbirth. Her burial, with its large store of bronze and jade and its human sacrifices, turns a grief-rite into a display of status. The reading of the tomb and the texts as one life would weaken if the oracle bone Fu Hao and the woman in tomb M5 turned out to be different persons.
--- question 1
How large were the armies she led, and how reliable are the oracle bone figures?

=== person/funmilayo-ransome-kuti
title: Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti
type: person
date: 1900 – 1978
themes: kinship, trade
src: eb72a94a7552
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti (1900 to 1978) worked as a Nigerian teacher and campaigner who led the Abeokuta Women's Union in protests against a tax on women and against the local ruler, the Alake of Egbaland, in the 1940s.
--- fact 1
Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti was born on 25 October 1900 in Abeokuta, southwestern Nigeria, and studied there and in Cheshire, England, from 1919 to 1922.
--- fact 2
In 1925 she married the Reverend Israel Oludotun Ransome-Kuti; their children included the musician Fela Kuti and the physicians Beko and Olikoye Ransome-Kuti.
--- fact 3
She founded the Abeokuta Ladies' Club in 1932; it became the Abeokuta Women's Union about 1946 and is reported to have had about 20,000 members.
--- fact 4
In the late 1940s the union protested a tax on women and the rule of the Alake, Ademola II, who was forced from the throne for a time in 1949.
--- fact 5
She helped found the Federation of Nigerian Women's Societies in 1953, and in 1970 received the Lenin Peace Prize.
--- fact 6
On 18 February 1977 soldiers stormed her son's compound in Lagos; her family says she was thrown from an upper-floor window, and she died of her injuries on 13 April 1978, in accounts that officially blamed unknown soldiers.
--- context
Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti worked in [[region/sub-saharan-africa|Sub-Saharan Africa]] during the years of [[event/decolonization|Decolonization]], within the [[era/information-age|Information Age]]. See [[event/women-s-suffrage-movements|Women's Suffrage Movements]], [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]] and [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]].
--- observer
I see a claim-line between a colonial administration, a local king, and a body of market women redrawn by organized refusal, not by arms — a redrawing done with petitions, not weapons. I see the 1977 raid turn that same kind of state force, once met only with petitions, on her family instead.

A specialist could argue that colonial officials' wish to avoid unrest mattered as much as the union's own strength, which would weaken a reading that credits the organization alone. What credit is ever owed to one cause alone?
--- question 1
Who ordered and carried out the 1977 raid, and was anyone held responsible?
--- question 2
How much of the Alake's departure was caused by the union rather than by colonial officials' decisions?

=== person/galileo-galilei
title: Galileo Galilei
type: person
date: 1564 – 1642
themes: science, religion
src: cb62d6dbefc2
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Galileo Galilei (1564 to 1642) worked as an Italian scientist who used the telescope and experiments to support the Copernican view that the Earth orbits the Sun.
--- fact 1
Across 1609 and 1610 he observed the Moon's mountains and four moons of Jupiter.
--- fact 2
He studied falling bodies and motion through experiment.
--- fact 3
In 1633 the Roman Inquisition tried him, and he spent the rest of his life under house arrest.
--- fact 4
His 'Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems' appeared in 1632.
--- context
Galileo stands as a central figure of the [[event/scientific-revolution|Scientific Revolution]] and a predecessor of [[person/isaac-newton|Isaac Newton]].
--- observer
I look at his case and see two authorities meet: one held the sky by doctrine, one claimed it by looking — a telescope set against an institution. Which one should hold the sky, the one that always held it, or the one that only just looked up? Perhaps every new way of knowing must stand trial before the old one.
--- question 1
How much did his trial turn on science versus politics?

=== person/genghis-khan
title: Genghis Khan
type: person
date: c. 1162 – 1227
themes: war, trade, kinship
src: d2798f298106
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Genghis Khan, born Temujin, about 1162 to 1227, united the Mongol tribes in 1206 and founded the Mongol Empire.
--- fact 1
He built his army on merit and loyalty rather than clan.
--- fact 2
His campaigns swept over Central Asia, northern China, and Persia.
--- fact 3
He promoted a written script and a legal code, the Yassa, and encouraged religious tolerance.
--- fact 4
He died in 1227; the location of his tomb remains unknown.
--- context
See [[event/mongol-conquests|Mongol Conquests]] and [[culture/mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]]; his empire brought the [[region/central-asian-steppe|Central Asian Steppe]] to the center of Eurasian history.
--- observer
The Observer notes that Genghis reorganized a kinship-based order into a merit-based institution, one root of both the empire's success and its brutality.
--- question 1
How reliable are the estimates of the deaths caused by his campaigns?
