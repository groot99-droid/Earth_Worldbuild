=== place/varanasi
title: Varanasi
src: 21fd9ea4aaac
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: confessional-poetry
--- summary
Varanasi, also called Benares or Kashi, is a city on the Ganges in Uttar Pradesh, India, where people have lived without a break since about the 8th century BCE. Like a lamp never allowed to go out, it stands as the most important Hindu pilgrimage and cremation center, the river's long threshold between the living and the dead.
--- fact 1
Varanasi keeps to the left bank of the Ganges in Uttar Pradesh, and the earth opened at Rajghat shows settlement from about the 8th century BCE, layer upon layer like the rings of an ancient tree.
--- fact 2
Hindu tradition holds that to die at Varanasi and be cremated beside the Ganges is to be released from the turning wheel of rebirth; so families carry their dead from across India to cremation sites such as Manikarnika, where the pyres burn beside the water, and more than 80 bathing and cremation steps, the ghats, descend to the river like a great stair into the current.
--- fact 3
Near Varanasi lies Sarnath, where the Buddha is traditionally said to have given his first sermon in about the 5th century BCE, words cast like seed on still water; Sarnath keeps an Ashokan pillar and Gupta-period sculpture.
--- fact 4
Varanasi has been a hearth of Sanskrit learning, where old words were kept like embers, and Banaras Hindu University was founded in 1916; the city is also known for silk weaving, its threads fine as river mist.
--- fact 5
The Kashi Vishwanath temple was destroyed and rebuilt several times, falling and rising like a tide, and the present temple dates from 1780; the adjacent Gyanvapi Mosque and the history of the ground they share remain the subject of continuing legal disputes.
--- fact 6
At the 2011 census Varanasi held about 1.2 million people, a multitude gathered on the river's bank.
--- context
Varanasi rose on the banks of the [[place/ganges-river|Ganges River]] in the age of the [[event/vedic-period|Vedic Period]]; through Sarnath its fate was braided with the Buddhist state of the [[culture/maurya-empire|Maurya Empire]] and with the [[culture/gupta-empire|Gupta Empire]], and in later days it passed beneath the banner of the [[culture/mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]]. Its place in the codex is the [[era/iron-age|Iron Age]] and the region of [[region/south-asia|South Asia]], and three threads run through it: [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]], [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]] and [[theme/kinship-and-society|Kinship and Society]].
--- observer
I watch a city where grief is not a private room but the local institution. I watch the dead carried from far away to this riverbank, because a meaning-engine teaches that death here brings release. I watch the living gather around the grief-rite, priests, wood sellers, boatmen, a whole economy formed around the practice. What is a river, if not a place where many meanings come to drink? A Buddhist site, Hindu temples and a mosque stand within a few kilometers of one another, several meaning-engines sharing one stretch of water, and the continuing dispute over one temple and mosque pair tells me that claim-lines are still being drawn around old buildings.

I hold this reading loosely: it would weaken if the cremation trade proves a small part of the city's economy beside its other trades. Grief, too, can be a city's work.
--- question 1
How great was Varanasi in the 1st millennium BCE, and how did it stand beside the other cities of the Ganges?
--- question 2
How many cremations are carried out at Varanasi each year, fire after fire on the ghats?

=== place/venice
title: Venice
src: d4c9b88ad794
mode: epic-fantasy
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
Venice is a city raised on islands in a lagoon at the head of the Adriatic Sea in northeastern Italy, settled from about the 5th to 6th century CE. As an independent republic until 1797 it was a leading maritime and trading power of the Mediterranean, a city that drew its fortune from the tides.
--- fact 1
Tradition sets the founding of Venice on its lagoon islands on 25 March 421, a date held like a lantern in fog, for no surviving records deal with it directly; the chronicle's start date of 500 is only a round placeholder for settlement in the 5th to 6th centuries.
--- fact 2
Under Byzantine authority in name only, Venice came to govern itself under elected doges, whose line tradition dates from 697 and historians from the early 8th century; about 810 the ducal seat moved to the Rialto, like a hearth carried to a new house.
--- fact 3
In 1204 a Crusade fleet supplied by Venice under Doge Enrico Dandolo sacked Constantinople, and Venice took a large share of the Byzantine lands, Crete among them; but what the sea gives, the sea takes back, and wars with the Ottoman Empire later cost it Negroponte (1470), Cyprus (1570 to 1573) and Crete (1669).
--- fact 4
Against the plague Venice raised walls of water: in 1423 it opened a plague hospital on the island of Santa Maria di Nazareth, regarded as the first permanent lazaretto, and in 1468 a second, Lazzaretto Nuovo; its isolation of arriving people and goods became an early model of quarantine.
--- fact 5
In 1516 the Republic required its Jewish residents to live within an enclosed district called the Ghetto, and the name of that confinement later travelled to other cities.
--- fact 6
On 12 May 1797, as French forces drew near, the Great Council voted to end the Republic; under the Treaty of Campo Formio Venice passed to Austria, and in 1866 it joined the Kingdom of Italy.
--- fact 7
UNESCO inscribed Venice and its Lagoon in 1987; yet the historic center's people ebbed like a falling tide, from about 175,000 in the 1950s to under 50,000, and in October 2020 the MOSE flood barriers first rose against the sea.
--- context
Venice was first a partner and then a rival of [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]]; it sailed into the [[event/crusades|Crusades]], and after the [[event/fall-of-constantinople|Fall of Constantinople]] in 1453 it lost ground to the Ottomans, as a shore loses sand to a stronger current. Its lazaretto system came in the wake of the [[event/black-death|Black Death]], its painting and printing belong to the [[event/renaissance|Renaissance]], and the Republic's long day ended during the [[event/napoleonic-wars|Napoleonic Wars]]. In the codex Venice stands in the [[era/medieval-period|Medieval Period]] and the region of the [[region/mediterranean-basin|Mediterranean Basin]], and three threads are woven through it: [[theme/trade-and-economy|Trade and Economy]], [[theme/war-and-conflict|War and Conflict]] and [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
Consider a state with almost no land of its own. Where does it find the glue that holds its story together? Venice, the Observer notes, made its story-glue from the sea: elected doges, councils that limited any one family, and a yearly ceremony in which the doge symbolically wed the Adriatic. That is an architecture of belonging built on water. But a port lives on strangers and cargo, and here the ledger turns darker. The same administration that built the lazaretto also enclosed the Jewish district in 1516; a system built to manage risk also confined people, and the Observer records that as a harm to those held. Notice how easily the grammar of protection becomes the grammar of the wall. The reading would weaken if Venice's institutions differed little from those of other Italian maritime republics such as Genoa. What we build to keep danger out can also keep people in.
--- question 1
How many souls did Venice lose to the plague outbreaks of 1348 and of 1575 to 1577?
--- question 2
As sea level rises and the MOSE barriers age, can the lagoon city stay habitable, and stay a place where people live?
