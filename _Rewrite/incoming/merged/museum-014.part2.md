=== people--mary-cassatt--1
src: 5cfcae97ae72
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Mary Cassatt (22 May 1844 to 14 June 1926) was an American painter who spent most of her grown life in France and was the only American to exhibit with the French Impressionists. She is honoured for tender, unsentimental images of women and children.

She was born in Allegheny City, now part of Pittsburgh, into a wealthy family. Against her father's wishes she studied at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts from about 1860, then crossed to Paris in 1866.

Women could not enter the École des Beaux-Arts, so she studied privately and copied Old Masters in the Louvre. Weary of the Salon's conservative taste, she took up Degas's invitation in 1877 to exhibit with the Impressionists, and she showed with them in 1879, 1880, 1881 and 1886.

Her parents and sister came to Paris to live with her, and her family became her favourite models.

In 1890 an exhibition of Japanese prints in Paris kindled a set of colour prints, usually counted as ten, joining drypoint and aquatint. From them she took flat colour, strong outlines and cropped compositions, like light seen through a cut window.

In 1893 she painted the mural Modern Woman for the Woman's Building at the Chicago World's Columbian Exposition; it has been lost.

=== people--menelik-ii--1
src: 5c8aec1f41c8
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Menelik II (1844 to 1913) was emperor of Ethiopia from 1889 and the leader who defeated Italy at Adwa in 1896.

He expanded the empire southward and founded Addis Ababa as the capital.

He imported modern arms and pursued technological modernization.

He won the Battle of Adwa on 1 March 1896, turning back a colonial invasion.

His empire's expansion brought conquest of neighboring peoples, and it is debated. A victory against empire and an empire's own conquests can belong to the same reign.
