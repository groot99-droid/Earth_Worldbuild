=== place/valley-of-the-kings

--- summary
The Valley of the Kings is a valley on the west bank of the Nile opposite Thebes, in Egypt, where pharaohs of the New Kingdom were buried in rock-cut tombs from about 1500 to 1070 BCE, their sleep hidden in the stone heart of the hills.
--- fact 1
The Valley of the Kings served for royal burials of the 18th to 20th Dynasties, from about 1500 BCE until about 1070 BCE, and holds more than 60 tombs cut into limestone hills, doors folded into the rock.
--- fact 2
The rulers chose hidden rock-cut tombs in place of the pyramids of earlier periods, such as those at Giza, trading the mountain made by hands for the mountain that already stood.
--- fact 3
Most tombs were robbed in antiquity; records of trials of tomb robbers survive from the 20th Dynasty, and priests later moved royal mummies to hidden caches, shepherding their kings from grave to grave like a flock through the night.
--- fact 4
The workmen's village of Deir el-Medina yielded ration accounts and an account of a strike over unpaid rations in about 1157 BCE, often described as one of the earliest recorded labor strikes: the hands that carved eternity laid down their tools.
--- fact 5
Howard Carter found the tomb of Tutankhamun (KV62) in November 1922, largely intact and holding thousands of objects, a hoard that had largely escaped the robbers' hands.
--- fact 6
UNESCO inscribed Ancient Thebes with its Necropolis, including the Valley of the Kings, in 1979, and the hidden valley was named among the world's treasures.
--- context
The Valley of the Kings served the [[culture/ancient-egyptians|Ancient Egyptians]] of the New Kingdom, whose main temple was [[place/karnak|Karnak]], and it took the place of the pyramid tradition of the [[place/giza-necropolis|Giza Necropolis]], as a river cuts a new channel. It stands in the [[era/bronze-age|Bronze Age]] and the region [[region/nile-valley|Nile Valley]], and belongs to the theme threads of [[theme/religion-and-belief|Religion and Belief]], [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]] and [[theme/technology|Technology]].
--- observer
I think of the move from monumental pyramids to hidden rock-cut tombs. I think of rulers changing how they guarded their grief-rite investment, concealment in place of display. I think of the tombs, robbed anyway.

What can be hidden from those who know where it is? The robberies suggest that no design made a store of wealth safe from the people who knew where it was.

And I think of the workforce at Deir el-Medina, building a ruler's afterlife, paid in rations, able to act collectively when payment failed. I remember that eternity was built by people who needed to be paid.

The reading would weaken if the shift from pyramids proves to have been driven mainly by changes in belief and not by fear of robbery. What we hide, we also reveal by hiding.

--- question 2
