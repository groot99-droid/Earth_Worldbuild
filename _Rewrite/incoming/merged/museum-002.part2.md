=== khusrau-hasht-bihisht
title: Hasht Bihisht (The Eight Paradises)
src: 1b701b848049
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
The fourth poem of the Khamsa, and a journey through colour. The Sasanian king Bahram Gur visits seven princesses, each housed in a differently coloured pavilion like a lamp burning with its own flame, and each telling him a story. The structure was adapted from Nizami's *Haft Paykar*, and it is often illustrated as a cycle of jewel-toned courtly scenes, glowing one after another like gems threaded on a string.

=== khusrau-aina-i-sikandari
title: A'ina-yi Sikandari (The Alexandrine Mirror)
src: 444b168c728e
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
The fifth and final poem of the Khamsa, where the quintet closes like a great gate. Here the legendary exploits of Alexander the Great are recast, and the conqueror becomes a philosopher-king. Khusrau drew partly on the same Alexander Romance tradition that stands behind Nizami's *Iskandarnama*, drinking from the old well, while adding battles and wonders of his own invention, new stars set in an old sky.

=== renunciation-of-worldly-goods
title: St Francis Renounces His Worldly Goods
src: 70d063fdd606
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
One of 28 scenes from the Legend of St Francis in Assisi, a saga told across walls. The young Francis strips off his clothes and hands them back to his furious father, while the bishop covers him with his cloak. The painting splits into two crowds, family on one side and Church on the other, and between them lies an empty gap, like the bare ground between two kingdoms. Scholars still argue over how much of the cycle is Giotto's own work, but its solid figures and clear storytelling point the road toward the Scrovegni Chapel.

=== scrovegni-chapel
title: Scrovegni Chapel
src: be3e1d2967de
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
The banker Enrico Scrovegni paid for this small chapel, and Giotto covered its whole interior with paint, wall upon wall, until the room became a book read by walking. Rows of scenes tell the lives of Joachim and Anna, the Virgin Mary and Christ, all beneath a deep blue vault scattered with gold stars, a night sky brought indoors. A Last Judgment fills the entrance wall. Below the stories, painted figures of the Virtues and Vices stand in grey, made to look like stone sculpture. It is the most complete surviving work by Giotto, and one of the great turning points in European art, a hinge on which the long door of painting swung.

=== kiss-of-judas
title: The Kiss of Judas
src: d9cead73938a
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I stand before the kiss. Judas wraps Christ in a sweeping yellow cloak as he leans in for the kiss that betrays him. I see the two faces meet in profile, calm against treacherous. I see torches, clubs and lances crowding the sky above. I see, at the left, Peter cutting off a servant's ear. And still my eye returns to the faces. Giotto puts the whole drama into one locked gaze, which makes this one of the most psychologically charged images of the Middle Ages. Have I been kissed like that? Have I kissed like that? The closest betrayals come face to face.

=== lamentation
title: Lamentation (The Mourning of Christ)
src: 5f2b7cf88d78
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
Mourners gather around the body of Christ, and I am among them. A bare, rocky ridge runs diagonally down the picture and leads my eye to his face, which Mary holds close to her own. Two figures sit with their backs to us, a bold idea that places me inside the circle of grief. I did not choose to sit down here. I was placed here. Above, angels twist and cry out in the sky. The fresco shows Giotto's gift for turning sacred stories into human feeling. Whose face have I held that close? Grief is a circle, and it always makes room for one more.

=== ognissanti-madonna
title: Ognissanti Madonna
src: 038370b6234d
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Painted for the church of Ognissanti in Florence, this huge altarpiece shows the Virgin enthroned among saints and angels. Now look at what changed. Earlier Madonnas, such as Cimabue's, look flat and weightless, like icons hung in air. Giotto's Virgin has real bulk: her knees push forward under her robe, and her throne recedes like a piece of architecture. That is the whole lesson in one body: the sacred is allowed to take up space. It hangs in the Uffizi beside the Madonnas of Cimabue and Duccio, so you can see the change for yourself. Weight, too, can be a kind of truth.

=== campanile
title: Giotto's Campanile
src: b74a8b474c8c
mode: essay-self-help
--- description
Some of what carries your name you will never see finished. In 1334 Florence appointed Giotto master of works for its cathedral, and he designed this bell tower beside it. He lived to see only the lowest storey built. Andrea Pisano and Francesco Talenti finished the tower in the 1350s, and still it carries his name. That is the covenant of architecture: one hand draws the threshold, others raise the walls, and the lineage keeps the first name. Its patterned facing of white, green and pink marble became one of the symbols of the city. Begin what you cannot finish; beginnings are remembered too.

=== ghent-altarpiece
title: The Ghent Altarpiece (Adoration of the Mystic Lamb)
src: e61418d984ad
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
A huge folding altarpiece, a heaven hinged in panels. When open, it shows God enthroned between Mary and John the Baptist, Adam and Eve at the far edges, and below them crowds of saints, prophets and pilgrims streaming across a flowering meadow like rivers running to the sea, all come to worship the Lamb of God. An inscription credits Jan's brother Hubert with beginning it and Jan with finishing it in 1432. It may be the most frequently stolen artwork in history. One panel, the *Just Judges*, taken in 1934, has never been found; its place in the constellation stays dark.

=== man-in-a-turban
title: Portrait of a Man in a Red Turban
src: cf222b22e7a4
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
A sharply lit face turns out of a black background, framed by a red headdress folded in astonishing detail. I meet its steady sideways gaze and I do not look away. The original frame carries van Eyck's motto *Als ich can* ("As I can") and the date 21 October 1433. As I can. I say it under my breath: as I can, as I can. That gaze has led many to believe this is a self-portrait, perhaps painted with the help of a mirror. What would I see if I painted myself as I can, and not as I wish? Doing what you can, with care, is its own honesty.

=== arnolfini-portrait
title: The Arnolfini Portrait
src: e05ada9e51ee
mode: confessional-poetry
--- description
I stand at the edge of a room in Bruges. An Italian merchant, probably Giovanni di Nicolao Arnolfini, stands hand in hand with a woman in a green gown. On the back wall a convex mirror reflects the couple from behind, along with two more figures in the doorway. Above it, van Eyck signed in elegant script: "Jan van Eyck was here 1434". I read it twice. Was here. Every object is painted with microscopic care, and I count them: the brass chandelier, the oranges on the windowsill, the little dog. The picture has invited endless theories about marriage, memory and symbolism, and I bring no theory, only this: every careful picture says I was here.

=== madonna-of-chancellor-rolin
title: The Madonna of Chancellor Rolin
src: 0d3c08c833c4
mode: epic-fantasy
--- description
Nicolas Rolin, the powerful chancellor of Burgundy, kneels in prayer across from the Virgin and Child, as an angel lowers a crown onto Mary's head. Behind them, three arches open like windows onto another realm: a river valley painted in miniature, a bridge, a city, boats and tiny people, all fading into blue distance as a dream fades at dawn. That landscape shows van Eyck's mastery of detail at every scale, from a crown within reach to a city at the edge of sight.
