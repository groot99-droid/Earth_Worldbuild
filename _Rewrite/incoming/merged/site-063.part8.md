=== source/pnas-2022-on-stature-and-health-of-early-european-farmers
src: 5d85e7e0ebac
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
The evidence here, drawn from 167 prehistoric Europeans, shows Neolithic individuals falling shorter than their genetic scores predicted, by an average of 3.82 cm relative to the Upper Paleolithic and Mesolithic and 2.21 cm relative to post-Neolithic groups, read as a sign of poorer health with the adoption of agriculture.
--- extra 1: Caveats
This one was read in full on 2026-09-26, drawn from the Europe PMC full-text XML of the open-access copy — PMC9169634, 57,163 characters. The claim of shorter stature and worse health 'in 19 of 21 societies', which an earlier check had cited here, is not in this paper and has been removed. The paper itself uses stature as a proxy for health; the results are modest in statistical terms, P = 0.040 and 0.068, and they were attenuated once genome-wide ancestry was also accounted for.

=== source/pnas-2024-civil-war-mortality-estimates
src: 2a290d47b7d9
mode: essay-self-help
observer_mode: essay-self-help
--- summary
A national estimate of about 698,000 Civil War deaths comes from full-count census records, with a bounding interval of roughly 50,000 either way, 647,439 to 748,561, set against the conventional count of 618,222 and the roughly 750,000 — 751,562, bounds 632,115 to 871,009 — that Hacker's 1 percent census sample gives when recomputed by the authors. It also finds that about 13 percent of military-age native-born white men in the core Confederate states died, against about 5 percent in the core Union states.
--- extra 1: Caveats
This one was read in full on 2026-09-25 from the Europe PMC full-text XML of the open-access copy — PMC11621511 — since pnas.org returns HTTP 403 to automated requests and the PMC web page shows a CAPTCHA, neither of which was bypassed. This is a Brief Report. The earlier citation gave 25 September 2024, which is the acceptance date; Europe PMC instead gives first publication as 18 November 2024. The claim that the toll was 'much greater in the Confederate states' concerns rates: about 13 percent of military-age native-born white men in the core Confederate states, against about 5 percent in the core Union states. In absolute numbers, though, the Union lost more men — about 400,000 or more, against roughly 300,000 for the Confederacy, on the paper's own tally. The estimate measures excess mortality, which includes deaths of veterans after the war and some civilian deaths, and it rests on standard assumptions for foreign-born and Black soldier deaths, figures the census method itself cannot estimate.
