--- summary
Artemisia Gentileschi (1593 to about 1654) was an Italian Baroque painter, known for powerful heroines rendered with Caravaggesque drama. She was the first woman admitted to Florence's Academy of the Arts of Drawing.
--- fact 1
Artemisia was born in Rome on 8 July 1593, the eldest child of the painter Orazio Gentileschi, a friend and follower of Caravaggio; she trained in his workshop and painted Susanna and the Elders by the age of 17.
--- fact 2
In 1611 Agostino Tassi, a painter working with her father, raped her. At the seven-month trial in 1612, thumbscrews were used on her to test her testimony. Tassi was convicted and sentenced to exile from Rome, but the exile was never carried out, and he spent less than a year in prison.
--- fact 3
She married the Florentine painter Pierantonio Stiattesi and moved to Florence, where she won the patronage of Grand Duke Cosimo II de' Medici, befriended Galileo Galilei, and in 1616 was admitted to the Accademia delle Arti del Disegno; she learned to read and write in these years.
--- fact 4
From about 1630 she settled in Naples; about 1638 she travelled to London to join her father at the court of Charles I. She is thought to have died in Naples about 1654, though some have speculated she died in the Naples plague of 1656.
--- fact 5
She specialised in strong women from the Bible and ancient history — Judith, Susanna, Jael, Cleopatra and Lucretia — and often lent her own features to saints and allegories.
--- fact 6
Key works include Susanna and the Elders (1610), Judith Slaying Holofernes (a first version about 1612 to 1613, a second about 1614 to 1620) and Self-Portrait as the Allegory of Painting (about 1638 to 1639).
--- fact 7
After her death, many works were credited to her father or others; the art historian Roberto Longhi wrote about her in 1916, and from the 1970s feminist scholars, notably Mary D. Garrard, made her a central figure. The National Gallery, London, held an exhibition in 2020.
--- context
Artemisia worked in [[region/europe|Europe]] during the [[era/early-modern-period|Early Modern Period]], learning from [[person/caravaggio|Caravaggio]]'s example and following the path opened by [[person/sofonisba-anguissola|Sofonisba Anguissola]]. She befriended [[person/galileo-galilei|Galileo Galilei]]. See [[theme/art-and-ritual|Art and Ritual]].
--- observer
I find that for centuries she was known through a trial and a father, before she was known through her own paintings — a story of harm placed ahead of the work. The court of 1612 tested her testimony with pain, calling it a procedure for finding truth. Her recovery since the 1970s is itself a change in the story we tell: a later movement found in her a founding figure. Attribution of some of her paintings remains uncertain, so the portrait I have of her may still change.
