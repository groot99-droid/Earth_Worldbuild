=== people--ludwig-ii--1
src: 4fa74e182687
mode: horror-prose
--- description
Ludwig II (1845 to 1886). Sometimes called the "Fairy Tale King." King of Bavaria from 1864 until his death. Remembered for extravagant romantic castles. Remembered for his patronage of the composer Richard Wagner.

He was an early, eager patron of Richard Wagner. He paid off the composer's debts. He funded productions. Then political opposition to Wagner's influence at court forced the composer to leave Munich.

Castles. Several, elaborate. Most famously Neuschwanstein, begun in 1869, inspired partly by Wagner's operas. Paid for from his personal fortune, not the Bavarian state treasury. Still, the debts grew deep.

Bavaria defeated alongside Austria in the 1866 Austro-Prussian War. Bavaria folded into the German Empire in 1871. His practical authority shrinking, year by year. The throne still his.

1886. A government commission cites his mounting debts. His reclusive behavior. He is declared mentally unfit to rule. Confined. The next day he dies in Lake Starnberg, under unclear circumstances. Accident. Suicide. Possible foul play. Still debated.

--- description
Kate Sheppard (1848 to 1934) was an English-born campaigner who led the movement for women's suffrage in New Zealand. The Electoral Act of 1893 made New Zealand the first self-governing country in which women voted in national elections. Her method is worth studying, because it was patient and it was counted.

She was born on 10 March 1848 in Liverpool, England, and emigrated to New Zealand, arriving in 1869. She died in Christchurch on 13 July 1934.

From 1887 she was national superintendent for franchise and legislation of the Women's Christian Temperance Union, which made votes for women a central aim.

Look at the numbers grow. Petitions to Parliament carried about 10,000 signatures in 1891, about 20,000 in 1892 and 31,872 in 1893, the largest petition presented to Parliament until then. Persistence, written down name by name, becomes a force no legislature can ignore.

The Governor signed the Electoral Act on 19 September 1893, and women voted for the first time in the election of 28 November 1893.

The Act gave the vote to women of all races, including Māori women in the Māori electorates, but women could not stand for Parliament until 1919. Every threshold crossed reveals the next one.

=== people--kate-sheppard--2

--- description
Kate Sheppard (1848 to 1934) was an English-born campaigner who led the struggle for women's suffrage in New Zealand. The Electoral Act of 1893 made New Zealand the first self-governing country in which women voted in national elections.

She was born on 10 March 1848 in Liverpool, England, and crossed the sea to New Zealand, arriving in 1869. She died in Christchurch on 13 July 1934.

From 1887 she was national superintendent for franchise and legislation of the Women's Christian Temperance Union, which made votes for women a central aim.

Petitions to Parliament gathered about 10,000 signatures in 1891, about 20,000 in 1892 and 31,872 in 1893, rising like a tide, the largest petition presented to Parliament until then.

On 19 September 1893 the Governor signed the Electoral Act, and on 28 November 1893 women voted for the first time.

The Act gave the vote to women of all races, among them Māori women in the Māori electorates, but women could not stand for Parliament until 1919.
