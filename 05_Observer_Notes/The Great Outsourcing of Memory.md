## Question
Why does a species with a large, capable memory keep moving its memory outside the body?

## What the Record Shows
- The earliest writing, in Mesopotamia about 3400 to 3200 BCE, recorded goods and debts; see [[Invention of Writing]].
- The [[Alphabet]] lowered the cost of learning to read and write.
- [[Paper]] and the [[Movable-Type Printing Press]] made copies cheap.
- Some societies used other memory systems, such as the Inca quipu and Aboriginal songlines; see [[Inca Empire]] and [[Aboriginal Australians]].
- The [[Digital Revolution]] now stores far more information than any individual could read.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer calls this memory-outsourcing. Each step made records cheaper, more durable, and more widely shared. The species' knowledge now grows across generations far faster than its biology can change. The Observer notes that memory stored outside the mind can outlast civilizations, but also that it must be read, and that scripts and formats can be lost.

## Limits of This Reading
'Outsourcing' can suggest a loss. Oral cultures often trained memory to remarkable precision, and writing has sometimes reduced that skill. The change is a trade-off, not simple progress.
