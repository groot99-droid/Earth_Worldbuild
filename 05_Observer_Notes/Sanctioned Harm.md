## Question
Why does a species with strong inhibitions against harming its own kind organize killing on a mass scale?

## What the Record Shows
- Evidence for organized violence appears in prehistory, but its scale grew with settled societies and states.
- Empires such as the [[Mongol Empire]] and the [[Romans]] used organized force at large scale.
- Industrial states scaled the harm in [[World War I]] and [[World War II]].
- The Holocaust shows that organized killing can be run as a bureaucratic process.
- The [[Nuclear Age]] introduced weapons that could end a civilization.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer calls this sanctioned harm: violence that a group approves through its story-glue. The same person who would not kill a stranger may do so under a uniform, a flag, or a doctrine. The Observer notes that the same story-glue that enables cooperation can be aimed against others. It notes with equal seriousness the counter-tradition: laws of war, pacifist movements, and the practice of [[Mahatma Gandhi]]'s nonviolence.

## Limits of This Reading
The Observer records harm without endorsing it. This essay does not aim to explain away responsibility: individuals and institutions made real choices that could have been different.
