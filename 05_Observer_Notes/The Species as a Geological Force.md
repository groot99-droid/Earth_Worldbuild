## Question
When does a species become a planetary process, and what does the record say about how such changes end?

## What the Record Shows
- Human population passed one billion around 1804 and reached about eight billion by 2022.
- Greenhouse gas emissions, especially since 1950, have measurably changed the climate; see [[Modern Climate Change]].
- Species extinction rates are far above the long-term background rate.
- Some geologists proposed the [[Anthropocene]] as a new epoch; the formal proposal was declined in 2024, though the concept is widely used.
- Earlier biological transformations of the planet include the [[Great Oxidation Event]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the species has become a driver of planetary change without having chosen this role. The [[Great Oxidation Event]] shows an earlier case of life reshaping the air. The [[Permian-Triassic Extinction]] shows how rapid carbon release can end most life. What is new is not the effect but the awareness: this is the first planetary shift whose cause can describe itself, model its future, and choose otherwise. Whether it does so is the open question of the record.

## Limits of This Reading
The record cannot say what the species will do next. The Observer's historical parallels are illustrations, not predictions, and the Anthropocene is an interpretation, not a settled fact.
