## Question
Why did foragers who lived well without farms take up a way of life that often looked worse for the individual?

## What the Record Shows
- Farming arose independently in several regions; see [[The Agricultural Revolution]].
- Skeletons from early farmers often show more disease and shorter stature than those of foragers.
- Farming supported larger populations, storage, and inequality.
- The sites [[Göbekli Tepe]] and [[Çatalhöyük]] show that monuments and towns arose early, and in different ways.
- The [[Green Revolution]] made a second large jump in food production in the 20th century.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer calls this the Settling. Its central bargain was to exchange leisure and variety for numbers and security. Once populations grew, going back was not easy, since fewer people could be fed by foraging. The species became dependent on its own creation. The Observer treats this as a repeated pattern: adopt a technology for advantage, then find that the technology has reshaped the conditions of life.

## Limits of This Reading
The 'bargain' story is contested. Some scholars, such as [[The Dawn of Everything (Graeber and Wengrow)]], stress that societies also chose and changed their arrangements freely and that many early communities experimented widely.
