## Question
Most animals cooperate with kin or with familiar individuals. How does one species cooperate in groups of millions who will never meet?

## What the Record Shows
- Small foraging bands relied on personal knowledge and kin ties.
- Cities from about 3500 BCE required cooperation among strangers; see [[The Urban Revolution]].
- Shared gods, rulers, and laws supplied common stories; see [[Sumerians]] and [[Ancient Egyptians]].
- Universal religions and ethical systems of the [[Axial Age]] addressed strangers as members of one moral community.
- Modern states, corporations, and currencies also rest on shared belief; see [[Age of Revolutions]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer calls this story-glue. A corporation, a nation, a currency, and a law code exist chiefly because many people act as though they do. This lets the species coordinate on scales no other animal reaches, and also lets it be led into shared errors. Changing the story can change behavior quickly, without changing anyone's biology.

## Limits of This Reading
The concept can be stretched too far. Material conditions, force, and self-interest also matter, and not every institution is only a story.
