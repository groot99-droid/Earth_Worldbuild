## Citation
Web search results on lunar oxygen isotopes and the giant impact hypothesis, listing Science ('Oxygen Isotopes and the Moon-Forming Giant Impact', 2001; 'Identification of the giant impactor Theia in lunar rocks', 2014) and PNAS (2024).

## What It Is Used For
Oxygen isotope ratios in Apollo samples matching Earth's very closely, which is consistent with the giant impact hypothesis only if the impactor (Theia) had an Earth-like composition.

## Caveats
Consulted only through web search result summaries on 2026-09-25 (results listed Science, PNAS, PubMed, astronomy.com and arXiv); none of the pages was opened. The summaries describe the near-identical isotopes both as support and as a challenge for the hypothesis, and a 2024 paper reports a small (12 parts per million) difference. The note states it as consistent with the hypothesis and also a puzzle.
