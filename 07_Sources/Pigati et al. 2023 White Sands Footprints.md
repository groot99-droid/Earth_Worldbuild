## Citation
Pigati, J. S., Springer, K. B., Honke, J. S., Wahl, D., Champagne, M. R., Zimmerman, S. R. H., Gray, H. J., Santucci, V. L., Odess, D., Bustos, D., and Bennett, M. R. 2023. 'Independent age estimates resolve the controversy of ancient human footprints at White Sands.' Science 382(6666): 73-75.

## What It Is Used For
The age of the White Sands footprints (reportedly about 21,000 to 23,000 years ago from earlier radiocarbon dating of Ruppia seeds), and the two new checks named in the abstract: calibrated radiocarbon ages of terrestrial pollen from the same stratigraphic horizons and optically stimulated luminescence ages of sediments from the footprint-bearing sequence. The authors conclude that the original chronology is robust and that humans were present in North America during the Last Glacial Maximum.

## Caveats
Only the abstract was read (the PubMed record, fetched on 2026-09-25 through NCBI E-utilities). science.org returns HTTP 403 and the PubMed web page shows a cookie wall, neither of which was bypassed, and no open-access copy of the full paper turned up. This source therefore confirms only what the abstract says: the abstract calls the dated material 'terrestrial pollen' and the luminescence dating 'of sediments', so the wording 'conifer pollen', 'quartz' and the year of the first seed dates (2021), which the Peopling of the Americas note used to carry, is not confirmed here and was removed from that note until the full paper is read; sample counts, error ranges and methods are also unread. The abstract says the earlier seed ages were controversial because of possible old-carbon reservoir effects. Whether the age is universally accepted is not something the abstract can show; the vault treats it as well supported but contested, following Wikipedia on the Settlement of the Americas.
