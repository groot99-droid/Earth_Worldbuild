## Citation
'An integrative skeletal and paleogenomic analysis of stature variation suggests relatively reduced health for early European farmers.' PNAS (2022). Also PubMed, 'Stature and robusticity during the agricultural transition.'

## What It Is Used For
Evidence from 167 prehistoric Europeans that Neolithic individuals were shorter than their genetic scores predicted, by an average of 3.82 cm relative to the Upper Paleolithic and Mesolithic and 2.21 cm relative to post-Neolithic groups, read as a sign of poorer health with the adoption of agriculture.

## Caveats
Read in full on 2026-09-26 from the Europe PMC full-text XML of the open-access copy (PMC9169634; 57,163 characters). The claim of shorter stature and worse health 'in 19 of 21 societies', which an earlier check cited here, is not in this paper and was removed. The paper uses stature as a proxy for health; the results are modest in statistical terms (P = 0.040 and 0.068) and were attenuated when genome-wide ancestry was also accounted for.
