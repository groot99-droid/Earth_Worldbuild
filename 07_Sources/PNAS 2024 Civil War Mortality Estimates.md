## Citation
Barceló, J., Jensen, J. L., Peisakhin, L., and Zhai, H. 2024. 'New estimates of US Civil War mortality from full-census records.' Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 121(48): e2414919121. First published 18 November 2024.

## What It Is Used For
A national estimate of about 698,000 Civil War deaths from full-count census records, with a bounding interval of roughly 50,000 either way (647,439 to 748,561), set against the conventional count of 618,222 and the roughly 750,000 (751,562, bounds 632,115 to 871,009) that Hacker's 1 percent census sample gives when recomputed by the authors. Also the finding that about 13 percent of military-age native-born white men in the core Confederate states died against about 5 percent in the core Union states.

## Caveats
Read in full on 2026-09-25 from the Europe PMC full-text XML of the open-access copy (PMC11621511); pnas.org returns HTTP 403 to automated requests and the PMC web page shows a CAPTCHA, neither of which was bypassed. This is a Brief Report. The earlier citation gave 25 September 2024, which is the acceptance date; Europe PMC gives first publication as 18 November 2024. The claim that the toll was 'much greater in the Confederate states' concerns rates: about 13 percent of military-age native-born white men in the core Confederate states against about 5 percent in the core Union states. In absolute numbers the Union lost more men, about 400,000 or more against roughly 300,000 for the Confederacy on the paper's own tally. The estimate measures excess mortality, which includes deaths of veterans after the war and some civilian deaths, and it rests on standard assumptions for foreign-born and Black soldier deaths, which the census method cannot estimate.
