## Citation
Web search results on the age of the Solar System and Earth, listing Science ('The Absolute Chronology and Thermal Processing of Solids in the Solar Protoplanetary Disk'), Wikipedia ('Age of Earth', 'Clair Patterson'), Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and NASA Astrobiology.

## What It Is Used For
Calcium-aluminum-rich inclusions dated to about 4.567 billion years, Earth's age of 4.54 plus or minus 0.05 billion years, and Clair Patterson's 1956 lead-isotope age of about 4.55 billion years from the Canyon Diablo meteorite.

## Caveats
Consulted only through web search result summaries on 2026-09-25 (results listed Science, Wikipedia, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and NASA Astrobiology); none of the pages was opened. The summaries note that the inclusion age marks the Solar System's earliest solids, while Earth's age marks accretion or core formation and is slightly younger; the two numbers are not in conflict.
