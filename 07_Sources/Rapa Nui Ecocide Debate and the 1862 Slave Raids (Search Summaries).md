## Citation
Web search results on the 'ecocide' interpretation of Rapa Nui and the Peruvian slave raids, listing American Scientist ('Rethinking the Fall of Easter Island'), The Conversation, Scientific American, HISTORY, EBSCO Research Starters and Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution.

## What It Is Used For
The challenge to the ecocide story (a peak population nearer 3,000, a large role for introduced rats in deforestation) and the Peruvian slave raids of December 1862 to 1864 with the later smallpox epidemic.

## Caveats
Consulted only through web search result summaries on 2026-09-25 (results listed American Scientist, The Conversation, Scientific American, HISTORY, EBSCO Research Starters and several popular pages); none of the pages was opened. Figures such as about 1,000 captured in December 1862, more than 1,400 taken in 1862 to 1864, about a thousand smallpox deaths and a fall to about 100 people come from popular summaries and differ between accounts, so the note states them as reported. The maximum population of about 3,000 rests on a revised chronology and is itself debated.
