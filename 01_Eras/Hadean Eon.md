## Summary
The Hadean is the informal first eon of Earth's history, running from the planet's formation about 4.54 billion years ago to roughly 4.0 billion years ago. Earth was molten, then cooling, and heavily bombarded.

## Facts
- Earth and the Moon formed in the first tens of millions of years; see [[Formation of the Solar System and Earth]] and [[Theia Impact and the Formation of the Moon]].
- The oldest known Earth material is zircon crystals from the Jack Hills in Western Australia, dated to about 4.4 billion years ago.
- Those zircons suggest liquid water and some crust existed very early.
- The Hadean is not an official ICS unit; it is an informal name for the pre-rock-record interval.

## Context & Connections
The Hadean sets up everything later: the Moon stabilizes Earth's tilt, and volatile-rich material delivered to the young planet gave it oceans. The eon ends where the surviving rock record begins, at the start of the [[Archean Eon]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer files the Hadean under preconditions. Nothing here is a subject of study, but every later fact leans on the planet's composition, its Moon, and its water.

## Open Questions
- How early did stable oceans and continents exist?
- Was there a Late Heavy Bombardment, or a gradual decline in impacts?
