## Summary
The Tonian Period is the first period of the Neoproterozoic Era, running from 1.0 billion to 720 million years ago. It covers the break-up of the supercontinent Rodinia and the diversification of eukaryotes before the Cryogenian ice ages.

## Facts
- The Tonian Period spans 1.0 billion to 720 million years ago. Its base and its top are both set by numerical ages, and the top falls close to the onset of the first Cryogenian glaciation.
- The supercontinent Rodinia began to rift apart during the Tonian, roughly 800 to 750 million years ago.
- Eukaryotic microfossils show growing diversity. Vase-shaped microfossils in the Chuar Group of the Grand Canyon, roughly 780 to 740 million years old, are interpreted as the shells of amoeba-like protists.
- Phosphatic scale microfossils from the Fifteenmile Group of Yukon, Canada, reported from rocks roughly 810 to 720 million years old, are among the earliest evidence of eukaryotes forming mineral parts.
- The environmental record becomes more variable toward the end of the period, with proposed rises in oxygen; possible glacial deposits near 750 million years ago are debated.

## Context & Connections

## Observer's Reading
The Observer reads the Tonian as a period of loosening: a supercontinent starting to split, a climate record becoming less steady, and protists diversifying into new niches. None of this points at animals, but it is the background against which the later ice ages and the first large bodies happened. The reading would weaken if Tonian records showed an environment as steady as the Mesoproterozoic before it.

## Open Questions
- Did the break-up of Rodinia help trigger the Cryogenian glaciations?
- How diverse were Tonian eukaryotes, given how sparse the record is?
