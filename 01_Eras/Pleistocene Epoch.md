## Summary
The Pleistocene Epoch (about 2.58 million to 11,700 years ago) is the age of recurring ice sheets. The genus Homo evolved and spread during it.

## Facts
- Glacial and interglacial cycles repeated many times; see [[Last Glacial Maximum]].
- Homo habilis, Homo erectus, Neanderthals, Denisovans and Homo sapiens all lived in this epoch; see [[Homo erectus]] and [[Homo sapiens]].
- Sea levels fell during glacial periods, exposing land bridges such as the one used in the [[Peopling of the Americas]].
- Many large mammals, including [[Woolly Mammoth]], went extinct near its end.

## Context & Connections
The Pleistocene is the geological frame for the [[Paleolithic]]. It ends when the climate warms into the [[Holocene Epoch]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer sees the Pleistocene as an evolutionary pressure chamber: repeated climate swings favored a species that could adapt by learning and by copying others, rather than by waiting for genes to change.

## Open Questions
- How much did climate variability drive the growth of hominin brains?
