## Summary
Classical Antiquity (about 500 BCE to 500 CE) is the era of the great territorial empires and the philosophical and religious traditions that still shape the world.

## Facts
- Empires include Persia, Macedon, Rome, Maurya India, and Han China.
- Major intellectual traditions emerge, including Greek philosophy, Confucianism, Buddhism, and Hebrew prophetic tradition; see [[Axial Age]].
- Trade routes link Eurasia; see [[Opening of the Silk Roads]].
- The era in the Mediterranean ends with the [[Fall of the Western Roman Empire]].

## Context & Connections
Classical Antiquity follows the [[Iron Age]] and leads into the [[Medieval Period]]. Major polities included the [[Ancient Greeks]], [[Romans]], [[Han Dynasty]] and [[Achaemenid Persians]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that several distant societies, largely out of contact, developed comparable ethical and philosophical questions around the same centuries.
