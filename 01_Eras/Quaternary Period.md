## Summary
The Quaternary Period is the latest period of the Cenozoic Era, beginning 2.58 million years ago and continuing today. It is the interval of the repeated ice ages, of the genus Homo, and of all recorded human history.

## Facts
- The Quaternary Period began 2.58 million years ago and has two epochs, the [[Pleistocene Epoch]] and the [[Holocene Epoch]]. Its base is defined at Monte San Nicola in Sicily, and its status as a period, once disputed, was confirmed in 2009.
- Ice sheets grew and shrank across the Northern Hemisphere in cycles about 41,000 years long at first and about 100,000 years long after a shift in the middle Pleistocene, roughly 1.25 to 0.7 million years ago.
- At the [[Last Glacial Maximum]], sea level was about 120 metres lower than today, and land bridges such as [[Beringia]] were exposed.
- Nearly all species of the genus Homo lived in this period, including [[Homo erectus]], [[Homo neanderthalensis]], [[Denisovans]] and [[Homo sapiens]], and many large mammals disappeared; see [[Quaternary Megafauna Extinctions]].
- A proposal to add an Anthropocene epoch was rejected by the International Union of Geological Sciences in 2024; the term remains in informal use. See [[Anthropocene]].

## Context & Connections
The Quaternary Period follows the [[Neogene Period]] and is the last period of the [[Cenozoic Era]] so far. It belongs to the theme thread of [[Earth Systems and Life]] and is the frame for the [[Paleolithic]] and everything after it.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the Quaternary is under three million years long, about 0.06 percent of Earth's history, yet it holds nearly all of the record of the genus Homo and every human society. The Observer reads the period's climate cycling as the setting in which a species that learns by copying could do well, one hypothesis among several. The reading would weaken if climate variability had little effect on hominin evolution.

## Open Questions
