## Summary
The Neoproterozoic Era is the last era of the Proterozoic Eon, running from 1.0 billion to 538.8 million years ago. It holds the break-up of the supercontinent Rodinia, the global glaciations known as Snowball Earth, and the first large complex organisms.

## Facts
- The Neoproterozoic Era spans 1.0 billion to 538.8 million years ago and has three periods: the [[Tonian Period]], the [[Cryogenian Period]] and the [[Ediacaran Period]]. Its top is the base of the [[Cambrian Period]].
- The supercontinent Rodinia, assembled by about 1.0 billion years ago, began to rift apart roughly 800 to 750 million years ago.
- The Cryogenian glaciations, the Sturtian (about 717 to 660 million years ago) and the Marinoan (about 650 to 635 million years ago), are the basis of the [[Snowball Earth]] hypothesis.
- Oxygen is proposed to have risen again sometime between about 800 and 540 million years ago; its timing and size are debated.
- Molecular-clock estimates place the origin of animals in or before the Cryogenian, but the fossil record of the first animals is disputed. The [[Ediacaran Biota]], large soft-bodied organisms, appear by about 575 million years ago.

## Context & Connections
The Neoproterozoic Era follows the [[Mesoproterozoic Era]] and closes the [[Proterozoic Eon]], giving way to the [[Paleozoic Era]] at the [[Cambrian Explosion]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[Earth Systems and Life]].

## Observer's Reading
Animals arrive at the end of the Neoproterozoic, and it is tempting to read the ice, the oxygen and the break-up of Rodinia as steps toward them. The Observer declines that reading: these were physical events with their own causes, and animals were one of several possible outcomes. Evidence that any one of them was strictly required for animals would weaken this view.

## Open Questions
- How much did the Cryogenian ice and the second oxygen rise contribute to the appearance of animals, if at all?
- When did the first animals appear?
