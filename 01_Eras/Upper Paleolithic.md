## Summary
The Upper Paleolithic is the last part of the Paleolithic, from about 50,000 to about 10,000 BCE. It is the interval of blade tools, bone and antler technology, abundant art and ornament, and the spread of Homo sapiens across the globe.

## Facts
- The Upper Paleolithic runs from about 50,000 to about 10,000 BCE, ending with the warming at the close of the last ice age (Africa uses the name Later Stone Age). Toolmakers made long blades and worked bone, antler and ivory; eyed bone needles from Denisova Cave in Siberia, about 40,000 to 50,000 years old, suggest sewn clothing.
- Homo sapiens spread across the globe: Sahul by about 50,000 years ago ([[Peopling of Sahul]]), Europe by about 45,000 years ago, Beringia by about 30,000 years ago ([[Beringia]]), and the Americas by at least about 23,000 years ago ([[Peopling of the Americas]]). Neanderthals disappear from Europe about 40,000 years ago.
- Painted, carved and engraved images are abundant: [[Chauvet Cave]] in France has paintings about 36,000 years old, and cave paintings in Sulawesi, Indonesia, include a narrative scene about 51,000 years old.
- The [[Last Glacial Maximum]], about 26,500 to 19,000 years ago, put ice over much of northern Eurasia and North America. Pottery appears in East Asia by about 20,000 years ago ([[Pottery]]), and dogs were domesticated from wolves by at least about 15,000 years ago.

## Context & Connections
The Upper Paleolithic follows the [[Middle Paleolithic]] and is followed by the [[Epipaleolithic]] in the Near East, within the [[Paleolithic]] and the [[Pleistocene Epoch]]. It belongs to the theme threads of [[Art and Ritual]], [[Technology]] and [[Kinship and Society]]. See also [[Quaternary Megafauna Extinctions]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Upper Paleolithic sites hold the first large body of objects that seem made for display, ritual or play. It treats beads and ornaments as possible status-signals and burials as possible grief-rites, and it keeps in mind that a practice cannot be read from a surviving object alone. The reading would weaken if comparable objects proved common long before this period, which would make it a change in what survives, not in behaviour.

## Open Questions

- How did the Upper Paleolithic industries relate to specific human populations?
