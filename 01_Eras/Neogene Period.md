## Summary
The Neogene Period, from 23.03 to 2.58 million years ago, is the second period of the Cenozoic Era. It is the interval in which grasslands spread, the climate cooled toward the ice ages, and the hominin lineage separated from that of the other apes.

## Facts
- The Neogene Period spans 23.03 to 2.58 million years ago and has two epochs, the Miocene and the Pliocene. Its base is defined at Lemme-Carrosio in Italy, and its top, the base of the Quaternary, at Monte San Nicola in Sicily.
- The climate was warm in the early Miocene and then cooled. The East Antarctic ice sheet expanded about 14 million years ago, and Northern Hemisphere glaciation intensified about 2.7 million years ago.
- Grasses using the C4 pathway of photosynthesis spread widely about 8 to 6 million years ago, and open grassland and savanna expanded, favouring grazing mammals.
- Apes were diverse in Africa, Europe and Asia during the Miocene. The hominin lineage diverged from that of the chimpanzees around 7 million years ago (see [[Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]]), and [[Sahelanthropus tchadensis]] and [[Australopithecus afarensis]] date to this period.
- The [[Messinian Salinity Crisis]], about 5.96 to 5.33 million years ago, left the Mediterranean largely dried out. The land bridge of Panama, generally dated to about 3 million years ago although the timing is debated, joined the Americas; see [[Closure of the Isthmus of Panama]].

## Context & Connections

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the Neogene is where the line that includes humans separates from the line of the other apes, in a world that was drying and cooling into grassland and ice. The Observer does not read the drying as a push toward humans: the same changes affected many lineages, and most Miocene apes left no descendants. The reading would weaken if the traits of the hominin line could be tied directly to the spread of grassland.
