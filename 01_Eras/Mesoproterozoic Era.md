## Summary
The Mesoproterozoic Era is the middle era of the Proterozoic Eon, running from 1.6 billion to 1.0 billion years ago. It falls inside the interval often called the "Boring Billion", a long stretch of little evident change in climate, ocean chemistry or life, though how uneventful it was is debated.

## Facts
- The Mesoproterozoic Era spans 1.6 to 1.0 billion years ago. Its three periods, the Calymmian, Ectasian and Stenian, and its boundaries are set by numerical ages.
- The "Boring Billion" is an informal name for about 1.8 to 0.8 billion years ago, with no well-established ice ages and slow apparent change in the fossil record; the term is debated.
- Atmospheric oxygen is thought to have stayed well below today's level, and parts of the deeper ocean are reconstructed as oxygen-poor or rich in sulfide, though estimates vary widely.
- [[Stromatolites]] remained common in shallow seas. Eukaryotes diversified: Tappania microfossils are about 1.5 billion years old, and the red alga Bangiomorpha, dated in different studies to between roughly 1.05 and 1.2 billion years ago, is often cited as the oldest taxonomically resolved eukaryote.
- The supercontinent Nuna, or Columbia, is proposed to have broken up about 1.6 to 1.3 billion years ago, and Rodinia began assembling late in the era.

## Context & Connections
The Mesoproterozoic Era sits between the [[Paleoproterozoic Era]] and the [[Neoproterozoic Era]] within the [[Proterozoic Eon]]. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer reads the apparent quiet of the Mesoproterozoic as information: a planet's surface can stay much the same for hundreds of millions of years without heading anywhere in particular. What arrived later was not overdue. The reading would weaken if new records showed that the quiet hides substantial change in eukaryote diversity.

## Open Questions
- Was the Boring Billion truly stable, or does a sparse record hide change?
- What held atmospheric oxygen low for so long after the Great Oxidation Event?
