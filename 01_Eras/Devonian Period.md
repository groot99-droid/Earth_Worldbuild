## Summary
The Devonian Period, from 419.2 to 358.9 million years ago, is the fourth period of the Paleozoic Era. It is often called the Age of Fishes, and it is the interval of the first forests, the first limbed vertebrates and a prolonged late extinction.

## Facts
- The Devonian Period spans 419.2 to 358.9 million years ago and is named after Devon in England. Its base is defined at Klonk in the Czech Republic, and its top, the base of the Carboniferous, at La Serre in France.
- Fishes diversified widely, including armoured placoderms, sharks, lobe-finned fishes and ray-finned fishes, and the Middle Devonian was a peak of Paleozoic reef building.
- The Rhynie chert in Scotland, about 407 million years old, preserves early land plants and arthropods in fine detail. Archaeopteris, a tree with woody trunks, formed early forests, and the Gilboa fossil forest in New York is about 385 million years old.
- [[Tiktaalik]], about 375 million years old, has fins with wrist-like bones. Ichthyostega and Acanthostega from Greenland, about 365 million years old, had limbs with digits.
- The [[Late Devonian Extinction]], about 372 to 359 million years ago, came in at least two pulses and removed an estimated 75 percent of species, heavily affecting reef builders.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer reads the Devonian as a period in which life rebuilt the land surface: roots, soils and the first forests changed weathering, rivers and the carbon cycle. The spread of land plants is a candidate cause of the late extinction, a case of one group's success altering conditions for many others. That link is a hypothesis, and it would weaken if the extinction proved to have mainly non-biological causes.

## Open Questions
- How large was the role of land plants in the Late Devonian extinction?
- Did the first limbed vertebrates evolve limbs for life in shallow water rather than for moving onto land?
