## Summary
The Jurassic Period, from 201.4 to about 143.1 million years ago, is the middle period of the Mesozoic Era. It is the interval of the largest land animals, the first known birds, and the opening of the Atlantic as Pangaea broke apart.

## Facts
- The Jurassic Period spans 201.4 million years ago to about 143.1 million years ago and is named after the Jura Mountains. Its top is less firm: the base of the Cretaceous has no agreed marker section, and older references give 145.0 million years ago.
- The [[Breakup of Pangaea]] began with the opening of the Central Atlantic, and Laurasia and Gondwana began to separate. The climate was warm and sea level high, with no lasting polar ice caps.
- Sauropods, stegosaurs and large theropods such as Allosaurus are known from the Morrison Formation of the western United States, about 155 to 148 million years old. See [[Dinosaurs]].
- [[Archaeopteryx]] from the Solnhofen limestone of Bavaria, about 150 million years old, is the best-known early bird. The feathered theropod Anchiornis from China, about 160 million years old, shows that feathers were widespread among small theropods.
- Ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs and pliosaurs were the main marine reptiles. Mammals remained mostly small, though Castorocauda, an otter-like swimmer from China, is about 164 million years old.

## Context & Connections

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the Jurassic holds both the largest land animals known and, beside them, the first records of a small feathered lineage that would outlast every large dinosaur. What looks dominant at one time is not a reliable guide to what continues, and the Observer reads this as a caution about judging any period by its most conspicuous groups. The reading would weaken if the survival of birds followed from traits already obvious in the Jurassic.
