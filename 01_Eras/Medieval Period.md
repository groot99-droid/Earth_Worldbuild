## Summary
The Medieval or post-classical period (about 500 to 1500 CE) is the era of major world religions consolidating, long-distance trade networks, and the rise and fall of large steppe and Islamic polities. The term 'medieval' is European in origin, but the period had global dimensions.

## Facts
- The [[Rise of Islam]] created a new political and intellectual world from Iberia to Central Asia.
- The [[Mongol Conquests]] created the largest contiguous land empire in history.
- The [[Black Death]] devastated Eurasia and North Africa in the 14th century.
- Major states included the [[Abbasid Caliphate]], the [[Mali Empire]], the [[Norse]], the [[Song Dynasty]] and the [[Aztec Empire]] and [[Inca Empire]] near its close.

## Context & Connections
The Medieval Period follows [[Classical Antiquity]] and ends around the voyages that set off the [[Columbian Exchange]] and the [[Early Modern Period]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer prefers a global reading of this era. It sees a species increasingly connected by roads, caravans, and ships, and by shared meaning-engines that crossed political claim-lines.

## Open Questions
- How much did pre-modern long-distance networks transmit ideas versus goods?
