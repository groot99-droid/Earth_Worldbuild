## Summary
The Cretaceous Period, from about 143.1 to 66 million years ago, is the last period of the Mesozoic Era and the longest of the Phanerozoic Eon. It is the interval of the first flowering plants and a warm greenhouse world, and it ends with the extinction of the non-avian dinosaurs.

## Facts
- The Cretaceous Period spans about 143.1 to 66.0 million years ago; older references give its start as 145.0 million years ago. The name comes from the Latin word for chalk. Its top, the base of the Paleogene, is defined at El Kef in Tunisia.
- The climate was a warm greenhouse, and sea level was high, with shallow seas across the continents, including the Western Interior Seaway of North America, and thick chalk deposits.
- [[Flowering Plants]] appear, with the oldest widely accepted pollen about 134 million years old, and diversified rapidly alongside pollinating insects. The [[Breakup of Pangaea]] continued as the South Atlantic opened.
- Late Cretaceous [[Dinosaurs]] include Tyrannosaurus and Triceratops in North America, birds diversified, and mosasaurs were large marine reptiles. Mammals were mostly small, and whether the modern placental orders arose before or after the end-Cretaceous extinction is debated.
- The [[Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]], about 66 million years ago, followed the Chicxulub impact and coincided with the eruption of the Deccan Traps, and killed about three-quarters of species.

## Context & Connections
The Cretaceous Period follows the [[Jurassic Period]] and closes the [[Mesozoic Era]], giving way to the [[Paleogene Period]]. It belongs to the theme thread of [[Earth Systems and Life]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the Cretaceous ends with dinosaurs at or near their greatest diversity, ended by a rock from space and, probably, volcanic stress. The Observer reads long, stable-looking intervals as provisional, since nothing in the record of the last million years before the boundary announces what was about to happen. Evidence of a long decline in dinosaur diversity before the impact would weaken this reading.

## Open Questions
- Were non-avian dinosaurs already in decline before the end-Cretaceous impact?
- Did the modern orders of placental mammals originate in the Cretaceous or only after the extinction?
