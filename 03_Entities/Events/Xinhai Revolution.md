## Summary
The Xinhai Revolution of 1911 to 1912 began with an army mutiny at Wuchang and ended the Qing dynasty, which had ruled China since 1644, replacing imperial rule with the Republic of China.

## Facts
- On 10 October 1911 members of the New Army mutinied in Wuchang, in Hubei province. The rebellion spread to surrounding regions, and by the end of 1911 revolutionaries were forming a new government.
- Sun Yat-sen led the republican movement but lacked the means to overthrow the Qing by himself. He reached a compromise with the powerful military leader Yuan Shikai, whose intervention led to the abdication of the child emperor Puyi on 12 February 1912.
- Sun Yat-sen resigned the presidency to unify the country, and Yuan Shikai was chosen as his successor. By early 1912 the Qing dynasty was gone and the Republic of China had begun.

## Context & Connections
The revolution ended the [[Qing Dynasty]] after decades of internal rebellion and foreign pressure, including the [[Taiping Rebellion]] and the [[Opium Wars]]. The republic that followed was unstable, with power passing quickly to military leaders.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a transfer of authority that was arranged by a compromise between a movement and an army, and not by the movement alone. A story-glue as old as the imperial title dissolved in a few months, but what replaced it had to be built while the guns were still in the hands of others.
