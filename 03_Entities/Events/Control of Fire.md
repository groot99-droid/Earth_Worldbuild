## Summary
Human ancestors began controlling fire at least around a million years ago, and habitual fire use is well established by about 400,000 years ago.

## Facts
- Evidence at Wonderwerk Cave in South Africa dates to about one million years ago.
- Burned seeds, wood, and flint at Gesher Benot Ya'aqov in Israel suggest human control of fire nearly 790,000 years ago.
- Earlier claims, at about 1.5 million years, are disputed.
- Cooked food is easier to chew and digest, and may have supported larger brains.

## Context & Connections
Fire control is a key event of the [[Paleolithic]] associated with [[Homo erectus]]. See the essay [[Fire, Then Everything]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer calls this heat-domestication and treats it as the first true engine the species built: a source of energy outside the body that changed diet, night, and social life.
