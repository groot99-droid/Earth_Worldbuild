## Summary
Movements to win women the right to vote succeeded country by country between the 1890s and the late 20th century.

## Facts
- New Zealand granted women the vote in national elections in 1893, the first self-governing country to do so.
- Australia's Commonwealth Franchise Act of 1902 let women vote and stand for federal office, but it excluded Aboriginal people and many other non-European people. Finland (1906) was the first European country to grant women the vote, adopting universal and equal suffrage, and 19 women were elected to its parliament in 1907. The UK gave a first group of women the vote in 1918, and the US in 1920.
- Many women were excluded in practice by race or class; in the US, Black women largely gained access only after the 1965 Voting Rights Act.
- Switzerland granted women the vote in national elections in 1971.

## Context & Connections
The movements grew out of the ideas of the [[Age of Revolutions]] and gained ground after [[World War I]]. See [[Kinship and Society]].
