## Summary
The transatlantic slave trade forcibly transported about 12.5 million Africans to the Americas between the early 16th and the mid-19th centuries, and millions died in the process.

## Facts
- About 10.7 million people are recorded as surviving the voyage.
- The trade supplied labor for plantations producing sugar, tobacco, cotton, and other goods.
- It was carried out by European, American, and African traders and states.
- Other slave trades operated at the same time, including across the Sahara and the Indian Ocean.

## Context & Connections
The trade grew from the [[Columbian Exchange]] and reshaped [[Sub-Saharan Africa]], the Americas, and Europe. The Age of Revolutions produced abolition movements; see [[Age of Revolutions]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer records this as one of the largest forced migrations in history and as one of the clearest cases where economic incentive and story-glue were used to justify cruelty. Detachment here does not mean neutrality about harm.
