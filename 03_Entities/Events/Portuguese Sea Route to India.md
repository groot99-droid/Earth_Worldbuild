## Summary
Vasco da Gama's fleet reached Calicut in India on 20 May 1498 after sailing around Africa, opening a direct sea route from Europe to Asia.

## Facts
- Da Gama left Lisbon in July 1497 and rounded the Cape of Good Hope in November.
- He reached Calicut on the Malabar Coast in May 1498.
- The Portuguese armed ships with cannon and later seized ports, including Goa in 1510.
- Their entry gave Europe direct access to Asian spices without Middle Eastern intermediaries.
- The Indian Ocean had earlier been crossed by large Chinese fleets under Zheng He, whose voyages ended in 1433.

## Context & Connections
The voyage created a new connection in the [[Indian Ocean Trade Network]] and, with the voyages behind the [[Columbian Exchange]], began direct European trade across the oceans. The Chinese voyages of [[Zheng He]] had reached the same waters decades earlier. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the newcomers entered a busy ocean with no dominant navy and made it dangerous with cannon-armed ships. What changed was less the route than the willingness to fight for control of it.
