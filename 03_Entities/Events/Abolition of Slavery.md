## Summary
Between 1807 and 1888, the transatlantic slave trade and then slavery itself were legally abolished across the Atlantic world, ending a system that had lasted for centuries.

## Facts
- Britain banned the slave trade in 1807 and abolished slavery in its colonies in 1833. The government paid £20 million in compensation to slaveholders and nothing to the people who had been enslaved.
- The United States ended slavery in 1865 with the 13th Amendment.
- Brazil abolished it on 13 May 1888 with the Lei Áurea, the last country in the Americas to do so.
- Forced labor and human trafficking persist; recent estimates suggest about 50 million people live in modern slavery.

## Context & Connections
Abolition followed resistance by enslaved people, including the [[Haitian Revolution]], and campaigns by abolitionists. See [[Atlantic Slave Trade]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that an institution once defended by law, economics, and religion was, within a few generations, declared a moral wrong. Moral norms can shift within lifetimes, though the practice does not vanish.
