## Summary
The Scramble for Africa was the rapid partition of the African continent by European powers between about 1881 and 1914, in which the share of Africa under European control rose from roughly 10 percent to about 90 percent.

## Facts
- The Berlin Conference of November 1884 to February 1885 set rules for claiming territory but did not itself divide the continent.
- By 1914 only Ethiopia and Liberia were independent of European rule.
- African societies resisted in many wars, including the [[Battle of Adwa]] and the Anglo-Zulu War; see [[Zulu Kingdom]].
- Colonial borders were drawn with little regard for existing societies; many remain today.

## Context & Connections
See [[Congo Free State]] and [[Sokoto Caliphate]]. The partition is the background to [[Decolonization]] in [[Sub-Saharan Africa]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a process of claim-lines drawn on maps, in a distant capital, over the homes of millions who were not consulted. The lines outlasted the empires that drew them. See [[The Fiction of Claim-Lines]].
