## Summary
The African Humid Period, or Green Sahara, ran from about 14,800 to between 5,500 and 5,000 years ago, when the Sahara held lakes, rivers, and savanna instead of desert, driven by a stronger monsoon. Its end is debated: marine records show an abrupt collapse, archaeological records a slower, staggered desiccation.

## Facts
- A marine sediment core off Mauritania shows a low-dust interval from about 14,800 to 5,500 years ago, read as an abrupt onset and end.
- Radiocarbon dating of roughly 150 sites across the eastern Sahara instead shows humid conditions beginning suddenly around 8,500 BCE, then drying out gradually, region by region, over thousands of years.
- A later synthesis proposed the end was locally abrupt but happened progressively later toward lower latitudes, reconciling the two pictures.
- Onset and end occurred at similar levels of Northern Hemisphere summer sunlight, consistent with a threshold set by the precession cycle, one of the Milankovitch cycles, amplified by vegetation and soil-moisture feedbacks.
- Evidence for the wetter Sahara includes rock art at Tassili n'Ajjer showing hippopotamuses and giraffes, lakeside cemeteries at Gobero, Niger, and fossil river networks visible only under satellite radar.
- Similar episodes recurred over at least 800,000 years. A 2025 ancient-DNA study at Takarkori, Libya, found a largely isolated North African ancestry, complicating the idea that the Green Sahara was always an open migration corridor.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that today's Sahara is not its default state but one phase of a cycle repeated for hundreds of thousands of years, read differently depending on whether the evidence comes from the sea floor or the land people once lived on. The disagreement is itself a caution against expecting one clean date for uneven desertification. The reading would weaken if further dating showed the two records were measuring the same thing after all.

## Open Questions
- Was the end a single threshold-crossing event or a gradual, region-by-region process?
- Did the Green Sahara function as a migration corridor throughout its history, or only in some periods?
