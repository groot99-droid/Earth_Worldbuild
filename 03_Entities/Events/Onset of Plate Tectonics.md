## Summary
The onset of plate tectonics is the start of the motion of rigid plates of Earth's outer shell, with ocean floor recycled into the mantle at subduction zones. Proposed dates range from before 4 billion to about 0.8 billion years ago, and the question remains one of the most disputed in Earth science.

## Facts
- Plate tectonics moves about a dozen large plates and recycles ocean floor at subduction zones. Earth is the only planet known to have it.
- Proposed onsets include before 4.0 billion years ago, about 3.2 billion years ago, about 2.5 billion years ago and about 0.8 billion years ago. Some researchers argue for a gradual transition from a rigid outer lid, or for episodic subduction long before modern-style plate motion.
- Blueschists, which form in cold subduction zones, and ultra-high-pressure metamorphic rocks appear in the record only after about 0.8 billion years ago, which some geologists read as a marker of modern-style subduction.
- Alfred Wegener proposed continental drift in 1912, and plate tectonics gained wide acceptance in the 1960s.
- Plate tectonics builds continents and recycles carbon; whether it was needed for a long-lived biosphere is debated.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Earth's surface has recycled its own earliest evidence, so when the process began may be settled only indirectly. Some geologists treat plate tectonics as a precondition for a long-lived biosphere, and the Observer keeps that as an open hypothesis. It reads the current disagreement as information about the limits of the record, and it would weaken if a reliable early rock record were found.
