## Summary
The Siberian Traps eruptions were one of the largest volcanic outpourings of the last 500 million years, in what is now northern Siberia, at about 252 to 251 million years ago. They coincide with the end-Permian mass extinction.

## Facts
- The eruptions produced lava flows and intrusions over a huge area of northern Siberia, with estimates of the volume running from about one to several million cubic kilometres.
- Precise radiometric dating places the main phase within a window of about a million years or less that brackets the extinction, near 251.9 million years ago.
- Proposed killing mechanisms include large releases of carbon dioxide and methane that warmed the planet, ocean acidification and oxygen loss, and toxic gases from sulfur and halogens. Magma intruding coal, carbonate and salt-bearing rocks may have released large gas volumes.
- Marine and land records show a rapid warming of the tropics and oceans at the extinction; how much of it the eruptions caused is debated.
- The extinction is described in [[Permian-Triassic Extinction]].

## Context & Connections
The Siberian Traps Eruptions close the [[Permian Period]] and are the leading proposed cause of the [[Permian-Triassic Extinction]], after which the [[Triassic Period]] opens the Mesozoic. The event belongs to the theme thread of [[Earth Systems and Life]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a volcanic episode lasting about a million years was enough to remove most of the life then living, and that comparisons with any modern process hold or fail on rates, not on totals. The Observer treats the timing question as central for that reason. The reading would weaken if the eruptions were shown to have started well before the extinction with little effect.
