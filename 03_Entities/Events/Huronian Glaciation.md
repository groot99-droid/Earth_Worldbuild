## Summary
The Huronian glaciation was a series of ice ages recorded in the Huronian Supergroup of Ontario, Canada, in the early Paleoproterozoic, about 2.4 to 2.2 billion years ago. It is one of the oldest well-documented ice ages.

## Facts
- The Huronian Supergroup, north of Lake Huron, contains three glacial deposits, the Ramsay Lake, Bruce and Gowganda formations, separated by sediments laid down in warmer conditions.
- Glacial deposits of similar age are reported from other continents, including Wyoming and Finland. The Makganyene Formation in South Africa, about 2.2 billion years old, formed near the equator, which supports an early snowball state, though its link to the Huronian is debated.
- The glaciation follows the first rise of oxygen. One hypothesis is that oxygen destroyed atmospheric methane, a strong greenhouse gas, and so cooled the planet; other explanations involve volcanic and tectonic change.
- How long the ice lasted, and whether it covered the whole planet, are debated.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer files the Huronian under a caution about attribution: an ice age and an oxygen rise that share a date invite a causal story, and the story is plausible, but the record allows other explanations. The reading would strengthen or weaken with tighter dating of the glacial deposits against the first traces of oxygen.

## Open Questions
- Did the oxygen rise cause the glaciation, or did the two arise from a common cause?
- Was the ice global?
