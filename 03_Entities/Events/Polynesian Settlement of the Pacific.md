## Summary
Polynesian navigators settled the last remote islands of the central and eastern Pacific, culminating with Aotearoa New Zealand around 1250 to 1300 CE.

## Facts
- Ancestors of Polynesians, associated with the Lapita culture, reached Samoa and Tonga about 3,000 years ago.
- A pause of roughly a thousand years preceded the final expansion to Hawai'i, Rapa Nui, and Aotearoa.
- Voyagers used star paths, swells, and bird flight rather than instruments.
- Ancient DNA and language link the settlers to Austronesian speakers from Island Southeast Asia.

## Context & Connections
The settlement was the last great human dispersal into unoccupied lands, after the [[Peopling of the Americas]]. See [[Polynesians]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer regards this as one of the species' most extraordinary achievements in knowledge: crossing thousands of kilometers of open ocean without instruments, using memorized navigation.
