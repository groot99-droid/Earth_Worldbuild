## Summary
Symbolic behavior, including engraved ochre, beads, and cave paintings, appears in the archaeological record from about 100,000 years ago onward.

## Facts
- Engraved ochre from Blombos Cave in South Africa dates to about 75,000 to 100,000 years ago.
- Cave paintings in Sulawesi, Indonesia, include a warty-pig painting at least 45,500 years old, and narrative scenes reported in 2024 to be at least 51,200 years old.
- European sites such as Chauvet and Lascaux date to about 36,000 and 17,000 years ago.
- Some evidence suggests that Neanderthals also made symbolic objects, though this is debated.

## Context & Connections
Symbolic art belongs to the [[Paleolithic]] and probably relates to the spread of language and belief; see [[Language and Writing]] and [[Art and Ritual]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer treats this as the first clear sign of a species that lives partly in imagined worlds. A painted animal on a wall implies a viewer who understands that a picture can stand for something absent.
