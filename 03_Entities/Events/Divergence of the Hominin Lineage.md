## Summary
The hominin lineage, the line leading to humans, split from the line leading to chimpanzees and bonobos between roughly 6 and 8 million years ago in Africa.

## Facts
- Genetic estimates of the split depend on assumed mutation rates and generation times: one 2006 study placed it no earlier than about 6.3 million years ago, while studies using longer generation times imply earlier dates.
- Early candidate hominins include [[Sahelanthropus tchadensis]] (about 7 million years ago).
- Upright walking (bipedalism) is a key early trait; see [[Australopithecus afarensis]].
- A 2006 genetic study proposed that the split was gradual, with interbreeding between the lineages for a time, though this model is disputed.

## Context & Connections
The divergence occurs during the [[Cenozoic Era]] and sets the biological stage for [[Origin of Homo sapiens]].
