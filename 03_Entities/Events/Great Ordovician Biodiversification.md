## Summary
The Great Ordovician Biodiversification was a long rise in the diversity of marine animals, running from about 485 to 460 million years ago. It multiplied the number of species and families within body plans that had first appeared in the Cambrian.

## Facts
- By some counts, marine diversity at the family and genus level rose several-fold between the Early and Middle Ordovician.
- Brachiopods, bryozoans, molluscs, echinoderms such as crinoids, corals and sponges all diversified, and reefs and filter-feeding communities grew more complex. Plankton diversified as well.
- The [[Cambrian Explosion]] produced the body plans; the Ordovician diversification filled out the variety within them.
- Proposed causes include a cooling from the hot Cambrian climate, more oxygen, nutrients from volcanism, more shallow shelf habitat and ecological feedbacks. The mix is debated.
- The rise ended near the close of the period with the [[Late Ordovician Extinction]].

## Context & Connections
The Great Ordovician Biodiversification is the main event of the [[Ordovician Period]] within the [[Paleozoic Era]] and follows the [[Cambrian Explosion]]. The event belongs to the theme threads of [[Earth Systems and Life]] and [[Science]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that variety depends on ecological space as well as on novelty: the same body plans, given new habitats and richer food webs, yielded far more forms. It reads the event as a slow build after a fast start. The reading would weaken if diversity had risen with no link to habitat or climate.

## Open Questions
- What drove the biodiversification: climate, ocean chemistry, ecology, or a combination?
- How much of the apparent rise reflects better preservation of Ordovician rocks?
