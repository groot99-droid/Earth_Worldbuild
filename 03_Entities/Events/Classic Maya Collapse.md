## Summary
Between about 800 and 950 CE, many Classic Maya cities of the southern lowlands were abandoned or stopped building monuments, while Maya communities elsewhere continued.

## Facts
- The last dated monument in the Long Count calendar, at Tonina, is from 909 CE.
- Populations in the southern lowlands fell sharply, but the Maya did not disappear: several million people speak Mayan languages today.
- Cities of the northern Yucatan, such as Chichen Itza, flourished afterwards.
- Proposed causes include severe droughts, shown in cave and lake records, warfare, political fragmentation, and environmental degradation.
- No single cause is accepted.

## Context & Connections
The collapse ended the height of the [[Maya]] city-states in the southern lowlands and shifted power to the north, as at [[Chichen Itza]]. It followed the decline of the earlier metropolis of [[Teotihuacan]].
