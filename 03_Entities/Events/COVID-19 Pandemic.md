## Summary
The COVID-19 pandemic was a global outbreak of the coronavirus SARS-CoV-2, first identified in late 2019.

## Facts
- The World Health Organization declared a pandemic on 11 March 2020 and ended the global health emergency on 5 May 2023.
- More than 7 million deaths were officially reported; WHO estimated about 14.9 million excess deaths in 2020 and 2021 (range 13.3 to 16.6 million), nearly three times the reported COVID deaths for that period.
- Vaccines using mRNA technology were developed and authorized within about a year.
- Lockdowns, remote work, and supply-chain disruption affected economies worldwide.

## Context & Connections
The pandemic followed the pattern of earlier disease spread along trade networks; compare the [[Black Death]]. It also relied on the [[Digital Revolution]] and on modern [[Vaccination]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a virus crossed the planet in weeks along the same exchange-webs that carry goods. It also notes an unusual feature: scientists around the world shared data in real time and produced effective vaccines in a fraction of the historical time.
