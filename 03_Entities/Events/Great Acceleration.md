## Summary
The Great Acceleration is the sharp, simultaneous rise since about 1950 in human population, energy use, economic output, and environmental change.

## Facts
- The term was used by the International Geosphere-Biosphere Programme; a 2015 study charted 24 indicators.
- Global population rose from about 2.5 billion in 1950 to about 8 billion in 2022.
- Energy use, fertilizer use, and greenhouse gas emissions rose steeply.
- Many Earth-system indicators (extinction rates, ocean acidity, carbon dioxide) changed in parallel.

## Context & Connections
The Great Acceleration is a main candidate for the start of the [[Anthropocene]]. See [[The Species as a Geological Force]] and [[Modern Climate Change]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer treats 1950 as a hinge: curves for population, energy, and impact all bend upward at once. The scale of change within a single human lifetime has no earlier parallel.
