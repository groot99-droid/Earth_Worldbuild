## Summary
The Indian plate collided with Eurasia beginning about 50 million years ago, raising the Himalaya and the Tibetan Plateau, and the convergence continues today.

## Facts
- India separated from Gondwana and drifted north, unusually fast for a continental plate.
- Most estimates place the start of collision at about 50 to 55 million years ago, and the final closure of the Tethys Ocean between about 50 and 60 million years ago.
- The collision built the Himalaya, whose highest peak, Mount Everest, is 8,848.86 meters by the 2020 China-Nepal survey.
- India still converges on Asia at a few centimeters a year.
- The uplift shaped the South Asian monsoon and the sources of the Indus, Ganges, and Brahmaputra rivers.
