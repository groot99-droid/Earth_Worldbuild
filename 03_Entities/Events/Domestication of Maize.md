## Summary
Maize (corn) was domesticated from a wild grass called teosinte in southwestern Mexico about 9,000 years ago, and became a staple crop across the Americas.

## Facts
- Genetic studies trace maize to the wild teosinte Zea mays subsp. parviglumis.
- Five major morphological differences separate maize from teosinte, and regions throughout the genome contributed to the change. Early maize had tiny cobs; selection over thousands of years produced large ears.
- The Maya and Aztecs treated maize as sacred; cooking it with lime (nixtamalization) makes its nutrients available.
- Maize is now among the world's most-produced crops by volume.

## Context & Connections
Maize was the basis of [[Mesoamerica]]n states such as the [[Maya]] and [[Aztec Empire]]. It reached the rest of the world through the [[Columbian Exchange]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer treats this as a second, independent run of the Settling. A grass with a few seeds was reshaped by human selection into a crop that could support cities.
