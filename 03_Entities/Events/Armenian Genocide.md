## Summary
The Armenian Genocide was the deliberate destruction of most of the Ottoman Empire's Armenian population during World War I, through mass killings, forced deportations, and death from starvation and exposure. It is recognized as genocide by many countries and historians and is denied by the government of Turkey.

## Facts
- On 24 April 1915 Ottoman authorities arrested about 240 Armenian leaders in Constantinople, including writers, doctors, religious leaders, and political figures, and deported them eastward. The date is now used to commemorate the victims.
- In spring 1915 the Ottoman government began deporting Armenians from the northeastern border regions and then extended deportations to almost every province, including regions far from combat.
- Victims died in local massacres, and on forced marches from starvation, dehydration, exposure, and disease, and in or on the way to the deserts of the southern empire.
- Estimates of the number killed run from at least 600,000 to more than 1,000,000; the exact number is not settled.
- The Turkish state has long denied that the events were genocide and has worked against official recognition abroad. More than twenty countries formally recognize the genocide; Uruguay did so first, in 1965.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer records this as an atrocity: a state deliberately destroyed much of one of its own peoples. It also records that the denial has itself lasted a century, so that the question of what happened has become bound up with the question of who is permitted to say it.

## Open Questions
