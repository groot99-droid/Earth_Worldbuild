## Summary
The Younger Dryas was a sudden return to near-glacial cold in the Northern Hemisphere that lasted from about 12,900 to 11,700 years ago, just before the start of the Holocene.

## Facts
- It interrupted the warming that followed the last glacial period and ended abruptly, with several degrees of warming within decades.
- A leading explanation is a disruption of Atlantic Ocean circulation by a large pulse of glacial meltwater.
- A proposed extraterrestrial impact trigger, the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis, is disputed and not widely accepted.
- Its end is used to define the start of the Holocene, dated to about 11,700 years before 2000 CE.
- It overlaps the early stages of plant management in Southwest Asia, though the causal link is debated.

## Context & Connections
The Younger Dryas is the last major cold snap before the stable climate of the [[Holocene Epoch]], within which [[The Agricultural Revolution]] took place. It follows the [[Last Glacial Maximum]].
