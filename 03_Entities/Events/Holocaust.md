## Summary
The Holocaust was the persecution of European Jews by Nazi Germany from 1933 and the systematic murder of about six million of them from 1941 to 1945, with the help of allies and collaborators.

## Facts
- Persecution began after the Nazi rise to power in 1933 and included exclusion, expropriation, and the Nuremberg Laws of 1935.
- Systematic mass murder began in 1941, first by shootings in the occupied Soviet Union, then in extermination camps such as Auschwitz-Birkenau, Treblinka, and Sobibor.
- About six million Jews were killed.
- The Nazis also murdered Roma and Sinti, disabled people, Soviet prisoners of war, and political opponents.
- The word genocide was coined by Raphael Lemkin in 1944, and the UN adopted the Genocide Convention in 1948.
