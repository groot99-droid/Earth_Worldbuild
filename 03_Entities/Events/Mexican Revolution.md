## Summary
The Mexican Revolution (1910 to 1920) overthrew the long rule of Porfirio Díaz and passed through a series of civil wars among revolutionary leaders, producing the Constitution of 1917. Its death toll is uncertain but is usually put at more than a million.

## Facts
- The revolution is dated from 20 November 1910 to 1 December 1920. Porfirio Díaz was forced out and went into exile in France in May 1911.
- Francisco I. Madero won the 1911 election and was assassinated in February 1913. A civil war followed among Venustiano Carranza, Francisco 'Pancho' Villa, and Emiliano Zapata, who led the peasant movement in the state of Morelos.
- The Constitution of 1917 came out of the revolution and brought far-reaching political and economic change.
- Successive revolutionary leaders were assassinated: Madero in 1913, Zapata in 1919, Carranza in 1920, Villa in 1923, and Álvaro Obregón in 1928.
- The upheaval is often said to have left more than one million dead. Estimates vary widely, and the figure includes deaths from disease and emigration effects that are hard to separate from combat deaths.

## Context & Connections
The revolution belongs to the sequence of Latin American upheavals that followed the wars of independence; see [[Latin American Wars of Independence]] and [[Age of Revolutions]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a revolution which began by removing one strongman was followed by a decade in which almost every leading figure was killed by another. The 1917 constitution shows how a written story-glue can be drafted amid the fighting and outlast most of those who fought for it.

## Open Questions
- What was the real death toll, and how much of it was due to disease and famine rather than battle?
- How far did the land reforms promised by Zapata's movement actually take effect?
