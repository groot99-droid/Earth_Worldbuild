## Summary
The Late Devonian extinction was a prolonged period of biodiversity loss around 372 to 359 million years ago that heavily affected marine life, especially reef builders.

## Facts
- It comprised at least two main pulses (Kellwasser and Hangenberg).
- Around 75 percent of species are estimated to have been lost.
- Proposed causes include ocean anoxia, climate change, and the effects of newly spreading land plants on nutrients.

## Context & Connections
It is part of the [[Paleozoic Era]] and follows the rise of forests; see [[Colonization of Land]].
