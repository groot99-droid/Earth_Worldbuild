## Summary
According to the giant-impact hypothesis, a Mars-sized body called Theia struck the young Earth about 4.5 billion years ago, and the debris formed the Moon.

## Facts
- The hypothesis explains the Moon's relatively small iron core and its similarity to Earth's mantle in isotopes.
- Lunar rocks returned by the Apollo missions helped test the idea.
- The exact timing is uncertain, with estimates ranging from about 4.52 to 4.35 billion years ago: lunar zircons require the Moon to have formed within the first 60 million years of the Solar System, while the youngest estimates fall about 150 to 200 million years after it began.
- The Moon stabilizes Earth's axial tilt and produces most of Earth's tides.

## Context & Connections
The impact is an early event of the [[Hadean Eon]]. The Moon's stabilizing effect on climate is one reason Earth's long-term conditions stayed steady enough for complex life.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer sees a single accident with very long consequences. Human calendars, tides, and myths about the Moon all trace to a collision billions of years before any observer existed.
