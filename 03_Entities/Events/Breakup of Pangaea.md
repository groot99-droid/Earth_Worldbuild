## Summary
Pangaea, the supercontinent that had assembled by about 300 million years ago, began to split about 200 million years ago, opening the Atlantic Ocean and reshaping the map of the world.

## Facts
- Rifting began in the Late Triassic to Early Jurassic, and the Central Atlantic opened first.
- Large volcanic outpourings, the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province, coincided with the Triassic-Jurassic extinction, about 200 million years ago.
- The South Atlantic opened much later, in the Early Cretaceous.
- Alfred Wegener proposed continental drift in 1912; plate tectonics gained wide acceptance in the 1960s.
- Continental motion continues today.

## Context & Connections
The breakup of Pangaea isolated continental faunas and set the stage for the later biogeography of the [[Dinosaurs]] and mammals. Its opening overlaps the [[Triassic-Jurassic Extinction]].
