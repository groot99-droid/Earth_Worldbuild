## Summary
Plants, then arthropods and vertebrates, moved onto land beginning about 470 million years ago, transforming the planet's surface.

## Facts
- The earliest land plant spores are found in Ordovician rocks (about 470 million years old).
- Forests appeared in the Devonian, and vast swamp forests in the Carboniferous formed coal.
- Early tetrapods evolved from lobe-finned fishes; see [[Tiktaalik]].
- Land plants changed soils, rivers, and the carbon cycle.

## Context & Connections
Colonization of land unfolds through the [[Paleozoic Era]] and helped set up the [[Late Devonian Extinction]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Earth's terrestrial life is a late arrival on a planet where life began in water. Coal, the fuel later burned in industrial societies, is a byproduct of this era.
