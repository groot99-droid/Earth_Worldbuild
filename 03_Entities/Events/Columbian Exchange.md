## Summary
The Columbian Exchange was the transfer of plants, animals, people, and diseases between the Americas and the rest of the world after 1492.

## Facts
- The term was coined by historian Alfred Crosby in 1972.
- Old World diseases such as smallpox caused catastrophic mortality among Indigenous peoples of the Americas; estimates of the loss vary widely, from about 50 to 95 percent of the population by 1650.
- Maize, potatoes, tomatoes, and cassava spread worldwide, and horses, cattle, and wheat spread to the Americas.
- The exchange underpinned the growth of European empires and the [[Atlantic Slave Trade]].

## Context & Connections
The exchange opens the [[Early Modern Period]] and ended a long separation of the two hemispheres. See [[Aztec Empire]], [[Inca Empire]] and [[Mesoamerica]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer regards this as the moment when the biosphere of the entire planet was mixed at once, an event with no parallel since the continents last joined.
