## Summary
Bantu-speaking peoples spread from a homeland in West-Central Africa (present-day Nigeria and Cameroon) across central, eastern, and southern Africa over about four thousand years.

## Facts
- Today about 350 million people speak one of roughly 500 Bantu languages; estimates of the number of languages range from about 440 to 680, depending on how languages are counted.
- Linguistic and archaeological evidence trace the spread from about 3000 BCE.
- The expansion is associated with farming and, in places, iron working.
- It involved migration, intermarriage, and language shift, not only population replacement.

## Context & Connections
The expansion is a defining event of the [[Iron Age]] in [[Sub-Saharan Africa]]. Its states include [[Great Zimbabwe]].
