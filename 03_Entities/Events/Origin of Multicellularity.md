## Summary
Multicellularity, in which cells stay attached, divide labour and reproduce as a unit, arose independently many times. The clearest early fossil evidence is about 1.05 billion years old, and earlier claims are disputed.

## Facts
- Multicellularity evolved independently in bacteria, in several groups of algae, in fungi, in plants and in animals; estimates for eukaryotes alone run to more than twenty separate origins.
- Older claims include the Francevillian biota of Gabon, about 2.1 billion years old, and the coiled fossil Grypania, about 1.9 billion years old; their interpretation is disputed.
- The red alga Bangiomorpha, dated in different studies to between roughly 1.05 and 1.2 billion years ago, shows differentiated cells and sexual reproduction.
- Experiments have produced simple multicellular clusters from single-celled algae and yeast within weeks to months. The step to complex bodies with many cell types is much rarer.
- Animals evolved multicellularity separately from plants, and large soft-bodied animals appear in the Ediacaran; see [[Ediacaran Biota]] and [[Cambrian Explosion]].
