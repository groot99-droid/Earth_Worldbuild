## Summary
The incense trade carried frankincense and myrrh from southern Arabia and the Horn of Africa to Mediterranean and Mesopotamian markets, mostly by camel caravan, for well over a thousand years.

## Facts
- Frankincense comes from Boswellia trees growing in southern Arabia, including Oman and Yemen, and in the Horn of Africa.
- Incense was burned in temples and at funerals across the Egyptian, Greek, and Roman worlds.
- Caravans traveled northward through western Arabia to the Levant.
- Kingdoms such as Saba and the Nabataeans profited from taxing and guiding the caravans.
- The trade declined in the later Roman period, with reduced demand often cited as one cause.
