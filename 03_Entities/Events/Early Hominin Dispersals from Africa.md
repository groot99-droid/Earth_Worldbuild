## Summary
Hominins first left Africa between about 2.1 and 1.8 million years ago and spread across Eurasia in several dispersals over the next million years. The oldest hominin fossils outside Africa, at Dmanisi in Georgia, are about 1.85 to 1.78 million years old, and stone tools from the Loess Plateau of China are about 2.1 million years old.

## Facts
- Dmanisi has yielded five well-preserved skulls and many body bones, dated to about 1.85 to 1.77 million years ago, with simple Oldowan-style stone tools.
- The skulls have small brains, about 546 to 775 cubic centimeters. A 2013 study argued that their variation fits one population, which doubts separate early Homo species.
- Stone tools at Shangchen on the Chinese Loess Plateau date from about 2.1 to 1.3 million years ago. No hominin fossils were found there, so the toolmakers are unidentified.
- Homo erectus was on Java by about 1.5 to 1.3 million years ago, and hominins were in Spain by about 1.4 to 1.2 million years ago and in England about 0.9 million years ago.
- Whether the first dispersers were Homo erectus is debated; small brains and simple tools at Dmanisi suggest they left before that form was typical.

## Context & Connections
The early dispersals follow the [[Emergence of the Genus Homo]] and the [[Oldowan Stone Tools]] and belong to the [[Lower Paleolithic]]. They center on [[Homo erectus]]. [[Homo floresiensis]] on Flores may descend from an early dispersal; its ancestry is debated. Later dispersals are separate; see [[Out of Africa Dispersal]]. See [[Earth Systems and Life]] and [[Technology]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the first hominins outside Africa had small brains and simple stone tools, which suggests that leaving Africa did not require the large brain of later humans. The Observer reads dispersal as repeated range expansion into new habitats, of which the species' later spread is one more instance. The reading would weaken if the Dmanisi hominins proved an isolated dead end.

## Open Questions
- Who made the 2.1-million-year-old stone tools at Shangchen?
- Did the first hominins to leave Africa belong to Homo erectus or to an earlier form?
