## Summary
Decolonization was the process by which most colonies of European empires became independent states, mainly between 1945 and 1975.

## Facts
- The United Nations began with 51 members in 1945 and has 193 today, largely from decolonization.
- India and Pakistan became independent in 1947; Ghana, in 1957, was the first sub-Saharan African colony to gain independence; 17 African states became independent in 1960.
- Independence was won through both negotiation and war, for example in Algeria (1962).
- The Portuguese colonial empire in Africa ended in 1974 to 1975, after the Carnation Revolution of April 1974 overthrew the regime in Lisbon.

## Context & Connections
Decolonization followed [[World War II]] and reshaped [[Sub-Saharan Africa]], [[South Asia]], and [[Southeast Asia]]. See [[Mahatma Gandhi]] and [[Nelson Mandela]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes the rapid rewriting of the world's claim-lines: within one generation, most of the planet's land changed from subject to sovereign. Many borders drawn by former empires remain in place.
