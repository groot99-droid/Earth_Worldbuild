## Summary
The Meiji Restoration was the political and social transformation of Japan that followed the arrival of American warships in 1853 and ended the Tokugawa shogunate in 1868, beginning rapid industrialization.

## Facts
- Commodore Matthew Perry's fleet arrived at Uraga in July 1853, and the Convention of Kanagawa (1854) opened the ports of Shimoda and Hakodate to American ships.
- The domains of Choshu and Satsuma formed an alliance against the shogunate in 1866, and on 3 January 1868 imperial rule was 'restored' in the name of Emperor Meiji, then fifteen years old.
- The Charter Oath of 1868 promised reform, deliberative assemblies, and the pursuit of knowledge from around the world.
- The Iwakura Mission (1871 to 1873) toured the United States and Europe to study institutions.

## Context & Connections
The restoration ended the [[Tokugawa Shogunate]] and made [[Emperor Meiji]] the symbol of a modernizing state. Japan adopted [[Steam Engine]] and railway technology within a few decades.
