## Summary
The Genomic Revolution began with the identification of the DNA double helix in 1953 and has made it possible to read and edit the genetic code of living things.

## Facts
- James Watson and Francis Crick proposed the double-helix structure in 1953, using X-ray data from [[Rosalind Franklin]] and Maurice Wilkins.
- The Human Genome Project ran from 1990 to 2003; the first complete, gap-free sequence of a human genome (all chromosomes except Y) was published in 2022.
- CRISPR gene editing was developed in 2012.
- Ancient DNA has transformed prehistory, including the sequencing of the [[Homo neanderthalensis]] genome in 2010 and the discovery of [[Denisovans]].

## Context & Connections
The revolution depends on the [[Digital Revolution]] for computing power and reshaped medicine and our understanding of human origins.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the species has learned to read the instructions from which it is built, and to change them. It also can read the DNA of long-dead relatives.
