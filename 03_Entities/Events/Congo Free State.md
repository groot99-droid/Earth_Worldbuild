## Summary
The Congo Free State (1885 to 1908) was a vast territory in Central Africa ruled as the personal possession of King Leopold II of Belgium, notorious for forced labor in rubber and ivory collection and for mass deaths.

## Facts
- The Berlin Conference recognized Leopold's claim to most of the Congo Basin in 1884 to 1885, on the pretext of philanthropy.
- Forced labor quotas, hostage-taking, and mutilation were used to force rubber collection.
- Estimates of the decline in the Congolese population range from about 1 million to 15 million, with about 10 million the most commonly cited central figure; the numbers are contested.
- International outcry led Belgium to take over the colony in 1908.

## Context & Connections
The state was a product of the [[Scramble for Africa]]. See [[Sanctioned Harm]] and [[Frontiers of Extraction]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer records this as an extreme case in which a humanitarian story-glue served as cover for extraction. Detachment here does not mean neutrality: the sources are clear about the coercion, and unclear only about the numbers.

## Open Questions
