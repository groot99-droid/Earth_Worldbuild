## Summary
The Latin American wars of independence (about 1808 to 1826) ended Spanish rule over most of the Americas and created a series of new republics.

## Facts
- They followed Napoleon's invasion of Spain in 1808, which weakened Spanish authority and set off a crisis across the Spanish Empire, though it was not their only cause.
- Simón Bolívar and José de San Martín met at Guayaquil on 26 and 27 July 1822. They did not agree on how the new states should be governed, and San Martín then withdrew from the campaigns.
- The Battle of Ayacucho on 9 December 1824, won by Antonio José de Sucre, ended effective Spanish resistance in South America.
- Bolívar's Gran Colombia was proclaimed in December 1819 and covered what are now Venezuela, Colombia, Panama, and Ecuador. It dissolved in 1830 and 1831, about a decade later, into Venezuela, Ecuador, and New Granada.

## Context & Connections
The wars were part of the [[Age of Revolutions]], with the [[Haitian Revolution]] as an earlier example. See [[Simón Bolívar]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that independence in Latin America was won largely by colonial elites, and that many hierarchies of the colonial era outlasted the change of flags.
