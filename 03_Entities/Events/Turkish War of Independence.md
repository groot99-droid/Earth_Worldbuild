## Summary
The Turkish War of Independence (1919 to 1923) was fought by a nationalist movement under Mustafa Kemal against the Allied occupation of Anatolia and the invading armies of Greece and Armenia. It ended the Ottoman sultanate and established the Republic of Turkey.

## Facts
- After World War I, Allied forces occupied parts of the Ottoman Empire, and Greece and Armenia moved to claim territory.
- Mustafa Kemal, later known as Atatürk, organized a nationalist movement centred at Ankara. He convened congresses at Erzurum and Sivas, and a national parliament met in Ankara on 23 April 1920. He was elected commander in chief and created a new army from remaining Ottoman forces and irregular units.
- On 1 November 1922 the new parliament abolished the sultanate, ending about 623 years of Ottoman rule.
- The Treaty of Lausanne of 24 July 1923 recognized Turkey's borders and sovereignty, and a population exchange between Greece and Turkey followed. The Republic of Turkey was proclaimed on 29 October 1923.

## Context & Connections
The war grew out of the defeat and breakup of the [[Ottoman Empire]] after [[World War I]] and took place in the same region as the earlier [[Armenian Genocide]]. It belongs to the wider postwar reordering that includes [[Decolonization]] in later decades.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the same movement that fought off partition ended the dynasty in whose name the war began. An old legitimacy was set aside in three years, and a new one, built on a defined homeland, took its place.
