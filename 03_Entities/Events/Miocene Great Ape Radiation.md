## Summary
The Miocene great ape radiation was the diversification of apes in Africa, Europe and Asia between about 23 and 7 million years ago. Many more ape forms lived then than survive today.

## Facts
- Early Miocene apes of Africa include Proconsul from Kenya, about 20 million years old. Apes then spread into Europe and Asia, about 17 to 16 million years ago, when a land connection opened.
- Later forms include Dryopithecus and Danuvius from Europe, Ouranopithecus from Greece, and Sivapithecus from South Asia, which is related to orangutans. Only about twenty ape species live now.
- Molecular studies place the split of the gibbon lineage roughly 17 to 20 million years ago and the orangutan lineage roughly 13 to 16 million years ago, and that of the gorilla lineage later still.
- Apes declined in Europe and Asia as the climate cooled and dried, while the ancestors of the African apes and of the hominins survived in Africa.
- Whether the last common ancestor of the African apes and humans lived in Africa or in Europe is debated.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the species belongs to a group once far more diverse than it is now, and that its living relatives are a small remnant. Reading the history of apes from the survivors alone would give a false picture. The reading would weaken if Miocene apes turned out to be mostly close variants of a few forms.

## Open Questions
