## Summary
The Cretaceous-Paleogene (K-Pg) extinction, about 66 million years ago, wiped out the non-avian dinosaurs and about three-quarters of species after an asteroid struck what is now Mexico.

## Facts
- The Chicxulub impact crater lies beneath the Yucatan Peninsula.
- A global layer rich in iridium, an element common in asteroids, was reported by Luis and Walter Alvarez and colleagues in 1980.
- Volcanism in the Deccan Traps probably contributed to the stress on ecosystems.
- Birds, descended from small feathered dinosaurs, survived; see [[Dinosaurs]].

## Context & Connections
The event ends the [[Mesozoic Era]] and begins the [[Cenozoic Era]]. It gave mammals, including the ancestors of primates, new ecological room.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer regards this as the most consequential accident for the species' own story. Human history begins, in a real sense, with a rock falling on Yucatan.
