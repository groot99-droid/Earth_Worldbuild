## Summary
The Fish-to-Tetrapod Transition was the evolution of four-limbed vertebrates from lobe-finned fishes in the Middle and Late Devonian, roughly 385 to 360 million years ago. The sequence is well documented, but its start is debated.

## Facts
- Tetrapods descend from lobe-finned fishes, a group whose fins hold a central skeleton of bones.
- Transitional fossils include Panderichthys from Latvia, about 380 million years old, [[Tiktaalik]] from the Canadian Arctic, about 375 million years old, and Acanthostega and Ichthyostega from Greenland, about 365 million years old. The last two had limbs with digits but lived mostly in water.
- Limbs probably evolved for moving through shallow, vegetated water, and were used on land later; that reading is a hypothesis.
- Trackways from Zachełmie in Poland, dated to about 395 million years ago, have been read as tetrapod footprints. That would place limbed animals earlier than the known body fossils, but the interpretation is debated.

## Context & Connections
The Fish-to-Tetrapod Transition falls in the [[Devonian Period]] and is one thread of the [[Colonization of Land]]. It precedes the [[Late Devonian Extinction]]. The event belongs to the theme threads of [[Earth Systems and Life]] and [[Science]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the transition shows a feature's later use need not match its origin: limbs seem to have arisen for water and been used on land afterwards. It also notes that the fossils were found in part by predicting the rock age and setting where they should occur, which is an unusually testable case for the record. The reading would weaken if the earliest limbs proved to have served mainly for walking on land.

## Open Questions
- How old is the origin of limbed tetrapods, given the Zachełmie trackways?
- Which living fish groups, if any, preserve traits of the first limbed animals?
