## Summary
Between 1582 and 1639, Russian Cossacks and traders advanced across Siberia to the Pacific, driven by the fur trade and by tribute (yasak) taken from Indigenous peoples.

## Facts
- In 1582 the Cossack leader Yermak, with about 540 Cossacks and 300 Livonian war prisoners, seized the capital of the Khanate of Sibir.
- Russians reached the Pacific at Okhotsk by 1639.
- The advance was driven by European demand for luxury furs.
- Yasak, a fur tribute, was demanded of men aged eighteen to fifty; Indigenous populations suffered from disease, violence, and forced levies.

## Context & Connections
The advance followed the collapse of steppe khanates after the [[Mongol Empire]] and is a parallel to the [[Columbian Exchange]]. See [[Siberia and the Arctic]] and [[Yakut (Sakha)]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a frontier that moved across thousands of kilometers in about six decades, powered by small bands living off the fur trade. Commodity demand can move borders faster than armies.
