## Summary
The Tanzimat (Reorganization) was a series of reforms in the Ottoman Empire between 1839 and 1876 that tried to modernize the state, promise equality before the law to all subjects, and move it toward constitutional government.

## Facts
- The era ran from 1839 to 1876 under the sultans Abdülmecid I and Abdülaziz.
- The Edict of Gülhane, proclaimed on 3 November 1839, promised security of life, property, and honour for all subjects, fair taxation and conscription, public trials, and an end to tax farming.
- The Imperial Reform Edict of 18 February 1856 promised equality in education, government appointments, and justice regardless of creed.
- The period is said to end in 1876, when Abdulhamid II took the throne. The 1876 constitution created a parliamentary system but was suspended in 1878.

## Context & Connections
The Tanzimat was a response to European pressure and to earlier reformers in the empire. Compare [[Meiji Restoration]] in Japan and the reforms of [[Muhammad Ali of Egypt]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a state trying to change its own story-glue while the empire was still being defended, extending equality to subjects of every faith in the hope of holding a plural population together. Reforms promised on paper met the resistance of those with something to lose, and the constitution lasted only two years in practice.

## Open Questions
- How far were the Tanzimat promises of equality carried out in practice outside the capital?
- How much of the reform agenda was driven by European pressure and how much by the empire's own officials?
