## Summary
The Scientific Revolution was the shift, roughly 1543 to 1687, toward systematic experiment, mathematics, and observation as the basis for knowledge of nature, centered in Europe.

## Facts
- Marker works include Copernicus's heliocentric model and Vesalius's anatomy (both 1543).
- [[Galileo Galilei]] used the telescope and experiments, and [[Isaac Newton]]'s Principia (1687) unified terrestrial and celestial motion.
- It drew on earlier work in the Islamic world, India, and China; see [[Al-Khwarizmi]].
- Historians debate whether it was a single 'revolution'.

## Context & Connections
The revolution sits in the [[Early Modern Period]] and was enabled by the [[Movable-Type Printing Press]]. See [[Science]].
