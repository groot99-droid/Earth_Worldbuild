## Summary
From 1206, the Mongols under Genghis Khan and his successors built the largest contiguous land empire in history, stretching from Korea to Eastern Europe.

## Facts
- At its height the empire covered about 24 million square kilometers.
- The sack of Baghdad in 1258 ended the Abbasid Caliphate's rule there.
- Estimates of deaths run to tens of millions and are uncertain.
- The 'Pax Mongolica' briefly made long-distance overland travel safer and connected Eurasia.

## Context & Connections
See [[Mongol Empire]] and [[Genghis Khan]]. The conquests intersect the [[Opening of the Silk Roads]] and possibly the spread of the [[Black Death]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a paradox: enormous destruction, followed by a period of unusually free movement of goods, ideas, and people across Eurasia.
