## Summary
Humans domesticated animals independently in several regions. The dog came first, by at least 15,000 years ago, and livestock (goats, sheep, pigs, and cattle) followed from about 11,000 to 8,500 years ago, with the first goats and sheep about 10,500 years ago (about 8500 BCE), which is the date used for this note.

## Facts
- Dogs, descended from wolves, were the first domesticated animal; the exact timing and location remain debated.
- Goats and sheep were domesticated in Southwest Asia around 10,000 years ago.
- Cattle, pigs, chickens, horses, camels, llamas, and others were domesticated in different places.
- Herd animals provided meat, milk, hides, wool, traction, and transport.

## Context & Connections
Domestication is part of [[The Agricultural Revolution]] and reshaped the [[Neolithic]] economy. It also brought new diseases from animals to humans.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a two-way relationship: the species altered the bodies and behavior of other animals, and those animals altered the species' diet, work, disease environment, and even genes such as lactase persistence.
