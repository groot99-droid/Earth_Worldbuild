## Summary
The Permian-Triassic extinction, about 251.9 million years ago, was the largest mass extinction in Earth's history. It killed an estimated 80 to 96 percent of marine species.

## Facts
- The leading cause is enormous volcanic eruptions in Siberia (the Siberian Traps).
- Consequences included rapid warming, ocean acidification, and oxygen loss in the seas.
- It ended the dominance of trilobites, which disappeared.
- Full ecological recovery took roughly ten million years.

## Context & Connections
It marks the end of the [[Paleozoic Era]] and the start of the [[Mesozoic Era]]. See [[Trilobites]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer regards this as the nearest the biosphere has come to ending. Its cause, a carbon-releasing planetary change, has an uncomfortable resemblance to a process the species is now carrying out.
