## Summary
Between 336 and 323 BCE, Alexander III of Macedon conquered the Achaemenid Persian Empire and extended his rule from Greece and Egypt to the edge of India.

## Facts
- Alexander became king at about twenty and died in Babylon at thirty-two.
- His campaigns ended the [[Achaemenid Persians]]' empire.
- After his death, his generals divided the empire into Hellenistic kingdoms.
- Greek language and culture spread widely and blended with local traditions.

## Context & Connections
See [[Alexander the Great]]. The conquests reshaped [[Classical Antiquity]] and helped open the [[Opening of the Silk Roads]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes the outsized role of one individual's ambition, and, more lastingly, the exchange it created: a region-wide Greek-speaking elite that made ideas move more easily.
