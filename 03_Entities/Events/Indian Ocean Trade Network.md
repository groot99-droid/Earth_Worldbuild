## Summary
The Indian Ocean trade network was a system of sea routes linking East Africa, Arabia, South Asia, Southeast Asia, and China, and it was busiest from about the 8th to the 15th century CE.

## Facts
- Seasonal monsoon winds allowed ships to sail east in one season and return in another.
- Traded goods included spices, cotton textiles, ivory, gold, and Chinese porcelain.
- Swahili city-states such as Kilwa on the East African coast grew rich on it; see [[Swahili Coast City-States]].
- Portuguese ships entered the ocean in 1498 and began to seize control of key ports.

## Context & Connections
The network complemented the overland [[Opening of the Silk Roads]] and carried Islam and other religions along its ports. See [[Zheng He]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the wind itself acted as a scheduler. A predictable natural cycle allowed strangers of many faiths to run a shared exchange-web for centuries with little central authority.
