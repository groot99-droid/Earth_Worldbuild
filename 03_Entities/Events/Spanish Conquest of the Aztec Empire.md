## Summary
Between 1519 and 1521, a small Spanish force under Hernan Cortes, allied with many Indigenous city-states and aided by epidemic disease, overthrew the Aztec Empire and captured Tenochtitlan.

## Facts
- Cortes landed on the Mexican coast in 1519 with several hundred men.
- Tlaxcala and other city-states resentful of Aztec tribute provided large numbers of allied fighters.
- A smallpox epidemic in 1520, introduced by the Spanish, killed a large part of the population of the region.
- Malintzin (Dona Marina) served as an interpreter and negotiator for Cortes.
- Tenochtitlan fell on 13 August 1521, after a siege of about three months.

## Context & Connections
The conquest ended the [[Aztec Empire]] and destroyed its capital, [[Tenochtitlan]]. It is an early episode of the [[Columbian Exchange]], in which disease played a central part. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the conquest is sometimes told as a victory of a few hundred men, but the record shows Indigenous allies and disease doing much of the work. Simple stories of small forces conquering empires tend to leave out the local politics.
