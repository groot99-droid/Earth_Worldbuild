## Summary
The Age of Revolutions is a period of political upheaval around the Atlantic world, beginning in the 1770s and running to the revolutions of 1848. Its dates are a matter of historians' choice, and this note uses 1775 to 1848.

## Facts
- Historians periodize the era differently. Eric Hobsbawm's *The Age of Revolution* covers 1789 to 1848; Robert Palmer's *The Age of the Democratic Revolution* starts from about 1760; some accounts begin as early as 1765.
- Major events: the American Revolution (to 1783), the French Revolution (1789 to 1799), the Haitian Revolution (1791 to 1804), the Spanish American wars of independence (beginning about 1808 to 1810), and the revolutions of 1848.
- The Haitian Revolution began with a mass uprising of enslaved people in northern Saint-Domingue on 22 August 1791. Independence was declared on 1 January 1804, and Haiti is widely described as the first state founded by formerly enslaved people. It was the second independent country in the Americas, after the United States.
- Ideas of popular sovereignty, citizenship, and nationalism spread widely.
- Slavery and the rights of women were contested and largely excluded at first.

## Context & Connections
The revolutions overlap the [[Industrial Revolution]] and reshape claim-lines and legitimacy; see [[The Fiction of Claim-Lines]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a change in the story-glue of legitimacy: authority increasingly claimed to come from 'the people' rather than from tradition or the divine.

## Open Questions
- How connected were the revolutions to each other? Older histories treated them as separate episodes; recent scholarship increasingly treats them as a linked chain, and the question of causation is still argued.
