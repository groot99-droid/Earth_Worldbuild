## Summary
From the mid-8th to the 10th century, scholars in Abbasid Baghdad translated Greek, Persian, Syriac, and Indian works into Arabic and extended them.

## Facts
- The caliphs sponsored translation and scholarship; the House of Wisdom in Baghdad is its best-known institution, though its exact nature is debated.
- Translators included Hunayn ibn Ishaq, who worked on Greek medical and philosophical texts.
- Cheap [[Paper]] made copying books practical.
- Later Latin translations of Arabic texts fed European scholarship in the 12th century.

## Context & Connections
The movement was a project of the [[Abbasid Caliphate]] and enabled scholars like [[Al-Khwarizmi]]. It fed the later [[Scientific Revolution]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a society can treat other peoples' knowledge as a resource to be collected, rather than as a threat. Translation is a technology of memory-outsourcing across languages.
