## Summary
The Agricultural Revolution was the gradual shift, beginning about 11,500 years ago, from foraging to farming and herding, which arose independently in several parts of the world.

## Facts
- The earliest farming appears in the Fertile Crescent (wheat, barley, lentils, and later livestock).
- Independent centers include China (rice, millet), Mesoamerica (maize, beans, squash), the Andes (potato), and New Guinea.
- Farming supported denser populations and permanent settlement.
- Some evidence suggests poorer health among early farmers: in a study of 167 prehistoric Europeans, Neolithic individuals were shorter than their genetic scores predicted, by about 2 to 4 cm relative to earlier and later groups.

## Context & Connections
Farming defines the [[Neolithic]] and sets the conditions for [[The Urban Revolution]]. See [[Göbekli Tepe]], [[Çatalhöyük]], and the essay [[The Settling and Its Bargain]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer calls this the Settling and treats it as the species' most consequential bargain: more food and more people in exchange for more work, more inequality, and more disease.

## Open Questions
