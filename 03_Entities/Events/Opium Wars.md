## Summary
The Opium Wars were two conflicts between Qing China and Britain (and, in the second, France) over trade and diplomatic relations, fought 1839 to 1842 and 1856 to 1860, which opened China to Western commerce on unequal terms.

## Facts
- The Treaty of Nanking (1842) ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain and opened five treaty ports: Canton, Amoy, Foochow, Ningpo, and Shanghai.
- The Convention of Beijing (1860) gave Britain the Kowloon peninsula and granted foreign powers permanent diplomatic presence in Beijing.
- It also legalized the opium trade and gave Christians the right to own property and preach.
- The wars are remembered in China as the start of a 'century of humiliation'.

## Context & Connections
The wars weakened the [[Qing Dynasty]] just before the [[Taiping Rebellion]]. Industrial-age naval and firearm superiority, from the [[Industrial Revolution]], decided the outcome.
