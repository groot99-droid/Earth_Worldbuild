## Summary
World War II (1939 to 1945) was a global conflict that killed an estimated 60 to 85 million people (estimates vary by source), including about six million Jews murdered in the Holocaust.

## Facts
- Fighting spanned Europe, Asia, Africa, and the Pacific.
- The Holocaust was the state-organized genocide of European Jews; millions of others, including Roma and disabled people, were also murdered.
- The war ended with atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945; see [[Nuclear Age]].
- Its aftermath brought the United Nations, decolonization, and the Cold War.

## Context & Connections
The war ends the [[Industrial Age]] and begins the [[Information Age]]. See [[Sanctioned Harm]] and [[Alan Turing]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer records the war as the deadliest in the record and the Holocaust as an example of industrial methods used for organized killing. It is a case where the species' most sophisticated organization produced its worst outcomes.
