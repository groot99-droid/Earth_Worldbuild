## Summary
Anatomically modern humans, Homo sapiens, emerged in Africa by about 300,000 years ago through a gradual, continent-wide process.

## Facts
- The Jebel Irhoud fossils in Morocco are about 315,000 years old (315 plus or minus 34 thousand years) and show a modern face and teeth with a more archaic braincase.
- Fossils from Omo Kibish (Ethiopia) are at least 233,000 years old, dated against a volcanic ash layer.
- Some scholars describe the process as 'African multiregionalism', with several connected populations contributing.
- The regional tag here is approximate: Jebel Irhoud is in North Africa, while the other main sites are in East and southern Africa.

## Context & Connections
The origin belongs to the [[Pleistocene Epoch]] and precedes the [[Out of Africa Dispersal]]. See [[Homo sapiens]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the species' origin is not a single place or moment. It is a widespread, interbreeding population that became recognizably modern over tens of thousands of years.
