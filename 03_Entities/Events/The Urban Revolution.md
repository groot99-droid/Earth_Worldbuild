## Summary
The Urban Revolution is the emergence of the first true cities, beginning around 3500 BCE in southern Mesopotamia, with dense populations, specialized labor, and central institutions.

## Facts
- The term was popularized by archaeologist V. Gordon Childe in the mid-20th century.
- [[Uruk]] may have held 40,000 to 80,000 people by about 3100 to 2900 BCE (the estimates are uncertain).
- Cities also arose independently in the Indus valley, China, Mesoamerica, and the Andes.
- Cities coincide with administrative record keeping, temples or palaces, and social stratification.

## Context & Connections
Cities appear in the [[Bronze Age]] and are closely tied to [[Invention of Writing]]; see [[Sumerians]] and [[Mesopotamia]].
