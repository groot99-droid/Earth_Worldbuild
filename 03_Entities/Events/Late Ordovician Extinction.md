## Summary
The Late Ordovician extinction (about 445 to 443 million years ago) is the first of the 'Big Five' mass extinctions, and it eliminated an estimated 85 to 86 percent of species, mostly marine.

## Facts
- It occurred in two pulses.
- Glaciation and falling sea levels are the leading proposed causes, followed by rising sea levels and ocean anoxia.
- Marine life was hit hardest, since most animals still lived in the sea.

## Context & Connections
It is one of the extinctions of the [[Paleozoic Era]] and is one of five 'Big Five' mass extinctions.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer counts mass extinctions as the planet's editing process. Each one removes most of a biosphere and lets survivors expand.
