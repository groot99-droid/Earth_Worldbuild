## Summary
The Silk Roads were overlapping overland and sea routes linking East Asia, South Asia, Central Asia, and the Mediterranean, which grew from the second century BCE.

## Facts
- Missions by the Han envoy Zhang Qian, around 138 to 126 BCE, are often taken as a starting point.
- Goods included silk, horses, glass, spices, and paper; ideas and religions such as Buddhism spread along them.
- Disease also traveled the routes; see [[Black Death]].
- The name 'Silk Road' was coined in 1877 by the German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen.

## Context & Connections
The routes link [[East Asia]], the [[Central Asian Steppe]], [[South Asia]] and the [[Mediterranean Basin]], and grew under the [[Han Dynasty]] and later the [[Mongol Empire]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer treats the Silk Roads as an exchange-web with no owner. Very few travelers went the whole way; goods and ideas moved through chains of intermediaries.
