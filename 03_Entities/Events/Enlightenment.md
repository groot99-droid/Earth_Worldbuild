## Summary
The Enlightenment was an 18th-century intellectual movement in Europe that emphasized reason, evidence, and individual rights, and challenged traditional authority.

## Facts
- Key figures include John Locke, Voltaire, Montesquieu, Jean-Jacques Rousseau, and Immanuel Kant.
- The Encyclopedie, edited by Denis Diderot and Jean d'Alembert, was published between 1751 and 1772.
- Adam Smith published The Wealth of Nations in 1776.
- Its ideas circulated in salons, coffeehouses, and journals.
- Enlightenment thinkers held divergent views on slavery and colonialism: many, including Diderot and Kant, criticized them, while some contributed to racial classifications that were used to justify them.
