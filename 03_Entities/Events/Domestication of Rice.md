## Summary
Rice (Oryza sativa) was domesticated from wild rice in the Yangtze River region of China beginning around 9,000 to 7,000 years ago, over a long process rather than a single event.

## Facts
- Wild ancestor populations of rice are usually identified as Oryza rufipogon.
- Early evidence for rice use and cultivation comes from Yangtze valley sites such as Shangshan, where rice phytoliths date to about 9,000 years ago, and later Kuahuqiao and Hemudu.
- Domestication traits such as non-shattering seed heads appeared gradually over thousands of years.
- The japonica subspecies is generally traced to the Yangtze region; the indica subspecies is thought to have arisen through later crossing with other rice populations, an origin that is debated.
- Rice is a staple for more than half of the world's population.
