## Summary
The Last Glacial Maximum was the most recent time when ice sheets reached their greatest extent, about 26,500 to 19,000 years ago.

## Facts
- Sea level was about 134 meters below today's at its lowest, according to a 2014 global reconstruction.
- Ice sheets covered much of northern North America and Europe.
- Land exposed by lower seas connected Siberia and Alaska (Beringia) and joined New Guinea and Australia.
- Human populations retreated to refuges in some areas and adapted in others.

## Context & Connections
The maximum falls within the [[Pleistocene Epoch]] and just precedes the warming that leads to the [[Holocene Epoch]]. It affected the [[Peopling of the Americas]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer sees a bottleneck: the coldest, driest stretch of the species' history preceded the warm stability in which farming and cities appeared.
