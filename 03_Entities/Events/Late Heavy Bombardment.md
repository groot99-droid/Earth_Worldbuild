## Summary
The Late Heavy Bombardment is a hypothesized spike in asteroid impacts on the inner Solar System, roughly 4.1 to 3.8 billion years ago, inferred mainly from radiometric ages of lunar impact rocks.

## Facts
- The hypothesis grew from Apollo-era rock samples whose impact-melt ages cluster near 3.8 to 3.9 billion years.
- Some researchers argue the cluster reflects sampling of a few huge basin impacts rather than a real surge in the whole Solar System's impact rate.
- The Moon preserves the impact record; Earth's earliest crust has been largely destroyed by plate tectonics and erosion.
- Some dynamical models, such as the Nice model, propose that a shift in the giant planets' orbits could have triggered such a surge.
- Whether the episode happened at all, and whether it would have sterilized early life, remains debated.
