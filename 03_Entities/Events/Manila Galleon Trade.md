## Summary
From 1565 to 1815, Spanish galleons sailed each year between Manila and Acapulco, carrying Chinese goods to the Americas and American silver to Asia in the first regular trans-Pacific trade.

## Facts
- Andres de Urdaneta found the return route across the North Pacific in 1565.
- Manila served as the exchange point where Chinese merchants sold silk and porcelain for silver.
- Silver from the Americas, much of it from Potosi, flowed to China.
- Goods were carried overland across Mexico and reshipped to Spain.
- The trade ended after Mexican independence.

## Context & Connections
The route linked the [[Ming Dynasty]]'s economy to Spanish American silver from [[Potosi]] and was a part of the wider [[Columbian Exchange]]. Manila Galleon Trade sits in the [[Early Modern Period]] and the region [[Southeast Asia]], and belongs to the theme thread of [[Trade and Economy]].
