## Summary
The Battle of Talas in 751 CE, near the Talas River in present-day Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan, pitted a Tang Chinese army against an Abbasid army, and ended in Tang defeat.

## Facts
- The Tang army was led by Gao Xianzhi, and Karluk mercenaries in the Tang army are reported to have defected, though sources differ on whether the Karluks were Abbasid allies from the start.
- The defeat, followed by the An Lushan Rebellion beginning in 755, ended Tang expansion in Central Asia.
- A tradition holds that Chinese captives taught papermaking to the Islamic world at Samarkand; historians debate the role of the battle in the transfer of the technology.
- Paper mills were operating in Samarkand and Baghdad by the later 8th century.
- It is often used as a symbolic marker of Islamic influence in Central Asia.

## Context & Connections
The battle fixed the frontier between the [[Abbasid Caliphate]] and the [[Tang Dynasty]] in the oases of [[Samarkand]], and is linked to the diffusion of [[Paper]] westward. The [[Sogdians]] were the merchants of the region.
