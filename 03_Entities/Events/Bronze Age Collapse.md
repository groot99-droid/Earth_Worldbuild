## Summary
Around 1200 BCE, many states of the eastern Mediterranean and Near East collapsed within a few decades, ending the Late Bronze Age palace economies.

## Facts
- The Hittite Empire fell, Mycenaean palaces were destroyed, and Ugarit was burned.
- Egyptian inscriptions record attacks by the 'Sea Peoples', around 1177 BCE.
- Proposed causes include drought, earthquakes, migrations, and the breakdown of trade networks.
- Egypt survived but was weakened.

## Context & Connections
The collapse ends the [[Bronze Age]] and leads to the [[Iron Age]]. It affected the [[Minoans]]' successors and the Aegean world.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer treats the collapse as a case study in interdependence: a densely connected system can fail at once. The lesson repeats across eras.
