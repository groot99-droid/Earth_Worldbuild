## Summary
Mesa Verde is a plateau in the Four Corners region of southwestern Colorado, USA, where Ancestral Puebloan farmers lived from about 550 CE until about 1300 and built the cliff dwellings of the late 12th and 13th centuries.

## Facts
- Mesa Verde lies in Montezuma County, Colorado; people used the area for thousands of years before farming communities settled the mesa, and they lived there continuously for about 700 years until the late 13th century.
- By the end of the 12th century residents began building cliff dwellings in sandstone alcoves beneath the mesa rim; the park holds more than 5,000 archaeological sites, including about 600 cliff dwellings, and Cliff Palace is among the largest cliff dwellings in North America.
- Tree-ring records show a severe drought from 1276 to 1299, and drought, resource depletion and overpopulation are all proposed as causes of instability; the last residents left by about 1300.
- Pueblo peoples such as the Hopi, Zuni and Acoma regard the Ancestral Puebloans as their ancestors, and many Pueblo people prefer "Ancestral Puebloan" to the older term "Anasazi".
- The ranchers Richard Wetherill and Charlie Mason reached Cliff Palace on 18 December 1888, artifact collecting followed, and the site was protected as a national park in 1906.
- UNESCO inscribed Mesa Verde in 1978.

## Context & Connections

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a place called "abandoned" in one telling is "left" in another: the people who built the cliff dwellings did not vanish, and their descendants keep the memory of the move in sound-code and in place rather than in a mark-code. Building homes in alcoves and leaving them within about a century shows a community adjusting to a drying climate, and the Observer records the leaving as a choice made under hunger and strain. The reading would weaken if the dwellings prove to have served mainly as seasonal storage or ritual sites instead of homes.

## Open Questions
- Why did residents build in the alcoves after centuries of living on the mesa top, and how much was defense against conflict?
- Where did the people who left go, and how did they shape the later Pueblo communities of the Rio Grande?
