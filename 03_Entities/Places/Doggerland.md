## Summary
Doggerland was a lowland region of hunter-gatherers that connected Britain to continental Europe and lay where the North Sea is today until it was gradually flooded by rising seas.

## Facts
- After the last ice age, sea level rose as ice sheets melted, and the land now under the southern North Sea was progressively submerged.
- It was largely underwater by around 6000 BCE, though a 2020 study argues that remnant islands survived for centuries after the tsunami and that the last land was submerged about a thousand years later.
- The Storegga Slide, a huge submarine landslide off Norway, produced a tsunami around 6200 BCE that struck the coasts of the region.
- Fishing vessels have dredged up animal bones and worked bone points from the seabed, and surveys have mapped submerged river valleys.
- Its inhabitants were Mesolithic hunter-gatherers.

## Context & Connections
Doggerland is a European counterpart to [[Beringia]], a lost landscape drowned by post-glacial sea-level rise, and it followed the [[Last Glacial Maximum]] and the [[Younger Dryas]].
