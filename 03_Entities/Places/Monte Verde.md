## Summary
Monte Verde is an archaeological site in southern Chile that, for decades, was considered the best evidence that people lived in South America about 14,500 years ago, before the Clovis culture of North America; its age is now debated.

## Facts
- Excavations led by Tom Dillehay from the 1970s exposed wooden hut foundations, leather, rope, and plant remains preserved in a peat bog.
- In 1997 a panel of archaeologists inspected the site and accepted an age of about 14,500 years.
- The site helped overturn the 'Clovis-first' model; see [[Peopling of the Americas]].
- A recent study has proposed a much younger age for the site; specialists dispute this, and the question is unsettled.

## Context & Connections
Monte Verde is a key site for the [[Peopling of the Americas]], dating from the end of the [[Last Glacial Maximum]].

## Observer's Reading
