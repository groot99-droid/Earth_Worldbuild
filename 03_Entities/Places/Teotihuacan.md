## Summary
Teotihuacan was a huge planned city in the Valley of Mexico that flourished from about 100 BCE to 550 CE, home to the Pyramids of the Sun and Moon.

## Facts
- At its peak, around 450 to 500 CE, it may have had 125,000 to 200,000 inhabitants.
- The city was laid out on a grid along the 'Avenue of the Dead'.
- It had wide trade and political links, including with Maya cities.
- The Aztecs later gave it its name, meaning roughly 'place where the gods were created'.

## Context & Connections
Teotihuacan influenced the [[Maya]] and other peoples of [[Mesoamerica]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that its builders left no deciphered writing, and that later peoples treated the ruins as sacred. Even ruins are read through a later culture's meaning-engine.
