## Summary
Monte Alban was the hilltop capital of the Zapotec people in the Oaxaca Valley of Mexico, founded around 500 BCE and abandoned by about 800 CE.

## Facts
- The site sits on a leveled mountaintop overlooking three valleys.
- Carved stones known as Danzantes, dated to the early centuries of the city, show figures with glyphs, part of an early Mesoamerican writing tradition.
- The city grew to a population of well over ten thousand in its Classic period.
- It maintained relations with Teotihuacan, and depictions at Teotihuacan show Zapotec visitors.
- It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1987.

## Context & Connections
Monte Alban was a major political center of Mesoamerica in the period between the [[Olmecs]] and [[Teotihuacan]], and one of the birthplaces of [[Invention of Writing]] in the region.
