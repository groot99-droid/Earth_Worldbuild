## Summary
Varanasi, also called Benares or Kashi, is a city on the Ganges in Uttar Pradesh, India, continuously settled since about the 8th century BCE and the most important Hindu pilgrimage and cremation center.

## Facts
- Varanasi lies on the left bank of the Ganges in Uttar Pradesh, and excavations at Rajghat show settlement from about the 8th century BCE.
- Hindu tradition holds that dying at Varanasi and being cremated beside the Ganges gives release from the cycle of rebirth, so families bring their dead from across India to cremation sites such as Manikarnika, and more than 80 bathing and cremation steps (ghats) line the river.
- The Buddha is traditionally said to have given his first sermon at Sarnath, near Varanasi, in about the 5th century BCE; Sarnath has an Ashokan pillar and Gupta-period sculpture.
- Varanasi has been a center of Sanskrit learning, and Banaras Hindu University was founded in 1916; the city is also known for silk weaving.
- The Kashi Vishwanath temple was destroyed and rebuilt several times, and the present temple dates from 1780; the adjacent Gyanvapi Mosque and the history of the site are the subject of continuing legal disputes.
- Varanasi had about 1.2 million people at the 2011 census.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Varanasi is a place where the grief-rite is the local institution: bodies from far away are brought to the riverbank because a meaning-engine teaches that death there brings release, and a supporting economy of priests, wood sellers and boatmen has formed around the practice. A Buddhist site, Hindu temples and a mosque within a few kilometers show several meaning-engines using one stretch of river, and the continuing dispute over one temple and mosque pair shows claim-lines still being drawn around old buildings. The reading would weaken if the cremation trade proves to be a small part of the city's economy compared with its other trades.
