## Summary
L'Anse aux Meadows is an archaeological site on the northern tip of Newfoundland, Canada, where Norse people from Greenland built a small settlement of sod buildings around 1000 CE, the only widely accepted Norse site in North America.

## Facts
- L'Anse aux Meadows lies at the northernmost tip of Newfoundland; Helge and Anne Stine Ingstad found it in 1960 with the help of a local resident, George Decker, and excavated it in the 1960s.
- The site has eight buildings of sod over wooden frames, including an iron smithy, a carpentry workshop and a boat-repair area, and more than 800 Norse objects were found.
- Radiocarbon dates place activity between about 990 and 1050 CE; a 2021 study of tree rings, using a solar-storm signal in the year 993, dated felled timber cut with metal tools to 1021 CE.
- The occupation was short, perhaps about 20 years, though this is debated, and no burials or signs of farming were found, so the site appears to have been left in an orderly way; the date_end of 1030 is a rough placeholder.
- Butternuts found at the site grow farther south, so Norse travelers reached or traded with regions beyond Newfoundland.
- The Icelandic sagas, written down in the 13th century, describe voyages to a land called Vinland, and whether L'Anse aux Meadows is that place or a base for voyages farther south is debated.
- UNESCO inscribed L'Anse aux Meadows in 1978.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that here a story-glue, the sagas, and a physical record, the sod buildings and iron working, were long treated as separate until the Ingstads matched them. The site shows a link between two hemispheres that did not become an exchange-web, because no lasting settlement, herds or diseases were left to change either side. The sagas describe meetings with the region's Indigenous people, and how those meetings went is known only from the Norse side. The reading would weaken if evidence shows that contact between the Norse and northeastern peoples was more frequent and lasting than a short-lived camp suggests.
