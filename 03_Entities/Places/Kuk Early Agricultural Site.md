## Summary
The Kuk Early Agricultural Site in the highlands of Papua New Guinea preserves drainage ditches and gardens showing that people cultivated wetlands there for at least 7,000 years.

## Facts
- The site is in the Wahgi Valley of Papua New Guinea and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2008.
- It is reported to show agricultural use for at least 7,000 years, possibly beginning as early as 10,000 years ago.
- Excavations show a gradual shift from exploiting taro and yam on wetland margins about 7,000 years ago to organized cultivation of bananas on drained ground about 4,000 years ago.
- It is one of the few places worldwide widely regarded as an independent center of plant domestication.
- The highlands of New Guinea were settled by people descended from those who reached Sahul more than 50,000 years ago.

## Context & Connections
Kuk shows that agriculture arose independently in Oceania, in parallel with [[The Agricultural Revolution]] in Southwest Asia and [[Domestication of Rice]] in East Asia.
