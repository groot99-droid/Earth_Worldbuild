## Summary
Cahokia was the largest city of the Mississippian culture, near present-day St. Louis, flourishing from about 1050 to 1350 CE.

## Facts
- At its peak, about 1050 to 1150, it may have held 10,000 to 20,000 people, though some estimates are much higher.
- Monks Mound, about 30 meters high, is the largest earthen mound in the Americas.
- A circle of posts, known as Woodhenge, likely tracked the sun.
- Its population relied on maize agriculture and declined after about 1250, for reasons that are debated. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1982.

## Context & Connections
Cahokia is the largest known urban center in pre-Columbian [[North America]] north of Mexico. See [[Domestication of Maize]].

## Observer's Reading
