## Summary
Marib is a city in Yemen on the edge of the Ramlat al-Sabatayn desert, east of Sanaa, which was the capital of the Kingdom of Saba from about the 8th century BCE and the site of a great dam that irrigated the oasis.

## Facts
- Marib appears in Sabaean inscriptions from about the 8th century BCE and was the capital of the Kingdom of Saba, whose wealth came from the incense trade.
- The Great Dam of Marib, first built in about the 8th century BCE, diverted seasonal floods from Wadi Adhanah into irrigation canals and was raised and repaired over many centuries; an inscription records a repair in 542 to 543 CE.
- The dam failed for the last time around the 6th century CE; the Quran refers to a flood that ended the prosperity of Saba, which tradition connects with the dam.
- Marib has temples of Almaqah, the chief god of Saba, including the oval Awwam temple, which local tradition calls Mahram Bilqis.
- A modern dam at Marib, built in the 1980s with funding from the United Arab Emirates, replaced the ancient system.
- Since 2015 Marib has been a frontline city in the Yemeni civil war and has taken in large numbers of people displaced by the fighting.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Marib shows a state built on water as much as on incense: a dam that turned flood into farmland needed labor, tribute and coordination, and the inscribed repairs show that upkeep was a recurring political act. When order failed, so did the dam, which suggests that infrastructure and authority rise and fall together. The later story linking the dam's failure to a moral cause is a story-glue about the fall of a prosperous society. Historians debate whether the dam's collapse caused the decline or reflected a decline already under way, and the reading would weaken if the dam's failures are shown to be mainly hydrological.
