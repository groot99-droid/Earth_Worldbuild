## Summary
Chauvet Cave in the Ardeche region of southern France contains some of the oldest known figurative paintings, made by early Homo sapiens about 36,000 years ago.

## Facts
- The cave was found in December 1994 by Jean-Marie Chauvet, Eliette Brunel, and Christian Hillaire.
- It holds hundreds of paintings and engravings of animals including horses, lions, mammoths, rhinoceroses, and bears.
- Radiocarbon dates place the main painted phase at about 37,000 to 33,500 years ago, in the Aurignacian, with a second phase of use about 31,000 to 28,000 years ago.
- A rockfall sealed the cave entrance, preserving the art until its discovery.
- It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2014; a full-scale replica, the Caverne du Pont d'Arc, opened nearby in 2015.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the earliest painted animals show skilled drawing, not clumsy beginnings, suggesting that a longer tradition lies behind them. Access to the cave is now restricted and a replica is visited instead, a modern echo of the choice to make images rather than only look at the animals.
