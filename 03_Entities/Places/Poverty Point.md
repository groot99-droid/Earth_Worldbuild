## Summary
Poverty Point is a large earthwork complex in Louisiana built by hunter-gatherer-fishers between about 1700 and 1100 BCE, with concentric ridges and huge mounds.

## Facts
- The site has six concentric earthen ridges and several mounds, the largest about 22 meters high.
- It was built by a society without agriculture, according to current evidence.
- Stone and other materials were brought from across the Mississippi valley, some from hundreds of kilometers away.
- It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2014.
- The site was abandoned around 1100 BCE.

## Context & Connections
Poverty Point is the earliest of the large earthwork centers of eastern North America, long before [[Cahokia]] and the [[Chaco Canyon]] settlements.
