## Summary
Caral is an ancient city in Peru's Supe Valley, occupied from about 2600 BCE, and one of the oldest known urban centers in the Americas.

## Facts
- Its monumental platform mounds and sunken plazas date to the same era as the Egyptian pyramids.
- The society had no pottery and no evidence of large-scale warfare.
- Coastal fishing provided anchovies and sardines, and cotton for nets, while inland farming provided crops.
- Knotted cords found at the site may be early relatives of the quipu; see [[Quipu]]. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2009.

## Context & Connections
Caral shows [[The Urban Revolution]] occurring independently in the [[Andes]], long before the [[Inca Empire]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a city built on a mix of sea and farm foods, with monuments but seemingly little violence. It challenges a picture in which cities depend on grain and conquest.
