## Summary
Great Zimbabwe was a large stone-walled capital city in southeastern Africa, occupied from about the 11th to 15th century CE, ancestral to the Shona-speaking peoples.

## Facts
- Its Great Enclosure has dry-stone walls up to about 11 meters high.
- Older estimates gave 10,000 to 20,000 people; more recent modeling suggests the population did not exceed about 10,000.
- It traded gold and ivory with the Swahili coast and beyond.
- The modern country Zimbabwe is named after it.

## Context & Connections
The city is part of the history of the [[Bantu Expansion]] and of Indian Ocean trade linked to [[Sub-Saharan Africa]].
