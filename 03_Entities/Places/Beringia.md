## Summary
Beringia was the land region, including a broad land bridge, that joined northeastern Siberia and Alaska when sea levels were lower during the last ice age, and that later flooded to form the Bering Strait.

## Facts
- Lower sea level during glacial periods exposed the shallow floor of the Bering and Chukchi Seas.
- At its widest, the exposed land was more than a thousand kilometers from north to south.
- Much of Beringia was cold, dry steppe-tundra that supported mammoths, bison, and horses, and was largely unglaciated.
- Genetic evidence suggests ancestors of Native Americans may have been isolated in Beringia for several thousand years before moving south; this is the Beringian standstill hypothesis, still debated.
- Rising seas began flooding the land bridge around 11,000 years ago, and it was fully submerged by roughly 10,000 to 9,000 years ago.
