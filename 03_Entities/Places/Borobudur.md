## Summary
Borobudur is a large Mahayana Buddhist monument in central Java, built around 800 CE under the Sailendra dynasty and often described as the world's largest Buddhist temple.

## Facts
- Construction is dated to roughly the late 8th and early 9th centuries.
- The monument has nine stacked platforms and is decorated with 2,672 relief panels and originally 504 Buddha statues.
- It was abandoned in the centuries after the shift of power in Java and later covered by vegetation and volcanic ash.
- Thomas Stamford Raffles's agent, Hermann Cornelius, cleared the site in 1814.
- It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1991 after major restoration.

## Context & Connections
Borobudur reflects the Buddhist world of [[Srivijaya]] and the neighboring Khmer temple-building tradition at [[Angkor]]. It represents the teachings of [[Siddhartha Gautama]] as a path of ascent.
