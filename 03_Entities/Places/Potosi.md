## Summary
Potosi is a mining city in the Bolivian Andes, founded in 1545 beside the silver mountain Cerro Rico, whose output supplied Spanish and world trade for centuries.

## Facts
- Silver was discovered at Cerro Rico in 1545.
- By around 1600 Potosi was one of the largest cities in the Americas, with population estimates around 150,000.
- The Spanish organized labor through the mita, a forced-labor draft adapted from an Inca system.
- The mercury amalgamation process introduced in the 1570s increased silver output.
- It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1987.

## Context & Connections
Potosi turned the conquered [[Inca Empire]] into a source of silver for the world economy. Its silver crossed the Pacific through the [[Manila Galleon Trade]], and its labor system followed the [[Spanish Conquest of the Inca Empire]].
