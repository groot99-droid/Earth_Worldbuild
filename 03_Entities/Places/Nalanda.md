## Summary
Nalanda is an archaeological site in Bihar, India, of a Buddhist monastic university founded in about the 5th century CE that drew students from across Asia until it was sacked around 1200.

## Facts
- Nalanda was founded in about the 5th century CE, traditionally under the Gupta ruler Kumaragupta I, and grew into a large monastic university.
- The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang studied at Nalanda in the 630s and 640s and reported about 10,000 residents; another Chinese pilgrim, Yijing, described it in 675.
- Students and visitors came from China, Tibet, Korea, Central Asia and Southeast Asia, and in the 9th century a ruler of Srivijaya funded a monastery at Nalanda.
- Nalanda taught Buddhist philosophy, logic, grammar and medicine.
- Forces under Bakhtiyar Khalji sacked Nalanda around 1200; later traditions say its library burned for months, and historians debate how completely it was destroyed and whether its decline began earlier.
- UNESCO inscribed the Archaeological Site of Nalanda Mahavihara in 2016, and a new Nalanda University opened in 2014.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Nalanda concentrated teachers, texts and students from across Asia, an exchange-web of ideas in which monks carried texts and methods to China and Tibet, a case of memory-outsourcing in an institution and not in a single book. Its loss around 1200 shows that a store of knowledge depends on the institution that keeps it, and that the loss of an institution can be slower and less dramatic than later traditions of one great fire suggest. The reading would weaken if new evidence shows that Nalanda's decline was mainly the result of reduced patronage before its sack.

## Open Questions
