## Summary
Cusco is a city in the Andes of southern Peru, at about 3,400 meters, that Inca tradition says was founded around 1200 CE and that served as the capital of the Inca Empire until the Spanish took it in 1533.

## Facts
- Cusco lies at about 3,400 meters in the Peruvian Andes; Inca tradition credits its founding to Manco Cápac around 1200, but archaeology shows that the Killke culture lived in the region from about 900 to 1200 and that the city grew slowly before Pachacuti.
- Under Pachacuti, whose reign is traditionally dated from 1438, and his successors, Cusco was the center of the Inca Empire, with the Coricancha (Temple of the Sun) at its ritual heart and the fortress of Sacsayhuamán above the city.
- Spanish accounts, notably Bernabé Cobo's in the 17th century, describe 41 lines called ceques radiating from the Coricancha and linking about 328 shrines (huacas).
- Some writers say the city was laid out in the shape of a puma, a claim that remains debated.
- Francisco Pizarro's force entered Cusco on 15 November 1533, and Manco Inca besieged the Spanish there for about ten months in 1536 to 1537 without success.
- The Spanish built churches and houses on Inca foundations; an earthquake in 1650 destroyed almost all colonial buildings and one in 1950 damaged more than a third of the city's structures, while many Inca walls remained standing.
- Túpac Amaru II was executed in Cusco's Plaza de Armas in 1781 during his rebellion against Spanish rule.
- UNESCO inscribed Cusco in 1983, and it had 428,450 residents in 2017.

## Context & Connections
Cusco was the capital of the [[Inca Empire]] and the seat of [[Pachacuti]], whose reign is linked to the empire's expansion, and it fell in the [[Spanish Conquest of the Inca Empire]]. It is the usual gateway to [[Machu Picchu]], and [[Túpac Amaru II]] was executed there. Inca administrators kept records on knotted cords, the [[Quipu]]. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Cusco was arranged as a map of belief and rule at once: the ceque lines tied kin groups and shrines to the capital, and the Coricancha placed the ritual center where the emperor's authority sat, a story-glue that could be read by walking the city. Its Inca walls outlasted earthquakes that felled Spanish buildings, and the Spanish built on them, so a later claim-line rests on an earlier one. Much of what is known of the plan comes from Spanish writers with their own aims, since Inca knotted-cord records are only partly understood. The reading would weaken if excavations show the ceque system was less regular than Cobo's account implies.

## Open Questions
- How regular was the ceque system in practice, and how much of Cobo's account reflects Inca practice rather than Spanish understanding?
- How much of Cusco's Inca plan and stonework lies beneath the colonial city?
