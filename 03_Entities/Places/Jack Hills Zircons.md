## Summary
The Jack Hills zircons are tiny crystals from the Jack Hills of Western Australia, some dated to about 4.4 billion years, the oldest known pieces of Earth's crust.

## Facts
- The zircon grains occur in ancient sedimentary rock in the Narryer Terrane of Western Australia, reworked into rocks that are themselves about 3 billion years old.
- The oldest grains are dated by uranium-lead decay to roughly 4.4 billion years, only about 150 million years after Earth formed.
- Oxygen-isotope ratios in some grains have been interpreted as evidence that their parent magma had interacted with liquid water, though this reading is contested.
- The grains survive because zircon is chemically tough and resists weathering and remelting.
- No rock of comparable age is known on Earth; the grains are the only direct samples of Hadean crust.

## Context & Connections
The Jack Hills grains give the [[Hadean Eon]] its only direct mineral witnesses and are used to argue about when oceans and continental crust first formed. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the planet's earliest testimony consists of a few grains smaller than a pinhead, kept only because they were hard enough to outlast everything around them. What survives is not a fair sample of what existed, a lesson that recurs in every later record.
