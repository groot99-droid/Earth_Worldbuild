## Summary
The Ediacaran biota are the first large, complex, multicellular organisms in the fossil record, living in the sea in the last stretch of the Proterozoic, before the Cambrian.

## Facts
- The Ediacaran Period spans about 635 to 538.8 million years ago.
- The biota are named after the Ediacara Hills of South Australia and are known also from Newfoundland, Namibia, and the White Sea coast of Russia.
- Many forms were soft-bodied and frond-, disc-, or quilt-shaped.
- The relationship of many Ediacaran forms to living animal groups is unclear.
- The classic assemblages largely disappear near the start of the Cambrian.
