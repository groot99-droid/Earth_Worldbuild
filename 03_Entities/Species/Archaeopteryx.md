## Summary
Archaeopteryx was a feathered, winged dinosaur about the size of a raven, known from Late Jurassic limestone in Bavaria, Germany, about 150 million years old.

## Facts
- Fossils come from the Solnhofen limestone of Bavaria, Germany.
- It combined bird-like features, such as feathers and wings, with reptile-like ones, including teeth, clawed fingers, and a long bony tail.
- The first skeleton was found in 1861, two years after Darwin published On the Origin of Species, and was quickly cited as a transitional form.
- Feathered non-avian dinosaurs found in China from the 1990s onward strengthened the link between birds and theropod dinosaurs.
- Birds are the living descendants of theropod dinosaurs.

## Context & Connections
Archaeopteryx documents the origin of birds within the [[Dinosaurs]]; birds were the dinosaur lineage that survived the [[Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction]]. It was an early prop for the evolutionary ideas of [[Charles Darwin]].
