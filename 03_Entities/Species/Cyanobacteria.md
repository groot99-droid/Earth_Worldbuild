## Summary
Cyanobacteria are bacteria that perform oxygen-producing photosynthesis. Their activity led to the oxygenation of Earth's atmosphere.

## Facts
- The earliest unambiguous cyanobacterial fossils are about 2.15 billion years old, and direct fossil evidence of photosynthetic membranes is about 1.75 billion years old. Biomarker evidence once cited for 2.7 billion years was later shown to be younger contamination, so the 2.7-billion-year start date used for this note reflects an older, disputed estimate and is approximate.
- Chloroplasts in plants and algae descend from cyanobacteria through endosymbiosis.
- They still live in oceans, lakes, and soils around the world.
- The [[Great Oxidation Event]] followed their spread.

## Context & Connections
Cyanobacteria are the biological agents of the [[Great Oxidation Event]], which reshaped the planet's chemistry in the [[Proterozoic Eon]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer regards them as the earlier example of a species that changed the atmosphere. Their oxygen was a byproduct, not a goal.
