## Summary
Stromatolites are layered rock structures built by communities of microbes trapping sediment and precipitating minerals; the oldest widely cited examples are about 3.48 billion years old.

## Facts
- Stromatolites form when microbial mats, often including cyanobacteria, bind sediment into thin repeating layers.
- The oldest widely cited examples are in the Dresser Formation of the Pilbara region of Western Australia, dated to about 3.48 billion years; some researchers dispute that they are biological.
- Living stromatolites grow today in a few places, including Shark Bay in Western Australia.
- Stromatolites were abundant in shallow seas for much of the Precambrian.
- They declined later, a decline often attributed to grazing animals, though other causes have been proposed.

## Context & Connections
Stromatolites are the main fossil record of early microbial life and connect the [[Origin of Life]] to the oxygen-producing [[Cyanobacteria]] that drove the [[Great Oxidation Event]].
