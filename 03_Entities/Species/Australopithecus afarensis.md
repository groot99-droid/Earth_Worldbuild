## Summary
Australopithecus afarensis was an early hominin that lived in East Africa about 3.9 to 2.9 million years ago and walked upright, and is best known from the fossil 'Lucy'.

## Facts
- 'Lucy' was found in Hadar, Ethiopia, in 1974 and is about 3.2 million years old.
- Footprints at Laetoli, Tanzania (about 3.66 million years old), record upright walking.
- It had a small brain (estimates range from about 380 to 550 cubic centimeters) and long arms adapted to climbing.
- It is often considered close to the ancestry of the genus Homo.

## Context & Connections
The species follows the [[Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]] and precedes [[Homo habilis]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that upright walking came long before big brains. The species' most distinctive trait, a large brain, was a late addition to a lineage that had been walking for millions of years.
