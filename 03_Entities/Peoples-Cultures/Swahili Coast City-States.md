## Summary
The Swahili city-states were trading towns on the East African coast, including Kilwa, Mombasa, and Lamu, that flourished from about the 9th to the 15th century CE.

## Facts
- They spoke Swahili, a Bantu language with many Arabic loanwords.
- Coral-stone buildings and mosques characterized the towns.
- Kilwa minted its own coins and exported gold from the interior; see [[Great Zimbabwe]].
- The traveler Ibn Battuta visited Kilwa in 1331; Portuguese attacks began in 1505.

## Context & Connections
These towns were nodes in the [[Indian Ocean Trade Network]] and grew from the [[Bantu Expansion]]. See [[Ibn Battuta]].
