## Summary
The Franks were a Germanic people who moved into Roman Gaul and built one of early medieval Europe's most powerful kingdoms, first under the Merovingian dynasty of Clovis I and later under the Carolingians, whose most famous ruler was Charlemagne.

## Facts
- The Franks appear in Roman records in the third century CE on the lower Rhine and expanded into Gaul as Roman power weakened.
- Clovis I of the Merovingian dynasty defeated the last major Roman official in Gaul in 486 and had united the Frankish tribes by about 501.
- Clovis converted to Catholic Christianity, and his people's adoption of it helped unite the Franks and set them apart from neighboring Germanic kingdoms that followed other Christian traditions.
- The Merovingians ruled until the eighth century, when Pepin the Short, with papal support, founded the Carolingian dynasty.
- The Carolingian kingdom peaked under Charlemagne and later divided among his descendants. Its territories became the basis of medieval France, Germany and the Holy Roman Empire (not yet a vault note).

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a ruler's personal conversion to his Roman subjects' majority faith became story-glue that welded conquerors and conquered into one people, setting the Franks apart from rival kingdoms that kept a minority creed. Two centuries later a change of dynasty was legitimized by the same meaning-engine, papal blessing replacing a discredited bloodline. A specialist could argue that Clovis's conversion served political advantage with his Gallo-Roman subjects as much as belief, which would weaken a reading of it as faith alone.
