## Summary
The Kushan Empire, from about 30 to 375 CE, was a Central Asian empire spanning Bactria and northern India that controlled key Silk Road routes and patronized Buddhism.

## Facts
- It grew from the Yuezhi confederation and was consolidated by Kujula Kadphises around 30 CE.
- Kanishka, whose reign dates are uncertain, is remembered as a patron of Buddhism.
- Gandhara art, which blended Greek and Indian styles, produced some of the earliest images of the Buddha in human form.
- Kushan coins bore deities from Greek, Iranian, and Indian traditions.
- The empire connected Rome, Parthia, India, and Han China commercially.

## Context & Connections
The Kushans occupied the crossroads of the [[Opening of the Silk Roads]] and carried the traditions of [[Siddhartha Gautama]] eastward. They ruled where the [[Han Dynasty]] and Indian states met.
