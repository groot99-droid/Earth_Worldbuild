## Summary
Kanem-Bornu was a long-lived Muslim state around Lake Chad, governed by the Sayfawa dynasty, that survived by shifting its centre from Kanem to Bornu and lasted until 1893.

## Facts
- Around 1396 the Bulala forced Mai Umar Idrismi to abandon Njimi and move the Kanembu people to Bornu, on the western side of Lake Chad. Over time intermarriage between the Kanembu and the peoples of Bornu produced a new people and language, the Kanuri.
- With control of both capitals the Sayfawa dynasty became more powerful than before, and political authority came to rest in Bornu.
- The empire peaked under Mai Idris Alooma (about 1571 to 1603), remembered for military skill, administrative reforms, and Islamic piety. He introduced legal and administrative reforms based on Islamic law, sponsored mosques, and made a pilgrimage to Mecca, where he set up a hostel for pilgrims from his empire.
- His reforms and campaigns sustained the empire until the mid-17th century, when its power began to fade. In 1808 Fulani warriors conquered the capital, Ngazargamu. The Sayfawa dynasty ended in 1846 and the empire itself fell in 1893.

## Context & Connections
Kanem-Bornu was a neighbour of the [[Songhai Empire]] to the west and was overtaken in part by the Fulani movement associated with [[Usman dan Fodio]] and the [[Sokoto Caliphate]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a state that survived an exile by moving its centre of gravity, and whose people fused with those who had displaced them into a new people. Where the political story-glue failed at one capital, the kin-lattice held it together long enough to build another.
