## Summary
The Gupta Empire ruled much of northern India from about 320 to 550 CE and is often associated with a flowering of mathematics, astronomy, and the arts.

## Facts
- The astronomer and mathematician Aryabhata (born 476) worked in this period and gave an accurate value of pi.
- The decimal place-value system with zero as a digit was developed in India; see [[Zero and Place-Value Notation]].
- Nalanda, a major Buddhist center of learning, flourished.
- 'Golden Age' is a later label; historians debate how far it applies.

## Context & Connections
The Gupta period belongs to [[South Asia]] in [[Classical Antiquity]]. Its mathematics passed westward through scholars such as [[Al-Khwarizmi]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that mathematical ideas developed in one place travelled far. A symbol for 'nothing' changed how numbers work across Eurasia.
