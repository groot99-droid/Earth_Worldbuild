## Summary
The Nabataeans were an Arab people who controlled caravan routes between Arabia and the Mediterranean from the 4th century BCE, building Petra and Hegra, until Rome annexed their kingdom in 106 CE.

## Facts
- They are first mentioned in Greek sources in 312 BCE.
- Petra, in present-day Jordan, has tombs and temples cut into sandstone cliffs, and an elaborate system of water channels and cisterns.
- Hegra (Mada'in Salih) in northwestern Saudi Arabia has similar rock-cut tombs and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2008.
- The Nabataean script is widely considered an ancestor of the Arabic script.
- The Roman emperor Trajan annexed the kingdom in 106 CE.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the Nabataeans built monuments in rock, where they could not be moved, on a trade based on goods that moved constantly. Their rock-cut tombs preserved their name while the caravans left no trace.
