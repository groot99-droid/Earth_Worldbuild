## Summary
The Sokoto Caliphate was an Islamic state in what is now northern Nigeria, founded through the jihad of Usman dan Fodio from 1804, and the largest state in West Africa in the 19th century.

## Facts
- The jihad began in February 1804 and lasted until about 1808; by 1815 the caliphate included most of northern Nigeria and parts of Cameroon and Niger.
- Some estimates give the caliphate 10 to 20 million people by the 1830s, making it very populous.
- It was a center of scholarship and of trade across the Sahel.
- British forces conquered it in 1903.

## Context & Connections
The state inherited the region of the [[Songhai Empire]] and fell during the [[Scramble for Africa]]. See [[Usman dan Fodio]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a state founded by a scholar and a reformer, who wrote extensively as well as fought. The caliphate produced a large body of writing in Arabic, Hausa, and Fulfulde.
