## Summary
The Chinchorro were fisher-hunter-gatherers of the Atacama Desert coast in what is now northern Chile and southern Peru, known for the earliest deliberate artificial mummification, which began by about 5050 BCE.

## Facts
- The Chinchorro lived on the coast from about 5450 to 890 BCE.
- They preserved men, women, and children of the community, not only an elite, by reconstructing bodies with clay, wigs, and mineral pigments.
- The tradition lasted about 3,000 years, ending about 1800 BCE.
- UNESCO inscribed the settlement and mummification sites as a World Heritage Site in 2021.

## Context & Connections
The Chinchorro's practice is the earliest known artificial mummification, thousands of years before the [[Ancient Egyptians]]. See [[Why the Species Buries Its Dead]] and [[Andes]].
