## Summary
Al-Andalus was the Muslim-ruled part of the Iberian Peninsula from 711 to 1492, a region of Muslim, Christian, and Jewish communities that contributed to science, architecture, and literature.

## Facts
- Muslim forces crossed from North Africa into Iberia in 711.
- An Umayyad emirate, later a caliphate, ruled from Cordoba from 756.
- Cordoba became a large center of learning, and its caliphal library is reputed to have held hundreds of thousands of volumes.
- The Alhambra in Granada was built mainly in the 13th and 14th centuries.
- Granada, the last Muslim kingdom, fell to Castile and Aragon in January 1492.

## Context & Connections
Al-Andalus grew from the [[Umayyad Caliphate]] and was a channel for the knowledge of the [[Translation Movement and the House of Wisdom]] into Latin Europe. Its end in 1492 coincided with the start of the [[Columbian Exchange]].
