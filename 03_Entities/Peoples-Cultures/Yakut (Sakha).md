## Summary
The Sakha (Yakut) are a Turkic-speaking people of northeastern Siberia who developed horse and cattle herding in one of the coldest inhabited regions on Earth; the timing of their northward migration is debated, but is often placed around the 13th to 15th centuries.

## Facts
- Sakha is their own preferred name; the 2010 Russian census counted about 478,000 Sakha.
- Russian penetration of the region began in the 17th century, and a major revolt against Russian rule occurred in 1642.
- Yasak, a fur tribute, was levied on the Yakuts and neighboring peoples until 1917.
- The Sakha supplied Russian expeditions with food, horses, and guides.
