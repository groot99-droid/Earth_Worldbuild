## Summary
The Jomon were hunter-gatherer-fishers of the Japanese archipelago who made some of the world's earliest pottery and lived in settled villages for about 14,000 years before rice farming arrived.

## Facts
- The name Jomon means cord-marked, after the patterns pressed into their pottery.
- Some of the earliest Jomon pottery is dated to around 16,000 years ago.
- They made clay figurines called dogu.
- The site of Sannai-Maruyama in Aomori was occupied for about 1,700 years, from around 3900 BCE.
- Rice-farming migrants, the Yayoi, arrived from the continent beginning around the first millennium BCE, and the dates are debated.

## Context & Connections
The Jomon show that [[Pottery]] and sedentary life preceded [[The Agricultural Revolution]] in this region. Rice farming, as described in [[Domestication of Rice]], entered later. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the Jomon lived in permanent villages and made elaborate pots without farming, which shows that the sequence of fire, farming, and settlement is not universal. Settling does not require agriculture.
