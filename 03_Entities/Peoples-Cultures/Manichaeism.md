## Summary
Manichaeism was a dualistic religion founded in third-century Persia by Mani, teaching a cosmic struggle between light and darkness. It spread from North Africa to China within four centuries and died out under persecution by the 14th century.

## Facts
- Mani, born about 216 CE in the Persian Empire, called himself the Apostle of Light and taught that the material world imprisons divine light in darkness, with salvation lying in freeing that light.
- He drew on Zoroastrian, Christian, Buddhist and Gnostic ideas and presented himself as the final prophet after Jesus and the Buddha.
- The religion spread from Roman North Africa to Central Asia and China, helped by trade routes. A missionary reached the Tang court in 694, and freedom of worship followed in 732.
- In 296 the emperor Diocletian ordered Manichaean leaders burned with their scriptures, and Roman and Persian persecution largely ended the faith in the west by the sixth century.
- In China the anti-Buddhist persecution of 843 also seized Manichaean property and executed clergy, and the religion faded into Buddhism and Daoism by the 14th century.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a meaning-engine assembled from older traditions crossed Eurasia in four centuries along trade routes, then was suppressed by Roman, Persian and Chinese states, each for its own stated reasons. It survived roughly a thousand years under official persecution nearly everywhere it took root. A specialist could argue that its decline in China reflects absorption into Buddhism and Daoism as much as suppression, which would weaken a reading of persecution as the sole cause of its extinction.
