## Summary
The Tokugawa shogunate was the military government that ruled Japan from 1603 to 1867, a long period of peace and restricted foreign contact.

## Facts
- Tokugawa Ieyasu became shogun in 1603 and ruled from Edo (now Tokyo).
- The policy known as 'sakoku' limited foreign trade, mainly to Dutch and Chinese merchants at Nagasaki.
- Edo grew to about a million people, one of the world's largest cities.
- The shogunate collapsed in 1867, and imperial rule was restored the following year in the [[Meiji Restoration]].

## Context & Connections
The shogunate ruled through the [[Early Modern Period]]. See [[Meiji Restoration]] and [[Ming Dynasty]] for the neighboring Chinese state.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a society that had a long internal peace and a developed urban culture with limited foreign contact. Peace let art and commerce flourish, and left the state unprepared for outside pressure.
