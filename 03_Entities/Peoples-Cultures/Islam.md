## Summary
Islam is the religion founded by Muhammad in seventh-century Arabia, centered on the Quran as the word of God and on the practices set out in its five pillars, and today the second-largest religion in the world.

## Facts
- Muslims hold that Muhammad, an Arabian merchant, began receiving revelations from God around 610 CE, later compiled as the Quran, and that he united the tribes of Arabia under the new faith before his death in 632.
- A dispute over his successor split his followers soon after his death: most accepted the elected caliphs (becoming Sunni), while a smaller group held that leadership belonged to his family through his son-in-law Ali (becoming Shia).
- The faith spread rapidly under the early caliphates, then under empires such as the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates, from Spain to Central and South Asia within a few centuries.
- Sunni Muslims make up roughly 85 percent of the world's estimated 1.9 billion Muslims, and Shia Muslims about 15 percent, with Shia forming the majority in Iran, Iraq, Bahrain and Azerbaijan.

## Context & Connections
Islam began in the [[Arabian Peninsula]] with [[Muhammad]] at the start of the [[Medieval Period]], part of the [[Rise of Islam]], and spread through empires including the [[Umayyad Caliphate]] and [[Abbasid Caliphate]]. See [[Religion and Belief]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a succession dispute settled within a generation of the founder's death created a claim-line, Sunni against Shia, that fourteen centuries of later history have not erased, showing how a single kin-lattice question about legitimate leadership can outlast the empires built around it. A specialist could argue that many local and political factors, not the original dispute alone, kept the split alive in specific regions, which would weaken a reading of the divide as a single continuous grievance.

## Open Questions

- What let the faith spread so quickly across such different regions within its first century?
