## Summary
The Marajoara culture flourished on Marajo Island at the mouth of the Amazon, known for large earthen mounds and richly decorated ceramics, with its ceramic tradition dated about 400 to 1300 CE.

## Facts
- Marajo Island is roughly the size of Switzerland, and mound building there began around 300 CE.
- Mounds reach up to about 20 meters in height, and the largest multi-mound sites may have housed several thousand people.
- Ceramics include burial urns, figurines, and elaborately painted and incised vessels, suggesting specialized craft production.
- The mounds were largely abandoned by about 1350 CE.

## Context & Connections
See [[Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]]. Scholars have debated whether the mound-building Marajoara were a complex chiefdom sustained without intensive farming.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the Marajoara raised their own hills on a seasonally flooded plain. Building earth up was a way of making living space where there was none.
