## Summary
The Mycenaeans were the Bronze Age Greek-speaking civilization of mainland Greece, centered on palace-fortresses such as Mycenae, Tiryns, and Pylos, from about 1600 to 1100 BCE.

## Facts
- Their administrative records were written in Linear B, an early form of Greek, on clay tablets.
- Michael Ventris deciphered Linear B in 1952, with help from John Chadwick.
- Shaft graves at Mycenae, dated to about 1600 BCE, held gold masks and weapons; Heinrich Schliemann excavated them in 1876.
- The Mycenaeans took over or influenced the Minoan palace at Knossos on Crete by about 1450 BCE.
- Their palace system collapsed around 1200 to 1100 BCE.

## Context & Connections
The Mycenaeans inherited much of the [[Minoans]]' maritime trade and artistic style, and their palace economies fell in the [[Bronze Age Collapse]]. Later [[Ancient Greeks]] preserved memory of them in epic poetry. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the earliest Greek writing is an inventory of goods, not poetry. Writing began as bookkeeping for palaces, then was lost with the palaces, and was reinvented centuries later as an alphabet.
