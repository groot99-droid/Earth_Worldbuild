## Summary
Saba (Sheba) was an ancient South Arabian kingdom in what is now Yemen, centered on the city of Marib, and prosperous from the incense trade.

## Facts
- Its earliest inscriptions date to about the 8th century BCE; some scholars propose an earlier origin, around 1000 BCE.
- Marib was known for a great dam that irrigated the surrounding land, repaired several times before its final failure around the 6th century CE.
- Sabaeans wrote in the South Arabian script.
- The Queen of Sheba appears in the Hebrew Bible and the Quran; her historicity is not established.
- The neighboring kingdom of Himyar absorbed Saba around 275 CE.

## Context & Connections
Saba's wealth came from the [[Incense Trade]], which connected southern Arabia with the Mediterranean. It sits in the [[Arabian Peninsula]] alongside the [[Nabataeans]] of the north.
