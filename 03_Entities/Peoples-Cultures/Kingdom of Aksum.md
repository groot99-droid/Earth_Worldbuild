## Summary
The Kingdom of Aksum was a trading empire in the highlands of present-day Ethiopia and Eritrea, from about the 1st to the 10th century CE.

## Facts
- It controlled Red Sea trade between Rome, Arabia, and India.
- It minted its own coins and built tall carved stone obelisks (stelae).
- King Ezana adopted Christianity in the 4th century.
- The Persian prophet Mani listed Aksum as one of the four great powers of his time, with Rome, Persia, and China.

## Context & Connections
Aksum linked [[Sub-Saharan Africa]] to the Mediterranean and Indian Ocean worlds, and is a counterexample to narratives that leave Africa out of the ancient record.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that its own contemporaries counted it among the four great powers, and that later retellings often forgot that.
