## Summary
The Taino were an Arawakan-speaking people of the Greater Antilles and the Bahamas, who were the first Indigenous Americans encountered by Columbus in 1492.

## Facts
- They lived in villages ruled by chiefs called caciques, and grew cassava, maize, and sweet potato.
- They carved wooden and stone figures called zemis, associated with ancestors and spirits.
- English words including canoe, hammock, hurricane, and barbecue come from Taino.
- After 1492 their populations fell sharply because of disease, forced labor, and violence.
- Genetic studies show Indigenous Caribbean ancestry survives in present-day Caribbean populations, contrary to the older idea that the Taino vanished.
