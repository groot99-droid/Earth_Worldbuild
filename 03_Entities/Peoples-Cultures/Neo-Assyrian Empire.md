## Summary
The Neo-Assyrian Empire, from about 911 to 609 BCE, was a large empire based in northern Mesopotamia, known for its army, its palace reliefs, and the library of Ashurbanipal at Nineveh.

## Facts
- Its main capitals were Ashur, Kalhu (Nimrud), Dur-Sharrukin, and Nineveh.
- At its height in the 7th century BCE it controlled Mesopotamia, the Levant, and, briefly, Egypt.
- It used mass deportation to move conquered populations across the empire.
- The library of Ashurbanipal at Nineveh held tens of thousands of clay tablets and fragments, including a standard version of the Epic of Gilgamesh.
- A coalition of Babylonians and Medes captured Nineveh in 612 BCE, and Assyrian resistance ended by about 609 BCE.

## Context & Connections
The Neo-Assyrian Empire continued the cuneiform tradition begun by the [[Sumerians]] and preserved much of it in royal libraries. It is an early example of a state organized around [[Sanctioned Harm]], as described in its own propaganda. Neo-Assyrian Empire sits in the [[Iron Age]] and the region [[Mesopotamia]], and belongs to the theme thread of [[War and Conflict]] and [[Language and Writing]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the empire boasted of its violence in stone reliefs and also collected and copied the literature of its predecessors. A state can be both a destroyer and an archivist, and the archive is why we know about the destruction.
