## Summary
The Casarabe culture was a society of the Llanos de Mojos savanna in the Bolivian Amazon, active from about 500 to 1400 CE, whose monumental settlements were revealed by lidar in 2022.

## Facts
- Two large sites, about 147 and 315 hectares, sit at the top of a four-tiered settlement system.
- Monumental architecture includes stepped platforms, U-shaped structures, and conical pyramids up to 22 meters tall.
- Straight raised causeways, stretching several kilometers, connect the large sites to smaller ones.
- The culture area spans roughly 4,500 square kilometers; the settlements show low population density.

## Context & Connections
See [[Amazonian Urbanism and Dark Earths]]. The Casarabe sites lay east of the [[Andes]] and are among the few known examples of Amazonian low-density urbanism.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a society that moved earth on a great scale, building ramps, mounds, and roads, without metal tools or writing that survive. Its planning was legible from the air a thousand years later.
