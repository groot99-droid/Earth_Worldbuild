## Summary
The Mapuche are the Indigenous people of south-central Chile and Argentina, who resisted both the Inca and the Spanish and kept their independence for centuries.

## Facts
- According to Spanish chroniclers, the Mapuche halted the Inca advance at the Maule River in the late 15th century.
- The Arauco War with Spain ran from about 1546 to 1662 and was nicknamed the 'Indian Flanders'.
- By the Parliament of Quillin in 1641, Spain recognized the Bio-Bio river as a border and agreed to withdraw to its north.
- Chile's Occupation of the Araucania (1861 to 1883) incorporated Mapuche territory; sources cite that the Mapuche lost most of their land and were confined to reservations.

## Context & Connections
The Mapuche lived south of the reach of the [[Inca Empire]] and the Spanish empire after the [[Columbian Exchange]], keeping political autonomy longer than most.

## Observer's Reading
