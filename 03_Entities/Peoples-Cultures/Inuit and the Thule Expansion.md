## Summary
The Thule people, ancestors of the Inuit, spread from Alaska across the Canadian Arctic to Greenland beginning around 1000 CE, replacing the earlier Dorset culture.

## Facts
- The Thule culture emerged in the Bering Strait region around 900 to 1000 CE.
- Its people used dog sleds, skin boats (umiaks and kayaks), and toggling harpoons for hunting whales and seals.
- They expanded east across Arctic Canada and reached Greenland by roughly the 12th to 13th century.
- Genetic studies show the earlier Dorset population was largely replaced rather than assimilated.
- Norse colonists lived in southern Greenland from about 985 CE until the 15th century.

## Context & Connections
The Thule expansion carried people originating from [[Beringia]]'s region across the Arctic and brought them into contact with the [[Norse]].
