## Summary
The Muisca were the peoples of the Colombian highlands around present-day Bogota, organized in a loose confederation of rulers, whose gold ritual inspired the legend of El Dorado.

## Facts
- The main rulers were the zipa of Bacata, the zaque of Hunza, the iraca of Sugamuxi, and the ruler of Tundama.
- Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada led a Spanish expedition from Santa Marta in April 1536 with roughly 800 to 900 Spaniards, together with native carriers and enslaved Africans. Nearly three-quarters of the Spaniards died, mostly from illness and hunger; one scholarly account counts 179 survivors.
- The Spanish conquest of the Muisca took place from 1537 to 1540.
- According to legend, a Muisca ruler covered himself in gold dust and dived into Lake Guatavita; the Spanish found emeralds rather than golden cities.

## Context & Connections
The Muisca lived in the northern [[Andes]], outside the [[Inca Empire]]. Their fall is part of the [[Columbian Exchange]] era of conquest.
