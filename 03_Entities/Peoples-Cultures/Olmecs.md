## Summary
The Olmecs were an early Mesoamerican culture on the Gulf coast of Mexico, best known for colossal carved stone heads, from about 1200 to 400 BCE.

## Facts
- Major centers included San Lorenzo and La Venta.
- Colossal heads, some over 3 meters tall, were carved from basalt hauled long distances.
- They worked rubber. The name 'Olmec' is the Nahuatl word for 'rubber people', which the Aztecs applied to later Gulf-lowland neighbors and which archaeologists later applied to this ancient culture; its own name is unknown.
- Whether they were a 'mother culture' or one of several interacting societies is debated.

## Context & Connections
The Olmecs are among the earliest societies in [[Mesoamerica]] and influenced later cultures such as the [[Maya]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer sees a case of monumental art as a status-signal for rulers, in a region with no metal tools and no wheeled transport.
