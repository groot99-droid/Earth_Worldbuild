## Summary
The Mal'ta boy, a child who lived near Lake Baikal about 24,000 years ago, has a genome that revealed a previously unknown population of 'Ancient North Eurasians' who contributed to the ancestry of both Europeans and Native Americans.

## Facts
- The remains were excavated at Mal'ta in Siberia in the 1920s and their genome was sequenced in 2013.
- It was then the oldest modern human genome published.
- Between about 14 and 38 percent of the boy's genome is shared with present-day Native Americans, in different estimates.
- The result showed that Native American ancestry has two roots: East Asian and Ancient North Eurasian.

## Context & Connections
The finding reshaped the [[Peopling of the Americas]], which occurred around the [[Last Glacial Maximum]]. See [[Siberia and the Arctic]].
