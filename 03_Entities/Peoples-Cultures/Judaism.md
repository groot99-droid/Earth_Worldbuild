## Summary
Judaism is the religion and communal tradition of the Jewish people, tracing its origins to the patriarch Abraham and the covenant given to Moses at Sinai, and organized for most of its history around a body of law and commentary rather than a central authority.

## Facts
- Tradition dates Abraham, regarded as the first Hebrew and forefather of the Jewish people, to about 2000 to 1800 BCE, though this is traditional rather than documented history.
- Its founding text is the Torah, the five books attributed to Moses, later joined by other scriptures collected as the Hebrew Bible.
- The Second Temple in Jerusalem was destroyed by Rome in 70 CE. Without a central temple, rabbis took over interpreting the Torah, producing extensive commentary later compiled as the Talmud.
- Today's main movements, Orthodox, Conservative, Reform and Reconstructionist, range from traditional to progressive in how they apply Jewish law.
- Worldwide adherents numbered about 15 million as of the early 2020s, most living in Israel and the United States.

## Context & Connections
Judaism's traditional origins lie in the [[Levant and Anatolia]] in the [[Bronze Age]], among the empires of ancient Mesopotamia such as the [[Sumerians]], and its history includes conquest by the [[Neo-Assyrian Empire]] and later the [[Rise of Islam]] in the same region. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the destruction of a single building in 70 CE redirected an entire tradition's meaning-engine from temple sacrifice toward text and interpretation, a shift in memory-outsourcing that let the community persist without a capital. Adherents hold the tradition's core claims as historical and covenantal; a specialist could argue that its earliest patriarchal accounts describe an ancestor-weight built up over centuries of retelling rather than a documented individual, which would weaken any confident dating of its earliest layer.
