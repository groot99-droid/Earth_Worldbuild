## Summary
The Qing dynasty (1644 to 1912) was the last imperial dynasty of China, founded by the Manchus after they overthrew the Ming, and it expanded China's borders to their largest extent.

## Facts
- The Qing extended control over Mongolia, Tibet, and Xinjiang.
- Population and the economy grew strongly in the 18th century.
- The dynasty faced the [[Opium Wars]], the [[Taiping Rebellion]], and the [[Boxer Rebellion]] in the 19th century.
- The last emperor, Puyi, abdicated in February 1912.

## Context & Connections
The Qing followed the [[Ming Dynasty]] and ended with the 1911 revolution. See [[East Asia]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a dynasty of outsiders who ruled by adopting the forms of the ruled: Manchu rulers governed through Confucian institutions. Ruling a large population often means adopting its ways.
