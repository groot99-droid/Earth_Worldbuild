## Summary
Zoroastrianism is an Iranian religion based on the teachings of Zarathustra, centered on the god Ahura Mazda and a moral struggle between truth and falsehood.

## Facts
- Its oldest texts, the Gathas, are hymns attributed to Zarathustra in an archaic form of Iranian.
- The date of Zarathustra is uncertain; scholars have proposed dates from about 1500 to about 600 BCE.
- Its sacred collection is the Avesta.
- The religion became the state religion of the Sasanian Empire (224 to 651 CE).
- Zoroastrian communities persist in Iran and India, where they are called Parsis.

## Context & Connections
Zoroastrianism shaped the religious world of the [[Achaemenid Persians]], and is often discussed in connection with the [[Axial Age]]. After the [[Rise of Islam]] it became a minority faith. Zoroastrianism sits in the [[Iron Age]] and the region [[Iranian Plateau]], and belongs to the theme thread of [[Religion and Belief]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that its emphasis on a cosmic struggle between order and disorder, and on a final judgment, resembles ideas found in later traditions, though the direction of influence is debated. The claim of borrowing would be weakened if independent origins were demonstrated.
