## Summary
Insulin is a pancreatic hormone that regulates blood sugar. Before a Toronto team purified it in 1921 and 1922, type 1 diabetes was fatal, often within a year or two of diagnosis.

## Facts
- In summer 1921, Frederick Banting and Charles Best, working in J. J. R. Macleod's Toronto laboratory, obtained a glucose-lowering pancreatic extract from dogs.
- Leonard Thompson, aged 14, received the first human injection at Toronto General Hospital on 11 January 1922; James Collip's purer extract succeeded on 23 January.
- Banting and Macleod won the 1923 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine; Banting shared his prize money with Best, and Macleod with Collip.
- Banting, Best, and Collip sold their patents to the University of Toronto for one dollar each in 1923; Eli Lilly began shipping commercial supply that October.
- Nicolae Paulescu in Romania published pancreatic extract work in 1921 and patented it in 1922; priority is contested, and the Nobel committee did not recognize his claim.

## Context & Connections
Insulin belongs with [[Antibiotics]], [[Vaccination]], and [[Anesthesia]] among medical technologies that changed survival or care. It grew from laboratory [[Science]] in [[North America]] during the [[Industrial Age]]. It manages type 1 diabetes but does not cure it.
