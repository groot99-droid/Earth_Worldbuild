## Summary
The liquid-fueled rocket, burning a liquid propellant instead of solid powder, was first flown by Robert Goddard on 16 March 1926 in Auburn, Massachusetts, and matured within two decades into Germany's long-range V-2 missile.

## Facts
- Goddard's rocket, fueled by liquid oxygen and gasoline, flew for about two and a half seconds on 16 March 1926, reaching roughly 12 meters in altitude and landing about 56 meters away.
- Goddard continued research largely alone and with limited public recognition, later testing larger rockets at a private range near Roswell, New Mexico, from 1930 to 1942.
- German engineers, notably Wernher von Braun's team, developed liquid-fueled rocketry independently and far more heavily funded during the 1930s and early 1940s, culminating in the V-2 ballistic missile.
- Germany launched the V-2 operationally against Britain and other targets beginning in September 1944; it was the first rocket to reach the edge of space and could not be intercepted by contemporary defenses.
- After the war, the United States and the Soviet Union recruited German rocket engineers and used V-2 technology as a direct foundation for both ballistic missile and early space programs.
