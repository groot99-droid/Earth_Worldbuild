## Summary
Bronze is an alloy of copper and tin (or another metal) that was first widely produced in the Near East around 3300 BCE, giving its name to the Bronze Age.

## Facts
- Tin was scarce and had to be traded over long distances, from places such as Afghanistan and later Cornwall.
- Bronze was used for tools, weapons, and ritual objects.
- Earlier arsenical-copper alloys came first in some regions.
- The need for tin drove long-distance exchange-webs.

## Context & Connections
Bronze metallurgy defines the [[Bronze Age]] and led to [[Iron Smelting]]. Its trade networks were disrupted in the [[Bronze Age Collapse]].
