## Summary
The electric telegraph, first demonstrated commercially in the 1840s, and the submarine cables that followed, allowed messages to cross continents and oceans in minutes instead of weeks.

## Facts
- Samuel Morse sent the first public long-distance message, 'What hath God wrought', in 1844.
- A transatlantic cable was laid in 1858 but failed within weeks; a durable cable was completed in 1866.
- Cables linked Britain to India in the 1870s, and later most of the world.
- The telegraph changed news, markets, diplomacy, and warfare.

## Context & Connections
The telegraph followed the [[Steam Engine]] and railways, prepared the ground for the [[Electrical Power Grid]], and is a distant ancestor of the [[Digital Revolution]].

## Observer's Reading
