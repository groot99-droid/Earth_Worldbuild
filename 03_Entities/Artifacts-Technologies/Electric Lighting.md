## Summary
Practical incandescent lighting emerged from parallel work in Britain and the United States between 1878 and 1880, and spread from there to homes and workplaces.

## Facts
- Joseph Swan showed a carbon-filament lamp at Newcastle on 18 December 1878 and again on 3 February 1879.
- Thomas Edison's team made a lamp with a carbonized thread filament in October 1879; US patent 223,898 was issued on 27 January 1880.
- Humphry Davy demonstrated a carbon arc in 1809, and Yablochkov candles lit Paris streets from 1878, but arcs were too intense for homes.
- Edison's Pearl Street Station in New York began supplying current on 4 September 1882.
- Priority is debated: Swan's public demonstrations came earlier, and the two inventors merged their British interests as Ediswan in 1883.

## Observer's Reading
In the Observer's terms, the lamp is a tool-lineage product: glassworking, vacuum pumps, and dynamos matured together, so near-simultaneous lamps by Swan and Edison fit inheritance more than a lone inventor. Light after dark extended the species' working and social hours, though gas lighting had begun that shift. This reading would weaken if the two lamps were shown to rest on unrelated techniques; a rival reading stresses capital and patent strategy over technique.
