## Summary
Iron smelting, extracting iron from ore, began on a limited scale in Anatolia in the early 2nd millennium BCE, possibly earlier, and became widespread across the Near East from the late 2nd millennium BCE, about 1200 BCE onward. Ironworking also developed in sub-Saharan Africa, and whether it did so independently is debated.

## Facts
- Extractive iron metallurgy is attributed to Anatolia on a limited scale in the early 2nd millennium BCE, with the first major expansion in the late 2nd and early 1st millennium BCE. A small iron lump from Kaman-Kalehoyuk in Turkey, from a layer dated to about 2500 to 2250 BCE, has been argued to be earlier smelted iron, possibly made from ore from elsewhere; this is a single find and remains under study.
- Iron ore is much more common than copper and tin.
- Smelting requires higher temperatures and produced a tougher metal once carbon-steel techniques improved.
- The earliest reported dates for iron smelting in sub-Saharan Africa (about 2000 BCE at Lejja in Nigeria, Oboui in the Central African Republic, and Gbatoro in Cameroon) are contested, because it is unclear that the dated charcoal is securely tied to iron-working remains and because of old-carbon contamination. Iron working in the Nok culture of Nigeria dates between about the 9th century BCE and 550 BCE.
- Scholars are divided on whether African ironworking was independent of the Near East. Older theories of diffusion from Carthage or Meroe have been challenged, and current evidence leans toward independent African development.
- Iron tools made it easier to clear forests and to arm large populations.

## Context & Connections
Iron smelting defines the [[Iron Age]] and followed [[Bronze Metallurgy]]. In Africa, the [[Bantu Expansion]] spread ironworking across eastern and southern regions.

## Open Questions
- Did iron working arise independently in sub-Saharan Africa? Evidence leans that way, but the very oldest dates are disputed.
- How early did smelting begin in Anatolia, and did the 2500 to 2250 BCE lump come from a working smelting tradition or an isolated experiment?
