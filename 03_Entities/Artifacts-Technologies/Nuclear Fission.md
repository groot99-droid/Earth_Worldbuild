## Summary
Nuclear fission, the splitting of heavy atomic nuclei, was discovered by Otto Hahn and Fritz Strassmann in December 1938 and explained by Lise Meitner and Otto Frisch.

## Facts
- It releases enormous energy per unit of matter, roughly a million times more than chemical reactions.
- The first controlled chain reaction took place in Chicago in December 1942.
- It powers nuclear reactors and nuclear weapons.
- Meitner did not share in the 1944 Nobel Prize awarded to Hahn, a case often cited as an injustice.

## Context & Connections
Fission builds on the work of [[Marie Curie]] and [[Albert Einstein]] and led to the [[Nuclear Age]].
