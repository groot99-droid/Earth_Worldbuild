## Summary
The steam engine converts heat into mechanical work. Thomas Newcomen built the first commercially successful engine in 1712, and James Watt's improvements from the 1760s made it far more efficient.

## Facts
- Early engines pumped water out of coal mines.
- Watt's separate condenser (patented 1769) greatly improved fuel economy.
- High-pressure engines (Trevithick, 1802) made railways and steamships possible.
- Steam power freed factories from dependence on rivers.

## Context & Connections
The steam engine powered the [[Industrial Revolution]] and prepared the way for the [[Electrical Power Grid]].
