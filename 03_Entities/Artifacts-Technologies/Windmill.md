## Summary
The windmill converts wind into rotational mechanical power, historically used to grind grain and pump water. The earliest well-documented windmills are vertical-axis panemone mills in Sistan, on the Iranian Plateau, described by Islamic geographers in the 9th and 10th centuries CE.

## Facts
- Vertical-axis panemone windmills operated in Sistan, on the Iranian Plateau, by the 9th to 10th century CE.
- The geographers al-Masudi (writing 943 CE) and Ibn Hawqal (traveling 943-969 CE) both recorded Sistan's wind-driven grain mills.
- The mills used vertical wooden-frame sails of cloth or reed matting in tall adobe towers, turned by strong seasonal winds.
- Sistan mills ground grain and, in some structures, pumped water for irrigation, alongside the region's [[Qanat]] system.
- A structurally distinct horizontal-axis windmill, the post mill, appears in Europe from the 12th century CE, about three centuries later.
- Whether the European design diffused from Persian precedent or arose independently is debated, with no settled consensus.

## Context & Connections
The Sistan windmills arose in the same arid environment as [[Qanat]] irrigation on the [[Iranian Plateau]], part of [[Medieval Period]] technology history, and show a mechanical solution recurring, in modified form, far from its first attested site. Thread: [[Technology]].

## Observer's Reading
For the Observer, the windmill is a test case in tool-lineage: whether a device invented once becomes the ancestor of every later version, or the same fix for wind and water is assembled twice. Sistan holds clear textual priority, yet the vertical- and horizontal-axis designs differ enough that a shared exchange-web is not proven. The reading would weaken if a vertical-axis intermediate turned up between Sistan and northern Europe. An alternative reading treats the European post mill as independent engineering rather than inherited tool-lineage.

## Open Questions
- Did the European horizontal-axis windmill descend from Persian designs, or was it invented independently?
