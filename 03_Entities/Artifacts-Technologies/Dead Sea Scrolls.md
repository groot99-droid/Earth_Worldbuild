## Summary
The Dead Sea Scrolls are roughly 900 ancient Jewish manuscripts found in caves near Qumran by the Dead Sea, dating from about the 3rd century BCE to the 1st century CE.

## Facts
- In 1947 a Bedouin shepherd found the first scrolls in a cave near Qumran, later called Cave 1, yielding seven substantially intact scrolls.
- Between 1949 and 1956 ten more caves were found nearby, bringing the total to eleven Qumran caves with manuscripts.
- Scholars have reconstructed roughly 900 distinct manuscripts, most surviving only as fragments.
- Texts are dated paleographically and by radiocarbon analysis to about the 3rd century BCE to the 1st century CE; a 2025 study proposed some manuscripts are older, a claim still under review.
- Fragments survive of nearly every Hebrew Bible book except Esther, the oldest known Hebrew Bible manuscripts by roughly a thousand years.
- Many scholars associate Qumran with the Essenes, a Jewish sect, but this is contested.

## Context & Connections
The scrolls come from [[Levant and Anatolia]], near [[Jerusalem]], in [[Classical Antiquity]] under Hasmonean and then Roman rule. Their concealment is often linked to the First Jewish-Roman War (66-73 CE), after which the caves went unretrieved for nearly two millennia. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer reads the scrolls as a rare sample of memory-outsourcing caught mid-assembly: a community storing its meaning-engine's core texts before their wording had settled. Differing copies of the same biblical books suggest active editing was under way, not just copying. The reading weakens if the manuscripts belong to several unrelated groups passing through rather than one custodial community. An alternative view treats the find as routine disposal of worn-out texts, with no organized intent behind it.

## Open Questions
- Did one organized community, often identified as Essene, curate the collection, or did several groups deposit manuscripts independently?
- Will the 2025 dating study reshape the accepted range once independently replicated?
