## Summary
The Haber-Bosch process combines atmospheric nitrogen and hydrogen under high pressure over a catalyst to make ammonia. Developed in Germany between 1908 and 1913, it underpins synthetic nitrogen fertilizer.

## Facts
- Fritz Haber patented ammonia synthesis in October 1908 and demonstrated a working laboratory apparatus at Karlsruhe in 1909.
- Carl Bosch and BASF engineers scaled it up; the first industrial plant, at Oppau, began operating in 1913.
- Haber received the Chemistry Nobel for 1918 (awarded 1919); Bosch shared the 1931 prize with Friedrich Bergius.
- The Allied blockade cut off Chilean nitrate in World War I; synthetic ammonia supplied nitrogen for German explosives.
- Haber organized the first large-scale German chlorine gas release, at Ypres on 22 April 1915.
- Estimates of humanity fed by synthetic nitrogen range from about one third to one half, depending on assumptions.

## Context & Connections
Synthetic nitrogen supported the fertilizer growth of the [[Green Revolution]] and the [[Great Acceleration]], and altered the nitrogen cycle at planetary scale. Its explosives role ties it to [[World War I]]. It arose in [[Europe]] in the [[Industrial Age]]. Threads: [[Technology]] and [[Earth Systems and Life]].
