## Summary
Li Qingzhao (1084 to about 1155) was a Chinese poet of the Song dynasty, widely regarded as the greatest woman writer in the classical Chinese tradition. Her ci, a lyric verse form, moved from a settled, cultured life to loss and exile after northern China fell to invaders.

## Facts
- She was born in 1084 in Qi Prefecture, today's Zhangqiu in Shandong, to a literary family, and is thought to have died about 1155.
- In 1101 she married Zhao Mingcheng, a scholar who shared her interest in antiquities. The Jinshi lu, a catalog of bronze and stone inscriptions, is described as her husband's work and was published after his death.
- When the Jin dynasty invaded northern China in 1127, the couple fled south with part of their collection, much of which is said to have been lost along the way. Zhao Mingcheng died in 1129.
- After his death she wrote an afterword to the Jinshi lu describing their marriage and their losses. She briefly remarried, with the year traditionally given as about 1132, and divorced amid scandal soon after.
- Her poems, mostly short ci in personal, direct language, describe love, separation, and later grief and displacement. Only around a hundred of her poems are known to survive, out of a larger body assembled and praised by later editors.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that her verse recorded an ordinary domestic partnership, book-collecting and companionship, that a state's collapse turned into flight and loss, and that this turn from ease to grief became the reason later readers kept her work in mark-code. A specialist could argue that only around a hundred surviving poems, out of a much larger and partly lost body, cannot support firm claims about how her style changed over her life, which would weaken any confident before-and-after reading.
