## Summary
Ezana was king of Aksum, in present-day Ethiopia and Eritrea, from about 320 to about 360 CE, though sources differ on the exact dates. He is known for adopting Christianity and for a campaign in the Nile valley, and his inscriptions and coins record both.

## Facts
- Ezana came to the throne as a child and ruled the Kingdom of Aksum from about 320 until about 360. Dates for his life and reign are approximate, and sources differ.
- His early inscriptions and coins show older Aksumite religion, including a crescent and disc on his coins.
- Frumentius, a Christian from Tyre (described as Phoenician, and in some accounts Syrian) who rose in the Aksumite court, is reported to have been consecrated Aksum's first bishop by Athanasius of Alexandria, traditionally in 328, though other accounts say between 340 and 346.
- Ezana converted to Christianity in the 4th century, though the exact date is uncertain. His later inscriptions invoke a "Lord of Heaven," and his coins replace the crescent and disc with the cross, which is often described as among the earliest coins to do so.
- His inscriptions are in Ge'ez, Sabaean and Greek.
- About 350 an Aksumite force campaigned in the Nile valley against Kush and neighboring peoples. The campaign is often linked to the end of Kush's power, though historians debate the causes.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a king's change of religion is visible in stone and coin: the same ruler's inscriptions change gods and his coinage changes symbols. A state's meaning-engine was switched from above and announced as a status-signal to the traders who used the coins across the Red Sea world. The reading would weaken if evidence showed that most of his subjects kept older practices for a long time after his conversion.
