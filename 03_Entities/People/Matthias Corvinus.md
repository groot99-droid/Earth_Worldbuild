## Summary
Matthias Corvinus (1443 to 1490) was King of Hungary from 1458 to 1490, a Renaissance patron-king who built one of Europe's finest libraries and modernized his kingdom's army and administration.

## Facts
- He was elected king at age fourteen after his father, the military commander John Hunyadi, and older brother had died, with his mother's political maneuvering and his uncle Michael Szilágyi's troops intimidating the barons securing the throne; sources differ on whether a bribe to the nobility also played a part.
- He built the Black Army, a mostly mercenary standing force notable for the period, which he used to campaign against the Ottoman Empire, Bohemia, and the Holy Roman Empire, capturing Vienna in 1485.
- He assembled the Bibliotheca Corviniana, described as the largest library in Europe north of the Alps and second only to the Vatican Library, employing scribes and scholars to copy classical and contemporary texts; much of it was later dispersed or destroyed after the Ottoman conquest of Hungary.
- He centralized royal justice and taxation, reducing the independent power of the Hungarian nobility, though his reforms were substantially reversed by weaker successors after his death.

## Context & Connections
Matthias Corvinus ruled Hungary in [[Europe]] during the [[Medieval Period]], balancing conflicts with the Ottoman Empire and the Holy Roman Empire. See [[War and Conflict]], [[Art and Ritual]] and [[Science]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Matthias's centralizing reforms depended heavily on his own standing army and personal authority rather than durable institutions, so much of his state-building unraveled within a generation of his death, a pattern common to strong individual rulers who do not also build structures that outlast them.
