## Summary
Rembrandt Harmenszoon van Rijn (15 July 1606 to 4 October 1669) was the greatest artist of the Dutch Golden Age, a painter, etcher and draughtsman. His roughly 100 self-portraits form an extraordinary autobiography, from early fame and wealth to bankruptcy and loss.

## Facts
- Rembrandt was born in Leiden, the ninth child of a prosperous miller. He trained locally and for six months in Amsterdam with Pieter Lastman. About 1631 he moved to Amsterdam.
- The Anatomy Lesson of Dr Nicolaes Tulp (1632) came early in his successful career as a portraitist in the city. In 1634 he married Saskia van Uylenburgh. Three of their children died in infancy; only Titus, born 1641, survived. Saskia died in 1642, the year he completed The Night Watch.
- Debts led to bankruptcy in 1656, and his house and collection were sold. He kept working. His partner Hendrickje Stoffels died in 1663 and Titus in 1668.
- He died on 4 October 1669 and was buried in a rented grave in the Westerkerk.
- He used chiaroscuro to direct attention and mood. His late paintings are built from thick, rough layers applied with brush, palette knife and even fingers.
- As a printmaker he combined etching with drypoint and varied each impression. Key works include The Hundred Guilder Print (about 1647 to 1649) and The Return of the Prodigal Son (about 1661 to 1669).
- He ran a large workshop. Since 1968 the Rembrandt Research Project has reattributed many paintings. Goya is said to have named him one of his three masters.

## Context & Connections
Rembrandt worked in [[Europe]] during the [[Early Modern Period]], in Amsterdam, then a booming commercial capital and home port of the [[Dutch East India Company]]. See [[Art and Ritual]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a market of merchants, guilds and civic companies that paid for group portraits, while the same painter's late work found far fewer buyers, yet he kept working through bankruptcy and bereavement. The self-portraits are memory-outsourcing of a private face on a scale unusual for the age. Because he ran a large workshop, the line between his hand and his pupils' is a scholarly judgment that has moved since 1968, so the "Rembrandt" of any decade is partly a decision.
