## Summary
Sequoyah (about 1770 to 1843) was a Cherokee silversmith who created the Cherokee syllabary, a writing system for the Cherokee language, completed by 1821. It is one of the few writing systems whose invention by a single known person is well documented.

## Facts
- Sequoyah was born about 1770 in the Cherokee town of Tuskegee, in present-day Tennessee; the birth year is approximate.
- He did not read or write English. He began work on a writing system for Cherokee about 1809 and completed it by 1821.
- The syllabary has 85 characters (86 in early versions), each standing for a syllable.
- The system spread rapidly among the Cherokee. The Cherokee Phoenix, a newspaper printed in Cherokee and English, began publication in 1828 and was the first Native newspaper in the United States.
- He later lived among Cherokee who had moved west of the Mississippi River. He died in August 1843 in Mexico while searching for a group of Cherokee said to have settled there.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a man who could not read any writing designed a mark-code for his own sound-code, and that according to the accounts a whole people took it up within a few years, a rare case where the inventor of a writing system is known. That it spread among a people under pressure on their land shows memory-outsourcing serving to hold a community together and to deal with a state that ran on paper. The reading would weaken if evidence showed he drew more on existing writing systems than the accounts suggest.
