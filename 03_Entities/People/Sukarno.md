## Summary
Sukarno (1901 to 1970) was the leader of Indonesia's independence movement and its first president. He proclaimed independence in 1945, ruled until 1967, and was removed from power after the killings of 1965 and 1966.

## Facts
- Sukarno was born on 6 June 1901 in Surabaya, in the Dutch East Indies, and died on 21 June 1970 in Jakarta, under house arrest.
- He founded the Indonesian National Party in 1927. The Dutch arrested him in 1929 and released him in 1931, then exiled him to Flores in 1934 and to Sumatra in 1938.
- He cooperated with the Japanese occupation from 1942 to 1945, which forced millions into labor and whose food requisitions contributed to a famine that killed over a million people in Java in 1944 and 1945.
- On 1 June 1945 he presented Pancasila, five founding principles for the state, and on 17 August 1945 he proclaimed independence, becoming president the next day.
- He hosted the Bandung Conference of Asian and African states in 1955 and introduced "Guided Democracy" in 1959, ending parliamentary rule.
- After the 30 September Movement of 1965, an army-led campaign killed between 500,000 and over a million people, mostly communists and alleged sympathizers. General Suharto took power in 1966 and 1967.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a nation spread over thousands of islands was named and given five founding principles before its state existed, story-glue in its plainest form. He tried to hold nationalist, religious and communist camps under one roof, and the arrangement broke in 1965 in mass killing. A specialist could argue that army and foreign pressures made the collapse of Guided Democracy likely whatever he chose, which would weaken a reading that places the outcome in his leadership.
