## Summary
Rumi (Jalal ad-Din Muhammad Rumi, 1207 to 1273) was a Persian-language poet and Sufi teacher who spent his adult life in Konya, in Anatolia. His long poem, the Masnavi, and his lyric poems are among the most widely read works of Persian literature.

## Facts
- Rumi was born in 1207 in Balkh or nearby Wakhsh, in present-day Afghanistan or Tajikistan; sources differ. His family moved west, and he was living in Konya, in the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum, by about 1228.
- He trained as a scholar and preacher like his father, Baha ad-Din Walad, and taught in Konya.
- In 1244 he met a wandering mystic, Shams of Tabriz. Their friendship changed his life, and he began to write poetry. Shams disappeared about 1248, and accounts differ, with some saying he was killed and others that he simply moved on.
- He wrote the Masnavi, a long poem in six books, and thousands of lyric poems collected in the Divan of Shams. He wrote mostly in Persian.
- The Masnavi runs to some 25,000 to 27,000 couplets, with counts differing by source, composed over his last decade or so at the request of his disciple Husam al-Din Chalabi, who wrote the verses down as Rumi recited them. Some later readers called it "the Quran in Persian".
- Fihi Ma Fihi ("In It What Is In It") is a prose collection of his informal talks to disciples, valued as the clearest record of how he taught.
- He died in Konya on 17 December 1273. His followers founded the Mevlevi order, known for a ritual of whirling.
- Loose English adaptations from the late 20th century onward made him, by some measures, one of the best-selling poets in the United States.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a personal loss became the source of a body of verse that a community then turned into ritual, the whirling of the Mevlevi order, and a lineage. It shows one relationship carried across centuries by both sound-code and practice. A specialist could argue that later followers shaped the account of Shams and of Rumi's life, which would weaken any reading that treats the biography as settled.
