## Summary
Knud Rasmussen (1879 to 1933) was a Danish-Greenlandic explorer and ethnographer who recorded Inuit oral traditions and led the Fifth Thule Expedition (1921 to 1924), a dog-sled journey from Greenland across Arctic Canada to Alaska.

## Facts
- Knud Rasmussen was born on 7 June 1879 in Jakobshavn (Ilulissat), Greenland, to a Danish missionary father and a mother of part Greenlandic Inuit descent, and grew up speaking Greenlandic.
- In 1910 he and Peter Freuchen founded the Thule Trading Station at Cape York in northwest Greenland, which helped fund his expeditions.
- On the Fifth Thule Expedition he traveled with Greenlandic Inuit companions across northern Canada to Alaska. The expedition's archaeologist, Therkel Mathiassen, excavated sites that led to the identification of the Thule culture.
- He collected songs, stories and accounts of belief from the Iglulik and other Inuit groups, and wrote *Across Arctic America* (1927).
- He fell ill on the Seventh Thule Expedition and died in Copenhagen on 21 December 1933.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Rasmussen stood between two kin-lattices, Danish and Greenlandic, and that speaking Greenlandic from childhood let him record songs and accounts an outside visitor might have missed. His route followed a chain of related Inuit communities, a reminder that a language and kin network can run across lines drawn by states. His records depend on Danish trade and expedition funding, so they reflect his interests and informants as well as the communities. Inuit scholars could argue that Inuit-produced accounts deserve more weight than his volumes, which would weaken any reading that treats them as the standard record.
