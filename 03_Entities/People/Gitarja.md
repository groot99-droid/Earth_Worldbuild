## Summary
Tribhuwana Wijayatunggadewi, known as Gitarja, was the third monarch of the Majapahit empire, reigning in Java from about 1328 to 1350; her birth is placed before 1309 and her death about 1372 to 1375, though both dates are uncertain.

## Facts
- She became queen after the assassination of her half-brother Jayanegara in 1328 (the Nagarakretagama gives 1329 for her accession), ruling in the name of her mother, Gayatri Rajapatni, who had formally retired to a Buddhist convent.
- Her chief minister, Gajah Mada, is credited with a famous oath to abstain from certain pleasures until he had brought the surrounding archipelago under Majapahit's authority.
- Under her reign Majapahit forces conquered Bali in 1343, defeating the kingdoms of Pejeng and Bedahulu, a major step in the empire's expansion across the Indonesian archipelago.
- She stepped down in 1350 after her mother's death and was succeeded by her son, Hayam Wuruk, under whom Majapahit reached its greatest territorial extent.

## Context & Connections
Gitarja ruled [[Majapahit]] in [[Southeast Asia]] during the [[Medieval Period]], and her minister's campaigns set up the empire's height under her son. See [[War and Conflict]], [[Trade and Economy]] and [[Religion and Belief]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Gitarja's reign is remembered mainly through the ambitions of her minister rather than any directly recorded decision of her own, a common pattern for royal women whose authority depended on men who did the fighting and the writing. Whether she directed Gajah Mada's campaigns or simply did not stop them cannot be recovered from the surviving sources.
