## Summary
Chico Mendes (Francisco Alves Mendes Filho, 1944 to 1988) was a Brazilian rubber tapper, union leader and environmental activist in the state of Acre. He organized rubber tappers to defend the forest against clearing for cattle ranching and was murdered in December 1988.

## Facts
- Chico Mendes was born on 15 December 1944 near Xapuri, Acre, into a family of rubber tappers (seringueiros).
- He helped found the rural workers' union in Xapuri in 1977 and was among the founders of the Workers' Party in Acre in 1980.
- He organized *empates*, non-violent standoffs in which tappers and their families stood in front of crews clearing forest.
- In 1985 he helped found the National Council of Rubber Tappers, which promoted protected "extractive reserves" where residents could harvest forest products.
- In 1987 he lobbied the Inter-American Development Bank in the United States over highway funding in the Amazon, and received a United Nations Environment Programme Global 500 award.
- He was shot dead at his home in Xapuri on 22 December 1988. The rancher Darly Alves da Silva and his son Darci were convicted of the murder in 1990.
- Extractive reserves became a legal category in Brazil in 1990.

## Context & Connections
Chico Mendes worked in the [[Amazon Basin and South American Lowlands]] during the [[Information Age]], amid the forest clearing of the [[Great Acceleration]]. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Mendes joined a labor tactic, standing bodies in front of chainsaws, to an ecological argument, and that success came in the form of law only after his death. Ranchers described the forest as open land while tappers described it as a home and an income, so each side's claim-line rested on a different account of the same trees. A specialist could argue that extractive reserves have had mixed economic results for residents, which would weaken the claim that his approach protected both forest and livelihoods.
