## Summary
Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti (1900 to 1978) was a Nigerian teacher and campaigner who led the Abeokuta Women's Union in protests against a tax on women and against the local ruler, the Alake of Egbaland, in the 1940s.

## Facts
- Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti was born on 25 October 1900 in Abeokuta, in southwestern Nigeria. She studied there and in Cheshire, England, from 1919 to 1922.
- In 1925 she married the Reverend Israel Oludotun Ransome-Kuti. Their children included the musician Fela Kuti and the physicians Beko and Olikoye Ransome-Kuti.
- She founded the Abeokuta Ladies' Club in 1932. It became the Abeokuta Women's Union about 1946 and is reported to have had about 20,000 members.
- In the late 1940s the union protested a tax on women and the rule of the Alake, Ademola II, who was forced to leave the throne for a time in 1949.
- She helped found the Federation of Nigerian Women's Societies in 1953 and received the Lenin Peace Prize in 1970.
- On 18 February 1977 soldiers stormed her son's compound in Lagos. Her family says she was thrown from an upper-floor window, and she died of her injuries on 13 April 1978. Official accounts blamed unknown soldiers.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a claim-line between a colonial administration, a local king and a body of women, many of them market traders, was redrawn by organized refusal rather than by arms. The 1977 raid shows state force, once met with petitions, turned on her family. A specialist could argue that colonial officials' wish to avoid unrest mattered as much as the union's strength, which would weaken a reading that credits the organization alone.
