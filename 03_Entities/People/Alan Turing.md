## Summary
Alan Turing (1912 to 1954) was a British mathematician who formalized the concept of computation, helped break German ciphers in World War II, and proposed a test for machine intelligence.

## Facts
- In 1936 he described the abstract 'Turing machine', a foundation of computer science.
- At Bletchley Park he worked on breaking the Enigma cipher.
- In 1950 he published a paper proposing what became known as the Turing test.
- He was prosecuted in 1952 for homosexuality, then a crime in Britain, and received a posthumous royal pardon in 2013.

## Context & Connections
Turing's work underlies the [[Digital Computer]] and the [[Digital Revolution]], and his codebreaking affected [[World War II]]. Compare [[Ada Lovelace]].
