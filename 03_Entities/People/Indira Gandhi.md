## Summary
Indira Gandhi (1917 to 1984) was prime minister of India for most of 1966 to 1984. She led India through the 1971 war that created Bangladesh, imposed emergency rule from 1975 to 1977, and was assassinated by her own bodyguards after ordering an army raid on Sikhism's holiest shrine.

## Facts
- She was born on 19 November 1917 in Allahabad, daughter of Jawaharlal Nehru, India's first prime minister, and became prime minister herself in 1966.
- In 1971 India backed the independence of East Pakistan, which became Bangladesh, after a short war with Pakistan.
- On 25 June 1975 she had a state of emergency declared, suspending civil liberties, censoring the press and jailing opponents, until elections in 1977 that she lost.
- She returned to power in 1980. Government policy also pushed the Green Revolution's higher-yield crops during her years in office.
- In June 1984 she ordered Operation Blue Star, a military assault on the Golden Temple in Amritsar to remove armed Sikh militants, which damaged the shrine and angered many Sikhs.
- On 31 October 1984 two of her Sikh bodyguards assassinated her in New Delhi. Riots afterward killed thousands of Sikhs, mostly in Delhi.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a leader who suspended the young republic's own rules in 1975 was, nine years later, killed for ordering force against a meaning-engine's holiest site, and that sanctioned harm against her spread outward into killing among ordinary Sikhs. A specialist could argue that Operation Blue Star addressed an armed occupation of the temple and was not aimed at Sikh belief itself, which would weaken a reading of the raid as simple religious attack.
