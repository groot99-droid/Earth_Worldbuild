## Summary
Jayavarman VII (about 1122 to about 1218) was king of the Khmer Empire from about 1181 until his death. He drove out Cham invaders who had sacked Angkor and then led a large building program that included Angkor Thom and the Bayon.

## Facts
- Champa forces sacked Angkor in 1177. Jayavarman VII defeated them and was crowned about 1181; his birth and death years are estimates.
- Khmer forces later brought parts of Champa under Khmer control.
- He was a Mahayana Buddhist, while most earlier Khmer rulers had favored Hindu cults.
- His projects include Angkor Thom, the Bayon with its towers carved with large faces, Ta Prohm (dedicated to his mother) and Preah Khan (dedicated to his father).
- An inscription of 1186 at Ta Prohm credits him with 102 hospitals; the figure comes from the king's own inscriptions.
- The faces on the Bayon towers are variously read as a Buddhist deity, the king, or both.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Jayavarman VII took power after Angkor was sacked from outside and answered with building on an unusual scale: temples for his parents, hospitals, and a walled city whose towers carry giant faces. His inscriptions present these works as merit-making by a Buddhist king, which also served as a claim to legitimacy. The labor behind them appears in the record as the king's gift, and the workers are almost invisible. A specialist could argue that the program strained Angkor's resources, which would weaken a reading of it as simply benevolent.
