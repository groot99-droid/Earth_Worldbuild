## Summary
Attila (about 406 to 453) was ruler of the Huns from 434, first with his brother Bleda and from about 445 alone. He led Hunnic armies against the Eastern and Western Roman empires, and almost all written sources about him were composed by Greeks and Romans.

## Facts
- Attila's birth year is uncertain, with estimates from the 390s to the early 400s. He became joint ruler in 434 and sole ruler after Bleda died, probably about 445.
- After the Treaty of Margus (435) the Eastern Roman Empire doubled its yearly gold tribute, and after 443 it is reported to have reached 2,100 Roman pounds.
- In 451 he invaded Gaul and fought a Roman and Visigothic army at the Catalaunian Plains. In 452 he invaded northern Italy. He withdrew both times.
- He died in 453 after his wedding to Ildico. Priscus, as reported by Jordanes, describes a severe bleed. A later claim that his wife killed him is rejected by most historians. His empire broke up within a few years.
- Priscus, an envoy who visited his court in 449, left the only known eyewitness description. Attila's capital and tomb have not been found.

## Context & Connections
Attila's power was based on the middle Danube in [[Europe]] during [[Classical Antiquity]], and his campaigns added to the pressures behind the [[Fall of the Western Roman Empire]]. See [[Romans]], [[Byzantine Empire]] and [[War and Conflict]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the Huns left no writing of their own, so a ruler remembered as one of history's great enemies is known only through his opponents. The gold paid to him was an exchange-web run in reverse, payment in return for peace. A specialist could argue that Roman diplomacy and a Germanic revolt, more than any dependence on one man, broke the Hunnic polity, which would weaken a reading of the empire as personal.

## Open Questions
- How far can the accounts of his court be trusted, given that the main eyewitness was an envoy of the empire he was pressing?
- Where were his capital and his grave?
