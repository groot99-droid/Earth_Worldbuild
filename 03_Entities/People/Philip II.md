## Summary
Philip II (1527 to 1598) was King of Spain from 1556 to 1598, ruling an empire that spanned the Iberian Peninsula, the Netherlands, parts of Italy, and vast territories in the Americas and the Philippines, which were named after him.

## Facts
- He inherited Spain, its Italian territories and the Spanish Americas from his father, Charles V, in 1556, and briefly held the English throne jointly through his marriage to Mary I of England from 1554 to 1558.
- He directed a long, costly war to suppress a revolt in the Spanish Netherlands, beginning in 1566, that continued in various phases for decades and was never fully resolved during his reign.
- He sent the Spanish Armada against England in 1588 to support a planned invasion and end English support for Dutch rebels and privateering against Spanish shipping; storms and English naval tactics destroyed much of the fleet.
- He presided over Spain's peak territorial extent while also driving the crown into multiple bankruptcies, in 1557, 1560, 1575 and 1596, from the cost of near-constant warfare funded partly by American silver.

## Context & Connections

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Philip II's reign shows how a flow of American silver, often assumed to guarantee power, instead financed a level of continuous warfare that bankrupted the crown multiple times; wealth extracted from the Americas did not translate into lasting Spanish military dominance in Europe.
