## Summary
Qin Shi Huang (259 to 210 BCE) was the first emperor of a unified China, who standardized writing, currency, and measures and built a large tomb complex including the terracotta army.

## Facts
- He became king of Qin at thirteen and completed the unification of China in 221 BCE.
- He standardized the script, weights and measures, and axle widths.
- Later historians, hostile to the Qin, described book burning and harsh punishments; some of these accounts are debated.
- His tomb complex near Xi'an contains thousands of life-size clay soldiers.

## Context & Connections
See [[Unification of China under Qin]]. His state was inherited and continued by the [[Han Dynasty]].
