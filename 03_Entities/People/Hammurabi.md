## Summary
Hammurabi was the king of Babylon from about 1792 to 1750 BCE, who united much of Mesopotamia and is known for a famous inscribed law code.

## Facts
- The Code of Hammurabi, carved on a stone stele about 1754 BCE, lists 282 laws.
- It sets penalties that vary by social class and includes the principle of proportionate punishment.
- The stele was displayed publicly, making the law visible.
- The original is in the Louvre in Paris.

## Context & Connections
Hammurabi ruled in [[Mesopotamia]] during the [[Bronze Age]], inheriting the writing tradition begun by the [[Sumerians]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer sees a state converting custom into text. Putting law on stone changed what authority could claim: a rule that everyone can read, at least in principle, can bind the ruler as well as the ruled.
