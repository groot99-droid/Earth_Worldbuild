## Summary
Pakal the Great (K'inich Janaab' Pakal, 603 to 683) ruled the Maya city of Palenque, in present-day Chiapas, Mexico, from 615 until his death, a reign of about 68 years.

## Facts
- Pakal was born in 603 and became king at age 12 in 615.
- Palenque's inscriptions name his mother, Ix Sak K'uk', and show her presenting him with the crown at his accession.
- During his reign the Palace complex at Palenque was enlarged and decorated with carved panels and hieroglyphic texts.
- The Temple of the Inscriptions was built as his funerary monument and carries long hieroglyphic panels on Palenque's dynastic history.
- In 1952 Alberto Ruz Lhuillier reached a burial chamber inside it, holding a carved stone sarcophagus and a jade mosaic mask.
- Pakal died in 683, and his son K'inich Kan Bahlam II succeeded him in 684.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Pakal is unusually well known because Palenque's rulers wrote their descent in stone, so his life is known largely from texts he and his heirs commissioned. They give his mother a visible role and rest his authority on the kin-lattice. His tomb, hidden inside a monument that also carries the dynastic text, joins a grief-rite to memory-outsourcing. A specialist could point out that a ruler's own inscriptions are a status-signal as well as a record, which would weaken any reading that takes his reign and lineage at face value.
