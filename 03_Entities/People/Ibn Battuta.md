## Summary
Ibn Battuta (1304 to about 1369) was a Moroccan scholar and traveler whose account, the Rihla, describes journeys of about 117,000 kilometers (73,000 miles) across much of the Old World.

## Facts
- He left Tangier in 1325 for a pilgrimage and kept traveling for about 30 years.
- He visited Arabia, East Africa, Persia, India, Southeast Asia, and the Mali Empire (1352 to 1353).
- His account was dictated to the scholar Ibn Juzayy.
- Historians debate whether he actually visited all of the places he describes.

## Context & Connections
He traveled within the world of the [[Indian Ocean Trade Network]], visiting [[Swahili Coast City-States]] and the [[Mali Empire]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a person could travel most of the known world, and be received as a scholar, because a shared religion and law provided a common frame. The Islamic world worked as one large, connected network.
