## Summary
Marie Curie (1867 to 1934) was a Polish-born physicist and chemist who pioneered research on radioactivity and is the only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences.

## Facts
- She shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel.
- She won the 1911 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for discovering polonium and radium.
- She developed mobile X-ray units for use in World War I hospitals.
- She died of aplastic anemia, likely caused by long exposure to radiation.

## Context & Connections
Her work on radioactivity underlies [[Nuclear Fission]] and the [[Nuclear Age]]. Compare [[Albert Einstein]].
