## Summary
Fu Hao (died about 1200 BCE) was a consort of the Shang king Wu Ding, named in oracle bone inscriptions as a military commander and a ritual leader. Her intact tomb, found in 1976, is among the richest of the Shang period. Her birth year is unknown.

## Facts
- Fu Hao is named in oracle bone inscriptions as a wife of the Shang king Wu Ding, whose reign is dated about 1250 to 1192 BCE, though dates vary by chronology. She is thought to have died about 1200 BCE.
- The inscriptions record her leading military campaigns against neighboring groups and directing ritual sacrifices.
- Her tomb (M5) at Yinxu, the Shang capital site near Anyang in Henan, was found undisturbed in 1976 by a team led by Zheng Zhenxiang.
- It held 468 bronze objects and 755 jade objects, as well as hundreds of bone objects and thousands of cowry shells.
- Inscriptions on many bronzes name her, Fu Hao, or "Mother Xin," which is the basis for identifying the tomb with the woman of the oracle bones.
- Sixteen people and six dogs were buried with her, interpreted as sacrifices.

## Context & Connections
Fu Hao lived under the [[Shang Dynasty]] in [[East Asia]] during the [[Bronze Age]]; her tomb shows the scale of [[Bronze Metallurgy]] in the Shang state. See [[War and Conflict]], [[Religion and Belief]] and [[Kinship and Society]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the earliest written evidence for a woman commanding armies comes from divination records made by her husband for other purposes, records that also ask about her illness and childbirth. Her burial, with a large store of bronze and jade and with human sacrifices, uses a grief-rite to display status. The reading of the tomb and the texts as one life would weaken if the oracle bone Fu Hao and the woman in tomb M5 were different persons.
