## Summary
Gorgo (born about 518 BCE, last attested around 480 BCE) was queen of Sparta as the wife of King Leonidas I, remembered by the historian Herodotus as unusually sharp-witted and politically perceptive for a woman in his account of Greek affairs.

## Facts
- She was the daughter of the Spartan king Cleomenes I and, according to Herodotus, advised him as a child not to trust a bribe offered by an Ionian envoy seeking Spartan military aid.
- Herodotus credits her with detecting a warning about the Persian king Xerxes's invasion plans hidden on a wax-covered writing tablet sent by the exiled Spartan king Demaratus, when other Spartans could not work out its message.
- She was the wife of Leonidas I, who died leading the defense of Thermopylae against the Persian invasion in 480 BCE, the same year in which she is last clearly attested in the sources.
- Nearly everything known of her comes from Herodotus's Histories, written decades after her lifetime, and no independent Spartan record of her actions survives.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Herodotus, writing for a Greek audience that saw Spartan women as unusually outspoken compared to other Greek women, may have shaped Gorgo's anecdotes to fit that reputation as much as to record specific events; the stories flatter both her personal intelligence and a wider Greek stereotype about Spartan women at the same time.
