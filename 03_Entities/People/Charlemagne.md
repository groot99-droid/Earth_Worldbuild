## Summary
Charlemagne (c. 748 to 814) was king of the Franks who conquered much of western and central Europe and was crowned emperor by the pope in Rome on Christmas Day 800.

## Facts
- He became sole king of the Franks in 771.
- He conquered the Lombards in Italy, the Saxons, and parts of Spain's northern border.
- Pope Leo III crowned him emperor on 25 December 800.
- His court promoted a revival of learning, the Carolingian Renaissance, and the spread of a clear minuscule script.
- His empire was divided among his descendants after his death in 814 and, under the Treaty of Verdun in 843, split into three kingdoms.

## Context & Connections
Charlemagne's empire revived the imperial title in the west after the [[Fall of the Western Roman Empire]] and was recognized by, but competed with, the [[Byzantine Empire]]. His successors faced raids from the [[Norse]]. ## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a ruler who could barely write his own name sponsored one of the largest projects of copying texts in the early Middle Ages. Much ancient literature survives because Carolingian scribes copied it in a new, legible script.
