## Summary
Gustav Klimt (14 July 1862 to 6 February 1918) was an Austrian painter and the leader of the Vienna Secession. His golden, patterned paintings of women and love, above all The Kiss, are icons of turn-of-the-century Vienna.

## Facts
- Klimt was born at Baumgarten near Vienna, the second of seven children of a gold engraver. He won a scholarship to the Vienna School of Arts and Crafts in 1876 and, with his brother Ernst and Franz Matsch, painted murals and ceilings for the Burgtheater and the Kunsthistorisches Museum.
- In 1897 he became the first president of the Vienna Secession, a group breaking from the official academy with the motto "To every age its art, to art its freedom".
- His ceiling paintings for the University of Vienna, showing Philosophy, Medicine and Jurisprudence, caused a scandal over nudity and pessimism. He withdrew from the commission, and the paintings were destroyed in a fire in 1945.
- He never married, had a lifelong companionship with the designer Emilie Flöge and fathered several children with models. He supported younger artists such as Egon Schiele and Oskar Kokoschka. He died in Vienna after a stroke.
- In his "golden period" (about 1899 to 1910) he applied real gold and silver leaf, drawing on Byzantine mosaics he saw in Ravenna in 1903, and on Japanese and Egyptian art. Key works include The Kiss (1907 to 1908) and the Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I (1907).
- Many of his works were owned by Jewish collectors and looted by the Nazis. The return of the Adele Bloch-Bauer portrait to Maria Altmann made headlines worldwide. Ronald Lauder bought it in 2006 for a reported $135 million.
- In 2023 Lady with a Fan sold for £85.3 million, a record for a painting at auction in Europe.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a movement whose motto framed art as a claim on institutions, and a painter who left a public commission when it drew scandal, so that a university, a meaning-engine of knowledge, refused images of its own faculties. The gold leaf and the auction records show the status-signal of the object at its extreme. Provenance adds a second biography: paintings taken from Jewish owners by the Nazis and later the subject of restitution cases carry sanctioned harm as part of their history.
