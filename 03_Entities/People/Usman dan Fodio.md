## Summary
Usman dan Fodio (1754 to 1817) was a Fulani scholar and religious leader who led the jihad that created the Sokoto Caliphate.

## Facts
- He taught and preached in the Hausa state of Gobir and wrote widely in Arabic and Fulfulde.
- In 1804 he and his followers left Gobir, and the jihad began in February of that year.
- He is regarded as the founder of the Sokoto Caliphate and its first sultan or caliph.
- His brother and his son helped to administer the new state.

## Context & Connections
See [[Sokoto Caliphate]]. His movement was one of several reformist jihads in West Africa.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the state grew from a program of teaching and reform. The founder's writings, and not only his armies, are the source of the caliphate's identity.
