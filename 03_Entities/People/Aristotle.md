## Summary
Aristotle (384 to 322 BCE) was a Greek philosopher and scientist who studied logic, ethics, politics, physics, and biology, and tutored Alexander of Macedon.

## Facts
- He studied under Plato in Athens and later founded his own school, the Lyceum.
- He wrote on logic, ethics, politics, poetics, physics, and biology.
- His biological work involved careful observation of marine life.
- His works were preserved and extended in the Islamic world and later in Latin Europe.

## Context & Connections
Aristotle worked in [[Athens]] and Macedon among the [[Ancient Greeks]] and taught [[Alexander the Great]]. His works framed European and Islamic scholarship for centuries.
