## Summary
Jadwiga (about 1373 to 1399) was crowned "King" of Poland in 1384 at about age ten, often described as the first woman to rule Poland in her own right, and her marriage to the Grand Duke of Lithuania began a dynastic union between the two states.

## Facts
- She was crowned using the male title "rex" (king) rather than "regina" (queen) to underscore that she held full sovereign authority in her own right, not merely as a royal consort.
- In 1386 she married Jogaila, the pagan Grand Duke of Lithuania, who converted to Christianity and was crowned co-ruler as Wladyslaw II Jagiello, founding the Jagiellonian dynasty and beginning the Christianization of Lithuania.
- She helped fund the restoration of the Krakow Academy, later the Jagiellonian University, through jewellery donated and, under her will, sold to help finance it, helping revive an institution that became a major center of learning in Central Europe.
- She died in 1399, about three and a half weeks after giving birth to a daughter who also died in infancy, four days before her, ending her direct line; she was canonized by the Catholic Church in 1997.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Jadwiga's reign is remembered largely for a marriage alliance and a religious conversion she enabled rather than personal political acts, yet the union of Poland and Lithuania that resulted reshaped Central European power for centuries, an outsized consequence for a reign that ended before she turned thirty.
