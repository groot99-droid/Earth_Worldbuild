## Summary
Trajan (53 to 117 CE) was Roman emperor from 98 to 117, presiding over the empire's greatest territorial extent and a major program of public building and infrastructure across the Roman world.

## Facts
- Born in Hispania, in present-day Spain, he was the first Roman emperor born outside Italy, adopted as heir by the emperor Nerva in 97 after a career as a successful general.
- His conquest of Dacia, commemorated in the reliefs of Trajan's Column in Rome, added a wealthy new province rich in gold mines north of the Danube.
- He extended Roman territory to its largest extent by annexing Armenia and parts of Mesopotamia after a campaign against the Parthian Empire late in his reign, gains his successor Hadrian largely abandoned as unsustainable.
- His building programs included Trajan's Forum and Market in Rome, a new harbor at Ostia, and an extensive network of roads and bridges across the provinces, funded substantially by Dacian gold.

## Context & Connections
Trajan led the [[Romans]] from the [[Mediterranean Basin]] during [[Classical Antiquity]], expanding the empire to its widest extent. See [[War and Conflict]], [[Technology]] and [[Trade and Economy]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Trajan's eastern conquests, which later Roman writers celebrated as the empire's high-water mark, were abandoned within a year of his death by his own successor as indefensible; the empire's territorial peak and its practical limits were reached almost simultaneously.

## Open Questions
