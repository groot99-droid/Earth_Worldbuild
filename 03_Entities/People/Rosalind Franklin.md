## Summary
Rosalind Franklin (1920 to 1958) was a British chemist whose X-ray images of DNA were crucial to the discovery of its double-helix structure.

## Facts
- 'Photo 51', an X-ray image of DNA, was taken in 1952 by Franklin and her student Raymond Gosling.
- Her data were shown to James Watson without her knowledge and helped him and Francis Crick propose the structure in 1953.
- She died of ovarian cancer at 37, four years before Watson, Crick, and Maurice Wilkins received the 1962 Nobel Prize.
- She also did important work on coal and on viruses.
