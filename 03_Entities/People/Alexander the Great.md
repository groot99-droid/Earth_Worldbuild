## Summary
Alexander III of Macedon (356 to 323 BCE) was a military commander who conquered the Persian Empire and spread Greek influence from Egypt to India.

## Facts
- He became king at about twenty after the assassination of his father, Philip II.
- He won major battles at Issus and Gaugamela against the Persian king Darius III.
- His army turned back at the Hyphasis (Beas) river in India.
- He died in Babylon in 323 BCE; his empire was divided among his generals.

## Context & Connections
See [[Alexander's Conquests]]. His campaigns shaped [[Classical Antiquity]] and connected the world of the [[Ancient Greeks]] with that of the [[Achaemenid Persians]].
