## Summary
Hojo Tokimune (1251 to 1284) was the eighth regent (shikken) of Japan's Kamakura shogunate, ruling as its de facto military leader during the Mongol invasions of Japan in 1274 and 1281.

## Facts
- He became regent in 1268 at about age sixteen by Western reckoning (eighteen by traditional counting), shortly after the Mongol court of Kublai Khan first demanded Japan's submission.
- He refused all Mongol diplomatic demands and reportedly had several Mongol envoys executed, a decision that made war effectively certain.
- Japanese defenders repelled the 1274 and 1281 Mongol invasion fleets; in 1281 a great typhoon and, in 1274, traditionally a storm or sudden reverse wind, later remembered as kamikaze ("divine wind"), destroyed large parts of the Mongol fleets after the defenders had already inflicted losses on the invading forces.
- He was a patron of Zen Buddhism, inviting the Chinese monk Wuxue Zuyuan to Kamakura and founding the temple Engaku-ji, reportedly to honor the dead of both sides after the invasions.

## Context & Connections
Hojo Tokimune governed Kamakura Japan in [[East Asia]] during the [[Medieval Period]], defending against the [[Mongol Empire]] at the height of its westward and eastward expansion. See [[War and Conflict]] and [[Religion and Belief]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the "divine wind" traditionally said to have ended both invasions has often overshadowed the more mundane fact that Japanese defenders, organized under Tokimune's government, had already stalled both landings before the storms struck, while the coastal fortifications such as the Hakata Bay wall were built in 1276, after the first invasion. Crediting the weather alone understates the preparation his regency undertook.
