## Summary
Tupaia (about 1725 to 1770) was a priest and navigator from Ra'iatea in the Society Islands who sailed with James Cook on the Endeavour in 1769 and drew a chart of Pacific islands. He died at Batavia in 1770.

## Facts
- Tupaia was born about 1725 on Ra'iatea and became a priest and navigator. After warriors from Borabora took Ra'iatea, he took refuge on Tahiti.
- In July 1769 he joined Cook's Endeavour, usually said to be at Tahiti (sources differ on the place), as a guide and interpreter, with a young servant, Taiata.
- In August 1769 he began a chart of the Pacific with Cook, Joseph Banks and other officers. Sources differ: it is said to show about 130 islands, of which he could name about 74, and it is often described as centered on Ra'iatea.
- The chart did not match European map conventions, and modern scholars have argued that it follows a Polynesian way of organizing islands by voyages and directions.
- On reaching New Zealand in October 1769 he could speak with Māori, whose language is closely related to Tahitian, and he interpreted for the crew.
- He died at Batavia on 20 December 1770, of illness, and Taiata died there in the same month.

## Context & Connections
Tupaia belonged to the [[Polynesians]], whose ancestors made the [[Polynesian Settlement of the Pacific]]. That he could speak with [[Maori]] in New Zealand reflects the shared origin of their languages. See [[Science]] and [[Language and Writing]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Tupaia carried sailing knowledge in memory and moved it onto paper for outsiders, who found it hard to read because it did not use their grid. Two tool-lineages of navigation met on one ship, and the meeting is preserved only because one side wrote things down. The reading would weaken if scholars showed the chart's layout owed more to the European officers who helped draw it than to Tupaia.
