## Summary
Queen Seondeok (birth year unknown, died 647 CE) ruled the Korean kingdom of Silla as its first reigning queen, a period marked by Buddhist temple building, early astronomical observation and continued warfare with the rival kingdoms of Baekje and Goguryeo.

## Facts
- She succeeded her father, King Jinpyeong, in 632 after he left no surviving son, becoming Silla's first female monarch in her own right.
- Her reign saw the construction of Cheomseongdae, often described as one of the oldest surviving astronomical observatories in East Asia, and the Hwangnyongsa nine-story pagoda, built to help protect the kingdom according to Buddhist belief.
- Silla faced repeated attacks from Baekje during her reign, including the loss of border fortresses, and she decided to form a military alliance with Tang China, with her general Kim Yushin and diplomat Kim Chunchu, later King Muyeol, traditionally credited with the negotiations.
- A rebellion by the noble Bidam against her rule broke out in 647 and was suppressed by Kim Yushin and Kim Alcheon shortly after her death that same year, which some accounts link to the unrest.

## Context & Connections
Seondeok ruled in [[East Asia]] during the [[Medieval Period]], and the Tang alliance her court pursued eventually enabled Silla's unification of most of the Korean peninsula after her reign. See [[Religion and Belief]], [[Science]] and [[Kinship and Society]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that later chroniclers, writing under a dynasty uneasy about female rule, framed Seondeok's reign partly through omens and prophecy rather than policy, which can make her recorded wisdom look more like folklore than governance. The observatory and pagoda, built objects rather than reported speech, are harder to dismiss and point to a court capable of sustained, costly projects.
