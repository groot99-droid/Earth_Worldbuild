## Summary
Jane Austen (1775 to 1817) was an English novelist whose six completed novels of courtship, inheritance and class anxiety among the gentry remain among the most widely read in the language. All were published anonymously in her lifetime.

## Facts
- Austen was born in 1775 in Steventon, Hampshire, the seventh of eight children of a country clergyman, in a family that encouraged reading, amateur theatre and writing.
- Sense and Sensibility appeared in 1811, credited only to "A Lady", followed by Pride and Prejudice (1813), Mansfield Park (1814), Emma (1815) and, after her death, Northanger Abbey and Persuasion (1817).
- Only close family knew of her authorship. She fell ill, probably with Addison's disease though the cause is debated, and died in 1817 at 41. Her authorship was publicly acknowledged in a biographical notice her brother attached to her last two novels.
- She worked within a deliberately narrow social canvas, mainly the English gentry, courtship, income and inheritance, rather than the wider political events of her era.
- Her distinctive technique is free indirect discourse: narrating in the third person while letting the prose absorb a character's own irony or blind spots, so the reader senses a character's error before the character does.
- Her novels have remained continuously in print and are widely adapted for film, television and stage. Her reputation grew after her death rather than during her modest anonymous success.

## Context & Connections
Austen wrote in [[Europe]] in the [[Industrial Age]], during the [[Napoleonic Wars]], but kept to the gentry's household world. See [[Art and Ritual]], [[Language and Writing]] and [[Kinship and Society]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes a body of fiction in which the kin-lattice, marriage, inheritance and income, is the whole plot, so that the machinery of who may marry whom and who inherits is made a subject of comedy rather than law. Her authorship was hidden while she lived, and her fame is a later assignment. The claim that she chose a narrow canvas over the wars of her age is an interpretation of her silence, and a reader could equally see the wars as absent because the household itself was her subject.
