## Summary
Eleanor Roosevelt (1884 to 1962) was First Lady of the United States from 1933 to 1945 and afterward a diplomat who chaired the United Nations commission that drafted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

## Facts
- She was born on 11 October 1884 in New York City and married Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1905.
- As First Lady she redefined the role, holding her own press conferences, writing a daily newspaper column, and speaking for civil rights and the poor, sometimes publicly differing with her husband's policies.
- After his death in 1945, President Truman appointed her a delegate to the new United Nations in December 1945.
- She chaired the UN Commission on Human Rights, which drafted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, adopted by the General Assembly on 10 December 1948. She called this her greatest achievement.
- She later chaired a presidential commission on the status of women under President Kennedy.
- She died on 7 November 1962 in New York City.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a document meant to bind every state to common claim-lines around a person's treatment was drafted under the chairmanship of a woman with no formal power beyond her office, relying on public standing built as a president's wife. The declaration is story-glue with no enforcement behind it, only the weight of near-unanimous agreement. A specialist could argue that the Declaration's later adoption by states party to it commits them to little in practice, which would weaken a reading of 1948 as a settled achievement rather than a beginning.
