## Summary
Hypatia (about 350 to 415 CE) was a philosopher, mathematician, and astronomer in Alexandria, who taught many students and was murdered by a mob.

## Facts
- She was the daughter of the mathematician Theon and taught Neoplatonist philosophy.
- She is credited with commentaries on mathematical works; the details are uncertain.
- In 415 she was killed by a Christian mob during a political dispute between the Roman governor Orestes and Bishop Cyril.
- Later writers turned her into a symbol, so historical and legendary accounts are mixed.

## Context & Connections
Hypatia worked in Roman Egypt, in the [[Nile Valley]], at the end of [[Classical Antiquity]]. She continued the tradition of [[Aristotle]] and his successors.
