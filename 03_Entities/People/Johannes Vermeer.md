## Summary
Johannes Vermeer (1632 to 1675) was a painter of Delft in the Dutch Republic. About 35 paintings are accepted as his, mostly quiet interiors lit by daylight from a window. Almost forgotten for 200 years after his death, he is now among the most famous painters in the world.

## Facts
- Vermeer was baptised in Delft on 31 October 1632. His father was a silk weaver who also dealt in art and ran an inn. Nothing is known of Vermeer's training.
- In 1653 he married Catharina Bolnes, a Catholic from a wealthier family, and probably converted. The same year he joined Delft's Guild of St Luke, which later elected him one of its leaders several times.
- The couple lived with Catharina's mother and had 15 children, 11 of whom survived. He also worked as an art dealer, and his slow output was bought largely by one local patron, Pieter van Ruijven.
- In 1672, the Dutch Republic's "Disaster Year", France invaded and the art market collapsed. He died in 1675, aged 43, leaving his family deep in debt.
- He used costly natural ultramarine, ground from lapis lazuli, even in shadows. Key works include The Milkmaid (about 1657 to 1658), View of Delft (about 1660 to 1661) and Girl with a Pearl Earring (about 1665).
- Some features of his paintings have led scholars to argue that he used a camera obscura. The question remains debated.
- In the 1860s the critic Théophile Thoré-Bürger rediscovered him. In the 1940s Han van Meegeren sold a fake Vermeer to Hermann Göring. A 2023 Rijksmuseum exhibition of 28 of his works sold out within days.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the calm of the pictures sits beside a household of 15 children, debt and a collapsing art market, so the serenity was made under pressure. That he was forgotten for two centuries and then rediscovered shows that fame is assigned by later readers. Whether he used a camera obscura is a question about a tool-lineage in painting that remains unsettled, and forgeries such as van Meegeren's show how high the status-signal of his name has become.
