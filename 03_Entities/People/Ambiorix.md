## Summary
Ambiorix (active 54 to 53 BCE) was co-ruler of the Eburones, a Belgic tribe in what is now eastern Belgium, remembered for destroying a Roman legion during a revolt against Julius Caesar's occupation of Gaul.

## Facts
- He shared kingship of the Eburones with an older co-ruler, Cativolcus, an arrangement Caesar's own account describes but does not fully explain.
- In the winter of 54 BCE he lured a Roman force under Quintus Titurius Sabinus and Lucius Aurunculeius Cotta out of its fortified camp with false promises of safe passage, then ambushed and destroyed it, killing both commanders.
- Caesar responded with a campaign of deliberate devastation against the Eburones intended to eliminate the tribe entirely, but Ambiorix himself escaped capture and vanishes from the historical record after 53 BCE.
- Nearly everything known about him comes from Caesar's own Commentarii de Bello Gallico, written to justify Caesar's conduct of the war to a Roman audience.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Ambiorix survives in history almost entirely through the writing of the general whose legion he destroyed, an author with every incentive to portray the ambush as treacherous deceit rather than a defensible tactic of war waged by an occupied people. Ambiorix's own reasoning and any account other than Caesar's are lost.
