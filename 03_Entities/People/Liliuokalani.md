## Summary
Liliuokalani (1838 to 1917) was the last monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaii, reigning from 1891 until she was deposed in January 1893 by a group of residents backed by United States troops.

## Facts
- Liliuokalani was born in Honolulu on 2 September 1838 and died there on 11 November 1917.
- She succeeded her brother Kalākaua on 29 January 1891 and was the kingdom's only reigning queen.
- In January 1893 she moved to issue a constitution restoring royal powers reduced by the 1887 "Bayonet Constitution."
- On 17 January 1893 a Committee of Safety of mostly American and European residents overthrew her while United States Marines were ashore. She yielded to the United States government to avoid bloodshed and asked it to reinstate her.
- After a failed royalist uprising she was arrested and signed an abdication in January 1895.
- Native Hawaiians submitted mass petitions against annexation in 1897, and the United States annexed Hawaii in 1898.
- She composed songs, including "Aloha ʻOe," and published a memoir, *Hawaii's Story by Hawaii's Queen*, in 1898.
- In 1993 the United States Congress apologized to Native Hawaiians for the overthrow, carried out with the participation of agents and citizens of the United States.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Liliuokalani defended her kingdom's claim-line with the instruments her opponents also used: a written constitution, diplomatic appeals, and a published memoir. Her songs carried attachment to place and people in a form that outlasted the polity. The 1993 apology shows a claim-line redrawn by force being named as wrongful without being redrawn back. The reading of her as the main carrier of the resistance would weaken if the 1897 petitions show it rested chiefly on Native Hawaiian organizations.
