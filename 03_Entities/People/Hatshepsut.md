## Summary
Hatshepsut was an Egyptian ruler of the 18th Dynasty who held the title of pharaoh, sharing the throne with Thutmose III, until her death in 1458 BCE. Her reign is traditionally dated 1479 to 1458 BCE, and she is one of the first women known to have held the office.

## Facts
- She first acted as regent for her young stepson Thutmose III, then took the title of pharaoh around the seventh year of his reign (about 1473 BCE), sharing his regnal count.
- She sponsored a trading expedition to Punt, on the Red Sea coast of Africa.
- Her mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari is a major monument.
- Many of her images were later removed or defaced, possibly after her death.

## Context & Connections
Hatshepsut is a figure of the [[Ancient Egyptians]] in the [[Bronze Age]]. Her reign shows how kingship in the [[Nile Valley]] could accommodate a woman ruler.
