## Summary
Pedro II (1825 to 1891) was Emperor of Brazil from 1831 to 1889, presiding over six decades of relative political stability, territorial integrity, and gradual abolition of slavery before being deposed in a republican coup.

## Facts
- He became emperor at age five after his father abdicated, ruling under regents until being declared of age early, in 1840, to stabilize a period of regional revolts.
- His government fought the Paraguayan War from 1864 to 1870 alongside Argentina and Uruguay, the deadliest interstate war in Latin American history, which devastated Paraguay's population.
- He personally supported gradual abolition of slavery, and his daughter and regent, Princess Isabel, signed the Golden Law abolishing slavery outright in 1888, the last country in the Americas to do so.
- Brazilian military officers, landowners angered by abolition, and republican advocates combined to depose him in a bloodless coup in November 1889, ending the monarchy and sending him into exile in Europe, where he died in 1891.

## Context & Connections

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Pedro II was deposed not despite his support for abolition but partly because of it: the same landowning class that had backed the monarchy for decades turned against it once slavery, the institution their wealth depended on, was ended. A ruler's popular reforms can cost him the support of the people who kept him in power.
