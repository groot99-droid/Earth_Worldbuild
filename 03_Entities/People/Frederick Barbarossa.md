## Summary
Frederick I, called Barbarossa ("Redbeard"; about 1122 to 1190), was Holy Roman Emperor from 1155 until his death, and one of medieval Europe's most powerful rulers, spending much of his reign contesting control of northern Italy.

## Facts
- Elected King of Germany in 1152, he was crowned Holy Roman Emperor by the pope in 1155, and repeatedly invaded Italy to assert imperial authority over its wealthy independent city-states.
- The Lombard League, an alliance of northern Italian cities backed by the papacy, defeated his forces at the Battle of Legnano in 1176, forcing him to recognize significant city autonomy in the Peace of Constance of 1183.
- He arranged the marriage of his son Henry to Constance of Sicily, a match that later brought the Kingdom of Sicily under his family's control.
- He drowned in 1190 while crossing a river in Anatolia during the Third Crusade, before reaching the Holy Land; his death caused much of his army to disband or turn back rather than continue the campaign.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Barbarossa's defeat at Legnano, a rare and decisive loss for an emperor to city militias rather than a rival monarch, shows the limits of imperial authority once wealthy urban communities organized collectively. His accidental death crossing a river, not a battle, then ended a Crusade that a stronger start could not save from being cut short by chance.
