## Summary
Frida Kahlo (1907 to 1954) was a Mexican painter, best known for small, vivid self-portraits made in Mexico City. She worked through years of pain after a bus accident in 1925 and became a global cultural icon after her death.

## Facts
- Kahlo was born on 6 July 1907 in Coyoacán, now part of Mexico City. She often gave 1910, the year the Mexican Revolution began, as her birth year.
- A bus accident on 17 September 1925 broke her spine, pelvis and right leg and ended her plan to study medicine. She began painting during her recovery.
- She married the muralist Diego Rivera in 1929, divorced him in 1939 and remarried him in 1940.
- Estimates of her output run from fewer than 150 to about 200 paintings, most of them self-portraits, including The Two Fridas (1939) and The Broken Column (1944).
- Her first solo show was at the Julien Levy Gallery in New York in 1938. In 1939 the Louvre bought her painting The Frame, the first work by a Mexican artist in its collection.
- She died in Mexico City on 13 July 1954, officially of a pulmonary embolism. Her work was little known until art historians rediscovered it in the late 1970s.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a painter made her own body the subject of most of her work and moved her birth year to 1910, tying her life to a nation's revolution. Since the late 1970s her image has served as story-glue for movements that did not exist in her lifetime. A specialist could argue that this later fame flattens a private, ambivalent artist into an emblem, which would weaken any reading of the icon as what she intended.
