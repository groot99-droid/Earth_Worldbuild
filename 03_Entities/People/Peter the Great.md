## Summary
Peter I, called Peter the Great (1672 to 1725), ruled Russia from 1682, jointly at first and alone from 1696, until 1725, driving a sweeping program of westernizing reform and transforming Russia into a major European power.

## Facts
- He traveled incognito through Western Europe in 1697 to 1698 on the "Grand Embassy," working for a time as a shipwright in the Netherlands to study European shipbuilding and technology firsthand.
- He fought a long war against Sweden, the Great Northern War from 1700 to 1721, winning a decisive victory at the Battle of Poltava in 1709 and gaining territory on the Baltic Sea that made Russia a major naval power.
- He founded the city of St. Petersburg in 1703 on newly won Baltic territory, building it as a planned European-style capital and moving the seat of government there from Moscow.
- He reorganized the Russian government, military, and church administration along Western European lines, and required Russian nobles to adopt Western dress, in a well-known instance personally cutting off boyars' traditional long beards to symbolize the change.

## Context & Connections
Peter the Great ruled Russia in [[Europe]] during the [[Early Modern Period]], reorienting the country toward Western European models. See [[Technology]], [[War and Conflict]] and [[Trade and Economy]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Peter's reforms were imposed from the top down, often coercively, on a nobility and church that had not asked for Westernization, a pattern that produced fast institutional change but left the underlying tension between imposed modernization and existing tradition unresolved for later Russian history to work through.
