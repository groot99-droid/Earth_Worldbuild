## Summary
Mirabai (traditionally about 1498 to about 1546) was a Rajput princess from Rajasthan and one of the best-known poet-saints of the Bhakti movement, devoted to Krishna. Almost nothing about her life is documented, and the surviving poems were written down long after her time.

## Facts
- By tradition Mirabai was born about 1498 at Kudki in Rajasthan, into the Rathore Rajput family of Merta, and died about 1546 or 1547. Both dates come from later tradition.
- Tradition says she married Bhoj Raj, heir of Mewar, in 1516, and that he died about 1521 of battle wounds.
- Her poems (padas) are in Rajasthani and related Hindi, and address Krishna in the language of love and separation.
- Tradition credits her with a very large number of poems. Scholars think only a few hundred may be authentic, and only two are known from manuscripts older than the early eighteenth century.
- Stories that her in-laws tried to poison her, and that she vanished into an image of Krishna, are hagiographic legends. The earliest records, from the seventeenth century, do not mention her marriage or in-laws.

## Context & Connections
Mirabai lived in [[South Asia]] during the [[Early Modern Period]], as the [[Mughal Empire]] replaced the [[Delhi Sultanate]] in northern India in 1526. See [[Religion and Belief]] and [[Art and Ritual]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a figure who left almost no contemporary trace has become one of the most widely sung voices of her tradition. The songs and legends describe a woman who places devotion to a god above the duties of marriage and household, a claim about the kin-lattice that singers have restated for five centuries. A specialist could argue that a single historical Mirabai may never be recoverable, which would weaken any reading of the poems as her biography.
