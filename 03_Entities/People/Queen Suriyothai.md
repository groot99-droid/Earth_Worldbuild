## Summary
Queen Suriyothai was a consort of King Maha Chakkraphat of the Ayutthaya kingdom, in present-day Thailand, traditionally said to have died in 1548 defending her husband on elephant-back during a Burmese invasion. Her birth year is traditionally given as 1511, and her story rests on Siamese chronicles alone.

## Facts
- She was a consort of Maha Chakkraphat, who became king of Ayutthaya in 1546 or 1548, as sources differ.
- In 1548 the Burmese king Tabinshwehti invaded Ayutthaya. Siamese chronicles say Suriyothai, disguised and riding an elephant, placed herself between her husband and an enemy commander and was killed by his blow.
- No Burmese chronicle records this episode, and her historicity is questioned. Thai accounts add that her daughter, Princess Boromdilok, was also mortally wounded.
- A memorial stupa, the Chedi Si Suriyothai, was built by Maha Chakkraphat at Wat Suan Luang Sopsawan on the bank of the Chao Phraya near Ayutthaya, and is traditionally associated with the site of her death.
- Thai nationalist commemoration of her grew in the twentieth century, including a widely seen 2001 film.

## Context & Connections
Queen Suriyothai's story is set in [[Southeast Asia]] at the start of the [[Early Modern Period]], in the [[Ayutthaya Kingdom]] during its wars with Burma. See [[War and Conflict]] and [[Kinship and Society]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a death recorded only by the side that claims a battlefield can still outlast the state that recorded it, becoming a grief-rite performed anew by each generation that builds or visits her memorial. A national retelling three centuries later shows ancestor-weight added long after the fact. A specialist could argue that the story conflates an unnamed retainer's death with the queen's, which would weaken any reading of Suriyothai as a documented individual rather than a chronicle figure later fixed by nationalist memory.
