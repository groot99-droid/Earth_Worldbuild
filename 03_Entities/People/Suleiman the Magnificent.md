## Summary
Suleiman the Magnificent (1494 to 1566) was Ottoman sultan from 1520 to 1566, presiding over the empire's greatest territorial extent, military power, and legal and cultural achievement.

## Facts
- His armies captured Belgrade in 1521 and Rhodes in 1522, then advanced deep into Central Europe, defeating and killing the King of Hungary at the Battle of Mohacs in 1526, though a first siege of Vienna in 1529 failed to take the city.
- Known by the epithet Kanuni (the Lawgiver), which is absent from sixteenth and seventeenth-century Ottoman sources and may date only from the early 18th century, he issued a major legal code harmonizing Ottoman civil law with Islamic religious law across the empire's diverse territories.
- His navies under admirals such as Barbarossa Hayreddin Pasha contested Mediterranean control with Spain and its allies, and Ottoman forces also pushed into Persia and Mesopotamia, capturing Baghdad in 1534.
- His reign saw a major flourishing of Ottoman architecture under the chief architect [[Mimar Sinan]], and of court poetry, painting, and decorative arts, centered on Istanbul.

## Context & Connections
Suleiman led the [[Ottoman Empire]] from [[Levant and Anatolia]] during the [[Early Modern Period]], extending its reach across three continents. See [[War and Conflict]], [[Religion and Belief]] and [[Trade and Economy]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that Suleiman's failed 1529 siege of Vienna, rather than his many successes, marked the practical limit of Ottoman expansion into Central Europe for the rest of his reign; the empire's reputation for irresistible advance rested on decades of victories that stopped, in the end, at a single unsuccessful siege.
