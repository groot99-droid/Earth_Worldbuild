## Summary
Ho Chi Minh (1890 to 1969) was a Vietnamese revolutionary who led the movement against French rule, headed the provisional government in 1945, and became the first president of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in 1946, an office he held until his death during the Vietnam War.

## Facts
- He was born on 19 May 1890 in Nghệ An province, French-ruled Vietnam, as Nguyễn Sinh Cung, and later used many names, including Nguyễn Ái Quốc and, from about 1943, Hồ Chí Minh.
- He left Vietnam in 1911 and lived in Britain, France, the Soviet Union and China. In early 1930, in Hong Kong, he chaired the merger of Vietnamese communist groups into one party.
- He returned to Vietnam in 1941 to lead the Việt Minh independence front, and on 2 September 1945 in Hanoi read the declaration of independence, opening by quoting the American and French declarations of rights.
- The First Indochina War ended with the Việt Minh victory at Điện Biên Phủ in 1954. Vietnam was partitioned at the 17th parallel, and the 1956 reunification elections were never held.
- In the North's 1953 to 1956 land reform, many people were wrongly executed, with estimates from about a thousand to tens of thousands; he apologized in 1956 for serious errors.
- He died in Hanoi on 2 September 1969. Saigon was renamed Ho Chi Minh City after reunification in 1975.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a man who used dozens of names became the named symbol of a nation, and his declaration borrowed the mark-code of the powers he opposed. His name now marks the largest city of the country he did not live to see reunified. A specialist could argue that party and army leaders, more than one man, drove decisions in the 1960s, which would weaken a reading that centers his person.
