## Summary
The Arabian Peninsula is a large, largely arid peninsula between the Red Sea and the Persian Gulf. It was a corridor for trade and the origin of Islam.

## Facts
- Incense and spice caravans linked southern Arabia with the Mediterranean.
- Islam emerged in the Hejaz in the early 7th century; see [[Rise of Islam]] and [[Muhammad]].
- Monsoon-driven sea trade connected the peninsula with East Africa and South Asia.

## Context & Connections
The peninsula sits between Africa and Asia and shaped the growth of the [[Abbasid Caliphate]] and later Islamic states.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a sparse region produced a movement that within a century reached from Iberia to Central Asia. Population size and influence are not the same thing.
