## Summary
Sub-Saharan Africa is the region south of the Sahara. It is the birthplace of the hominin lineage and of Homo sapiens, and it holds the greatest human genetic and linguistic diversity.

## Facts
- Fossil evidence places the origins of [[Homo sapiens]] in Africa about 300,000 years ago.
- The [[Bantu Expansion]] spread Bantu languages across much of central, eastern and southern Africa.
- Major states included [[Kingdom of Aksum]], [[Mali Empire]], and the builders of [[Great Zimbabwe]].
- Trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean trade linked the region to Eurasia; see [[Timbuktu]].

## Context & Connections
The region is central to [[Divergence of the Hominin Lineage]], [[Out of Africa Dispersal]], and the [[Atlantic Slave Trade]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the species' longest history happened here. Every non-African population is, in genetic terms, a subset of African diversity.
