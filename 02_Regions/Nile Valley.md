## Summary
The Nile Valley is the narrow, fertile corridor of the Nile River through northeastern Africa, home to one of the earliest states.

## Facts
- The Nile floods annually, depositing silt that supported dense farming in a desert setting.
- Egypt was unified about 3100 BCE; see [[Unification of Egypt]].
- Nile Valley peoples included the [[Ancient Egyptians]] and, to the south, Nubian kingdoms.
- Monuments such as [[Giza Necropolis]] date from the third millennium BCE.

## Context & Connections
The valley links Africa with the Mediterranean and the Levant, and it was an early center of administrative writing.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer sees the Nile as a natural clock: predictable flooding produced predictable calendars, and predictable calendars supported large, centralized states.
