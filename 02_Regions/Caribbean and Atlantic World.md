## Summary
The Caribbean and Atlantic World is the Caribbean basin together with the Atlantic coasts of the Americas, Europe, and Africa that were linked by shipping, trade, and forced migration from the 15th century.

## Facts
- The Caribbean islands were settled by Indigenous peoples, including the Taino and Kalinago, several thousand years before 1492.
- Columbus reached the Caribbean in 1492; Indigenous populations collapsed within decades through disease, violence, and forced labor.
- Sugar plantations worked by enslaved Africans made the region among the most valuable in the early modern world.
- It was the setting of the [[Haitian Revolution]].

## Context & Connections
The region is the core arena of the [[Columbian Exchange]] and the [[Atlantic Slave Trade]], and of the movements to end slavery; see [[Abolition of Slavery]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer sees a zone created by connection: an ocean crossing that turned islands into a hinge between three continents. Its populations, languages, and foods are largely products of that forced mixing.
