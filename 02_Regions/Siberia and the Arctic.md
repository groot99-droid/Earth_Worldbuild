## Summary
Siberia and the Arctic are the northern lands of Asia from the Urals to the Pacific, and the Arctic coasts beyond, where cold and permafrost shaped both human ways of life and the preservation of the past.

## Facts
- Some of the oldest known human genomes come from Siberia; see [[Mal'ta and the Ancient North Eurasians]].
- Permafrost and ice preserved frozen tombs of the [[Scythians]] in the Altai.
- The Yakut (Sakha), Evenki, Chukchi, and many other peoples developed herding, hunting, and fishing economies; see [[Yakut (Sakha)]].
- Russia expanded across Siberia to the Pacific between 1582 and 1639; see [[Russian Expansion into Siberia]].

## Context & Connections
Siberia borders the [[Central Asian Steppe]] and [[East Asia]], and was linked to [[North America]] by Beringia during the [[Last Glacial Maximum]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that the coldest lands of the planet hold some of the best records of the species' past, since cold preserves what warmth destroys.
