## Summary
Mesopotamia is the land between and around the Tigris and Euphrates rivers, in present-day Iraq and parts of neighboring countries. It is a birthplace of cities and writing.

## Facts
- Irrigated farming supported some of the world's first cities, such as [[Uruk]].
- Cuneiform writing appeared here about 3400 to 3200 BCE; see [[Invention of Writing]].
- Early societies included the [[Sumerians]], later joined by Akkadian, Babylonian and Assyrian states.
- The region later became the center of the [[Abbasid Caliphate]].

## Context & Connections
See [[The Urban Revolution]] and [[Hammurabi]]. Mesopotamia is part of the wider Fertile Crescent, along with the [[Levant and Anatolia]].

## Observer's Reading
The Observer treats Mesopotamia as a laboratory for large-scale coordination: bookkeeping, law, and standardized measurement all appear early here.
