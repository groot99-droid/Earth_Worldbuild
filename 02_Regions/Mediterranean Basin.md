## Summary
The Mediterranean Basin is the sea and its surrounding coasts. It was one of history's most connected zones of trade, cultural exchange, and conflict.

## Facts
- The sea connects Europe, Africa, and Asia.
- Seafaring cultures included the [[Minoans]], [[Phoenicians]], [[Ancient Greeks]] and [[Romans]].
- Rome eventually ruled the entire coastline; see [[Rome]].
- The basin saw both the [[Bronze Age Collapse]] and the [[Fall of the Western Roman Empire]].

## Context & Connections
The sea served as a highway, and its cities, including [[Athens]] and [[Constantinople]], were nodes of exchange.

## Observer's Reading
The Observer notes that a shared inland sea acted like a village square for very different peoples, so it generated both intense trade and intense conflict.
